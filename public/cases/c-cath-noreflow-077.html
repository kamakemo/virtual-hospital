🚨 Clinical Presentation & Triage

🚨 YOU ARE HERE — 11:12, Cath Lab activationA 63-year-old arrives with 90 minutes of crushing chest pain, inferior ST elevation, and a heavy, thrombotic RCA occlusion. You wire it, aspirate, and stent — and then the screen betrays you: the vessel is open, but the contrast will not move. Before we get to that crisis (S5–S9), start where the case began: the STEMI that put him on the table.

🚦 Risk stratification & the indication

An inferior STEMI is a clinical + ECG diagnosis mandating immediate reperfusion — primary PCI within 120 minutes of first medical contact. Two features here foreshadow trouble: a dominant RCA culprit (supplies the SA and AV nodes → bradyarrhythmia and the Bezold–Jarisch reflex), and a late presenter with heavy thrombus burden — the single strongest set-up for the phenomenon this whole case is about: no-reflow.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A big thrombotic clot in a late-presenting RCA is a no-reflow trap. Open it — but respect the microcirculation downstream, because that is what actually keeps the muscle alive."

📋 Pre-Procedure Workup & Planning

🧾 The 60-second pre-PCI checklist (do NOT delay reperfusion for it)

  • ✍️ Consent — emergency/verbal if peri-arrest; document risks (bleeding, stroke, dissection, contrast nephropathy, no-reflow, emergency CABG, death).
  • 🫀 Anticipate the RCA physiology — inferior/RV STEMI is prone to bradycardia and hypotension; have atropine, fluids, vasopressors and a transvenous-pacing plan ready BEFORE you cross the lesion.
  • 🕸️ Flag the no-reflow risk factors NOW — late presentation, large thrombus burden, diabetes, long total ischaemic time, big vessel/large territory. Pre-load a mental plan for intracoronary vasodilators.
  • 🩸 Antithrombotic review — DAPT loaded (aspirin + potent P2Y₁₂); plan procedural anticoagulation; consider a GP IIb/IIIa or cangrelor plan for very high thrombus burden.
  • 🖐️ Access assessment — radial-first (Barbeau); keep a femoral plan for large-bore mechanical support if shock develops.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"No-reflow is a diagnosis you prepare for, not one you improvise. In a thrombotic RCA, draw up the vasodilators, the atropine and the pressor before you inflate — because when the flow stops you will have seconds, not minutes."

🩸 Vascular Access & Setup

🖐️ Radial vs femoral — and the support you may suddenly need

🖐️ Radial (default)

  • Fewer access-site bleeds & vascular complications
  • Lower mortality in ACS (MATRIX, RIVAL ACS subgroup)
  • Early ambulation, patient comfort
  • Confirm patency first (Barbeau/Allen)

🦵 Femoral (bail-out / support)

  • When radial fails, or when refractory no-reflow drives cardiogenic shock and you need large-bore support (IABP, Impella)
  • Higher bleeding — micropuncture + ultrasound guidance
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Wire the wrist, but know where the groin is. Refractory no-reflow can tip an inferior STEMI into shock in minutes — the operator who already has femoral access mapped is the one who can escalate to mechanical support without losing the patient."

💉 Sheath, anticoagulation & the guide for the RCA

  • 🔧 Sheath: 6 Fr radial (hydrophilic); intra-arterial spasmolytic "cocktail" to prevent radial spasm.
  • 🩸 Procedural anticoagulation: unfractionated heparin (70–100 U/kg, ACT ~250–300 s) or bivalirudin. Good anticoagulation reduces fresh thrombus forming on equipment — one contributor to distal embolisation and no-reflow.
  • 🧭 Guide catheter: for the RCA → a JR4 (or AL1 for support). A microcatheter should be on the table from the start — it is your distal drug-delivery device for no-reflow, letting you deliver vasodilators BEYOND the obstruction rather than washing them down the guide.

S5

🔬 Lesion & Flow Assessment — Physiology & Imaging

🫀 In a STEMI, physiology waits — but FLOW grading does notYou do NOT need FFR to justify treating an occluded culprit. FFR/iFR belong to non-culprit/stable lesions (FFR ≤ 0.80, iFR ≤ 0.89 = ischaemic → treat). What you DO need, moment to moment, is to grade flow and tissue perfusion — TIMI flow, myocardial blush grade, and the corrected TIMI frame count. That is how you diagnose no-reflow at the table.

🌊 The three ways to grade "is the muscle actually perfused?"

ToolScaleNo-reflow signature
🌊 TIMI flow grade0 (none) · 1 (penetrates, no distal) · 2 (delayed) · 3 (normal brisk)Epicardial patency but TIMI 1–2 with sluggish distal filling
🧠 Myocardial blush grade (MBG)0 (none) · 1 (minimal) · 2 (moderate) · 3 (normal myocardial "blush")MBG 0–1 despite an open epicardial artery = tissue-level no-reflow
🎞️ Corrected TIMI frame count (cTFC)Frames for dye to reach the distal landmark (≈ normal < 28)cTFC > 40 = slow flow; the higher the count, the worse the microvascular obstruction

🎮 Simulation — The Corrected TIMI Frame Count Dial

Run the cine and count the frames for contrast to reach the distal landmark. Low count = brisk tissue flow; high count = the microcirculation is shutting down.

Set the dial to explore normal flow vs slow-flow vs no-reflow.

🎯 cTFC < 28 → normal (TIMI-3, MBG 3). 28–40 → slow flow. 41–60 → severe slow-flow / evolving no-reflow. > 60 → no-reflow: open artery, dead microcirculation.

🖥️ Intravascular imaging — rule out a MECHANICAL cause first

Before you blame the microvasculature, make sure the epicardial vessel is truly optimised — no-reflow is a diagnosis of exclusion for slow flow. IVUS/OCT confirm that the "no-reflow" is not actually stent under-expansion, an edge dissection, distal thrombus/embolus, or spasm masquerading as microvascular shutdown.

🔵 IVUS

  • Ultrasound — sizes the vessel, quantifies thrombus/plaque burden, sees under-expansion behind calcium
  • No contrast flush needed (useful when flow is already poor)

🟠 OCT

  • Light — superb for thrombus, edge dissection, apposition
  • Needs a contrast flush — use cautiously when flow is sluggish
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Do not FFR the culprit — grade its FLOW instead. TIMI, blush and frame count tell you whether the heart muscle is perfused; a wide-open stent with MBG 0 is a shut microcirculation, not a success."

🛠️ Intervention — Step-by-Step Execution

🎈 Balloon angioplasty & stent deployment. Source: Wikimedia Commons (Blausen)

🪜 The sequence — engineered to PREVENT no-reflow, then to TREAT it

  1. 🧵 Wire the vessel — a workhorse guidewire into the distal true lumen (confirm distal position, not a side branch or subintimal).
  2. 🫧 Restore a channel gently — in heavy thrombus, a small balloon or selective aspiration; avoid repeated aggressive dilatation that showers distal emboli.
  3. 🕸️ Do NOT over-stent thrombus — a long stent crushed into a thrombotic segment extrudes clot through the struts and embolises the distal bed. Consider a minimalist strategy and the deferred-stenting debate (DEFER-STEMI): wire, restore flow, cool the thrombus with antithrombotics, and stent later.
  4. 💉 Treat no-reflow the moment you see it — intracoronary vasodilators delivered distal via a microcatheter: adenosine boluses, verapamil, nicardipine, or nitroprusside; add a GP IIb/IIIa inhibitor for high thrombus burden.
  5. 💓 Support the haemodynamics — for hypotensive no-reflow, intracoronary epinephrine (raises pressure AND improves flow) is preferred over agents that drop the BP; escalate to mechanical support if shock persists.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Every extra inflation in a thrombotic vessel is a handful of emboli thrown downstream. Less balloon, less stent, more thought — and vasodilators delivered where the block actually is."

🏠 Post-Procedure Care, Debrief & Assessment

🩹 Immediate aftercare

  • 🖐️ Radial: patent-haemostasis band; watch for radial occlusion & bleeding. If large-bore femoral support was placed, manage that access and anticoagulation carefully.
  • 💓 Rhythm & haemodynamics: an RCA territory with no-reflow can keep throwing bradyarrhythmia — keep pacing available; wean pressors/inotropes slowly; echo for RV and LV function.
  • 🧠 Document the perfusion result: final TIMI, MBG, cTFC and % ST resolution — these are prognostic, not cosmetic.

💊 Secondary prevention (start day 1)

  • DAPT — aspirin + a potent P2Y₁₂ inhibitor (prasugrel/ticagrelor) for 12 months (individualise by bleeding risk).
  • High-intensity statin (atorvastatin 80 mg) regardless of LDL; add ezetimibe/PCSK9i to target.
  • ACE inhibitor and beta-blocker once haemodynamically stable (careful with beta-blockade while bradycardic/shocked); MRA if EF ≤ 40% with HF/diabetes.
  • 🖼️ Cardiac MRI at follow-up — late microvascular obstruction on CMR is a powerful, independent predictor of adverse remodeling and death; it is the imaging legacy of the no-reflow you saw at the table.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"No-reflow does not end when you leave the lab — it writes itself onto the heart as microvascular obstruction on CMR, and that scar of shut capillaries predicts the remodeling and the deaths that follow."

🔭 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

  • 🕸️ The MVO vicious cycle — the "open but dead" myocardium: distal embolisation of thrombus/plaque debris + platelet–neutrophil micro-plugging + endothelial swelling (oedema) + micro-vascular vasospasm + reperfusion injury (oxidative stress, Ca²⁺ overload, capillary compression by myocyte oedema) → capillaries physically and functionally occlude → the epicardial artery is patent but the tissue cannot perfuse → ongoing ischaemia amplifies each of these → micro-vascular shutdown. Every arm feeds the next; that is why it is a cycle, and why one drug is often not enough.
  • 💉 The vasodilator toolbox & where each shines: adenosine (short-acting, transient AV block — beware in the RCA), verapamil/nicardipine (calcium-channel micro-vascular dilators), nitroprusside (NO donor, potent but drops BP), and epinephrine for hypotensive no-reflow (raises perfusion pressure AND recruits flow). Deliver distal via a microcatheter — a drug washed down the guide never reaches the shut bed.
  • 🕸️ Prevention beats rescue: avoid over-stenting thrombus, avoid needless high-pressure inflations, consider deferred stenting (DEFER-STEMI) and selective (not routine) thrombectomy (TOTAL/TASTE), and keep total ischaemic time short.
  • 🫀 Mechanical support in refractory no-reflow shock: IABP (no mortality benefit — IABP-SHOCK II), Impella/percutaneous LVAD (DanGer Shock), VA-ECMO for refractory collapse — unload the ventricle and buy the microcirculation time to recover.
  • 🖼️ Imaging the aftermath: contrast-enhanced CMR shows late microvascular obstruction and intramyocardial haemorrhage — the strongest imaging predictors of adverse remodeling; index of microcirculatory resistance (IMR) can quantify micro-vascular dysfunction invasively.
  • ⚖️ The over-treatment trap: confusing no-reflow with residual epicardial stenosis leads to more balloon, more stent, more emboli — recognising the difference (optimise, then image, then treat the microcirculation) is the fellowship-level skill.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Thrombus He Stented Into Oblivion"

The case: A late-presenting inferior STEMI with a huge, soft thrombotic RCA occlusion. Eager to finish fast, the operator did NOT restore flow gently — he ran a long balloon and immediately deployed two overlapping long stents directly through the thrombotic segment, crushing clot through the struts. The next cine showed the vessel open and the distal bed gone: TIMI 0–1, MBG 0, no runoff — massive distal embolisation and global no-reflow. He read it as "residual disease," ballooned harder, and embolised more. The BP collapsed, the rhythm degenerated, and the patient spiralled into refractory cardiogenic shock. Distal vasodilators, epinephrine and an IABP came too late. He did not survive.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🕸️ OVER-STENTED THROMBUSLong stents crushed into fresh clot extrude thrombus through the struts and shower the distal bed.
🎈 OVER-DILATEDRepeated aggressive inflations in a thrombotic vessel are an embolisation engine.
🔍 MISREAD NO-REFLOWCalled microvascular shutdown "residual stenosis" and treated the wrong problem.
💓 IGNORED HAEMODYNAMICSNo pressor/pacing plan for an RCA territory prone to brady-hypotension.
⏳ LATE ESCALATIONVasodilators, epinephrine and mechanical support arrived after shock was established.

🛡️ What would have saved him: restore flow gently, resist over-stenting a thrombotic segment (consider deferred stenting), and the instant flow fails to return, recognise no-reflow — deliver intracoronary vasodilators DISTAL via microcatheter, use epinephrine for the hypotensive component, support the rate and pressure early, and escalate to mechanical support before shock is fixed. Less balloon, less stent, earlier support. This is the drill in S6 and S9.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"You cannot stent your way out of no-reflow — every extra inflation in a thrombotic vessel throws more emboli downstream. When flow will not return, treat the microcirculation and the pressure, not the epicardial picture."

🎯 The five things to carry out of this case

  1. 🚫 Open is not perfused — grade TIMI, MBG and frame count; a patent stent with MBG 0 is no-reflow.
  2. 🕸️ Know the vicious cycle — emboli, micro-plugs, oedema, spasm, reperfusion injury → microvascular shutdown.

  4. 🕸️ Prevent it — do not over-stent thrombus, dilate gently, consider deferred stenting; thrombectomy is selective, not routine.
  5. 💓 Support early — hold the rate and pressure (atropine, pacing, pressor, mechanical support) while the microcirculation recovers.
