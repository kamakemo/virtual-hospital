🧭 The three questions that reframe 'resistant' hypertension

For every apparently resistant patient, ask in order: (1) Is it real? — confirm with ABPM/HBPM to exclude the white-coat effect. (2) Is he taking it? — non-adherence is the single commonest cause. (3) Is there a cause? — with true, adherent resistance (especially with low potassium, young onset or abrupt worsening) screen for a secondary cause (see S14/S20).

⚠️ The trap of "the clinic reading is high, so it's resistant"Up to a third of apparent resistance is white-coat effect or non-adherence. A single high office BP does NOT establish resistance — ambulatory (ABPM) or home (HBPM) monitoring and an honest adherence assessment do. Here, ABPM confirms 168/102 by day — this is true resistance.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the pressure won't come down (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧂 Sodium & volume: most resistant hypertension is volume-mediated — inappropriate renal sodium retention expands plasma volume. That is why a thiazide-like diuretic is essential and why spironolactone (a mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonist) works so well as the 4th agent.
  2. ⚗️ The RAAS: renin → angiotensin II (vasoconstriction) → aldosterone (Na⁺ retention + K⁺/H⁺ loss). In primary aldosteronism the adrenal makes aldosterone autonomously: renin is suppressed, sodium is retained, and potassium is dumped — hence hypertension + spontaneous hypokalaemia + metabolic alkalosis.
  3. 🧠 Sympathetic drive: obesity, OSA and stress raise sympathetic tone and renal sodium retention — a reason OSA and weight so often accompany resistance (and the rationale behind renal denervation).
  4. 💔 End-organ toll: chronic high afterload → LV hypertrophy, atrial stretch → AF, and pressure-natriuresis damage → CKD. Aldosterone excess adds direct vascular/cardiac fibrosis, so its CV harm exceeds what the BP alone predicts.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Most resistant hypertension is a salt-and-volume problem — which is why the right diuretic and spironolactone beat a random fifth vasodilator almost every time."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Pseudo-resistance vs True resistant hypertension

🎭 Pseudo-resistance

  • White-coat effect (office high, ABPM/HBPM normal)
  • Non-adherence — the commonest cause
  • Poor technique / wrong cuff / sub-optimal doses
  • Fix the artefact — do NOT keep escalating drugs

🩸 True resistant HTN

  • Out-of-office BP confirmed high; adherence verified; A+C+D optimised
  • Add spironolactone (PATHWAY-2)
  • Screen for a secondary cause — esp. with low K⁺, young onset, abrupt worsening

💡 The whole clinical skill is telling these two apart: one needs a better cuff and an honest conversation; the other needs a diagnostic work-up that can be curative.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The clinic order set (11:15)

  • 🏠 Confirm the diagnosis: review/repeat ABPM (or HBPM 7 days) — already 168/102 → true resistance.
  • 💊 Adherence check: pharmacy refill history, witnessed-dose BP response, and (if doubt) urine/serum antihypertensive drug-level assay.
  • 🧪 Bloods: U&E (K⁺, Na⁺, HCO₃⁻), creatinine/eGFR, HbA1c, lipids, calcium, TFTs.
  • 🔬 Aldosterone:renin ratio (ARR) — screen for primary aldosteronism first (commonest cause). Draw morning, seated; correct K⁺ first; be aware MRAs/β-blockers/ACEi affect it (stop MRA 4–6 weeks before).
  • 💦 Plasma free metanephrines (or 24-h urinary metanephrines) if any episodic sweating/palpitations/headache → phaeochromocytoma.
  • 🌙 OSA screen (STOP-BANG) → sleep study; overnight dexamethasone suppression if Cushingoid.
  • 🩻 Renal artery imaging (duplex / CT or MR angiography) if bruit, flash pulmonary oedema, or creatinine rise >30% after starting an ACEi/ARB.
  • 📈 ECG + echocardiogram for LVH; urine ACR for albuminuria — quantify end-organ damage.
  • 🥗 Lifestyle + drug review: stop the NSAID, cut alcohol/salt, weight loss; note any decongestants/steroids/OCP.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"An aldosterone:renin ratio costs less than a month of a fourth drug — and unlike the drug, it can end the disease instead of masking it."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Clinic-nurse priorities — the technique IS the diagnosis

  • 📏 Standardised BP measurement: validated device, correct (often large) cuff, patient rested 5 min, back & feet supported, arm at heart level, no talking; average ≥2–3 readings; measure both arms first visit.
  • 🏠 Fit and teach ABPM/HBPM: the nurse-led out-of-office reading is what separates real resistance from the white-coat effect — the single most useful "test" in this clinic.
  • 💊 Adherence support: non-judgemental review of the pill bag, blister packs/dosette boxes, single-pill combinations, and honest barriers (cost, side-effects, forgetting). Non-adherence is the commonest "resistance."
  • 🩸 Correct venepuncture timing for the ARR: morning, seated, potassium replaced beforehand — bad sampling gives a false result and a missed diagnosis.
  • 🥗 Lifestyle counselling: salt reduction, weight, alcohol, activity — each can shave several mmHg and reduce drug burden.
  • 🚫 Medication reconciliation: flag NSAIDs, decongestants, steroids, OCP, liquorice and herbal remedies that raise BP.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse with the right cuff and a properly fitted ABPM cures more 'resistant hypertension' before lunch than any new tablet does."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🏠 Out-of-office BP (ABPM/HBPM) is the monitoring standard — it tracks true control, unmasks white-coat & masked hypertension, and reveals non-dipping (<10% nocturnal fall = higher risk, think OSA/primary aldosteronism).
  • 🧪 U&E within 1–2 weeks of starting/uptitrating an ACEi/ARB or spironolactone — watch creatinine (>30% rise → renal artery stenosis) and potassium (hyperkalaemia on MRA + ACEi/ARB).
  • 🧂 Potassium trend: a low K⁺ that persists despite RAAS blockade is your standing prompt to (re)screen for aldosteronism.
  • 🎯 Titrate to target (<130/80 if tolerated), re-checking adherence at every step before adding another agent.
  • 💔 End-organ surveillance: periodic ECG/echo (LVH regression), renal function, albuminuria — regression is a marker of successful control.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Every time you touch an ACE inhibitor or spironolactone, book the potassium and creatinine — the drugs that control resistant hypertension are the same ones that raise potassium."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What untreated / mismanaged resistant hypertension costs

  • 💔 LV hypertrophy → heart failure (both HFpEF and HFrEF) — the pressure-loaded heart stiffens then fails.
  • 💓 Atrial fibrillation — atrial stretch; aldosterone excess is independently arrhythmogenic (atrial fibrosis).
  • 🧠 Stroke & vascular dementia — the dominant hypertensive end-organ event.
  • 🩺 Chronic kidney disease — hypertensive nephrosclerosis, progressive albuminuria.
  • 👁️ Hypertensive retinopathy; aortic aneurysm/dissection risk.
  • 🧂 Iatrogenic: hyperkalaemia (MRA + ACEi/ARB), AKI, symptomatic hypotension/falls from over-treatment, gynaecomastia (spironolactone).
  • ☠️ The lethal one — unopposed alpha: a β-blocker given before α-blockade in an unrecognised phaeochromocytoma → hypertensive crisis (see S21).
  • 🕳️ The system complication: the missed curable cause — years of drugs for a primary aldosteronism or renovascular lesion that surgery/angioplasty could have fixed.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The worst complication of resistant hypertension isn't the next stroke — it's the curable cause that was never screened for while the drug list grew."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🩸 Diagnosis: true resistant hypertension (ABPM 168/102, adherent, optimal A+C+D) with a positive ARR → primary aldosteronism.
  2. 🔬 Confirm & subtype: confirmatory suppression test → adrenal CT → adrenal vein sampling to lateralise.
  3. 🔪 Treat the cause: unilateral disease → laparoscopic adrenalectomy (potentially curative); bilateral → lifelong MRA (spironolactone/eplerenone).
  4. 💊 Meanwhile: add spironolactone, continue A+C+D, treat OSA, stop the NSAID, reduce salt/alcohol/weight.
  5. 🏥 Endocrine + hypertension MDT; monitor K⁺/creatinine; surveil for LVH regression & CV risk factors.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Primary aldosteronism from a single adenoma can be cured with an operation — but only if someone sends the aldosterone:renin ratio and then lateralises with adrenal vein sampling before cutting."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Two very different destinies — primary vs secondary hypertension

🧲 Secondary (this patient — a findable cause)

  • Primary aldosteronism from a unilateral adenoma → adrenalectomy potentially curative
  • Bilateral disease → lifelong MRA (spironolactone/eplerenone), K⁺/creatinine monitoring
  • Treat OSA (CPAP); stop the NSAID; salt/alcohol/weight
  • Endocrine + cardiology follow-up; screen relatives if familial

🩸 Primary (essential) hypertension

  • No single curable cause — lifelong A+C+D ± spironolactone titrated to target
  • Lifestyle the foundation; adherence support; single-pill combinations
  • Home BP monitoring, annual bloods, CV risk (statin, glycaemia)
  • Re-screen for a secondary cause if control is lost or K⁺ falls
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Every resistant hypertensive deserves one honest question — 'have we looked for a cause?' — because a curable secondary hypertension is a lifetime of tablets (and end-organ damage) avoided."
🎯 Targets & safety-netAim <140/90, then <130/80 if tolerated (individualised; 2024 ESC favours 120–129 SBP in most). Written plan: report muscle weakness/cramps (K⁺), swelling or breathlessness, and dizziness/falls from over-treatment; know when to seek same-day help for very high BP with chest pain, breathlessness, visual loss or neurological symptoms.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🧲 Primary aldosteronism in depth: screen with the aldosterone:renin ratio (correct K⁺ first; account for interfering drugs). Confirm with a suppression test (saline infusion, captopril challenge, or fludrocortisone suppression). Subtype with adrenal CT + adrenal vein sampling — the only reliable way to distinguish a unilateral aldosterone-producing adenoma (→ adrenalectomy, potentially curative) from bilateral hyperplasia (→ lifelong MRA). It is massively under-diagnosed (present in ~5–10% of hypertensives, ~20% of resistant), and carries excess CV risk — AF, stroke, MI, LVH, CKD — beyond what the BP alone predicts, because aldosterone drives direct cardiac/vascular fibrosis.
  • 🌀 Renovascular hypertension: two phenotypes — atherosclerotic RAS (older, diffuse vascular disease; angioplasty rarely helps BP — CORAL/ASTRAL) and fibromuscular dysplasia ("string of beads," young women; angioplasty can cure). Clues: abdominal bruit, flash pulmonary oedema, asymmetric kidneys, and a creatinine rise >30% after starting an ACEi/ARB (bilateral disease).
  • 💦 Phaeochromocytoma & the alpha-before-beta rule: catecholamine-secreting tumour → episodic headache/sweating/palpitations, labile BP. Diagnose with plasma free or urinary metanephrines; localise with CT/MRI ± MIBG. Mechanism of the rule: catecholamines act on both α (vasoconstriction) and β₂ (vasodilatation) receptors. β-blockade first removes the β₂ "escape valve," leaving α-vasoconstriction unopposed → severe hypertensive crisis. Always α-block first (phenoxybenzamine/doxazosin), volume-load, then add a β-blocker for reflex tachycardia before surgery.
  • 😴 Obstructive sleep apnoea: intermittent hypoxia → sympathetic surges & sodium retention → resistant, non-dipping hypertension. CPAP gives a modest BP fall and treats the sleep disorder.
  • 🌕 Cushing's syndrome: cortisol excess (exogenous steroids, pituitary/adrenal/ectopic ACTH). Screen with overnight dexamethasone suppression, late-night salivary cortisol, or 24-h urinary free cortisol.
  • 📊 ABPM phenotypes: white-coat (high office, normal out-of-office — pseudo-resistance), masked (normal office, high out-of-office — dangerously under-treated), and non-dipping (<10% nocturnal fall — worse prognosis; think OSA, CKD, primary aldosteronism, autonomic dysfunction).
  • ⚡ Renal denervation: catheter-based ablation of renal sympathetic nerves. After the negative sham-controlled SIMPLICITY HTN-3, better-designed trials (SPYRAL HTN-OFF/ON MED, RADIANCE) showed a modest but genuine BP reduction — a device adjunct in confirmed, adherent, drug-resistant hypertension, not a substitute for the work-up.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "A Decade of the Wrong Diagnosis"

The case: A 45-year-old man was labelled with "difficult essential hypertension" at 35 and treated for a decade — climbing to four antihypertensives — always with a potassium hovering around 3.2 that was documented, visit after visit, as "just the diuretic." No one ever sent an aldosterone:renin ratio. Over those years he developed LV hypertrophy, atrial fibrillation and chronic kidney disease. When a new registrar finally screened him, the ratio was grossly positive: primary aldosteronism from a unilateral adrenal adenoma — a curable cause missed for ten years. Adrenalectomy came too late to undo the AF and CKD.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Essential hypertension" label stuck; nobody re-questioned the diagnosis for ten years.
🧂 K⁺ EXPLAINED AWAYSpontaneous hypokalaemia repeatedly blamed on the diuretic — the loudest clue, ignored.
💊 ESCALATION REFLEXEach uncontrolled visit answered with another pill, never a screen.
🔬 NO ARRThe one cheap test that makes the diagnosis was never ordered.
⏳ END-ORGAN TOLLLVH, AF and CKD accrued silently while the cause sat unlooked-for.

🛡️ What would have saved him: treat resistant hypertension with spontaneous hypokalaemia as primary aldosteronism until an ARR proves otherwise; never explain away a persistent low potassium as "the diuretic" when an ACEi is on board; question the "essential hypertension" label in the young and the resistant; and remember the cause can be curable. This is exactly the discipline that changed the outcome for the patient in Bed 2.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"And the deadlier version: a 'resistant hypertensive' with surges who is handed a beta-blocker — an unrecognised phaeochromocytoma tips into a hypertensive crisis and dies. Never beta-block before you alpha-block. Symptom-labels and reflex prescriptions kill; the aldosterone:renin ratio and the secondary-cause screen save."
