📜 History — fatigability is the whole story

  • 👁️ Fatigable ptosis and diplopia for months — fine in the morning, drooping and doubling by evening or after sustained use. The cardinal feature.
  • 💪 Progression to generalised fatigable weakness: proximal limbs, neck flexors (cannot hold head up), and now bulbar (fading voice, weak chew/swallow) and respiratory muscles.
  • 🦠 A recent respiratory infection plus a newly-started antibiotic — two classic crisis triggers (infection is the commonest; certain drugs unmask MG).
  • 🧬 Known/likely autoimmune background; ask about thymoma symptoms and other autoimmune disease (thyroid).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Weakness that gets WORSE the more you use it and worse toward the end of the day is myasthenia until proven otherwise — and when it reaches the breathing muscles it is a crisis, not fatigue."
🔥 Why the crisis is under-recognisedEarly MG looks trivial — a droopy lid, some double vision that "comes and goes." But ~15–20% of MG patients have a myasthenic crisis at some point, and the breathing can fail fast once bulbar muscles go. As in GBS, the pump fails before the sats do — so the whole game is serial FVC and a monitored bed, not a reassuring oximeter.

🧭 The bedside tests that cost nothing

Sustained upgaze (ptosis worsens after 30–60 s — fatigability), ice-pack test (2 min of ice on a ptotic lid improves it — cold improves neuromuscular transmission), single-breath count and FVC, and looking for paradoxical abdominal breathing. A patient who cannot lift the head has weak neck flexors — which fail alongside the diaphragm.

⚠️ The trap of "the sats are fine"Myasthenic crisis is a ventilation (pump) failure. SpO₂ and PaO₂ stay near-normal until exhaustion; a rising PaCO₂ is a pre-terminal sign. Do NOT wait for desaturation or a bad gas — act on the falling FVC and the failing bulbar function.

"This is myasthenic crisis — fatigable weakness that has reached the bulbar and respiratory muscles, triggered by infection and a macrolide. 🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🫁 Serial FVC + single-breath count — now, then hourly; apply the 20/30/40 rule (S6). Intubate electively on the trend.
  2. 🛏️ Monitored / ICU bed; ICU + anaesthetics pre-alerted; NBM until swallow safe (aspiration risk).
  3. 🚫 Stop the macrolide; review the whole drug chart for MG-worsening agents (aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, beta-blockers, magnesium).
  4. 💉 IVIG 2 g/kg over 5 days OR plasma exchange for the crisis — the definitive acute immunotherapy.
  5. 🧪 Confirm/complete diagnosis: anti-AChR (and anti-MuSK if negative), repetitive nerve stimulation/SFEMG, CT chest for thymoma; treat infection.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In myasthenic CRISIS the answer is IVIG or plasma exchange plus airway protection — NOT more pyridostigmine. Piling on anticholinesterase drowns the patient in secretions without rescuing the diaphragm."
🧪 Resident's self-testThe night team suggests giving IV magnesium "because she looks agitated and might be low." Safe in this patient?

No — dangerous. Magnesium blocks presynaptic calcium-dependent acetylcholine release and markedly worsens myasthenic weakness; it can precipitate respiratory arrest in crisis. Only replace magnesium if genuinely and significantly low, cautiously, with respiratory monitoring — and never give it empirically. The same caution applies to aminoglycosides, macrolides, fluoroquinolones and beta-blockers.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY it fatigues (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🎯 The target: autoantibodies (usually anti-acetylcholine-receptor, sometimes anti-MuSK or anti-LRP4) attack the postsynaptic membrane of the neuromuscular junction.
  2. 🧱 The damage: antibodies block, cross-link (increasing turnover of) and — with complement — destroy AChRs, and flatten the postsynaptic folds. Fewer working receptors means a smaller "safety margin" of transmission.
  3. 🔁 WHY fatigability: at a healthy junction each nerve impulse releases plenty of ACh with receptors to spare. Physiologically, ACh release normally declines over a train of impulses (presynaptic run-down). In MG the receptor reserve is already low, so as release falls with repeated use, more and more junctions drop below threshold and fail to fire — the muscle gets weaker the more you use it, and recovers with rest.
  4. 🌙 WHY worse in the evening / with heat: cumulative use through the day and higher temperature both worsen transmission — hence end-of-day ptosis and the diagnostic ice-pack improvement (cold helps).
  5. 🫁 The crisis: when the failing junctions reach the bulbar and respiratory muscles — often pushed by infection or an unmasking drug — the pump fails: ventilatory (not oxygenation) failure.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"MG is a postsynaptic receptor shortage: with a thin safety margin, every repeated impulse recruits fewer receptors — so effort BUYS weakness, and rest BUYS strength."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Myasthenia vs Lambert-Eaton (LEMS)

🎯 Myasthenia Gravis (postsynaptic)

  • Anti-AChR antibodies; POST-synaptic receptor loss
  • Weakness WORSENS with use (fatigable); ocular/bulbar prominent
  • Associations: thymoma / thymic hyperplasia
  • Reflexes normal

🔋 Lambert-Eaton (presynaptic)

  • Anti-P/Q voltage-gated CALCIUM-channel antibodies; PRE-synaptic ACh release fails
  • Weakness IMPROVES briefly with use (post-exercise facilitation); proximal legs, autonomic features
  • Association: small-cell lung cancer (paraneoplastic)
  • Reflexes reduced/absent, may return after exercise

💡 "More you use it, weaker it gets" = myasthenia. "Warms up with use" + a smoker with a lung mass = Lambert-Eaton — go hunting for small-cell lung cancer.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (19:55)

  • 🫁 Bedside FVC + single-breath count now, then hourly in crisis; document each value and the trend.
  • 🛏️ Admit to ICU/HDU-monitored bed; ICU + anaesthetics alerted; NBM until swallow assessed (aspiration risk).
  • 🚫 STOP the macrolide immediately; screen the whole chart for MG-worsening drugs (aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, beta-blockers, magnesium, some anti-arrhythmics); flag them as contraindicated.
  • 💉 Start IVIG 0.4 g/kg/day × 5 days (total 2 g/kg) or plasma exchange for the crisis — one modality.
  • 💊 Pyridostigmine continued for symptom relief — but do NOT up-titrate to treat the crisis (secretions/cholinergic risk); some withhold it while ventilated to reduce secretions.
  • 🩺 Treat the trigger: appropriate (MG-safe) antibiotic for the chest infection, antipyretics; VTE prophylaxis for the immobile patient.
  • 🧪 Send anti-AChR (and anti-MuSK if negative); arrange repetitive nerve stimulation/SFEMG and CT chest for thymoma; bedside ice-pack test.
  • 📞 If FVC approaches 20 mL/kg / <1 L, or bulbar failure/exhaustion → elective controlled intubation, do not wait for an ABG.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In crisis, the two most important 'drugs' are the ones you STOP (the macrolide/magnesium) and the airway you secure — then IVIG or plasma exchange. More pyridostigmine is not the rescue."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Monitored-bed priorities — the nurse runs the safety net

  • 🫁 Measure and chart the FVC hourly in crisis — the nurse who spots the downward trend catches respiratory failure before it happens. Escalate on any fall.
  • 💊 Guard the drug chart — the nurse is the last line against a "routine" macrolide, aminoglycoside, fluoroquinolone, beta-blocker or magnesium being given. Challenge every new prescription.
  • 🗣️ Protect the airway: assess cough/swallow, keep suction ready, keep NBM until swallow cleared; sit upright; a weak cough cannot protect the lungs.
  • 😮‍💨 Watch for cholinergic excess if pyridostigmine is running (salivation, sweating, cramps, bradycardia, small pupils) vs worsening crisis — the picture guides whether to hold the drug.
  • 🩸 VTE prophylaxis, pressure-area care, eye care/lubrication if lids will not close; time activity to rest periods to limit fatigue.
  • 🧾 Keep the fully-alert, frightened patient reassured and communicating (she may struggle to speak) — plan a signalling method before speech fails.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In myasthenia the nurse who re-checks the FVC AND scrutinises every new drug is the most important safety system on the ward — one wrong antibiotic can stop the breathing."

🩻 Cholinergic vs myasthenic crisis — do not confuse them

🧠 Myasthenic crisis

  • Under-treated / triggered weakness reaching respiratory muscles
  • Normal or large pupils, dry-ish, tachycardia
  • Fix: airway + IVIG/PLEX; do NOT just add pyridostigmine

💧 Cholinergic crisis (over-treatment)

  • Excess anticholinesterase → SLUDGE: salivation, lacrimation, sweating, diarrhoea, small pupils, fasciculations, bradycardia
  • Fix: STOP anticholinesterase, support airway, atropine for muscarinic excess

💉 The MG treatment map — what, why, and the traps

🚫 Drugs that UNMASK / WORSEN myasthenia — a core safety point
  • 💊 Antibiotics: aminoglycosides (gentamicin), macrolides (azithromycin/clarithromycin), fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin), telithromycin.
  • 💓 Cardiac: beta-blockers, some anti-arrhythmics (procainamide, quinidine).
  • 🧂 Magnesium (IV Mg, and caution with high-dose antacids/laxatives) — blocks presynaptic ACh release.
  • 💉 Neuromuscular blockers (exaggerated/prolonged effect), penicillamine (can INDUCE MG), high-dose statins, iodinated contrast (rarely), checkpoint inhibitors (can trigger MG).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Before ANY new drug in a myasthenic, ask 'does this worsen MG?' — aminoglycosides, macrolides, fluoroquinolones, beta-blockers and magnesium can turn stable myasthenia into a crisis in hours."
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🫁 Serial FVC + single-breath count (the master signal) — hourly in crisis; act on the trend toward 20 mL/kg / <1 L, not on the sats or the ABG.
  • 🗣️ Bulbar function — voice, swallow, cough, secretions; NBM and suction ready; aspiration is a real killer.
  • 💊 Drug-chart vigilance — every new prescription screened against the MG-worsening list; the wrong antibiotic can precipitate arrest.
  • 😮‍💨 Cholinergic vs myasthenic picture — if pyridostigmine is running, watch pupils/secretions/HR to distinguish over- from under-treatment.
  • 📈 Response to immunotherapy — IVIG/PLEX effect over days; steroids may cause an early dip (watch the FVC when starting them).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you say 'crisis,' the monitor's question is 'is the diaphragm about to fail?' — the FVC trend and the fading voice answer it, and the drug chart decides whether you help or harm."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong

  • 🫁 Respiratory failure — the defining danger of crisis; ventilatory (pump) failure, warned by FVC not by sats.
  • 🗣️ Aspiration pneumonia from bulbar weakness — which then worsens the crisis (a vicious cycle).
  • 💊 Iatrogenic deterioration — an unmasking drug (magnesium, aminoglycoside, macrolide, fluoroquinolone, beta-blocker) precipitating arrest.
  • 💧 Cholinergic crisis from over-treatment with anticholinesterase — mimics/compounds weakness with SLUDGE features.
  • 📉 Steroid-induced early worsening when immunosuppression is started too fast, too high, unmonitored.
  • 🩸 ICU complications: VTE, ventilator-associated pneumonia, prolonged ventilation, deconditioning.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In myasthenic crisis the two great avoidable disasters are the FVC you did not measure and the drug you should not have given — either can end in respiratory arrest."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The disposition plan

  1. 🧠 Diagnosis: myasthenic crisis (anti-AChR positive, decremental RNS), triggered by infection + a macrolide. Impending respiratory failure.
  2. 🛏️ ICU/monitored bed with serial FVC; anaesthetics/ICU engaged; elective intubation on the trend (FVC ~20 mL/kg / <1 L or bulbar failure). Avoid crash intubation.
  3. 💉 Acute immunotherapy: IVIG 2 g/kg over 5 days (or PLEX) — one modality; stop the macrolide and all MG-worsening drugs; treat the infection with an MG-safe agent.

🏠 Discharge

📋 Living with MG — and never triggering the next crisis

🧠 This patient (post-crisis)

  • Maintenance immunosuppression (steroid + steroid-sparing agent), pyridostigmine for symptoms, thymectomy planned
  • Carry a drug-avoidance card: aminoglycosides, macrolides, fluoroquinolones, beta-blockers, magnesium
  • Red flags: breathlessness, swallowing difficulty, weak cough, rapid worsening → return / call for help urgently
  • Prompt treatment of infections; caution before any surgery/anaesthesia; vaccinate (avoid live vaccines on immunosuppression)

🟢 Ocular / mild MG

  • Often pyridostigmine ± low-dose steroid; watch for generalisation (commonest in first 2 years)
  • Same drug-avoidance rules; same safety-net education
  • Neurology follow-up; monitor for conversion to generalised disease
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Every myasthenic goes home with a drug-avoidance list and a 'come back if you cannot breathe or swallow' plan — the next crisis is usually an infection or a wrong prescription away."

🔁 Use it, lose itFatigable weakness worse with use and by evening = myasthenia; rest restores strength.
🫁 FVC over satsCrisis is pump failure — the FVC trend warns hours before the SpO₂ or ABG changes.
🔢 20/30/40 ruleFVC toward 20 mL/kg (or <1 L), MIP −30, MEP 40 → intubate electively on the trend.
💊 The drug listAminoglycosides, macrolides, fluoroquinolones, beta-blockers, magnesium — can precipitate crisis.
💉 Crisis = IVIG/PLEXNot more pyridostigmine; stop the offending drug and protect the airway.
⚠️ Steroid dipSteroids help but can transiently WORSEN early — start careful, often after stabilising.
🔋 MG vs LEMSMG worsens with use (anti-AChR); LEMS improves with use (anti-VGCC, small-cell lung cancer).

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the crisis

  • 🧬 Antibody subgroups drive treatment: AChR-positive (thymoma association, thymectomy benefit), MuSK-positive (bulbar/respiratory predominant, poor anticholinesterase response, rituximab often effective), LRP4/seronegative — the serology shapes the plan.
  • 💉 Targeted biologics: complement inhibitors (eculizumab, ravulizumab — REGAIN) for AChR-positive refractory disease; FcRn antagonists (efgartigimod — ADAPT; rozanolixizumab) that lower pathogenic IgG; B-cell depletion (rituximab, esp. MuSK).
  • 🩻 Thymectomy evidence: the MGTX RCT showed benefit in AChR-positive generalised MG without thymoma; all thymomas are resected regardless of MG severity.
  • 👁️ Ocular MG: ~50% generalise (mostly within 2 years); early immunotherapy may reduce generalisation — a debated but important management point.
  • 🤰 Special situations: MG in pregnancy (magnesium for eclampsia is hazardous — use alternatives), transient neonatal myasthenia from transplacental antibody, and peri-operative planning (regional over general where possible; careful relaxant choice).
  • 💊 Checkpoint-inhibitor myasthenia: immunotherapy for cancer can trigger a severe MG (± myocarditis/myositis overlap) — a growing, high-mortality iatrogenic cause to recognise.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Prescription That Stopped Her Breathing"

The case: A 34-year-old woman with known myasthenia gravis was admitted with a chest infection and early bulbar symptoms. Overnight she became agitated and tachypnoeic. Without a neurology review, the covering team treated a borderline-low magnesium with IV magnesium sulphate and, for the chest infection, started IV ciprofloxacin — both drugs that worsen myasthenia. Her FVC was never measured. Within the hour she became profoundly weak, then apnoeic, and suffered a respiratory arrest requiring emergency intubation; she survived but spent weeks ventilated with an aspiration pneumonia.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

💉 MAGNESIUM GIVENIV magnesium blocks ACh release — a classic MG precipitant, given empirically.
💊 WRONG ANTIBIOTICA fluoroquinolone (ciprofloxacin) chosen — another MG-worsening drug.
🫁 FVC NEVER MEASUREDNo serial vital capacity; the crisis was not tracked or anticipated.
🧠 CRISIS UNRECOGNISEDAgitation/tachypnoea misread as anxiety, not impending pump failure.
👩‍⚕️ NO SPECIALIST INPUTNo neurology review to flag the drug hazards or the airway risk.

🛡️ What would have saved her: know the MG-worsening drug list cold (aminoglycosides, macrolides, fluoroquinolones, beta-blockers, magnesium) and screen every prescription; never give magnesium empirically to a myasthenic; measure and trend the FVC in any myasthenic with bulbar or respiratory symptoms; and treat crisis with airway protection + IVIG/PLEX, not more pyridostigmine. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 4.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In myasthenia, the drug chart is a loaded weapon. A single magnesium infusion or the wrong antibiotic has stopped more myasthenics breathing than the disease did that day."
