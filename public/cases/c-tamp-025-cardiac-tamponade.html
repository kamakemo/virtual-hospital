🧬 Why THIS patient? — sac + fluid + rate

The pericardium is a stiff, non-distensible sac. Acutely it tolerates only a little fluid before pressure climbs steeply; chronically it can stretch to hold a litre or more. Mrs. P.L.'s malignant effusion built slowly enough that her pericardium stretched and she stayed compensated for weeks — until the volume reached the steep part of the pressure–volume curve. From there, even a small extra increment sends intrapericardial pressure soaring. Once that pressure exceeds the chamber filling pressures, the chambers can no longer expand in diastole. Filling fails, stroke volume collapses, and she tips into obstructive shock. Three ingredients: a non-compliant sac, accumulating fluid, and the rate at which it arrives.

🔥 Causes of pericardial effusion / tamponade 🎗️ Malignancy (breast, lung, lymphoma, melanoma) · 🧪 Uremia (renal failure) · 🦠 Viral / idiopathic pericarditis · 🔪 Trauma (penetrating or blunt) · 🩸 Aortic dissection rupturing into the pericardium · 💔 Post-MI free-wall rupture · 🏥 Post-cardiac-procedure / post-surgical · 🫁 Tuberculosis · 🧬 Connective tissue disease, hypothyroidism (myxedema)

🧪 Resident's self-test (answer before reading on) The covering doctor suggests "a quick RSI and tube to protect her airway, then we'll sort the heart out." Good idea in this patient?

No. She is in tamponade. If an airway is unavoidable, drain the pericardium first (or have the needle in and a surgeon scrubbed), keep her spontaneously breathing as long as possible, use a preload-sparing technique (e.g. ketamine), load volume, and have vasopressors and a pericardiocentesis kit ready before anyone sedates her.

🌬️ What IS pulsus paradoxus — and why does she have it?

Pulsus paradoxus = a fall in systolic BP of > 10 mmHg on inspiration. In tamponade the heart sits inside a fixed, fluid-filled volume, so the two ventricles compete for the same shrinking space — ventricular interdependence. On inspiration, negative intrathoracic pressure augments venous return to the right heart; the RV fills and, with nowhere to expand outward against the tense pericardium, bows the interventricular septum into the LV. The LV cavity shrinks, LV filling and stroke volume fall, and the systolic pressure drops audibly with each breath. Hers is 22 mmHg — large and ominous. (Note: severe asthma/COPD and massive PE can also produce a pulsus; the JVP and echo separate them.)

🧭 Consultant's three commandments for the next hour

  1. 🪡 "Decompensating tamponade = drain it. Echo-guided pericardiocentesis if it's a free effusion; surgical drainage if it's loculated, posterior, clotted, or surgical in origin. The fluid IS the disease tonight."
  2. 💧 "Fill her while you set up — IV volume is a bridge that raises filling pressure above the pericardial pressure. But never confuse the bridge with the destination: volume alone won't save a tamponade."
  3. 🩸 "Then ask why: malignant, uremic, infective, or a surgical bleed (dissection, free-wall rupture). If it's dissection, drain only the minimum to perfuse and get her to theatre — fully decompressing a dissection re-pressurizes the false lumen and she exsanguinates."

📖 Teaching Points

🔬 The mechanism — WHY fluid in the sac strangles the heart (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧱 The container: the pericardium is a tough, fibrous, non-distensible sac. It normally holds a few millilitres of lubricating fluid at near-zero pressure.
  2. 📈 The pressure–volume curve: as fluid accumulates, pressure rises along a J-shaped curve — flat at first, then suddenly steep. Past the inflection point, tiny additional volumes cause large pressure jumps.
  3. 🤏 Diastolic compression: once intrapericardial pressure exceeds the chamber filling pressures, the thin-walled, low-pressure chambers — the RA in systole and the RV in diastole — collapse inward. They can no longer expand to fill.
  4. ⏱️ Why RATE beats VOLUME: a rapid 150 mL (e.g. a stab wound or rupture) tamponades because the sac has no time to stretch; a slow 1.5 L (e.g. this malignant effusion over weeks) may be tolerated because the pericardium gradually remodels and stretches. It is not how much fluid — it is how fast.
  5. 🌬️ Ventricular interdependence → pulsus paradoxus: trapped in a fixed total volume, the ventricles share space. On inspiration the RV fills and bows the septum into the LV → LV filling and systolic BP fall > 10 mmHg.
  6. 💥 Why it kills: filling fails → stroke volume collapses → obstructive shock. Compensatory tachycardia and vasoconstriction buy only minutes; untreated it ends in PEA arrest.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"It's the RATE of filling, not the volume — 150 mL acutely tamponades, 1.5 L slowly may not. Drain the fluid and the heart re-expands the instant the pressure comes off."

⚖️ Contrast pair #1 — Tamponade vs the other obstructive shocks (the lethal mimics)

🫀 TAMPONADE is… (assume this)

  • Raised JVP, muffled heart sounds, pulsus paradoxus
  • Effusion + diastolic RV collapse + plethoric IVC on echo
  • Low voltage / electrical alternans on ECG
  • Treatment: drain the pericardium

🚨 …but raised JVP + shock is also…

  • Tension pneumothorax: raised JVP, tracheal deviation, absent breath sounds, hyper-resonance → needle/finger decompression
  • Massive PE: raised JVP, dilated RV, clear lungs, hypoxia → thrombolysis
  • RV infarction: raised JVP, clear lungs, inferior STEMI, ECG V4R → fluids + revascularize

😈 The trap: all four are obstructive shock with a high JVP and clear-ish lungs, and all four can be mislabelled as "respiratory distress" and intubated to death. The bedside echo (and a careful chest exam) separates them in seconds. A raised JVP with shock is never a reason to reach for a diuretic.

⚖️ Contrast pair #2 — Tamponade vs Constrictive pericarditis

💧 Tamponade (tonight)

  • Fluid under pressure compresses in all of diastole
  • Pulsus paradoxus; prominent x-descent; diastolic chamber collapse
  • Acute / subacute; an emergency
  • Therapy: pericardiocentesis / surgical drainage

🧱 Constrictive pericarditis

  • Rigid scar limits filling in late diastole only
  • Kussmaul sign, pericardial knock, "square-root" (dip-and-plateau) tracing
  • Chronic; post-TB, post-radiation, post-surgical
  • Therapy: pericardiectomy

💡 Why it matters: reach for a needle in pure constriction and you find no drainable fluid; the heart is trapped in scar, not water. Read the JVP waveform and the echo before you choose the tool.

🔬 Fellowship Corner — Advanced Topics

  • 🫀 Echo signs in depth: RA systolic collapse (sensitive), RV diastolic collapse (specific), a plethoric non-collapsing IVC (sensitive), respirophasic mitral/tricuspid inflow variation (>25% mitral, >40% tricuspid), and septal "bounce" — the "swinging heart" in a large effusion.
  • 🧪 Low-pressure tamponade: in hypovolemic or dialysis patients the classic high JVP may be blunted; tamponade physiology occurs at lower intrapericardial pressures — don't be reassured by a "normal" BP after volume depletion.
  • 🩸 Effusive-constrictive pericarditis: tamponade features persist after drainage because a visceral constricting layer remains — may need pericardiectomy after the effusion is removed.
  • 🪡 Drainage technique & access: echo-guided apical or subxiphoid approach, micro-puncture, agitated-saline contrast to confirm needle position, leave a pigtail catheter for ongoing/recurrent (especially malignant) effusions; surgical pericardial window for loculated, posterior, clotted, or recurrent malignant effusions.
  • 🎗️ Malignant effusions: high recurrence; send fluid for cytology; options include prolonged catheter drainage, pericardial window, intrapericardial sclerosing/chemotherapeutic agents, and balloon pericardiotomy.
  • 🔪 Traumatic / surgical tamponade: penetrating chest trauma → FAST echo, resuscitative thoracotomy if peri-arrest; post-cardiac-surgery tamponade may be localized clot needing re-exploration rather than a needle.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set, written as you'd actually write it (14:46)

  • 🚨 NO diuretics, NO vasodilators (nitrates/GTN), and AVOID intubation / positive-pressure ventilation until decompressed — flag on chart and whiteboard.
  • 🫀 Urgent bedside echocardiogram to confirm effusion, RV diastolic collapse, RA systolic collapse, plethoric IVC, and respirophasic inflow variation.
  • 💧 IV crystalloid bolus 250–500 mL as a temporizing bridge to augment filling; reassess BP and JVP. Repeat cautiously — it buys minutes, not a cure.
  • 🪡 Echo-guided pericardiocentesis (or surgical drainage) arranged immediately; pigtail catheter left for ongoing drainage of a malignant effusion. Call cardiology/EP ± cardiac surgery.
  • 🧍 Keep upright, awake and spontaneously breathing; high-flow O₂ to SpO₂ ≥ 94%; avoid sedation that drops preload.
  • 💉 Vasopressor on standby (e.g. noradrenaline) to support perfusion until fluid is removed — a holding measure only.
  • 🩸 Two large-bore IVs, bloods: CBC, U&E (uremia?), coagulation, group & save, troponin; if drained, send pericardial fluid for cytology, microbiology, TB, and biochemistry.
  • 🖼️ CXR (globular "water-bottle" heart), 12-lead ECG (low voltage, electrical alternans); if dissection suspected → CT aorta and cardiac surgery, NOT a full drain.
  • 📟 Continuous monitoring, arterial line for beat-to-beat BP and to quantify pulsus paradoxus; NPO in case of procedure/surgery.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In decompensating tamponade there are only two priorities: get the echo and get the needle. Fluids bridge, vasopressors hold, but only drainage cures — and never give a diuretic to a heart that's starving for filling."

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ CCU nursing priorities — minute by minute

  • 📟 Continuous monitoring with the pericardiocentesis kit and echo machine at the bedside. Keep everything ready so drainage happens in minutes, not after a hunt for equipment.
  • 🧍 Keep her sitting upright and awake. Upright posture and spontaneous breathing maximize venous return — never lie a tamponade flat or sedate it without senior cover.
  • 🩸 BP every 2–5 min (arterial line once placed) and watch the pulsus paradoxus on the waveform. A widening pulsus or falling BP means escalate now, not at the next set of obs.
  • 💧 Run the fluid bolus as prescribed as a bridge; have the vasopressor primed. Double-check that no diuretic or vasodilator has been charted in error.
  • 🪡 Prepare the pericardiocentesis trolley: needle/pigtail kit, sterile drapes, local anaesthetic, agitated saline, drainage bag — assist with sterile technique during the procedure.
  • 🧠 Neuro/consciousness checks — rising restlessness or drowsiness signals falling cerebral perfusion; a patient who slumps may be arresting → check the pulse.
  • 📋 Document JVP, heart sounds, pulsus and BP before and after drainage, the volume removed and the time — the response to drainage is the bedside proof of tamponade.
  • 🔇 Calm the room: a frightened, thrashing patient worsens oxygen demand. A composed bedside helps the failing circulation.
  • 👨‍👩‍👦 Keep the family updated and supported (see S16) — assign a liaison if staffing allows; this is terrifying to watch and the cancer context makes it harder.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who keeps the tamponade upright, awake, filled and one trolley away from a needle is doing half the resuscitation in the room."
📢 Escalate IMMEDIATELY forLoss of pulse (→ PEA arrest pathway + emergency drainage) · falling BP or widening pulsus paradoxus despite fluids · rising JVP · any chart entry for a diuretic, nitrate or sedation · plan to intubate before drainage · post-drainage breathlessness (re-accumulation or pulmonary edema) · drowsiness/restlessness.

📈 ECG / Imaging

🫀 The presenting ECG — read it like a detective

Low overall QRS voltage with beat-to-beat alternation of QRS amplitude — electrical alternans. The heart is swinging back and forth within a large effusion, changing its electrical axis with every beat. Low voltage + alternans + sinus tachycardia is the ECG signature of a big pericardial effusion.

🔎 The tamponade imaging toolkit (memorize the high-yield ones)

🫀 RV diastolic collapse (echo)The most specific echo sign — when intrapericardial pressure exceeds RV diastolic pressure, the thin RV free wall buckles inward in diastole. Drainable, treatable.
🫧 RA systolic collapse (echo)The most sensitive early sign — the low-pressure right atrium inverts in systole as pericardial pressure rises.
💧 Plethoric, non-collapsing IVCHigh right-sided filling pressures back up into a dilated IVC that fails to collapse with inspiration — a marker of raised central venous pressure.
🔁 Respirophasic inflow variationThe echo correlate of pulsus paradoxus: mitral inflow falls >25% and tricuspid inflow rises on inspiration — ventricular interdependence made visible.
📐 Low QRS voltageFluid insulates and attenuates the electrical signal reaching the chest leads.
↕️ Electrical alternansThe heart swings within the effusion, shifting its axis each beat → alternating QRS amplitude. Near-specific for a large effusion.
🖼️ Globular "water-bottle" heart (CXR)An enlarged, flask-shaped cardiac silhouette suggests a large effusion (≥ ~200 mL) — supportive, not diagnostic.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Low voltage and electrical alternans on the ECG, a water-bottle heart on the film, and a collapsing right ventricle on the echo — three views of one diagnosis. The echo gives the order to drain."

🫀 Post-drainage echo & imaging

  • 📈 Post-drainage echo: resolution of RV diastolic collapse, IVC now collapsing, normalized inflow variation, and an immediate rise in BP — the bedside proof the diagnosis and the treatment were correct.
  • 🩻 Residual fluid / effusive-constrictive check: persisting tamponade physiology after drainage suggests a constricting visceral layer — flag for surgical review.
  • 🧲 CT / staging (later): characterize the malignant burden, exclude dissection if any doubt, and plan a pericardial window if the effusion recurs.
⚠️ The imaging's favorite lie in the breathless cancer patient"The chest film looks like fluid overload / a big heart — give a diuretic." A globular heart from an effusion is not failure; diuresing it strips the preload the tamponade is starving for. The only thing that "fits" a raised JVP, muffled sounds and a pulsus is an echo of the pericardium — get the probe before the frusemide.
S11

💊 Medications

💉 Acute drugs — what, why, and the traps

DrugRole tonightMechanism / caution
IV crystalloid bolus (250–500 mL)Temporizing bridge to raise filling pressure above intrapericardial pressureAugments preload and output briefly. Effect is small and short-lived if already volume-replete — never a substitute for drainage. Reassess after each bolus.
Noradrenaline / vasopressorHolds perfusion pressure while you set up the drainMaintains afterload and coronary perfusion; a holding measure only. Does not relieve the obstruction — the fluid still has to come out.

❌ Vasodilators (nitrates/GTN)CONTRAINDICATEDReduce venous return and afterload — collapse a preload-dependent circulation. Avoid.
⚠️ Sedation / induction agentsHazardous before drainageVasodilate and drop preload; combined with positive-pressure ventilation they precipitate PEA arrest. If unavoidable, use preload-sparing agents (ketamine) with the needle ready.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Never give diuretics or vasodilators in tamponade — you'll drain the only filling it has left. The only thing that should leave this patient is the fluid in the pericardial sac, through a needle."
🚫 The 'never' list in suspected tamponade❌ Diuretics · ❌ Vasodilators (nitrates) · ❌ Elective intubation / positive-pressure ventilation before drainage · ❌ Lying the patient flat · ❌ Full drainage of a dissection-related or post-rupture effusion (re-pressurizes the bleed → exsanguination). When the circulation is preload-dependent, the safest "drug" is volume as a bridge and a needle in the sac.

🏠 Cause-directed therapy (this admission, not just tonight)

  • 🎗️ Malignant effusion: oncology referral, prolonged catheter drainage, pericardial window or sclerosis for recurrence; systemic anti-cancer therapy.
  • 🧪 Uremic effusion: intensify dialysis.
  • 🦠 Infective / purulent or TB pericarditis: antimicrobials/anti-TB therapy + drainage; colchicine ± NSAIDs for viral/idiopathic pericarditis.
  • 🩸 Surgical causes (dissection, free-wall rupture, post-op clot): emergency cardiac surgery — the operating theatre, not the medicine cabinet.
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📈 Continuous telemetry and SpO₂ with the pericardiocentesis kit at the bedside until drained and stable; watch the rhythm during needle insertion (ventricular ectopy = needle on myocardium).
  • 🩸 Invasive arterial line for beat-to-beat BP and to quantify pulsus paradoxus — a narrowing pulsus after drainage is the proof it worked; a widening one is a warning.
  • 🫧 JVP / CVP trend — falling central venous pressure after drainage confirms relief; a persistently high CVP suggests residual fluid or effusive-constrictive physiology.
  • 🫀 Serial bedside echo — track resolution of chamber collapse, IVC plethora and inflow variation, and detect re-accumulation early (especially malignant effusions).
  • 🪡 Pigtail catheter output — volume, character (serous vs hemorrhagic), and rate; a sudden brisk bloody output suggests cardiac perforation.
  • 🫁 Post-drainage watch — for the rare pericardial decompression syndrome (acute RV/LV dilation and pulmonary edema after rapid removal of a large effusion) → drain slowly and reassess.
  • 🧠 Conscious level and perfusion — restoration of clear mentation and warm peripheries is the clinical endpoint.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you drain a tamponade, the monitor's job changes from 'is the heart being crushed?' to 'is it filling again, and is it re-accumulating?' — and the two numbers that answer it are the pulsus paradoxus and the CVP."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — and the mechanism

  • ⚡ PEA cardiac arrest — untreated obstructive shock ends in pulseless electrical activity; CPR perfuses poorly because the heart can't fill. Emergency drainage IS the resuscitation.
  • 💉 Arrest on induction — sedation + positive-pressure ventilation strip preload from a preload-dependent circulation → collapse. The single most preventable disaster.
  • 🪡 Procedural injury — cardiac chamber or coronary laceration, pneumothorax, arrhythmia, or a dry/loculated tap; echo guidance minimizes these.
  • 🫁 Pericardial decompression syndrome — rapid removal of a large effusion can cause transient ventricular dilation and pulmonary edema; drain slowly.
  • 🔁 Re-accumulation — malignant and uremic effusions recur; needs a pigtail catheter, pericardial window, or definitive cause treatment.
  • 🩸 Exsanguination in dissection — fully draining a dissection-related effusion re-pressurizes the false lumen and the patient bleeds out; drain only the minimum and operate.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference — "The Tamponade That Was Put to Sleep"

The case: A 64-year-old woman with lung cancer arrived to a busy ED at 03:00 breathless, hypotensive (BP 90/72) with bulging neck veins. The tired night team called it "respiratory failure — probably a chest infection" and, with falling sats, induced and intubated her. The instant positive-pressure ventilation began, her preload vanished, the pressure collapsed and she went into PEA arrest on induction. The echo, finally done during the code, showed a huge pericardial effusion with a collapsing right heart. A second, separate M&M that quarter: a patient with a type A aortic dissection and tamponade whose effusion was fully drained by a well-meaning team — the false lumen re-pressurized and she exsanguinated before theatre.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"She's breathless, it's the lungs" — clear lungs + raised JVP + shock is obstructive, not pulmonary.
🫀 NO ECHOA ninety-second bedside echo would have shown the effusion before anyone induced.
💨 PPV IGNOREDPositive-pressure ventilation strips preload from a preload-dependent heart → arrest.
🩸 OVER-DRAINEDFully draining a dissection effusion re-pressurized the bleed → exsanguination.
🌙 SYSTEMNight fatigue, no senior review, no "echo-before-intubation" default.

🛡️ What would have saved them: apply the universal rule — raised JVP + muffled sounds + hypotension + pulsus paradoxus = tamponade, get the echo and the needle; never intubate a suspected tamponade before decompressing it; load volume and use preload-sparing technique if an airway is truly unavoidable; and in dissection/rupture, drain only the minimum and rush to theatre. This is exactly why your nurse held the furosemide and worried about putting her to sleep tonight.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Just a chest infection, let's tube her' is the sentence written on the chart of patients who then arrest from an undrained tamponade. Breathlessness with a raised JVP is a heart problem until the echo says otherwise."

🗺️ Plan

🧭 Tonight → this week → for life

  1. 🪡 Tonight (done): bedside echo confirmed tamponade; IV fluid bridge; echo-guided pericardiocentesis → ~600 mL hemorrhagic fluid removed; pigtail left in situ; BP and JVP normalize; CCU admission.
  2. 🎗️ Identify & treat the cause: pericardial fluid cytology and microbiology; oncology review for the malignant effusion; rule out uremia/infection; exclude dissection if any doubt.

  4. 🫀 Surveillance echo before catheter removal and after, to detect re-accumulation or effusive-constrictive physiology early.
  5. 💙 Optimize the patient as a whole: anti-cancer therapy plan with oncology, nutrition, symptom control, and a frank discussion of goals of care given metastatic disease.
  6. 👨‍👩‍👦 Shared decision-making & education — what tamponade was, why drainage worked, what symptoms mean re-accumulation, and a clear "come back immediately for breathlessness/dizziness" safety net.

🏠 Discharge

📋 Discharge plan & safety net

  • 💊 Medications: continue oncology-directed therapy; no diuretics added for the effusion; clear written schedule. Symptom-control plan agreed with palliative/oncology teams.
  • 🪡 Drain removed, wound care instructions; signs of catheter-site infection to watch for; documentation of fluid removed and cytology result for oncology.
  • 🫀 Surveillance plan: follow-up echo to detect re-accumulation; agreed threshold for repeat drainage or a pericardial window; cardiology and oncology appointments booked before discharge.
  • 🩺 Cause addressed: malignant effusion managed with oncology; uremia/infection excluded; dissection ruled out — documented clearly so any future team knows the substrate.
  • 🚩 Red flags → return immediately / call emergency services: recurrent breathlessness (especially worse lying flat), dizziness or fainting, swelling of the neck veins, palpitations, or chest fullness — these may signal re-accumulating tamponade.
  • 📚 Education: what tamponade is in plain language, why fluid was the problem (not the lungs), why drainage worked, and the importance of fast re-presentation if symptoms return given the recurrence risk.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Discharge after tamponade is not 'fluid drained, go home' — it is a surveillance echo, an oncology plan, a clear recurrence safety net, and a family who knows that breathlessness lying flat means come straight back."

💎 Clinical Pearls

🩸 Beck's triadHypotension + raised JVP + muffled heart sounds. Add pulsus paradoxus and it's tamponade until proven otherwise — get the echo and the needle.
🌬️ Pulsus paradoxusA >10 mmHg inspiratory systolic drop from ventricular interdependence across a fixed pericardial volume. The bigger it is, the sicker the heart.
⏱️ Rate > volume150 mL acutely tamponades; 1.5 L slowly may not. It's how fast the fluid arrives, because a chronic pericardium stretches.
🫀 RV diastolic collapseThe key specific echo sign. RA systolic collapse is the earliest/most sensitive; a plethoric IVC confirms high filling pressure.
🪡 Drainage curesEcho-guided pericardiocentesis (or surgery) is definitive. Removing the fluid re-expands the heart instantly. Drugs don't fix mechanics.
💨 PPV can killPositive-pressure ventilation and induction strip preload from a preload-dependent heart → arrest on induction. Decompress before you intubate.
💧 Fluids are a bridgeIV volume raises filling pressure above pericardial pressure for minutes. It buys time to drain — it is never the treatment.
🚫 No diuretics / nitratesThey empty an already underfilled heart. Never offload a tamponade.
📉 Low voltage + alternansThe ECG signature of a big effusion — fluid insulates the signal and the heart swings within it.
🩸 Dissection = drain the minimumFully draining a dissection or rupture re-pressurizes the bleed → exsanguination. Drain just enough, then theatre.
🧱 Tamponade vs constrictionPulsus paradoxus + diastolic collapse = tamponade (needle). Kussmaul + knock + square-root sign = constriction (surgery).
🔁 It recursMalignant and uremic effusions come back — pigtail catheter, pericardial window, and treat the cause.
