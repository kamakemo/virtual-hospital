🔥 Why coeliac hides for yearsCoeliac affects roughly 1 in 100 people, yet most remain undiagnosed. It rarely announces itself with dramatic diarrhoea; far more often it is "just tiredness," a stubborn anaemia, early osteoporosis, subfertility, or abnormal liver enzymes. The commonest reason it stays hidden is simple: nobody tests for it — and once the patient removes gluten, the tests turn falsely negative.

🧭 The key branch point: test first, treat later

The single most important management decision happens NOW, before any treatment: confirm the diagnosis while she is eating gluten. Serology (anti-tTG IgA + total IgA) then duodenal biopsy. If she has already started a gluten-free diet, you face a gluten challenge or HLA typing to sort it out — messy and avoidable. Do NOT prescribe a gluten-free diet "to see if it helps" before testing.

⚠️ The trap of the empirical gluten-free trialTelling a patient to "just try cutting out gluten" feels helpful and harmless. It is neither. Within weeks the antibodies fall and the villi begin to recover, so both the blood test and the biopsy can come back normal — leaving a patient with lifelong dietary restriction and no secure diagnosis, unable to access follow-up, bone monitoring, or genetic counselling for relatives.

The gastroenterology resident thinks out loud at 09:40:

"Textbook coeliac: refractory iron-deficiency anaemia, steatorrhoea, dermatitis herpetiformis, associated autoimmune disease. My job is to confirm the diagnosis while she is still eating gluten — anti-tTG IgA with a total IgA, then a duodenal biopsy for Marsh grading — quantify the malabsorption (haematinics, calcium, vitamin D, bone profile, coagulation), and screen the consequences (bone density, thyroid, liver). And I will make absolutely sure nobody tells her to stop gluten before the scope."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🧪 Confirm on gluten: anti-tTG IgA + total IgA (if IgA deficient, use IgG-based tests — anti-tTG IgG / anti-DGP IgG).
  2. 🔬 Duodenal biopsy (≥4 samples from second part + bulb) for Marsh grading — the gold standard in adults.
  3. 🩸 Deficiency panel: FBC, ferritin/iron, folate, B12, calcium, vitamin D, bone profile, coagulation (fat-soluble vitamin K), LFTs, thyroid function.
  4. 🦴 DEXA for bone density; pneumococcal vaccination (functional hyposplenism in coeliac).
  5. 🍽️ Dietitian referral for a lifelong gluten-free diet — only AFTER the diagnosis is secured.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Always pair anti-tTG IgA with a TOTAL IgA. In IgA deficiency — which coeliacs get more often — the IgA antibody is falsely negative, and you must switch to IgG-based serology."
🧪 Resident's self-testThe anti-tTG IgA is negative, but the total IgA is very low (IgA deficiency), and the story is classic. Do you reassure her she does not have coeliac?

No. A negative anti-tTG IgA is unreliable when the patient is IgA-deficient — the antibody it measures is missing. Repeat with IgG-based serology (anti-tTG IgG or anti-deamidated gliadin peptide IgG) and proceed to duodenal biopsy. Never exclude coeliac on an IgA test in an IgA-deficient patient.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY gluten flattens the gut, WHY the deficiencies (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🌾 Gluten & gliadin: gliadin peptides from wheat, barley and rye resist digestion and cross the small-bowel epithelium.
  2. 🧪 Deamidation by tissue transglutaminase (tTG): tTG modifies gliadin, making it bind tightly to HLA-DQ2/DQ8 molecules on antigen-presenting cells (>95% of coeliacs carry DQ2 or DQ8 — their near-absence makes coeliac very unlikely).
  3. ⚔️ T-cell autoimmune attack: gliadin-specific CD4⁺ T cells drive an immune response against the enterocytes; antibodies to tTG appear (the basis of the blood test). It is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune enteropathy, not an allergy.
  4. 🏚️ Villous atrophy → malabsorption: the immune assault causes intraepithelial lymphocytosis, crypt hyperplasia and flattening of the villi. Villi are the absorptive surface: their loss (worst in the proximal duodenum/jejunum, where iron/folate/calcium are absorbed) explains the iron, folate, calcium, vitamin-D deficiencies, steatorrhoea and osteoporosis. B12 (absorbed distally) is affected only in extensive disease.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Coeliac deficiencies map onto anatomy: the PROXIMAL small bowel (iron, folate, calcium) is hit first and hardest; B12, absorbed distally, drops only in severe disease."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Coeliac Disease vs Irritable Bowel Syndrome

🌾 Coeliac Disease

  • Autoimmune enteropathy driven by gluten (HLA-DQ2/DQ8)
  • Villous atrophy → true malabsorption
  • Objective signs: anaemia, low ferritin/folate/vit D, low bone density, positive anti-tTG
  • Real complications: osteoporosis, EATL, hyposplenism

🧻 Irritable Bowel Syndrome

  • Functional disorder of gut–brain interaction
  • No mucosal damage, no malabsorption
  • Normal bloods, normal anti-tTG, normal biopsy
  • Symptoms overlap — which is exactly why you must TEST, not assume

💡 The overlap in symptoms is the trap. Bloating and altered bowels are common to both — only the serology, the bloods and the biopsy tell coeliac apart.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Clinic & endoscopy-unit priorities — the nurse guards the diagnosis

  • 🍞 Reinforce "keep eating gluten until scoped" — the single most important message; patients often assume they should stop immediately and quietly do so, wrecking the biopsy.
  • 🩸 Support with anaemia symptoms (fatigue, dizziness); safe iron replacement counselling and adherence.
  • 🧴 Gentle skin care and dignity for the dermatitis herpetiformis rash; explain it will settle on a gluten-free diet (± dapsone).
  • 🦴 Falls/fracture awareness if osteoporotic; encourage calcium/vitamin-D intake.
  • 🍽️ Prepare her for the dietitian: gluten is hidden in sauces, beer, processed foods and cross-contamination — set expectations early.
  • 💉 Ensure pneumococcal vaccination is given and documented (hyposplenism).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who stops a newly-suspected coeliac from binning their bread before the biopsy has saved the diagnosis — the commonest reason a coeliac test comes back 'negative.'"

🧠 Why HLA typing helps mainly to EXCLUDE>95% of coeliacs carry HLA-DQ2 or DQ8, but so do ~30–40% of the general population — so a positive result does NOT diagnose coeliac. 🔬 Endoscopy / Histology + Visual Enhancements

🎥 Duodenal villi — healthy fronds vs flattened mucosa

Healthy villi are tall fronds giving huge absorptive surface. In coeliac the villi flatten (atrophy) with raised intraepithelial lymphocytes and crypt hyperplasia — Marsh 3. Lost surface = malabsorption.

🔎 What the endoscopy, biopsy and skin show

ModalityFindingsMeaning
🔬 OGD (macroscopic)Scalloped/notched duodenal folds, mosaic pattern, reduced foldsSuggestive but NOT diagnostic — always biopsy even if it looks normal.
🧫 Duodenal histologyVillous atrophy, crypt hyperplasia, >25 intraepithelial lymphocytes per 100 enterocytes → Marsh gradingThe adult gold standard; take ≥4 samples (patchy disease) incl. the bulb.
🧴 Skin biopsy (dermatitis herpetiformis)Granular IgA deposits at the dermal papillae on direct immunofluorescenceSkin expression of coeliac; effectively confirms the diagnosis.
🧬 HLA-DQ2/DQ8 typingPresent in >95% of coeliacsUseful to EXCLUDE (high NPV) when off gluten or diagnosis unclear.
🦴 DEXALow bone density (osteopenia/osteoporosis)Screens the metabolic-bone consequence of chronic malabsorption.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A normal-looking duodenum at endoscopy does NOT exclude coeliac — the atrophy can be patchy and subtle. If you suspect it, biopsy; the diagnosis lives under the microscope."

🩻 When to look harder

  • Persistent symptoms despite a strict gluten-free diet: reconsider adherence, then investigate for refractory coeliac and EATL (CT/MR enterography, capsule/enteroscopy).
  • Alarm features (weight loss, abdominal pain, GI bleeding) in an established coeliac warrant urgent small-bowel imaging.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to follow and why

  • 📉 Falling anti-tTG on a gluten-free diet is the objective marker of adherence and mucosal healing — recheck at 6–12 months; a persistently high titre means ongoing gluten exposure.
  • 🩸 Recheck the deficiencies (ferritin, folate, B12, calcium, vitamin D) — they should correct as villi recover.
  • 🦴 DEXA to track bone health; ensure calcium/vitamin-D repletion.
  • 🍽️ Dietitian review + symptom response: most feel dramatically better within weeks of strict gluten avoidance.
  • ⚠️ Red flags for complications: symptoms despite strict diet, new weight loss, abdominal pain, or bleeding → investigate for refractory coeliac / EATL / small-bowel adenocarcinoma.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A coeliac who does not improve on a gluten-free diet has one of three problems: they are still eating gluten, they were misdiagnosed, or they have developed a complication — check them in that order."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 Complications of untreated/undiagnosed coeliac disease

🍽️ Malabsorption & metabolic

  • 🩸 Refractory iron/folate-deficiency anaemia; B12 deficiency in extensive disease.
  • 🦴 Osteoporosis / osteomalacia from calcium and vitamin-D malabsorption → fragility fractures.
  • 🤰 Subfertility, recurrent miscarriage, growth failure in children.
  • 🛡️ Functional hyposplenism → risk from encapsulated organisms (vaccinate).

🎗️ The malignant/refractory complications (the ones you must not miss)

  • 🔴 Refractory coeliac disease — persistent villous atrophy and symptoms despite ≥12 months of a strict gluten-free diet (types I and II; type II is pre-lymphomatous).
  • 🧬 Enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma (EATL) — a small-bowel T-cell lymphoma; suspect with new weight loss, pain, obstruction or bleeding in an established coeliac.
  • 🎯 Small-bowel adenocarcinoma — increased risk; also increased ulcerative jejunitis.
⚠️ The alarm shift in an established coeliacWhen a stable coeliac develops NEW weight loss, abdominal pain, night sweats, or GI bleeding — especially if previously well-controlled — think EATL or small-bowel adenocarcinoma and image the small bowel urgently. A strict gluten-free diet is the single best protection against all these complications.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The strict gluten-free diet is not just for symptoms — it heals the gut, protects the bones, and lowers the lymphoma risk. Adherence is the whole treatment."

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🌾 Diagnosis: coeliac disease — strongly positive anti-tTG IgA (normal total IgA) + Marsh 3b villous atrophy, confirmed while eating gluten.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the basics

  • 🧬 The no-biopsy pathway: ESPGHAN allows selected children with anti-tTG ≥10× ULN plus positive endomysial antibody (EMA) on a second sample to be diagnosed without biopsy; adult guidelines increasingly explore high-titre no-biopsy strategies, but duodenal biopsy remains standard in adults.
  • 🔴 Refractory coeliac disease: type I (polyclonal IELs, better prognosis) vs type II (aberrant monoclonal IEL population, pre-EATL, poor prognosis) — distinguished by IEL immunophenotyping and T-cell receptor clonality; type II needs specialist management.
  • 🧪 Seronegative coeliac & the pitfalls: IgA deficiency, patients already off gluten, and very early disease can give negative serology — HLA typing (to exclude), gluten challenge and expert histology resolve ambiguity.
  • 🌍 Non-coeliac gluten sensitivity & wheat allergy: symptomatic gluten reaction without coeliac serology/histology or IgE allergy — a diagnosis of exclusion; important not to mislabel as coeliac.
  • 💊 Emerging therapies: adjuncts to the gluten-free diet under study — gluten-degrading enzymes, tight-junction modulators (larazotide), tTG inhibitors, and immune-tolerising approaches — none yet replaces the diet.
  • 🎗️ Cancer surveillance logic: strict lifelong gluten-free adherence is the main modifiable factor reducing EATL and small-bowel adenocarcinoma risk; new alarm features in an established coeliac mandate urgent small-bowel imaging and enteroscopy.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Gluten-Free Trial That Erased the Diagnosis"

The case: A 38-year-old woman with fatigue, bloating and a mild iron-deficiency anaemia was told by a clinician to "just try cutting out gluten for a few weeks and see." She felt much better, so she stayed gluten-free — and nobody ever tested her. Because there was no formal diagnosis, she was never referred to a dietitian, never had bone-density monitoring, was never vaccinated, and slowly relaxed the diet, eating gluten "on special occasions." Years later she developed weight loss, night sweats, crampy abdominal pain and a partial small-bowel obstruction. Imaging and enteroscopy revealed an enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma. Reviewing her old notes, an anti-tTG had never been sent while she was eating gluten; when finally tested — years into a partial gluten-free diet — the serology was equivocal and the initial biopsies were misread as "near-normal," delaying the cancer diagnosis further.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🍞 EMPIRICAL DIETGluten-free trial started before any testing — the diagnosis was erased.
🧪 NEVER TESTED ON GLUTENNo anti-tTG/biopsy while eating gluten; later tests falsely reassuring.
🚫 NO FOLLOW-UPNo dietitian, no DEXA, no vaccine, no structured coeliac care.
🎗️ MISSED EATLNew weight loss/night sweats/obstruction not urgently worked up.
🔬 MISREAD BIOPSYOff-gluten, near-normal histology delayed the lymphoma diagnosis.

🛡️ What would have saved her: never start a gluten-free diet before testing — send anti-tTG (with total IgA) and biopsy while the patient is eating gluten; give every confirmed coeliac structured follow-up (dietitian, deficiency correction, DEXA, vaccination); and treat new weight loss, night sweats or obstruction in an established coeliac as possible EATL until proven otherwise. This is exactly the discipline that protected the patient in Bed 5.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Just try cutting out gluten' has erased more coeliac diagnoses than any lab error. Test first — the diet is forever, so the diagnosis must be certain."
