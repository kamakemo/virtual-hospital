🧭 The three killers you exclude at the bedside first

Before ACS, your reflex differential for "sudden chest pain + unwell" is the immediately lethal trio: ACS, aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism (± tension pneumothorax, oesophageal rupture). One 12-lead, one focused exam, and a couple of targeted questions triage all three in minutes (see S14).

⚠️ The trap of "the first ECG is normal"A single normal ECG does NOT exclude ACS — up to a third of NSTEMIs have a non-diagnostic initial ECG, and early hyperacute changes are subtle. The answer is serial ECGs (every 15–30 min while symptomatic) and hs-troponin, not reassurance.

  2. 🧪 hs-troponin at 0 and 1 h — the delta matters more than the single value.
  3. 🧮 HEART score to place him in low/moderate/high risk (S5).
  4. 💊 Aspirin continued; P2Y₁₂ + anticoagulation deferred until dissection is off the table and a diagnosis of ACS is firm.
  5. 🩻 Portable CXR (mediastinum, oedema, pneumothorax) while he stays monitored with defib pads to hand.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Rule out the aorta before you anticoagulate the coronary. A tearing-to-the-back story or a wide mediastinum earns a second look BEFORE heparin and ticagrelor go in."
🧪 Resident's self-testHe asks for oxygen "to be safe" though SpO₂ is 95%. Give it?

No. Routine oxygen in normoxic ACS does not help and may harm — hyperoxia causes coronary vasoconstriction and, in the DETO2X-AMI and AVOID trials, showed no benefit (a signal of larger infarcts with oxygen in AVOID). Only give O₂ if SpO₂ < 90%.

S6

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the pain, WHY the danger (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧱 Plaque rupture: a lipid-rich atheroma's fibrous cap tears; sub-endothelial collagen and tissue factor are exposed.
  2. 🩸 Thrombosis: platelets adhere, activate and aggregate; a thrombus forms. Non-occlusive → NSTE-ACS; fully occlusive → STEMI.
  3. ⚡ Supply–demand mismatch: distal myocardium becomes ischaemic. Anaerobic metabolism → adenosine and lactate stimulate cardiac afferents → visceral, poorly-localised pain referred to arm/jaw (shared spinal segments) — which is exactly why it's "atypical."
  4. 💀 Necrosis over time: "time is muscle" — the ischaemic wavefront spreads from endocardium to epicardium. Troponin leaks from dying myocytes; it rises over hours (hence the delta).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A single troponin is a snapshot; ACS is a movie. It's the CHANGE (delta) over 1–3 hours that separates an evolving infarct from chronic troponin leak."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Type 1 vs Type 2 MI

🩸 Type 1 MI

  • Primary plaque rupture + thrombus
  • Treat the clot: antiplatelets, anticoagulation, revascularisation
  • Our patient's likely mechanism

🔋 Type 2 MI (supply–demand)

  • Ischaemia from a mismatch: tachyarrhythmia, anaemia, sepsis, hypotension, hypertensive crisis
  • Treat the CAUSE (transfuse, rate-control, treat sepsis) — stenting the wrong problem harms

💡 A raised troponin is not a diagnosis — it is a finding. Ask: is this a clot to open, or a stressed heart to unload?

🎮 Simulation — The hs-Troponin Delta Dial

Two values, one hour apart. Drag the 1-hour value and watch how the delta (not the absolute number) drives the decision.

🎯 A low, unchanging value with a reassuring story → rule-OUT. A rising delta beyond the assay threshold → rule-IN, regardless of how "normal" one number looks.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (03:14)

  • 📈 12-lead ECG now, repeat at 15 min and with any pain change; posterior (V7–V9) & right-sided (V4R) leads if inferior/posterior suspicion.
  • 🩸 Aspirin 300 mg chewed (given) → 75–100 mg daily.
  • 🧪 hs-troponin 0 & 1 h, plus CBC, U&E, glucose, lipid, coag, CRP.
  • 💉 GTN sublingual/infusion for pain (avoid if hypotensive, RV infarct, or recent PDE5 inhibitor).
  • 💊 Analgesia — small IV opioid if pain persists (note: morphine can slow P2Y₁₂ absorption; use judiciously).
  • 🚫 Hold P2Y₁₂ + anticoagulation until dissection excluded and ACS confirmed; O₂ only if SpO₂ < 90%.
  • 🩻 Portable CXR; continuous cardiac monitoring with defib pads on; IV access ×2.
  • 📞 If any ECG turns into STEMI/STEMI-equivalent → activate primary PCI immediately.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Aspirin and a repeat ECG cost pennies and save lives; the expensive mistakes are the ones you skip because 'he looks well.'"
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Resus-bay priorities — the nurse runs the safety net

  • 📟 Continuous telemetry, defib pads attached; the first hours carry the highest VT/VF risk.
  • 📈 Repeat the ECG the moment pain changes — nurses catch the dynamic ECG doctors miss between rounds. Time-stamp every strip.
  • 😖 Pain score every 5–15 min; escalate analgesia and flag ongoing pain (ongoing pain = ongoing ischaemia = pressure to re-image/re-troponin/cath).
  • 🩸 Cannulate ×2, draw the troponin set, label sample times precisely (the delta depends on accurate timing).
  • 🧾 Keep the patient calm and informed — anxiety drives HR and demand; a composed bedside is anti-ischaemic.
  • 🚫 Confirm no antithrombotics are given until the plan clears the aorta; challenge any premature heparin.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who re-does the ECG on every twinge of pain is the most sensitive ischaemia monitor in the department."

🩻 Adjunct imaging

  • CXR: normal mediastinum, no oedema, no pneumothorax — reassuring for dissection/alternative causes.
  • Bedside echo (POCUS): regional wall-motion abnormality (anterior hypokinesis) supports ischaemia; also screens for effusion, RV strain (PE), and gross valve pathology.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📈 Continuous telemetry with defib to hand — VT/VF peaks in the first hours; ST-segment monitoring catches re-occlusion.
  • 🧪 Serial troponin to define the trajectory; repeat 12-leads with every symptom change.
  • 🩸 BP each 5–15 min — hypotension may mean RV involvement, over-nitration, or evolving shock.
  • 🫁 SpO₂ (target ≥ 94% only if hypoxic), respiratory rate for evolving LV failure.
  • 🧮 Recompute risk (GRACE) once troponin returns — GRACE > 140 pushes toward early (< 24 h) invasive management.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you decide 'ACS,' the monitor's question changes from 'is he ischaemic?' to 'is he about to arrest or re-occlude?' — and the answers are the rhythm and the recurring pain."

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong in the ED phase

  • ⚡ VT / VF / cardiac arrest — commonest early killer; why pads stay on.
  • 🫀 Evolution to STEMI / re-occlusion — the "NSTEMI" that declares itself on the 4th ECG.
  • 💔 Acute LV failure / cardiogenic shock — large territory at risk.
  • 🔊 Mechanical (later, days 3–7): papillary muscle rupture (acute MR), VSD, free-wall rupture — a new murmur + crash.
  • 🧠 Bleeding from antithrombotics; contrast nephropathy around PCI.
  • 🩹 The system complication: premature discharge — the biggest "complication" of all in the ED.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The deadliest ED complication of ACS isn't in the patient — it's the decision to send them home. Treat disposition as a procedure with its own checklist."

🫀 This patient (confirmed MI)

  • DAPT 12 months, high-intensity statin, ACEi/ARB, beta-blocker, ± MRA if EF < 40%

  • Red flags: recurrent chest pain, breathlessness, syncope → 999

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🩻 The "Occlusion MI (OMI)" paradigm: the STEMI/NSTEMI dichotomy misses acute coronary occlusion without ST-elevation criteria. OMI-vs-NOMI thinking (subtle occlusion patterns, hyperacute T waves, modified-Sgarbossa, AI-ECG) identifies patients who benefit from emergent reperfusion despite "non-STEMI" labels.
  • 🧪 hs-troponin nuance: sex-specific 99th-percentile cut-offs; the value of very low single values (< limit of detection) for immediate rule-out; assay-specific 0/1-h and 0/2-h absolute delta thresholds (ESC 2023).
  • 🫀 MINOCA (MI with non-obstructive coronaries): work up plaque disruption (intravascular imaging — OCT/IVUS), coronary spasm (provocation), spontaneous coronary artery dissection (SCAD — think young women, peripartum), microvascular disease, and cardiac MRI to distinguish true MI from myocarditis/Takotsubo.
  • 🧬 Antiplatelet timing debate: routine pre-treatment with P2Y₁₂ before angiography in NSTE-ACS is no longer recommended (no ischaemic benefit, more bleeding — ISAR-REACT 5 favoured prasugrel over ticagrelor with a deferred-loading strategy).
  • 🖥️ Coronary CT angiography in low–intermediate-risk ED chest pain: high NPV for ruling out CAD (ROMICAT-II, PROMISE), useful when troponin/ECG are equivocal and pre-test probability is modest.
  • 🩸 Cardiogenic-shock ACS: culprit-only PCI (CULPRIT-SHOCK), early revascularisation (SHOCK), judicious use of mechanical support.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Antacid That Killed"

The case: A 61-year-old diabetic man came to a crowded ED at 02:00 with "burning" epigastric/chest discomfort and one vomit. Triaged as "dyspepsia." He was given an antacid "GI cocktail," felt "a bit better," and — with the department full — was moved to the corridor to await review. The single ECG taken at triage showed tall R waves and ST depression in V1–V3; no one recorded posterior leads and no one repeated it. Ninety minutes later he was found unresponsive in the corridor chair — a posterior STEMI that had extended, then arrested in VF. Resuscitation failed.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Dyspepsia" label stuck; the GI cocktail "response" falsely reassured.
📈 ECG NOT READPosterior STEMI pattern (ST↓ V1–V3) unrecognised; posterior leads never done.
🔁 NO SERIAL ECGOne snapshot, never repeated despite ongoing symptoms.
🏥 SYSTEMOvercrowding → corridor care → no monitoring, no defib to hand.
🩺 RISK IGNOREDDiabetic with atypical presentation treated as low-risk.

🛡️ What would have saved him: treat every diabetic with chest/epigastric pain as high-risk; read ST depression in V1–V3 as a possible posterior STEMI and record V7–V9; repeat the ECG on any ongoing symptom; keep suspected ACS monitored with defib to hand — never in an unmonitored corridor; and never let a "GI cocktail response" close the door.
