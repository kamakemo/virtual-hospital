🧭 The three time-critical spinal emergencies you separate at the bedside

For "back pain + new neurology" your reflex differential is the compressive trio: metastatic cord compression, cauda equina syndrome, and spinal epidural abscess/haematoma. A focused exam — power, tone, reflexes, a sensory level, saddle sensation, anal tone and a bladder scan — triages all three in minutes (see S14).

⚠️ The trap of "he is still walking, so it can wait"The neurology of MSCC can collapse from "walking with a stick" to "dense paraplegia" within HOURS. Motor function is often the LAST thing to go and the FIRST thing you cannot get back. Do not be reassured by a patient who is still ambulant — that is precisely the window in which to act.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the cord dies, WHY the clock matters (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🦴 Metastasis to vertebral body: tumour seeds the bone marrow of the vertebra (the commonest site), grows, and pushes backward into the epidural space — or the weakened bone collapses and retropulses fragments onto the cord.
  2. 🩸 Compression of the epidural venous plexus: the first casualty is venous drainage. Rising epidural pressure obstructs the valveless venous plexus → vasogenic oedema of the cord (this is what dexamethasone targets).
  3. ⚡ Arterial compromise and infarction: as pressure climbs, arterial supply fails → cord ischaemia, then irreversible infarction. This is the switch from "salvageable oedema" to "dead cord."
  4. 💀 Time is cord: the vicious cycle — oedema raises pressure, pressure worsens ischaemia, ischaemia worsens oedema. Once infarction occurs, decompression cannot restore function. That is why hours matter and why lost function rarely returns.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"MSCC is a race between rising epidural pressure and the cord's blood supply. Dexamethasone slows the oedema; decompression or radiotherapy removes the pressure — but only before the cord infarcts."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Cord (conus) lesion vs Cauda equina lesion

🧠 Spinal cord / conus (UMN)

  • A clear sensory LEVEL on the trunk
  • UMN signs below the lesion: spasticity, hyperreflexia, upgoing plantars, clonus
  • Early, painless urinary retention
  • Symmetrical bilateral weakness; conus → mixed with early sphincter involvement

🐴 Cauda equina (LMN)

  • Saddle (perianal) anaesthesia, bilateral sciatica
  • LMN signs: flaccid, areflexic, downgoing/absent plantars
  • Urinary retention with overflow, loss of anal tone
  • Asymmetrical, radicular leg pain and weakness

💡 Both are emergencies needing urgent MRI. The exam distinguishes UMN (cord) from LMN (cauda equina), but the action — image now, steroids if malignant, urgent decompression — is the same urgency.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Ward priorities — the nurse guards the neurology

  • 📉 Serial neuro-obs of leg power and sensory level — the nurse who repeats the power exam catches deterioration between rounds. Any drop is an emergency escalation.
  • 🛏️ Spinal precautions and pressure care — log-roll, keep flat until stability known; an immobile, sensory-impaired patient is at high risk of pressure ulcers over the sacrum and heels.
  • 🚽 Bladder and bowel care — catheter on free drainage, monitor output; watch for constipation from opioids and immobility.
  • 😖 Pain score regularly; ongoing/escalating pain may signal progression or instability — flag it, do not just re-dose.
  • 🦵 VTE and skin — mechanical prophylaxis, reposition on a schedule, protect heels.
  • 🧾 Keep the patient and family informed — this is frightening; a calm, honest bedside and early palliative-care involvement matter.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who documents the leg power every few hours is the single most sensitive monitor of the cord — a falling number is the alarm that reopens the whole plan."

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🦵 Serial leg power + sensory level — the core neuro-obs; any deterioration triggers urgent re-imaging and an MDT re-discussion. Chart the level, do not just say "moving legs."
  • 🚽 Bladder and bowel function — catheter output, and watch for ileus/constipation; new incontinence or worsening retention = progression.
  • 🩸 BP and HR — neurogenic shock (hypotension + bradycardia) if a high or unstable lesion; also monitor for autonomic dysreflexia in high lesions.
  • 🧪 Glucose (steroid-induced hyperglycaemia) and corrected calcium.
  • 🛏️ Skin/pressure areas and VTE surveillance in an immobile, sensory-impaired patient.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After 'MSCC confirmed,' the monitor's question becomes 'is the cord getting worse right now?' — and the answer is the leg power chart and the bladder, checked in hours, not days."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong

  • 🦽 Permanent paraplegia / paraparesis — the feared outcome when treatment is delayed past cord infarction.
  • 🚽 Permanent bladder, bowel and sexual dysfunction — neurogenic bladder, faecal incontinence, impotence.
  • 🩸 VTE (DVT/PE) — immobile cancer patient; a leading cause of death.
  • 🛏️ Pressure ulcers over insensate areas; spasticity and contractures as UMN signs evolve.
  • 🧱 Spinal instability / further collapse — mechanical pain and neurological worsening; may need stabilisation.
  • 🧪 Steroid harms — hyperglycaemia, GI bleed, psychosis, proximal myopathy, infection; hypercalcaemia from the underlying malignancy.
  • 🩹 The system complication: delayed MRI / delayed treatment — the biggest and most preventable "complication" of MSCC.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The deadliest complication of MSCC is not in the cord — it is in the calendar. A scan deferred to 'the morning list' is how a walking patient becomes a wheelchair patient."

📋 The MSCC survivor vs the low-risk mechanical back pain — two different worlds

  • Steroid taper plan, PPI, bone-protective agent, oncology follow-up for systemic therapy
  • Rehab, physiotherapy/occupational therapy, catheter/bowel management, pressure care

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🧱 Spinal instability — the SINS score: the Spinal Instability Neoplastic Score (location, pain, bone lesion quality, alignment, collapse, posterolateral involvement) flags mechanically unstable spines that need surgical stabilisation regardless of radiosensitivity.
  • 🔪 "Separation surgery" + SBRT: for radioresistant tumours, minimal surgery to create a margin between tumour and cord followed by stereotactic body radiotherapy achieves durable local control while sparing the cord — a paradigm shift from wide decompression.
  • 🎯 Stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT): high-dose conformal RT for oligometastatic or radioresistant disease; cord tolerance and vertebral compression fracture risk are the key constraints.
  • 🩻 Radioresistant vs radiosensitive tumours: myeloma, lymphoma, small-cell and germ-cell are exquisitely radiosensitive (often RT alone); renal, melanoma, sarcoma and thyroid are radioresistant (favour surgery/SBRT).
  • 🧬 Prognostic tools: revised Tokuhashi and Tomita scores estimate survival to guide how aggressive the local treatment should be — matching the operation to the prognosis.
  • 🚑 Systems of care: MSCC coordinators, 24/7 MRI access and defined pathways (NICE NG234) reduce the door-to-treatment interval — the single most modifiable determinant of walking again.

🎭 M&M — "The Back Pain That Was Left Until Morning"

The case: A 68-year-old woman with known metastatic breast cancer attended at 23:00 with two weeks of worsening mid-back pain, now with heavy legs and difficulty passing urine. She was still walking, if slowly. The night team documented "chronic back pain, on treatment for cancer" and "for morning review." She was given oral morphine and a bed. No one examined for a sensory level or scanned her bladder. By the 08:30 ward round she had a dense paraplegia with a T8 sensory level and 900 mL of retained urine. The MRI at 14:00 the next day showed T7–T8 cord compression with established cord signal change. Decompression and radiotherapy followed — but she never walked again.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Chronic back pain in a cancer patient" label stuck; the new leg and bladder symptoms were not treated as red flags.
🩺 EXAM NOT DONENo sensory level, no power chart, no bladder scan — the cord signs were never sought.
⏱️ MRI DELAYEDScan deferred to the "morning list" then the afternoon — hours in which the cord infarcted.
💊 NO STEROIDSDexamethasone never given while awaiting imaging.
🏥 SYSTEMNo out-of-hours MSCC pathway or coordinator; "still walking" was mistaken for "can wait."

🛡️ What would have saved her: treat every cancer patient with new back pain and any leg/bladder/bowel symptom as MSCC; examine for a sensory level and scan the bladder; give dexamethasone and arrange an emergency whole-spine MRI overnight, not on the morning list; and never mistake "still ambulant" for "not urgent" — that is the exact window in which walking is preserved. This is the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 5.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Review in the morning' has paralysed more cancer patients than any tumour biology. In suspected MSCC the scan is tonight, the steroids are now, and the exam is at the bedside — not at 08:30."
