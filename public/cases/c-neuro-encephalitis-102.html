🧭 The bedside test that reframes the case

The combination of fever + altered mental state + a focal cortical sign (dysphasia) + a focal seizure should snap your differential from "delirium/UTI" to encephalitis. Confusion with a focal neurological sign and fever is encephalitis until proven otherwise.

⚠️ The trap of "confusion = infection screen and wait"Treating this as routine delirium — send a urine dip, start ward antibiotics, "review the confusion tomorrow" — is exactly how HSV encephalitis is missed. Fever + focal cortical dysfunction demands urgent neuroimaging, LP, EEG AND empirical aciclovir, not a next-day review.

  2. 🦠 Add empirical ceftriaxone ± dexamethasone until bacterial meningitis is excluded — the pictures overlap.
  3. 🧲 Urgent MRI brain (temporal-lobe changes) and EEG (periodic lateralised discharges); CT first only if MRI is delayed or herniation risk suggests it.

🧪 Resident's self-testThe night team suggests holding aciclovir until the CSF HSV PCR result is back "to avoid over-treating." Agree?

No. Start aciclovir empirically on suspicion. A negative early HSV PCR (within the first ~72 hours) does NOT exclude encephalitis — false negatives are well described very early — so you continue aciclovir and repeat the LP/PCR in 3–7 days. Aciclovir is comparatively safe (mainly renal — hydrate and dose-adjust); the risk of withholding it is catastrophic, irreversible temporal-lobe necrosis. This is exactly the S21 M&M error.

🧠 Why the temporal lobes — and why that matters

HSV-1 has a striking predilection for the medial temporal lobes, insula and orbitofrontal cortex (thought to reach the CNS via the olfactory/trigeminal routes). That anatomy explains the syndrome: memory disturbance, personality/behavioural change, dysphasia, olfactory/gustatory hallucinations and focal (often temporal-lobe) seizures. Recognising the temporal-lobe syndrome lets you treat before any test returns.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the temporal lobes, WHY the urgency (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🦠 Reactivation and spread: HSV-1 (usually a reactivation of latent virus in the trigeminal ganglion, sometimes primary) reaches the CNS via the olfactory or trigeminal nerve routes, seeding the medial temporal and orbitofrontal cortex.
  2. 🔥 Necrotising inflammation: viral replication in neurons and the host immune response cause a haemorrhagic, necrotising encephalitis — oedema, neuronal death and, on imaging, swollen, bright temporal lobes.
  3. ⚡ Cortical irritation and mass effect: the inflamed cortex generates focal seizures; progressive oedema raises ICP, drops the GCS, and can cause herniation.
  4. 💀 Irreversible loss: necrosis of memory-critical structures (hippocampi) produces the classic surviving deficit — dense anterograde amnesia and epilepsy. Aciclovir halts viral replication; it cannot regrow dead cortex — hence "time is brain."
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Aciclovir stops the virus replicating; it cannot resurrect necrosed temporal lobe. The neurons you save are the ones still alive when the drug goes in — which is why the drug goes in first."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Encephalitis vs Meningitis

🧠 Encephalitis (brain parenchyma)

  • Altered behaviour/personality, confusion, focal deficits, seizures
  • Problem is inflamed BRAIN tissue
  • Empirical IV aciclovir is the emergency drug

🦠 Meningitis (the meninges)

  • Headache, neck stiffness, photophobia, fever
  • Problem is inflamed MENINGES
  • Empirical ceftriaxone (± dexamethasone) is the emergency drug

💡 They overlap ("meningoencephalitis") — so in the febrile confused patient, cover BOTH: aciclovir for the brain and ceftriaxone for the meninges until the work-up separates them.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (14:26)

  • 💉 IV aciclovir 10 mg/kg three times daily immediately (weight-based, ~840 mg/dose at 84 kg), with generous IV fluids to protect renal function; adjust dose for renal impairment.
  • 🦠 IV ceftriaxone 2 g ± dexamethasone as empirical cover for bacterial meningitis until CSF excludes it (overlapping picture).
  • 🧲 Urgent MRI brain (temporal-lobe signal change); CT head first if MRI delayed or herniation risk suspected before LP.
  • 💧 LP once safe: opening pressure, cell count/differential, protein, glucose (paired serum), HSV-1/2 PCR, enterovirus/VZV PCR, and save CSF for autoimmune antibodies.
  • 🧠 EEG — periodic lateralised epileptiform discharges (PLEDs) over the temporal region support the diagnosis and detect non-convulsive seizures.
  • ⚡ Anti-seizure medication for the focal seizure; bloods (FBC, U&E, LFTs, glucose, cultures), consider autoimmune/paraneoplastic screen (anti-NMDAR) if picture fits.
  • 📟 Continuous monitoring, seizure precautions, neuro-obs, senior neurology + critical-care review.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Aciclovir first, then MRI, then LP, then EEG — and keep bacterial cover running until the CSF clears it. The order of the tests is negotiable; the timing of the aciclovir is not."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Resus-bay priorities — the nurse runs the safety net

  • 💉 Get the aciclovir in the room FAST — calculate the weight-based dose, run it with fluids, and challenge any plan that sends him to MRI with the drug unprescribed. The nurse who asks "shall I start the aciclovir now?" saves temporal lobe.
  • 💧 Generous IV hydration + monitor renal function/urine output — aciclovir can crystallise in the tubules; hydration and correct dosing prevent nephrotoxicity.
  • ⚡ Seizure precautions — padded sides, suction and oxygen to hand, time and describe any seizure; watch for subtle non-convulsive seizures (staring, automatisms).
  • 📟 Frequent GCS, pupils and neuro-obs — a falling GCS from cerebral oedema needs urgent escalation and re-imaging.
  • 🛡️ Orientate and reassure a confused, frightened patient and family; a calm, low-stimulus environment reduces agitation.
  • 🩹 Aspiration precautions post-ictally; nil by mouth until swallow is safe.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who refuses to move a suspected-encephalitis patient to the scanner before aciclovir is started is the single most effective safety barrier in the department."

  • MRI temporal-lobe change + EEG temporal PLEDs + lymphocytic CSF together strongly support HSE — often before the PCR confirms.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🧠 Serial GCS, pupils and neuro-obs — cerebral oedema from necrotising encephalitis can raise ICP; a falling GCS demands re-imaging and critical-care escalation.
  • ⚡ Seizure monitoring ± EEG — watch for recurrent focal seizures and non-convulsive status (unexplained persistent low GCS).
  • 🩺 Renal function + urine output — the key aciclovir toxicity; keep well hydrated and adjust the dose.
  • 🧪 Repeat LP / HSV PCR if the early PCR was negative and suspicion persists, and to help guide duration of therapy.
  • 💧 Sodium / fluid balance — SIADH can complicate CNS infection and worsen oedema/seizures.
  • 🧠 Autoimmune reassessment — if virology stays negative or a relapsing/psychiatric-movement picture emerges, send anti-NMDAR antibodies and reconsider the diagnosis.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After aciclovir is running, the monitor's questions become: is the ICP rising (GCS/pupils), are there hidden seizures (EEG), and are the kidneys tolerating the drug (U&E/urine output)? Those three trends drive the next 24 hours."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong

  • 🧠 Cerebral oedema + raised ICP / herniation — the acute killer in fulminant necrotising encephalitis.
  • ⚡ Status epilepticus / non-convulsive seizures — from cortical irritation; may need EEG to detect.
  • 🧩 Dense anterograde amnesia — hippocampal necrosis leaves the classic Korsakoff-like memory deficit, the commonest surviving disability.
  • 🎭 Personality/behavioural change & dysphasia — may persist as long-term sequelae.
  • ♻️ Relapse — clinical relapse (sometimes immune-mediated, including post-HSV anti-NMDAR encephalitis) days-to-weeks after treatment.
  • 💊 Aciclovir nephrotoxicity — preventable with hydration and correct dosing.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"HSV encephalitis kills through the ICP and disables through the hippocampus. And beware the second illness — a post-HSV anti-NMDAR relapse that returns weeks later with new psychiatric and movement symptoms."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🧠 Diagnosis: suspected HSV-1 encephalitis (fever, temporal-lobe syndrome, focal seizure). Empirical IV aciclovir started within minutes; bacterial cover in parallel.
  2. 🧲 Confirm around the drug: MRI (temporal change), EEG (PLEDs), LP with HSV PCR — repeat LP if early PCR negative.
  3. ⚡ Treat seizures, manage ICP/oedema, protect renal function with fluids and correct aciclovir dosing.
  4. 🧬 Keep the autoimmune differential open — if virology negative or a relapsing/psychiatric-movement picture emerges, send anti-NMDAR antibodies and screen for teratoma.
  5. 🏥 Admit to HDU/ICU as needed; neurology; complete 14–21 days IV aciclovir; plan neuro-rehab for cognitive/behavioural sequelae.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The plan is one sentence: aciclovir now, cover the meninges, confirm with MRI/EEG/LP, treat the seizures, and never close the door on autoimmune encephalitis. Anything that reorders 'aciclovir' to later is the mistake."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Recovery, rehab, and the relapse safety-net — the after-story

🧠 The patient (recovering HSE)

  • Complete the full 14–21 day IV aciclovir course
  • Neuro-rehabilitation for memory, language and behaviour; neuropsychology input
  • Epilepsy follow-up (many need ongoing anti-seizure medication) and driving advice per local rules
  • Return red flags: recurrent fever, new confusion, seizures, worsening behaviour

♻️ The relapse safety-net

  • Warn about post-HSV relapse — including immune-mediated anti-NMDAR encephalitis weeks later (new psychiatric/movement symptoms, dyskinesias)
  • Low threshold to re-investigate (repeat MRI/LP, antibody testing) if symptoms return
  • Clear written information for patient and family
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Surviving the acute illness is only half the story — HSV encephalitis leaves an amnestic, epileptic aftermath needing rehab, and can trigger a delayed autoimmune (anti-NMDAR) relapse. Safety-net for both before you discharge."

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🧬 Post-HSV anti-NMDAR encephalitis: a well-described immune-mediated relapse weeks after HSE — new psychiatric symptoms, movement disorder and choreoathetosis, often PCR-negative; treat with immunotherapy, not more aciclovir. A key reason to keep the autoimmune door open.
  • 🧫 Aciclovir resistance & alternatives: rare thymidine-kinase-deficient HSV (mainly immunocompromised) may need foscarnet; recognise treatment failure vs immune relapse vs under-dosing.
  • 🧠 Refractory raised ICP in necrotising encephalitis: neuro-critical-care measures, osmotherapy, and the rare role of decompressive craniectomy in fulminant temporal-lobe swelling.
  • 💊 Adjunctive corticosteroids in HSE: biologically plausible (dampening oedema/immunopathology) and studied (e.g. the DexEnceph question) but not established standard — a live evidence gap.
  • 🧾 Duration & repeat-PCR-guided therapy: the 14–21 day standard, and protocols that repeat CSF HSV PCR at the end of treatment before stopping to reduce relapse.
  • 🌍 The wider encephalitis differential: autoimmune (anti-NMDAR, LGI1, GABA-B), paraneoplastic, VZV vasculopathy, arboviral and, in the immunocompromised, opportunistic causes — structured antibody and PCR panels with a tumour screen where indicated.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Aciclovir That Waited for the PCR"

The case: A 52-year-old man was admitted with two days of fever and "confusion, ?UTI." He was disinhibited and dysphasic, and a nurse noted brief arm-twitching. The plan was sensible on paper — MRI, LP, CSF HSV PCR — but the team decided to wait for the PCR result before starting aciclovir "to avoid unnecessary treatment." The LP was delayed to the next day; the PCR took another day. Aciclovir was finally started ~48 hours after arrival. By then MRI showed extensive bilateral temporal-lobe necrosis. He survived with a dense, permanent amnesia and refractory temporal-lobe epilepsy — unable to return to work or live independently.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

💉 ACICLOVIR DELAYEDDrug withheld pending PCR — the single decision that cost his temporal lobes.
🧠 ANCHORING"Confusion, ?UTI" label hid a brain-parenchyma emergency.
🔁 PCR MISUNDERSTOODTeam believed they needed the PCR to start; it only confirms — and can be early-negative.
🏥 SYSTEM DELAYLP and PCR spread over two days while the drug waited.
⚡ FOCAL SIGNS MISSEDDysphasia + focal seizure not recognised as encephalitic red flags.

🛡️ What would have saved him: start IV aciclovir empirically the moment encephalitis is suspected — MRI, EEG, LP and PCR are done around the drug, never before it; treat fever + confusion + focal signs/seizures as encephalitis, not "confusion, ?UTI"; and understand that the PCR confirms but never gates treatment, and can be falsely negative early. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 2 — twenty minutes to the drug, and it stayed running through an early-negative PCR.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'We will start aciclovir once the PCR is back' has destroyed more temporal lobes than the virus itself. The drug is the resuscitation — the PCR is confirmation you can afford to wait for; the treatment is not."
