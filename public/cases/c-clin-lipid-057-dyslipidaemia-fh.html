🔥 Why the clinic is where FH lives or diesHeterozygous FH affects roughly 1 in 250 people — commoner than type 1 diabetes — yet fewer than 1 in 10 are ever diagnosed. Untreated, heterozygous FH carries a 10–13× higher risk of premature coronary disease; homozygous FH can cause MI in childhood. The tragedy is that it is eminently treatable: the whole task is to recognise it, treat to target, and screen the family.

🧭 The three questions that frame every new dyslipidaemia

For any abnormal lipid panel, ask in order: (1) Is it primary or secondary? (screen thyroid, renal, liver, glucose, drugs). (2) Is this monogenic FH or polygenic/lifestyle? (height of the LDL, tendon signs, family pedigree, genetic testing). (3) What is this person's overall cardiovascular risk, and therefore their LDL target? Everything that follows — how hard you treat, and whom else you test — flows from these three answers.

⚠️ The trap of "he's young and fit, so he's low-risk"Standard risk calculators (SCORE2, QRISK, pooled-cohort equations) systematically under-estimate risk in FH because they cannot capture a lifetime of LDL exposure from birth. FH is automatically treated as high (or very-high) risk regardless of a low calculated score. Never let a reassuring 10-year risk number talk you out of aggressive treatment in FH.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The cardiology resident thinks out loud after seeing him:

🧠 Frame it as a lifelong burden, not a single number

"This is not 'a slightly high cholesterol to watch.' The LDL is 6.8 with tendon xanthomata and a father dead at 40 — that's clinical familial hypercholesterolaemia. My job is to (1) make the diagnosis formally with a validated tool, (2) treat him to a risk-based LDL target starting with a high-intensity statin, and (3) screen his family — his kids and siblings each have a 50% chance of the same gene. Treating him and forgetting them would be half a job."

🎯 The resident's order of operations

  1. 🧮 Score it: apply the Dutch Lipid Clinic Network (or Simon Broome) criteria → this man scores in the "definite FH" range (S15).
  2. 🎯 Set the target: FH is (at least) high-risk → LDL < 1.8 mmol/L (<70 mg/dL); with any ASCVD or a major risk factor he becomes very-high-risk → < 1.4 mmol/L (<55) and ≥50% reduction.
  3. 💊 Start high-intensity statin (atorvastatin 40–80 mg or rosuvastatin 20–40 mg) — expect ~50% LDL fall.
  4. ➕ Plan escalation: if not at target, add ezetimibe, then a PCSK9 inhibitor for very-high-risk FH still above goal.
  5. 🧬 Cascade-screen first-degree relatives — the single highest-yield public-health act in this clinic.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Diagnose the index case, treat to target, and screen the family — three moves. Miss any one of them and FH keeps killing down the generations."
🧪 Resident's self-testHe wonders whether to try "6 months of diet and lifestyle first" before any drug, since the patient is fit. Reasonable?

No — not in FH. Lifestyle is essential adjunctive advice, but diet alone lowers LDL by only ~5–15% and cannot bring a genetic LDL of 6.8 anywhere near target. Delaying a statin adds months of unnecessary LDL burden to arteries that have already been marinating in cholesterol since birth. In confirmed FH, start the statin at diagnosis alongside lifestyle advice — the "lower for longer" the arteries have been exposed, the sooner you must intervene.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY LDL is causal and WHY FH is lethal (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧬 The LDL receptor: hepatocytes clear circulating LDL via the LDL receptor (LDLR). In FH, a loss-of-function mutation in LDLR (or APOB, the ligand, or a gain-of-function PCSK9 that destroys the receptor) means the liver cannot pull LDL out of the blood — so LDL rises from birth.
  2. 🩸 Retention & oxidation: excess LDL infiltrates the arterial intima, is oxidised, and is engulfed by macrophages → foam cells → fatty streaks → atheromatous plaque. The higher and the longer the LDL, the faster the plaque.
  3. ⏳ Cumulative burden ("area under the curve"): atherosclerosis integrates LDL × time. An FH patient with LDL 6.8 from birth accumulates by age 35 the arterial cholesterol burden a normocholesterolaemic person reaches only in old age — hence MI in the 40s.
  4. 💥 The event: a vulnerable plaque ruptures, thrombosis occludes the vessel, and the first symptom is often a myocardial infarction — sometimes fatal, in a person who felt perfectly well the day before.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Atherosclerosis is LDL multiplied by time. FH loads the artery from birth — so the earlier you lower LDL and the longer you keep it low, the more disease you prevent. Lower for longer."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Monogenic FH vs Polygenic / lifestyle hypercholesterolaemia

🧬 Monogenic FH

  • Single high-impact LDLR/APOB/PCSK9 mutation, autosomal dominant
  • Very high LDL (often >4.9–8), present from birth
  • Tendon xanthomata, premature CAD, vertical family pedigree
  • CASCADE-SCREEN the family — 50% of first-degree relatives affected

🧩 Polygenic / lifestyle

  • Many small-effect gene variants + diet/obesity/inactivity
  • Moderately raised LDL, no tendon xanthomata
  • No clear dominant pedigree; relatives not at fixed 50% risk
  • Lifestyle has more leverage; cascade screening not indicated

💡 The height of the LDL, the tendon signs, and the pedigree separate the two — and the distinction changes whether you screen the whole family.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The clinic order set

  • 🧪 Confirm the lipid profile (repeat fasting/non-fasting lipids, calculate/measure LDL; consider apoB and Lp(a) once).
  • 🧾 Secondary-cause screen: TSH, LFTs, U&E/eGFR, urine ACR (nephrotic), HbA1c, fasting glucose; medication review.
  • 🧮 Score for FH: Dutch Lipid Clinic Network or Simon Broome criteria; refer for genetic testing where available.
  • 💊 High-intensity statin now: atorvastatin 40–80 mg (or rosuvastatin 20–40 mg) — do not defer for a "diet trial" in FH.
  • ➕ Pre-plan escalation: add ezetimibe 10 mg if not at target at 4–12 weeks; refer for a PCSK9 inhibitor if very-high-risk and still above goal.
  • 🧬 Initiate cascade screening: counsel and consent to contact first-degree relatives (children, siblings, parents) for lipid ± genetic testing.
  • 📅 Baseline safety bloods (ALT, CK if symptomatic) and a follow-up lipid check in 8–12 weeks to titrate to target.
  • 🩻 Consider a coronary artery calcium score or imaging in selected FH adults to refine risk and motivate adherence.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In FH the statin script and the family-screening referral are written on the SAME visit — one saves the patient, the other saves the family."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Lipid-clinic nursing priorities — adherence is the whole game

  • 💬 Education: explain FH in plain language — "an inherited gene that keeps cholesterol high from birth; excellent treatments exist." Understanding drives lifelong adherence.
  • 💊 Statin counselling: take it long-term, expect ~50% LDL fall, report — but do not silently stop for — muscle symptoms; most aches are not the statin (nocebo).
  • 🧬 Family engagement: help the patient list first-degree relatives, provide FH-support-group information, and coordinate cascade-testing invitations.
  • 🥗 Lifestyle: reinforce a Mediterranean-style diet, activity, smoking avoidance and weight — adjuncts that lower risk even though they cannot normalise a genetic LDL.
  • 📅 Follow-up & monitoring: arrange the 8–12-week lipid recheck; teach what "at target" means so the patient co-owns the number.
  • 🧷 PCSK9i / inclisiran practicalities: teach subcutaneous self-injection technique and the twice-yearly (inclisiran) or fortnightly/monthly (PCSK9 mAb) schedule where prescribed.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The most powerful lipid drug is the one the patient actually keeps taking — the nurse who secures adherence and family buy-in prevents more MIs than any single prescription."

🧠 Why genetic testing matters — and its limitsA pathogenic LDLR, APOB or PCSK9 variant confirms FH and makes cascade screening of relatives simpler and cheaper (test relatives for the one family variant). Treat the phenotype, not just the genotype.

🩻 Adjunct imaging

  • Coronary artery calcium (CAC) score: a non-contrast CT quantifying calcified plaque; a rising CAC in an FH adult refines residual risk and powerfully motivates adherence (though CAC can be zero early despite soft plaque).
  • Carotid ultrasound / CT coronary angiography: selectively, to document subclinical atherosclerosis in higher-risk FH patients.

💊 Medications

💉 The lipid-lowering drug map — what, why, and the traps

DrugRoleMechanism / caution
High-intensity statin (atorvastatin 40–80, rosuvastatin 20–40)First-line, foundation therapy for all FHHMG-CoA reductase inhibition → ↑LDL-receptor expression → ~50% LDL fall. Watch ALT; true myopathy rare (check CK if symptomatic).
Ezetimibe 10 mgAdd when not at target on max statin (IMPROVE-IT)Blocks intestinal Niemann-Pick C1-like-1 cholesterol absorption → extra ~15–20% LDL fall. Very well tolerated.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Statin first, ezetimibe next, PCSK9 inhibitor for the very-high-risk who are still above goal — you escalate combination therapy until the LDL hits target, you don't stop at 'on a statin'."
🚫 The 'don't-give-up-the-statin' trap❌ Do NOT abandon statins for minor, non-specific muscle aches without a proper rechallenge. Most "statin intolerance" is nocebo (SAMSON, ODYSSEY-style n-of-1 data). Rechallenge, try a lower dose, alternate-day dosing, or a different statin before declaring true intolerance and reaching for bempedoic acid — the statin is the single most protective drug you can prescribe here.
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to monitor and why

  • 🎯 LDL to target: recheck lipids at 8–12 weeks after each change; the endpoint is the risk-based number (<1.8, or <1.4 if very-high-risk), not "on a statin".
  • 🧪 Liver enzymes: baseline ALT and after dose changes; asymptomatic mild rises rarely require stopping. Routine CK is not needed unless there are muscle symptoms.
  • 💪 Muscle symptoms: characterise them — true statin myopathy is uncommon; assess CK only if symptomatic; distinguish nocebo from genuine intolerance with structured rechallenge.
  • 🩸 Glucose: statins slightly raise new-onset diabetes risk — a small, accepted trade-off vastly outweighed by CV benefit; monitor HbA1c per risk.
  • 🧬 Family screening progress: track which relatives have been contacted, tested, diagnosed and treated — the cascade is a monitored outcome, not a one-off suggestion.
  • ❤️ Emergent ASCVD: reassess risk category over time — a new diagnosis of diabetes, hypertension, smoking or established ASCVD moves the target from <1.8 to <1.4.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Treat to a NUMBER, then keep it there for life — dyslipidaemia has no finish line, only a target you must hold decade after decade."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — of the disease and of the mismanagement

  • ❤️ Premature ASCVD: MI, angina, stroke and peripheral arterial disease decades early — the whole point of treatment is to prevent this.
  • 🫀 Aortic-valve / supravalvular disease in severe/homozygous FH from cholesterol deposition.
  • 👶 Homozygous FH: LDL often >13 mmol/L, xanthomata in childhood, MI/aortic stenosis in the teens — needs specialist care, PCSK9i, lomitapide/evinacumab, or lipoprotein apheresis.
  • 💪 Statin adverse effects: myalgia (usually nocebo), rare true myopathy/rhabdomyolysis, transaminitis; a small increase in new-onset diabetes.
  • 🧠 The management complications (the real killers here): under-treatment ("on a statin" but never to target), premature statin cessation for nocebo myalgia, and — worst of all — failing to screen the family, leaving affected relatives to present with their first, sometimes fatal, MI.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The deadliest complication of FH is not in your patient's arteries — it is the untested sibling and the untreated child who were never invited in. Cascade screening is complication prevention."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🧬 Diagnosis: clinical FH (very high LDL + tendon xanthomata + premature family CAD) — score with Dutch Lipid Clinic Network / Simon Broome; refer for genetic confirmation.
  2. 🎯 Set the target: FH ⇒ at least high-risk (<1.8 mmol/L, ≥50% reduction); very-high-risk if any ASCVD/major risk factor (<1.4).

  5. 🥗 Lifestyle + risk-factor control and structured follow-up to hold LDL at target for life.

  • No family cascade required
  • Periodic risk reassessment and lipid recheck
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Dyslipidaemia is discharged to a target, not to a date — the plan is 'keep the LDL here for the rest of your life, and make sure your family has been tested.'"

💎 Clinical Pearls

🧬 LDL is causalEvery 1 mmol/L LDL fall ≈ 22% fewer events (CTT) — and benefit is cumulative. Lower for longer.
🎯 Target by riskVery-high <1.4, high <1.8, moderate <2.6 — plus ≥50% reduction for high/very-high.
💛 See the diagnosisTendon xanthomata + premature corneal arcus + high LDL in the young = FH.
🪜 Escalate to targetStatin → ezetimibe → PCSK9i (or inclisiran/bempedoic acid) — don't stop at "on a statin".
🧬 Cascade the familyOne index case → test every first-degree relative → extend outward. Save people you never meet.
💪 Nocebo, not statinMost "statin intolerance" is nocebo — rechallenge before abandoning the most protective drug.
🧾 Rule out secondariesThyroid, nephrotic, cholestasis, drugs — before you call a high LDL "primary".
🩸 Measure Lp(a) onceAn independent, genetically-fixed risk multiplier, often high in FH.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the clinic basics

  • 🧬 FH genetics: loss-of-function LDLR (commonest), defective ligand APOB, and gain-of-function PCSK9 cause monogenic FH. Heterozygous (~1 in 250, one bad allele) vs homozygous/compound-heterozygous (rare, LDL often >13, MI in childhood, needs PCSK9i, lomitapide, evinacumab or apheresis). Distinguish monogenic from polygenic hypercholesterolaemia (a burden of many small-effect variants) — genetics guides cascade screening.
  • 🩸 Lipoprotein(a): an LDL-like particle with apolipoprotein(a), genetically determined, an independent causal ASCVD (and aortic-stenosis) risk factor largely unmoved by statins. Emerging therapies — antisense oligonucleotides (pelacarsen) and siRNA (olpasiran) — are in outcome trials.
  • ⏳ Cumulative LDL burden / "lower for longer": Mendelian-randomisation data show that a lifetime of modestly lower LDL prevents far more disease than the same reduction started late — the argument for early, sustained treatment in FH.
  • 💪 Statin intolerance — true vs nocebo: blinded n-of-1 and crossover designs (SAMSON) show most symptoms occur equally on placebo. Strategies: rechallenge, lower/alternate-day dosing, switch statin, then bempedoic acid, ezetimibe, PCSK9i, inclisiran. Reserve the "intolerant" label for genuine, reproducible, dechallenge/rechallenge-confirmed symptoms.
  • 🧈 Hypertriglyceridaemia: very high TG (>10 mmol/L) risks acute pancreatitis (fibrate, fish oil, treat secondary causes, avoid alcohol); moderate TG with high residual risk on a statin — icosapent ethyl reduced events in REDUCE-IT. Distinguish this from the LDL/ASCVD problem.
  • 🥓 Secondary dyslipidaemias: hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome, cholestasis, poorly-controlled diabetes, alcohol, and drugs (corticosteroids, ciclosporin, some antiretrovirals, atypical antipsychotics) — always screen before labelling "primary".
  • 🩻 Imaging as a risk-refiner: coronary artery calcium scoring reclassifies intermediate-risk patients and, in FH, can motivate adherence — though a zero CAC does not exclude soft plaque in young FH.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Cholesterol Nobody Named"

The case: At 30, a young man saw his GP with an incidental cholesterol of 9 mmol/L (LDL 8) and firm lumps on his Achilles tendons. He was lean and fit. He was told his cholesterol was "a bit high" and to "cut down on fatty food"; no statin was started, the letters "FH" were never written, and nobody asked to test his brothers or his two young children. He returned to a busy life. At 44 he collapsed and died of a myocardial infarction — his first and only cardiac symptom. Years later, one of his children was found, incidentally, to have an LDL of 6 in a routine screen; both children carried the same LDLR variant and had been untreated the whole time.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🍔 "IT'S THE DIET"A genetic LDL of 8 blamed on food; lifestyle advice given instead of a statin.
💛 SIGNS IGNOREDTendon xanthomata in a young man not recognised as diagnostic of FH.
🧬 NO DIAGNOSISFH never formally scored, named or genetically confirmed — so no urgency.
👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 NO CASCADESiblings and children never screened — affected relatives left untreated for years.
💊 NO TREATMENTThe single most protective drug — a statin — was never even offered.

🛡️ What would have saved him — and his children: read a very high LDL with tendon xanthomata in a young person as familial hypercholesterolaemia; score it, name it, and start a high-intensity statin to target at that first visit; measure Lp(a); and — critically — cascade-screen every first-degree relative, because each child and sibling had a 50% chance of the same lethal gene. This is exactly the discipline that protected the patient — and his whole family — in Bed 3.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Cut down on fatty food' has never treated familial hypercholesterolaemia. Name the diagnosis, start the statin, and screen the family — anything less lets the gene keep killing down the generations."
