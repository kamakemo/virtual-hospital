🧬 Why THIS patient? — scar + trigger + low potassium

Sustained monomorphic VT is almost always scar-related re-entry. The infarct left islands of dead, non-conducting tissue surrounded by channels of slowly-conducting surviving myocytes. A premature beat enters one channel, finds the exit still refractory, loops back through a now-recovered path, and a self-sustaining circuit is born — a wavefront chasing its own tail at 188 times a minute. The substrate (scar) was laid down four years ago. Tonight, low potassium and low magnesium shortened refractoriness and made the surrounding myocardium twitchy enough for a single ectopic to light the fuse. Stopping amiodarone removed the only thing damping it. Three ingredients, one storm.

🔥 Risk factors for sustained monomorphic VT 🫀 Prior MI / scar (the #1 cause) · 💔 Low EF (< 35%) · ⚡ Electrolyte derangement (low K⁺, low Mg²⁺) · 🧬 Cardiomyopathy (dilated, hypertrophic, ARVC, sarcoid) · 💊 Antiarrhythmic non-adherence or pro-arrhythmia · 🍷 Ischemia/acute MI · 🧪 Digoxin toxicity

🧭 The single most important question: STABLE or UNSTABLE?

Tachyarrhythmia management forks on one decision. Look for the adverse features (the "4 S's"): Shock (SBP < 90, poor perfusion), Syncope/presyncope, Severe ischemia (chest pain, ST changes), Severe heart failure (pulmonary edema). Mr. D.R. has shock, presyncope and pulmonary edema — UNSTABLE.

⚡ The decision is made: UNSTABLE → SYNCHRONIZED DC CARDIOVERSIONAn unstable wide-complex tachycardia gets electricity, not chemistry. Drugs are too slow and too negatively inotropic for a patient sliding into shock. Sedate if time and consciousness allow, then deliver a synchronized shock. Do not chase a perfect diagnosis while the pressure falls.

The cardiology resident arrives at 23:16. Her reasoning, out loud:

🧠 Pattern recognition first, then physiology

"Wide. Regular. Fast. Old anterior MI, EF 28%. That is VT — I don't need a single Brugada criterion to bet my license on it. The base-rate alone settles it: in a patient with structural heart disease, a regular wide-complex tachycardia is VT in ~95% of cases. The dangerous instinct is to talk yourself out of VT because the patient is still conscious. People can be awake in VT for minutes — right up until they aren't."

🎯 Immediate priorities (her actual order of operations)

  1. 🚫 Kill the SVT pathway: no verapamil, no diltiazem, no beta-blocker bolus. Say it out loud and remove the syringe from the bedside.
  2. ⚡ He is unstable → synchronized cardioversion. Call for senior help and the arrest trolley in parallel, but do not wait for them to shock.
  3. 💤 Procedural sedation if he can tolerate the seconds: small dose of ketamine or midazolam + fentanyl. A conscious shock is agony — but a falling GCS means do not delay the shock for sedation.
  4. 🔌 Synchronized shock, biphasic, full energy for a deteriorating adult (e.g. 150–200 J biphasic). Confirm the machine is in SYNC and marking each R wave.
  5. 🧪 Treat the reversible trigger in parallel: IV magnesium and potassium are drawn up now; a twitchy, hypokalemic ventricle will just re-enter VT after the shock if you don't correct it.
  6. 🩻 Hunt for ischemia: 12-lead after conversion, troponin, and ask whether this VT is the symptom of an acute coronary occlusion that needs the cath lab.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Shock the unstable patient first; correct the potassium second; find the cause third — but draw up the magnesium before you charge, because the ventricle you cardiovert is the one you must keep."

🤔 "But what if it's SVT with aberrancy and I'm wrong to shock?"

"Then I have synchronized-cardioverted an SVT — which is also the correct treatment for an unstable SVT. The decision to cardiovert an unstable wide tachycardia is right whether it's VT or SVT. That's the beauty of the algorithm: instability collapses the differential. The only way to be wrong here is to treat VT as SVT with an AV-nodal blocker."

🧪 Resident's self-test (answer before reading on) Someone suggests adenosine "to see if it breaks — and if it doesn't, at least it's diagnostic." Good idea in this patient?

No. He is unstable — the answer is electricity, not a diagnostic drug. Adenosine has a role only in a stable, regular, monomorphic wide tachycardia where SVT with aberrancy is genuinely possible, as a diagnostic-therapeutic trial under continuous ECG. It will not terminate true VT, wastes precious seconds, and in an unstable patient that delay can be fatal. It is also dangerous in irregular wide tachycardia (pre-excited AF).

🎓 Consultant Round

The on-call consultant electrophysiologist, by phone at 23:20, then at the bedside:

🗂️ Name the rhythm precisely — it changes the drug

🔴 Monomorphic VT (this patient)

  • Uniform QRS shape beat-to-beat → one stable re-entry circuit, usually around scar
  • Cause: prior MI, cardiomyopathy, ARVC, sarcoid
  • Unstable → synchronized cardioversion; stable → amiodarone/procainamide
  • Long-term: ICD ± catheter ablation

🔵 Polymorphic VT

  • Continuously changing QRS morphology/axis
  • With long QT = Torsades de Pointes → IV magnesium, correct K⁺, stop QT drugs, overdrive pace
  • With normal QT → think acute ischemia → revascularize
  • Often unstable/degenerates to VF → defibrillation
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Monomorphic VT is a circuit you shock and ablate. Polymorphic VT is a sick myocardium you must fix — magnesium and the long QT, or the cath lab and the ischemia. Same letters, opposite causes."

🌀 What is "VT storm" — and is he in it?

VT storm (electrical storm) = ≥ 3 sustained episodes of VT/VF in 24 hours, each requiring intervention (cardioversion, defibrillation, or anti-tachycardia pacing). It is a sympathetically-driven emergency with its own playbook: beta-blockade is therapeutic, not contraindicated here — and non-selective blockade (propranolol) or even stellate ganglion block / deep sedation can quiet the storm. After his first shock tonight, watch closely: if he re-enters VT twice more, the management pivots from "treat the episode" to "calm the storm."

🧭 Consultant's three commandments for the next hour

  1. ⚡ "Unstable wide-complex tachycardia = a synchronized shock. The only tachycardias I defibrillate (unsynchronized) are pulseless VT and VF — and polymorphic VT too disorganized for the machine to sync. Get the mode right or you can shock him into VF."
  2. 🧪 "Replace magnesium to > 1.0 mmol/L and potassium to 4.5–5.0. An electrically irritable, hypokalemic ventricle is a re-entry machine. The shock buys you a rhythm; the electrolytes keep it."
  3. 🫀 "Then ask why now: ischemia, electrolytes, drug non-adherence, decompensated heart failure, QT-prolonging drugs. VT is a symptom. Treat the symptom tonight; treat the substrate (ICD ± ablation, GDMT) this admission."

📖 Teaching Points

🔬 The mechanism — WHY scar makes VT (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧱 The substrate: after an MI, dead myocardium is replaced by patchy fibrosis. Surviving myocyte bundles thread through the scar like alleys through rubble — they conduct, but slowly and unevenly.
  2. 🚦 Unidirectional block: a premature beat hits one channel where tissue is still refractory in one direction but not the other. The wavefront can only go one way.
  3. 🔁 Re-entry: by the time the wavefront crawls through the slow channel and comes out the far side, the tissue it first blocked at has recovered. It re-enters, loops, and a circuit is established — a wavefront perpetually chasing recovered tissue. Each lap = one wide QRS. 188 laps a minute = VT.
  4. ⚡ Why low K⁺/Mg²⁺ ignites it: hypokalemia hyperpolarizes at rest but paradoxically prolongs repolarization and increases automaticity and excitability; hypomagnesemia destabilizes membranes and worsens the effect. Shorter, more heterogeneous refractoriness = the perfect window for that triggering premature beat to find unidirectional block. Fix the electrolytes and you widen the refractory window — re-entry can no longer fit.
  5. 💥 Why it kills: 188/min gives the ventricle almost no diastole. Stroke volume collapses, coronary perfusion (which happens in diastole) falls, ischemia worsens the substrate, and the rhythm degenerates to VF — disorganized, pulseless, fatal in minutes.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"VT is a wavefront trapped in a maze of scar. Electricity resets every cell at once and collapses the maze; the electrolytes you give afterward make sure a new maze can't form."

⚖️ Contrast pair #1 — VT vs SVT with aberrancy (the lethal mimic)

⚡ VT IS… (assume this)

  • Structural heart disease / prior MI present
  • AV dissociation (cannon A waves, capture & fusion beats)
  • Very wide QRS (> 140 ms RBBB-like, > 160 ms LBBB-like)
  • Concordance (all precordial QRS same direction)
  • Extreme/"northwest" axis
  • QRS unlike any prior bundle-branch pattern

🫀 SVT-aberrancy is NOT that…

  • Younger, structurally normal heart
  • QRS matches a typical RBBB or LBBB template
  • Often a prior ECG shows the same bundle branch block
  • May terminate with vagal maneuvers / adenosine
  • No AV dissociation, no capture/fusion beats

😈 The trap: when uncertain, the brain reaches for the "less scary" diagnosis (SVT) — and reaches for AV-nodal blockers that are lethal in VT. The cost of treating VT as SVT (verapamil → VF) vastly exceeds the cost of treating SVT as VT (cardioversion, which works for both). The asymmetry of harm is why the rule is "treat as VT."

⚖️ Contrast pair #2 — Monomorphic VT vs Torsades (polymorphic, long QT)

📏 Monomorphic VT (tonight)

  • One fixed QRS shape; usually scar re-entry
  • Trigger: ischemia/scar
  • Therapy: synchronized cardioversion, amiodarone/procainamide, ICD ± ablation
  • Magnesium helps but isn't the headline drug

🌀 Torsades de Pointes

  • QRS twists around the baseline; needs a long QT
  • Trigger: ↓K⁺, ↓Mg²⁺, QT drugs, bradycardia, congenital LQTS
  • Therapy: IV magnesium, correct K⁺, stop offending drugs, overdrive pacing / isoprenaline if pause-dependent
  • Beta-blockers help congenital LQTS; avoid amiodarone (further prolongs QT)

💡 Why it matters: give amiodarone reflexively to a twisting, long-QT polymorphic VT and you can lengthen the QT and worsen the Torsades. Read the QT before you reach for the brown bottle.

🔬 Fellowship Corner — Advanced Topics

  • 🗺️ VT substrate mapping & ablation: electroanatomic mapping identifies the scar border zone and "channels"; targets include the diastolic isthmus, late potentials, and local abnormal ventricular activities (LAVA). Substrate-based homogenization vs activation/entrainment mapping of mappable VT.
  • 🧬 Idiopathic VT in normal hearts: RVOT VT (LBBB + inferior axis, adenosine-sensitive, cAMP-mediated) and fascicular VT (RBBB + left-axis, verapamil-sensitive) — the rare exceptions where verapamil is correct, but only after structural disease is excluded.
  • 🫀 Channelopathies & non-ischemic substrates: ARVC (epsilon waves, fibrofatty RV), Brugada (type 1 coved ST in V1–V2), CPVT (exercise-induced bidirectional VT), cardiac sarcoidosis (patchy scar, AV block — image with PET/MRI), HCM.
  • 💉 Refractory VT storm escalation ladder: deep sedation/intubation → IV beta-blockade (esmolol/propranolol) → amiodarone ± lidocaine → stellate ganglion block → mechanical support (IABP/Impella/VA-ECMO) → emergent ablation → in extremis, cardiac sympathetic denervation.
  • 🔌 The ICD as both therapy and trigger: repeated shocks are themselves pro-arrhythmic and traumatic; anti-tachycardia pacing (ATP) painlessly terminates much monomorphic VT — device programming (longer detection, ATP-before-shock, e.g. MADIT-RIT) reduces inappropriate and total shocks.
  • 🧪 Bidirectional VT (alternating QRS axis): think digoxin toxicity and CPVT — a high-yield exam pattern.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set, written as you'd actually write it (23:18)

  • 🚨 NO verapamil, NO diltiazem, NO IV beta-blocker bolus while hypotensive — flag on chart and whiteboard.
  • ⚡ Synchronized DC cardioversion — biphasic, 150–200 J, escalate if unsuccessful. Confirm SYNC mode (machine marks each R wave).
  • 💤 Procedural sedation if consciousness/time allows: e.g. ketamine 0.5 mg/kg IV or midazolam 1–2 mg + fentanyl 25–50 mcg. Do NOT delay the shock for sedation in a crashing patient.
  • 🧪 Magnesium sulfate 2 g IV over 10 min, then recheck; potassium replacement to target 4.5–5.0 mmol/L (IV via monitored line, max peripheral rate respected).
  • 💉 Amiodarone 300 mg IV over 20–60 min then 900 mg/24 h — to stabilize after conversion / for recurrence (or for the stable patient as first-line).
  • 🫀 12-lead ECG immediately after conversion; serial troponin; bloods: U&E, Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺, CBC, TSH, digoxin level if relevant.
  • 📞 Refer cardiology/EP; consider activating cath lab if post-conversion ECG suggests acute ischemia as the driver.
  • 🫁 High-flow O₂ to SpO₂ ≥ 94%, continuous monitoring, second large-bore IV, NPO.
  • 🔌 Keep defibrillator and pacing pads attached — anticipate recurrence (VT storm) and post-shock bradycardia.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ CCU nursing priorities — minute by minute

  • 📟 Continuous telemetry with the defibrillator at the bedside, pads attached. Keep the machine charged-ready and in the correct mode for the current rhythm.
  • 🔌 Know your modes cold: SYNC for cardioversion of a patient WITH a pulse; UNSYNC (defib) for pulseless VT/VF. Re-check SYNC after every shock — it commonly resets to OFF.
  • 🩸 BP every 2–5 min (cycle the NIBP, or read the arterial line once placed). A falling pressure means escalate now, not at the next set of obs.
  • 🧪 Run the magnesium and potassium as prescribed on a monitored line; double-check concentrations and rates — potassium errors are themselves arrhythmogenic and lethal.
  • 💤 Prepare sedation drugs and airway adjuncts before the shock; assign a team member to the airway.
  • 🧠 Neuro/consciousness checks — a patient who stops talking is losing output; a patient who slumps may have lost the pulse → check it, don't assume.
  • 📋 Document the rhythm strip before and after each intervention, the energy delivered, and the time — the post-event 12-lead and pre-conversion strip are gold for the EP team.
  • 🔇 Calm the room: fear and pain drive catecholamines that feed VT storm. A composed bedside is antiarrhythmic.
  • 👨‍👩‍👦 Keep the family updated and supported (see S16) — assign a liaison if staffing allows; this is terrifying to watch.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who re-checks SYNC after every shock and keeps potassium in the safe band is doing half the antiarrhythmic work in the room."
📢 Escalate IMMEDIATELY forLoss of pulse (→ switch to UNSYNC defib + CPR) · recurrent VT (→ VT storm pathway) · BP falling despite conversion · new chest pain / ST changes · post-shock bradycardia or pauses (have pacing ready) · falling consciousness · any prescription for an AV-nodal blocker in this patient.

📈 ECG / Imaging

🫀 The presenting rhythm — read it like a detective

Wide, regular, monomorphic tachycardia. A narrow capture beat and an intermediate fusion beat interrupt the run — both are produced by an independently firing atrium sneaking through the AV node, i.e. AV dissociation. These are near-diagnostic of VT.

🔎 The VT-vs-SVT toolkit (memorize the high-yield ones)

FeatureWhy it means VT
🔀 AV dissociationAtria and ventricles firing independently — the ventricle has its own pacemaker (the VT circuit). Look for P waves marching through at a different rate.
🎯 Capture beatsAn occasional supraventricular impulse "captures" the ventricle for one normal-width beat — proof the AV node is dissociated and the wide beats are ventricular.
🔗 Fusion beatsA supraventricular and a ventricular impulse collide → a hybrid QRS. Same logic as capture: dissociation = VT.
📏 Very broad QRS> 140 ms (RBBB-like) or > 160 ms (LBBB-like) favors VT — the impulse spreads cell-to-cell, not down the fast His-Purkinje highway.
🧭 Extreme axis"Northwest"/superior axis (negative I, II, III) strongly favors VT.
↕️ Precordial concordanceAll V1–V6 QRS pointing the same way (all up or all down) favors VT.
⏱️ Brugada / Vereckei criteriaStepwise algorithms (e.g. no RS in precordials, R-to-S > 100 ms, initial R in aVR — Vereckei) formalize the above. Useful, but never delay treating an unstable patient to run them.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Find a capture or fusion beat and the argument is over — it's VT. But you do not need them: in a scarred heart, wide-and-regular IS VT until proven otherwise."

🫀 Post-conversion 12-lead & echo

  • 📈 Post-shock 12-lead: sinus rhythm ~70/min, old anterior Q waves V1–V4 with poor R-wave progression (the scar), QTc 470 ms (borderline — relevant to drug choice), no new ST elevation → favors VT as primary, not an acute occlusion.
  • 🩻 Echo: EF 25%, large akinetic anteroapical scar with thinning, no LV thrombus seen tonight (re-image — apical aneurysms harbor clot), moderate functional MR.
  • 🧲 Cardiac MRI (later): delayed-enhancement mapping of the scar and border zone — informs ICD/ablation and excludes infiltrative causes (sarcoid).
⚠️ The ECG's favorite lie in wide-complex tachycardia"It looks like a bundle branch block, so it's SVT with aberrancy." A scar-related VT can mimic any bundle pattern. The only reassuring "aberrancy" is a QRS that matches the patient's own documented baseline bundle branch block. Absent that prior ECG, assume VT.

💉 Acute drugs — what, why, and the traps

🚫 The 'never' list in undifferentiated wide-complex tachycardia❌ IV verapamil / diltiazem · ❌ reflexive AV-nodal blockers · ❌ adenosine in irregular wide tachycardia (pre-excited AF → can precipitate VF) · ❌ amiodarone if the rhythm is Torsades (long QT). When the diagnosis is uncertain and the patient is unstable, the safest "drug" is synchronized electricity.

🏠 Substrate-modifying therapy (this admission, not just tonight)

  • 💙 Optimize HFrEF GDMT: the four pillars (ARNI/ACEi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i) reduce arrhythmic death by remodeling the substrate.
  • 🔌 ICD for secondary prevention (he just had sustained VT) — now a Class I indication, far stronger than the primary-prevention offer he declined.
  • 🗺️ Catheter ablation of the VT circuit, especially if recurrent or storm.
  • 💊 Restart/continue amiodarone (or sotalol) as adjunct to reduce ICD shocks while definitive therapy is arranged.
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📈 Continuous telemetry with the defibrillator at the bedside until ≥ 24–48 h arrhythmia-free; print strips of any ectopy or runs.
  • 🩸 Invasive arterial line for beat-to-beat BP through repeated cardioversions and antiarrhythmic infusions.
  • 🧪 Serial K⁺ and Mg²⁺ (e.g. q2–4h initially) — the most modifiable arrhythmia drivers; keep K⁺ 4.5–5.0, Mg²⁺ > 1.0.
  • 📐 QTc on every 12-lead — amiodarone, sotalol, low K⁺/Mg²⁺ all prolong it; a lengthening QT changes the rhythm risk from monomorphic VT to Torsades.
  • 🫀 Serial troponin — rising = ongoing ischemia (consider angiography); plateauing/falling = demand ischemia from the tachycardia.
  • 💤 Sedation/airway readiness kept at bedside in case of recurrence or storm.
  • 🔋 If/when an ICD is placed: monitor for appropriate vs inappropriate detections and shocks; ATP-before-shock programming.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you convert VT, the monitor's job changes from 'is he in VT?' to 'is he about to be again?' — and the two numbers that answer it are potassium and QTc."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — and the mechanism

  • ⚡ Degeneration to VF / cardiac arrest — the great fear; rate and ischemia disorganize the circuit. Treatment pivots to UNSYNC defibrillation + CPR.
  • 🌀 VT storm (≥ 3 episodes/24 h) — sympathetically driven; needs beta-blockade, sedation, ablation, sometimes mechanical support.
  • 🧠 Cardiogenic shock / multi-organ hypoperfusion — prolonged low output from the tachycardia and a failing ventricle.
  • 🩸 Post-cardioversion complications — skin burns, transient bradyarrhythmia/asystole (have pacing ready), thromboembolism (LV apical thrombus in an aneurysmal scar).
  • 💊 Pro-arrhythmia from therapy — antiarrhythmics prolong QT (Torsades); the very ICD shocks meant to save him are pro-arrhythmic and traumatic.
  • 🫀 Recurrence — if the trigger (low K⁺/Mg²⁺, ischemia) and substrate (scar, low EF) aren't addressed, VT returns.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference — "The Patient Who Looked Too Well"

The case: A 70-year-old man, anterior MI two years prior, arrived to a busy ED at 02:00 with palpitations and a regular wide-complex tachycardia at 170, BP 96/60, fully alert and chatting. The tired night team called it "SVT with aberrancy — he's far too stable for VT." They gave IV verapamil 5 mg. Within ninety seconds his pressure collapsed, the VT accelerated and degenerated into VF. A 38-minute resuscitation followed. He did not survive. The pre-treatment 12-lead, projected at the M&M, showed unmistakable AV dissociation and a capture beat.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"He looks too well to be VT" — being conscious does not rule out VT.
📈 ECG NOT READAV dissociation and a capture beat were on the strip and unread.
📞 BASE RATE IGNOREDPrior MI + wide + regular = ~95% VT, regardless of how well he looks.
💊 LETHAL DRUGVerapamil into VT → vasodilatation, negative inotropy → VF.
🌙 SYSTEMNight fatigue, no senior review, no "treat-as-VT" default.

🛡️ What would have saved him: apply the universal rule — wide + regular + structural heart disease = VT; never give AV-nodal blockers to undifferentiated wide-complex tachycardia; if unstable, synchronized cardioversion (correct for VT and SVT); read the strip for AV dissociation, capture and fusion beats; get senior eyes early. This is exactly why your nurse held the verapamil tonight.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Too stable to be VT' is the sentence written on the chart of patients who then die of VT. Stability is not a diagnosis."

🔀 Differentials

🧩 The wide-complex tachycardia differential

DiagnosisCluesWhy it matters
⚡ Ventricular tachycardia (this patient)Structural heart disease, AV dissociation, capture/fusion beats, very broad QRS, concordanceDefault assumption; unstable → cardiovert. ~80% of all wide tachy, ~95% if prior MI.
🔁 SVT with aberrancy / pre-existing BBBYounger, normal heart, QRS matches a known bundle pattern, may break with vagal/adenosineOnly "safe" if you can prove it; never assume it to justify AV-nodal blockers.
🌀 Pre-excited tachycardia (WPW + AF)Irregular, very fast, varying QRS width, delta-wave historyAV-nodal blockers (adenosine, verapamil, digoxin) can precipitate VF → use procainamide or cardiovert.
🌪️ Polymorphic VT / TorsadesChanging QRS morphology; long QT (Torsades) or ischemia (normal QT)Different therapy — magnesium & fix QT, or revascularize. Don't give amiodarone to Torsades.
🧪 Toxic / metabolic wide tachyHyperkalemia (sine wave), TCA/sodium-channel toxicity (wide QRS, R in aVR), digoxin (bidirectional VT)Specific antidotes — calcium & insulin/dextrose, sodium bicarbonate, digoxin-specific antibody.
📟 Paced rhythm / artefactPacing spikes; tremor or lead artefact mimicking VTAlways confirm a true tachycardia and a real pulse before treating.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"REGULAR wide tachycardia → think VT. IRREGULAR wide tachycardia → think pre-excited AF (and keep your hands off the AV node)."

💎 Clinical Pearls

⚡ Treat as VTWide + regular + structural heart disease = VT until proven otherwise. The asymmetry of harm makes this the only safe default.
🔌 Unstable = electricityAn unstable wide-complex tachycardia gets a SYNCHRONIZED shock — correct for VT and SVT. Instability collapses the differential.
🚫 No CCBsVerapamil/diltiazem in VT → vasodilatation + negative inotropy → VF. Never give AV-nodal blockers to undifferentiated wide tachycardia.
🎯 Capture & fusion = VTA capture or fusion beat proves AV dissociation. Find one and the argument is over.
🧪 Mg before KYou cannot correct potassium without magnesium — low Mg²⁺ makes the kidney waste the K⁺ you give.
🌀 Mono vs polyMonomorphic = circuit you shock/ablate. Polymorphic + long QT = Torsades → magnesium, fix QT, NOT amiodarone.
↕️ Regular vs irregularRegular wide → VT. Irregular wide → pre-excited AF; keep your hands off the AV node.
🔄 SYNC resetsDefibrillators often default OUT of SYNC after a shock — re-check before every cardioversion.
🩺 "Too well" is a trapPatients can be conscious in VT for minutes. Stability is not a diagnosis.
🔋 ICD after sustained VTSecondary prevention is Class I — far stronger than the primary-prevention offer commonly declined.
🧬 Verapamil-sensitive VTThe rare exception: fascicular VT in a structurally normal heart. Prove a normal heart first.
🩻 VT is a symptomAlways ask "why now?" — ischemia, electrolytes, drugs, decompensation. Treat the rhythm and the cause.
