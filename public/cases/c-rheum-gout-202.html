🧭 The bedside question set

For every acute mono-arthritis: (1) Is it septic? (fever, systemically unwell, immunosuppressed, cannot move the joint at all → aspirate/cover). (2) Is it crystal? (rapid overnight onset, first-MTP/knee, prior identical attacks, triggers). (3) Is it something else (trauma, reactive, first presentation of inflammatory arthritis)? Aspiration answers all three at once.

⚠️ The two traps of "obvious gout"Trap 1 — the septic mimic: anchoring on gout and missing a septic joint (see the septic-arthritis case). Trap 2 — the wrong drug: reflexively giving a full-dose NSAID to a CKD/heart-failure/GI-bleed-risk patient and causing iatrogenic harm. Both are avoidable with a moment's thought.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The rheumatology SHO thinks out loud at 07:45:

🧠 Separate the two problems: the FIRE now, the FUEL later

"This is almost certainly acute gout — classic podagra, classic triggers, prior attacks. Two jobs. First, the fire: settle the flare fast with an anti-inflammatory that is SAFE for his kidneys — so not high-dose NSAID; I will consider low-dose colchicine (dose-adjusted for CKD) or a short course of oral steroid. Second, the fuel: he is a textbook candidate for urate-lowering therapy — recurrent attacks, CKD, on a thiazide — but I will start that in a planned way with flare prophylaxis, not slam it in mid-attack."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🔬 Aspirate the joint if feasible to confirm negatively birefringent MSU crystals and exclude sepsis (send Gram stain/culture/cell count/crystals).
  2. 🧊 Treat the flare with a renally-appropriate agent: colchicine (low-dose, CKD-adjusted, watch interactions) OR corticosteroid; rest, ice, elevate.
  3. 🩸 Bloods: U&E (baseline renal function), FBC/CRP, glucose/HbA1c, lipids; serum urate now AND to recheck ~2–4 weeks later (it is often normal/low mid-flare).
  4. 💊 Review the thiazide — consider switching to a urate-neutral/lowering antihypertensive (e.g. losartan, a calcium-channel blocker).
  5. 📅 Plan urate-lowering therapy (allopurinol/febuxostat) to start with colchicine prophylaxis, titrated to target — not started acutely for the first time in the middle of this flare.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Treat the fire and plan the fuel: fast anti-inflammatory for the flare, then a titrated urate-lowering strategy to a target — the flare drug and the prevention drug are NOT the same job."
🧪 Resident's self-testHe is already on allopurinol from a private script he started last week. Do you stop it now because he is flaring?

No — do not stop established urate-lowering therapy during a flare. Stopping (or changing the dose of) allopurinol mid-attack causes urate flux that can prolong or worsen the flare. Continue it and treat the flare on top. The traditional teaching is only that you do not newly initiate ULT during the acute attack (and even that is softening) — but an already-established agent is continued through the flare with prophylactic cover.

🎓 Consultant Round + Evidence-Based Medicine

The rheumatology consultant at the bedside, 08:10:

🎯 The urate target — "treat-to-target," like blood pressure

ScenarioSerum urate targetRationale
Standard gout< 360 µmol/L (< 6 mg/dL)Below the saturation point for MSU — existing crystals dissolve, no new ones form.
Tophaceous / severe / frequent flares< 300 µmol/L (< 5 mg/dL)Lower target speeds tophus dissolution.
Do NOT over-lowerAvoid pushing very low long-termUrate has physiological roles; extreme lowering is unnecessary — aim to the target, then maintain.

Urate-lowering is a treat-to-target, lifelong strategy: start low, titrate up, check the urate, and cover with prophylaxis (low-dose colchicine, or NSAID/steroid) for the first ~3–6 months because falling urate itself provokes flares.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Urate-lowering to a target below 360 (or 300 with tophi) is the CURE; start low, go slow, and give flare prophylaxis — because dropping the urate paradoxically triggers attacks at first."

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY urate crystals cause fire (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧪 Hyperuricaemia: urate is the end-product of purine breakdown (via xanthine oxidase). Humans lack uricase, so we run "hot." Over-production (purine load, cell turnover) or — far commoner — under-excretion (CKD, diuretics, alcohol) pushes serum urate above its solubility limit (~360 µmol/L).
  2. 💎 Crystallisation: supersaturated urate precipitates as monosodium urate (MSU) crystals in cooler, peripheral, low-flow tissues — hence the big toe. Cold, acidosis and trauma favour crystal formation, explaining the classic nocturnal onset.
  3. 🔥 Inflammasome ignition: MSU crystals are engulfed by resident macrophages and activate the NLRP3 inflammasome → caspase-1 → cleavage of pro-IL-1β into active IL-1β. IL-1β is the master switch: it recruits a flood of neutrophils, driving the red, hot, agonising flare.
  4. 🔁 Self-limiting but recurrent: the attack settles as crystals are coated and cleared, but the underlying urate load remains — so flares recur and, untreated, crystals accumulate into tophi and cause chronic joint damage. Only lowering urate below saturation dissolves them.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Urate crystals ignite the NLRP3 inflammasome to release IL-1β — that single pathway explains the flare, the colchicine/steroid/IL-1-blocker treatments, and why only lowering the urate is curative."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Gout vs Pseudogout

💎 Gout (MSU)

  • Middle-aged men, first-MTP (podagra), rapid overnight onset
  • Negatively birefringent, needle-shaped crystals
  • Triggers: purines, alcohol, diuretics, CKD; treat + urate-lower

🔷 Pseudogout (CPPD)

  • Elderly, knees/wrists; associated with OA, haemochromatosis, hyperparathyroidism
  • Positively birefringent, rhomboid crystals; X-ray chondrocalcinosis
  • No urate-lowering role; treat the flare (NSAID/colchicine/steroid)

💡 Memory hook: goutis Needle-Negative; pseudogout is Positive-rhomboid. And CPPD in a young patient should prompt a hunt for haemochromatosis or hyperparathyroidism.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (07:50)

  • 🔬 Joint aspiration (if feasible) → synovial fluid for polarised-light crystals AND, if any sepsis doubt, Gram stain/culture/cell count.
  • 🧊 Acute flare treatment (renally-appropriate): low-dose colchicine (CKD-adjusted, e.g. 500 µg once–twice daily; watch drug interactions) OR oral prednisolone short course; rest/ice/elevate. Avoid high-dose NSAID given CKD 3b.
  • 🩸 Bloods: U&E (baseline + monitor), FBC, CRP, glucose/HbA1c, lipids, serum urate now (interpret with caution) and a repeat urate in 2–4 weeks.
  • 💊 Medication review: switch the thiazide to a urate-neutral agent (e.g. losartan/CCB); review any low-dose aspirin and other urate-raising drugs.
  • 📅 Plan (not start-today) urate-lowering therapy: allopurinol low-dose start with colchicine prophylaxis, titrate to urate < 360; arrange follow-up.
  • 🍎 Lifestyle: reduce alcohol/purines/fructose, weight loss, hydration, encourage low-fat dairy & coffee (urate-lowering).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In the CKD gout patient, colchicine (dose-reduced) or steroid — NOT a full-dose NSAID. The commonest iatrogenic harm in gout is the kidney the NSAID injured."

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Assessment-bay priorities

  • 😖 Analgesia and joint rest — elevate and support the foot; a bed cradle keeps the sheet off the toe; ice packs ease pain.
  • 💊 Give the right anti-inflammatory and watch for its side-effects — colchicine: warn about diarrhoea/nausea (dose-limiting); steroids: monitor glucose in this diabetic.
  • 🫘 Guard the kidneys — ensure NO full-dose NSAID is administered in CKD; encourage oral fluids; note urine output.
  • 🌡️ Re-check observations — a rising fever, rigors or spreading redness should prompt escalation to exclude sepsis.
  • 📋 Education — explain the difference between the flare drug (now) and the urate-lowering drug (later), and why not to stop any established allopurinol.
  • 🍎 Lifestyle reinforcement — brief, non-judgemental advice on alcohol, diet, weight and hydration.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who keeps the sheet off the toe, the NSAID away from the kidney, and the patient clear on 'flare drug now, prevention drug later' delivers most of the good outcome."

🩻 Imaging — supportive

  • X-ray: early gout is normal; chronic gout shows punched-out juxta-articular erosions with sclerotic overhanging margins and preserved joint space; CPPD shows chondrocalcinosis.
  • Ultrasound: the double-contour sign (urate on cartilage surface) and tophi; helps when aspiration is not possible.
  • Dual-energy CT (DECT): colour-codes urate deposits — useful in atypical/tophaceous cases.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🫘 Renal function & potassium — especially if any NSAID is used, and when dosing colchicine; recheck U&E after changes.
  • 🧪 Serum urate to target — recheck ~2–4 weeks after the flare and during ULT titration; aim < 360 (< 300 with tophi), then maintain and monitor periodically.
  • 🔥 Flare frequency during ULT start — expect early mobilisation flares; that is why prophylaxis runs for ~3–6 months. Do not abandon ULT because of a flare.
  • 💊 Colchicine toxicity — GI symptoms first; myelosuppression/neuromyopathy with interacting drugs or renal impairment.
  • 🩸 Allopurinol hypersensitivity — rash, eosinophilia, fever early after starting (DRESS/SJS) — stop immediately and escalate.
  • ❤️ Cardiometabolic risk — BP, glucose, lipids, weight; gout is a cardiovascular-renal risk marker.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Once the flare is out, the monitoring question becomes 'is the urate at target?' — gout is only cured when the number stays below saturation, checked like a blood pressure."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong

  • 💎 Chronic tophaceous gout — untreated hyperuricaemia deposits tophi (ears, elbows, fingers, tendons) causing deformity, joint destruction and even nerve compression.
  • 🦴 Erosive gouty arthropathy — punched-out erosions and chronic joint damage from recurrent flares.
  • 🫘 Urate nephropathy & uric-acid stones — renal deposition and calculi; gout and CKD amplify each other.
  • 💥 Iatrogenic — NSAID injury: acute kidney injury, hyperkalaemia, GI bleeding, fluid retention/heart failure — the most preventable complication here.
  • 🧪 Colchicine toxicity / allopurinol hypersensitivity — from mis-dosing or missed screening.
  • 🦠 Missed septic joint — anchoring on "gout" and overlooking coexisting infection.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Gout has two enemies: the crystals you fail to lower, and the drugs you give carelessly. Both are preventable — treat to target, and dose for the kidney."

🧭 The management plan

  1. 💎 Diagnosis: acute gout (negatively birefringent intracellular MSU crystals; sterile fluid) in a patient with metabolic syndrome and CKD 3b.
  2. 🧊 Flare (now): renally-appropriate anti-inflammatory — low-dose CKD-adjusted colchicine or short oral steroid; rest/ice/elevate. Avoid high-dose NSAID.

  5. 🏥 Rheumatology follow-up; recheck urate and renal function; patient education on flare-vs-prevention drugs.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the flare

  • 🧬 Urate handling & genetics: the renal urate transporters (URAT1/SLC22A12, GLUT9/SLC2A9, ABCG2) explain why most gout is under-excretion; ABCG2 variants drive gut excretion and early-onset disease. This underpins uricosurics.
  • 💊 Uricosurics & newer agents: probenecid, lesinurad (with a XO inhibitor), benzbromarone; pegloticase (recombinant uricase) for severe refractory tophaceous gout — dramatic urate-lowering but immunogenic (anti-drug antibodies, infusion reactions).
  • 🔬 The NLRP3 inflammasome: MSU → caspase-1 → IL-1β/IL-18; the target of colchicine (upstream, microtubule/NLRP3) and canakinumab/anakinra (IL-1). Emerging direct NLRP3 inhibitors are in trials.
  • ❤️ Asymptomatic hyperuricaemia & CV/renal outcomes: whether to treat asymptomatic hyperuricaemia remains debated (CKD-FIX, PERL suggested no renal benefit) — currently not routinely urate-lowered without gout/stones.
  • 🔷 CPPD spectrum: asymptomatic chondrocalcinosis, acute CPP crystal arthritis (pseudogout), chronic CPP inflammatory arthritis and "pseudo-RA"; secondary causes — haemochromatosis, hyperparathyroidism, hypomagnesaemia, hypophosphatasia.
  • 🧪 Diagnostics: ultrasound double-contour sign, dual-energy CT urate mapping, and the 2015 ACR/EULAR gout classification criteria for when aspiration is not possible.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Gout Drug That Broke the Kidney"

The case: A 63-year-old man with CKD stage 3 (eGFR 38), hypertension and known gout attended a crowded department with an acute flare. Busy and pattern-matching, the team discharged him with one week of high-dose diclofenac and no renal check. He took it with an ACE inhibitor and a diuretic (the "triple whammy"), dehydrated at home, and re-presented on day 5 with stage-3 acute kidney injury, potassium 6.4 and fluid overload, needing urgent dialysis assessment. In a separate near-miss the same week, a "gout knee" that was in fact septic was nearly injected with steroid — caught only because someone insisted on aspirating first.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 PATTERN-MATCH"Gout = NSAID" applied reflexively, ignoring the kidney.
🫘 CKD IGNOREDeGFR 38 not checked/acted on before prescribing.
💊 TRIPLE WHAMMYNSAID + ACEi + diuretic — a classic recipe for acute kidney injury.
🔬 NO ASPIRATEDiagnosis assumed, sepsis not formally excluded in the hot joint.
🏥 SYSTEMNo safety-net, no follow-up, no urate-lowering plan.

🛡️ What would have saved him: check renal function before choosing a flare drug; in CKD/heart failure use colchicine (dose-reduced) or a short steroid course, NOT a heavy NSAID; beware the NSAID + ACEi + diuretic triple whammy; aspirate the hot joint to confirm crystals AND exclude sepsis; and set up treat-to-target urate-lowering with follow-up. This is exactly the discipline that protected the patient in Bed 6.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In gout, the crystal rarely kills — the careless NSAID and the missed septic joint do. Diagnose the fluid, dose for the kidney, and lower the urate to target."
