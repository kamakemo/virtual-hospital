🧪 Resident's self-testThe GP already sent a stool antigen while the patient was on omeprazole, and it came back negative. Can we trust it?

No. PPIs suppress H. pylori and reduce urease activity, causing false-negative stool antigen and urea breath tests. A negative test on a PPI does not exclude infection. Stop the PPI for ≥2 weeks (and antibiotics/bismuth for ≥4 weeks) before non-invasive testing — or test on biopsies taken at endoscopy.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the mucosa ulcerates (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. ⚖️ Defence vs aggression: the gastroduodenal mucosa survives an acid bath through a defensive triad — a mucus-bicarbonate layer, tight epithelial junctions, and prostaglandin-driven mucosal blood flow. An ulcer forms when aggression (acid/pepsin) overwhelms defence.
  2. 🦠 H. pylori: a urease-producing spiral bacterium that colonises the antrum. Antral-predominant gastritis → increased gastrin → increased acid → duodenal ulcers. Body-predominant/atrophic gastritis → reduced acid, intestinal metaplasia → gastric ulcers and gastric cancer risk (a WHO class I carcinogen).
  3. 💊 NSAIDs: inhibit COX-1, cutting protective prostaglandins → loss of mucus/bicarbonate and mucosal blood flow → the mucosa is stripped of its shield → ulceration (classically gastric). This is a direct, dose-related, prostaglandin-loss mechanism.
  4. 🩸 Why they bleed and perforate: a deepening ulcer erodes into a vessel (posterior duodenal ulcer → gastroduodenal artery → brisk haemorrhage) or through the wall (anterior duodenal ulcer → free perforation, peritonitis, free air).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Two hands strangle the mucosa: H. pylori and NSAIDs. Find them, remove them — eradicate the bug, stop the drug — and most ulcers never come back."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Duodenal vs Gastric ulcer

🟢 Duodenal ulcer

  • Younger, pain relieved by food, night/hunger pain
  • Mostly H. pylori; high acid
  • Almost never malignant → no routine re-scope

🔴 Gastric ulcer

  • Older, pain often worse with food, weight loss
  • H. pylori + NSAIDs; can be malignant
  • Biopsy + re-scope at 6–8 weeks to confirm healing / exclude cancer

💡 The single most important behavioural rule of PUD: a gastric ulcer must be biopsied and re-scoped; a duodenal ulcer generally need not be. Never assume a gastric ulcer is benign on appearance alone.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Clinic & day-case priorities — the nurse guards against the bleed

  • 🩸 Watch for GI bleeding — ask about and observe for melaena, haematemesis, dizziness, syncope; check HR/BP for occult blood loss; a rising pulse is an early warning.
  • 💊 Medication reconciliation — confirm the ibuprofen is stopped, flag any aspirin/anticoagulant, and educate that OTC NSAIDs are the hidden culprit.
  • 🦠 Test-timing counselling — teach the patient to stop the PPI 2 weeks before a breath/stool test and antibiotics 4 weeks before, and why.
  • 💉 Fasting and consent for OGD; ensure IV access and check clotting.
  • 🔁 Safety-net the re-scope — make sure the gastric-ulcer follow-up endoscopy is actually booked and the patient knows to attend; missed re-scopes are how cancers slip through.
  • 🍎 Lifestyle advice: smoking cessation, alcohol moderation — both impair ulcer healing.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who ensures the gastric-ulcer re-scope is booked before the patient walks out has closed the exact gap that lets a gastric cancer be missed."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🩸 Bleeding signs — melaena, haematemesis, resting tachycardia, postural drop, falling Hb. An ulcer that bleeds is an emergency (S13, GI bleed pathway).
  • 😖 Symptom response — persistent or worsening pain despite PPI + eradication should prompt re-scope and rethink (refractory ulcer, wrong diagnosis, Zollinger–Ellison).
  • 🔁 Re-scope the gastric ulcer at 6–8 weeks — confirm complete healing; re-biopsy if not healed; do not sign off an unhealed gastric ulcer.
  • 🦠 Eradication confirmation — UBT/stool antigen ≥4 weeks post-treatment, off PPI ≥2 weeks.
  • 💊 NSAID vigilance — confirm cessation; if reintroduced, ensure gastroprotection.
  • 🤮 Outlet obstruction — recurrent vomiting, early satiety, succussion splash → suspect gastric outlet obstruction (scarring or malignancy).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"An ulcer is not 'done' when the pain settles — it is done when a gastric ulcer is re-scoped to healing and H. pylori eradication is confirmed off the PPI."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 The four classic ulcer complications

  • 🩸 Bleeding (commonest) — haematemesis/melaena. Resuscitate (the GI bleed pathway: ABC, IV access ×2, fluids/blood, transfuse to a restrictive Hb target ~70 g/L), risk-score (Glasgow-Blatchford/Rockall), IV PPI, urgent endoscopy with therapy (adrenaline injection + clip/thermal), and eradicate H. pylori. Posterior duodenal ulcers erode the gastroduodenal artery.
  • 🕳️ Perforation — sudden severe abdominal pain, rigid "board-like" abdomen, the acute abdomen with free air under the diaphragm on erect CXR. Resuscitate, IV antibiotics, NG tube, urgent surgery (omental/Graham patch).
  • 🚧 Gastric outlet obstruction — vomiting, early satiety, succussion splash, hypochloraemic hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis; from scarring or malignancy.
  • 🎗️ Malignant ulcer — a "benign-looking" gastric ulcer that was cancer all along — the reason to biopsy and re-scope.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Ulcers bleed, perforate, obstruct, or turn out to be cancer — every one of those is prevented or caught by removing the cause and re-scoping the gastric ulcer."

📋 Two very different destinations

  • Re-scope to healing; permanent NSAID caution / gastroprotection if unavoidable

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • ⚡ Zollinger–Ellison syndrome: a gastrin-secreting tumour (gastrinoma, often duodenal/pancreatic) causing multiple, refractory or distal (post-bulbar) ulcers, diarrhoea and reflux. Diagnose with a high fasting gastrin off PPI (PPIs raise gastrin — a pitfall) ± secretin stimulation test; localise with somatostatin-receptor imaging. Consider MEN1 (parathyroid + pituitary + pancreatic).
  • 🦠 H. pylori resistance & tailored therapy: rising clarithromycin resistance drives a shift to bismuth quadruple therapy or susceptibility-guided regimens; molecular resistance testing; the role of high-dose dual therapy (PPI + amoxicillin) and potassium-competitive acid blockers (vonoprazan).
  • 🎗️ Gastric cancer & premalignant cascade: Correa cascade (chronic gastritis → atrophy → intestinal metaplasia → dysplasia → cancer); MALT lymphoma that can regress with H. pylori eradication; endoscopic surveillance of extensive intestinal metaplasia.
  • 🩸 Bleeding-ulcer nuances: restrictive transfusion (Villanueva NEJM 2013 — target ~70 g/L outperformed liberal), pre-endoscopy erythromycin as a prokinetic, second-look endoscopy, and interventional-radiology embolisation of the gastroduodenal artery when endoscopy fails.
  • 💊 Antithrombotic dilemmas: managing DAPT/anticoagulation around an ulcer bleed; when to restart, and gastroprotection strategies (co-prescribed PPI).
  • 💭 Functional dyspepsia mechanisms: gut–brain axis, visceral hypersensitivity, impaired accommodation, and the role of neuromodulators (e.g. tricyclics) and prokinetics.

🎭 M&M — "The Ulcer Nobody Re-Scoped and the Test Nobody Trusted"

The case: A 59-year-old man had epigastric pain and a stool H. pylori antigen taken while he was already on a PPI — reported negative. Reassured it "was not an infection or an ulcer," he was continued on the PPI. An OGD eight weeks later did show a gastric ulcer; it "looked benign," was not biopsied thoroughly, and — crucially — no re-scope was booked. His symptoms improved on the PPI so he did not return. Ten months on he presented with weight loss and vomiting: the gastric ulcer had been an ulcerated gastric adenocarcinoma all along, now with outlet obstruction and nodal spread. His H. pylori, which the PPI-era test had "cleared" him of, was present and untreated.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

💊 FALSE-NEGATIVE ON PPIH. pylori tested on a PPI, negative believed — the bug was there.
🔬 UNDER-BIOPSIED"Looked benign" — gastric ulcer not adequately biopsied.
🔁 NO RE-SCOPEGastric ulcer never re-scoped to confirm healing / exclude cancer.
😌 PPI MASKINGSymptom relief on the PPI falsely reassured everyone.
📞 NO SAFETY-NETFollow-up depended on the patient returning, not on a booked recall.

🛡️ What would have saved him: never trust a negative H. pylori test taken on a PPI — stop the PPI and re-test, or biopsy at endoscopy; biopsy every gastric ulcer thoroughly; always book the gastric-ulcer re-scope at 6–8 weeks before the patient leaves; confirm H. pylori eradication off PPI; and treat symptom relief on a PPI as masking, not cure. This is exactly the discipline that protected the patient in Bed 2.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A gastric ulcer that is not re-scoped, and a negative H. pylori test taken on a PPI, are two of the quietest ways to miss a gastric cancer — close both gaps every single time."
