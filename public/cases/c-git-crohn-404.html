The cost of the "it is just stress/IBS" label is months of untreated transmural inflammation — and transmural inflammation is what forms strictures and fistulae you can never fully reverse.

🧭 The three things you exclude before calling it Crohn's flare

Before you commit, exclude the mimics that need different treatment: infection (stool culture, C. difficile toxin, ova/parasites — never immunosuppress an infection), a septic collection/abscess (a fluctuant, febrile perianal or intra-abdominal mass needs drainage, not more steroids), and obstruction from a stricture (vomiting, distension, no flatus). All three are covered in S13/S14.

⚠️ The trap of "just treat the flare"A young patient with fever, an RIF mass and rising CRP might have an abscess, not just active inflammation. Piling on steroids/anti-TNF into an undrained collection is dangerous. Image first (MR enterography / CT if unwell), drain pus, cover with antibiotics — THEN escalate immunosuppression.

🎯 Her order of operations

  2. 🔬 Ileocolonoscopy with terminal ileal intubation + segmental biopsies — the diagnostic cornerstone.
  3. 🧲 MR enterography for small-bowel extent/strictures/fistulae; pelvic MRI for perianal disease.
  4. 🛡️ Pre-immunosuppression screen NOW: latent TB (IGRA/chest X-ray), hepatitis B & C, HIV, VZV status, TPMT before thiopurines, vaccination review.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Screen for latent TB and viral hepatitis BEFORE you start steroids-sparing immunosuppression or anti-TNF — the drug that heals the gut can wake up a dormant infection and kill the patient."
🧪 Resident's self-testShe wants to start high-dose steroids AND infliximab today to "get on top of it fast," before the TB screen or MRI. Right move?

No. Two problems. First, you have not excluded an undrained abscess (fever + RIF mass) — anti-TNF into sepsis is dangerous. Second, starting anti-TNF before screening for latent TB and hepatitis B risks reactivation (miliary TB, fulminant hepatitis). Induce with corticosteroids (or exclusive enteral nutrition), image, drain any pus, complete the infection screen — then plan the biologic.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY transmural, WHY skip lesions, WHY the complications (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧬 Dysregulated immunity: in a genetically susceptible gut (e.g. NOD2/CARD15 variants), the mucosal immune system mounts an exaggerated Th1/Th17 response to luminal bacteria. TNF-α, IL-12 and IL-23 drive chronic granulomatous inflammation.
  2. 🧱 Transmural inflammation: unlike UC (mucosa only), Crohn's inflammation penetrates the full thickness of the bowel wall. This single fact explains almost every complication: full-thickness scarring → strictures; full-thickness ulcers tunnelling out → fistulae and abscesses.
  3. 🦘 Skip lesions, anywhere mouth-to-anus: discontinuous patches of disease with normal bowel between, classically the terminal ileum, giving cobblestone mucosa, deep serpiginous ulcers and non-caseating granulomas on biopsy.
  4. 🩸 Malabsorption: diseased/resected terminal ileum cannot absorb vitamin B12 and bile salts → B12 deficiency, bile-salt diarrhoea and gallstones; general inflammation and reduced intake cause iron/folate deficiency, low albumin and weight loss.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"One word explains Crohn's complications: TRANSMURAL. Mucosa-only disease bleeds; full-thickness disease strictures, fistulates and abscesses."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Ulcerative Colitis vs Crohn's Disease

🟥 Ulcerative Colitis

  • Continuous inflammation from the rectum proximally
  • Mucosa/submucosa only
  • Bloody diarrhoea, tenesmus, urgency
  • No perianal fistulae, no granulomas
  • Smoking is protective; PSC association; colectomy is curative

🟦 Crohn's Disease

  • Patchy skip lesions, mouth to anus (ileum classic)
  • Transmural — cobblestoning, granulomas
  • Often non-bloody diarrhoea, pain, weight loss
  • Perianal disease + fistulae; B12/bile-salt malabsorption
  • Smoking worsens it; surgery is NOT curative

💡 Remember the giveaways: perianal fistulae, mouth ulcers, granulomas and terminal-ileal disease = Crohn's; continuous bloody rectal-onset colitis = UC.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (11:10)

  • 🧪 Bloods: FBC, CRP, ESR, U&E, LFTs, albumin, ferritin/iron studies, B12, folate, vitamin D, bone profile, coagulation, group & save.
  • 💩 Stool: culture & sensitivity, ova/cysts/parasites, C. difficile toxin, faecal calprotectin — exclude infection before immunosuppressing.
  • 🔬 Ileocolonoscopy + segmental & terminal-ileal biopsies — confirm diagnosis, define extent, look for granulomas.
  • 🧲 MR enterography (small-bowel extent, strictures, fistulae) + pelvic MRI for perianal disease.
  • 🛡️ Pre-biologic screen: IGRA/QuantiFERON + chest X-ray for latent TB, HBsAg/anti-HBc/anti-HCV, HIV, VZV serology, TPMT level, up-to-date vaccinations (avoid live vaccines once immunosuppressed).
  • 💊 Induction: corticosteroid (e.g. prednisolone taper, or budesonide for ileocaecal disease) OR exclusive enteral nutrition; correct iron/B12/folate; VTE prophylaxis if admitted (IBD is prothrombotic).
  • 🚭 Smoking cessation referral and dietitian referral — disease-modifying, not optional.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Before the first dose of any immunosuppressant: a negative stool infection screen, a TB and hepatitis screen, and a drained abscess. Skip those and the treatment becomes the disease."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Ward priorities — the nurse runs the safety net

  • 📈 Stool chart (frequency, consistency, blood) + fluid balance — the objective record of flare severity; a rising frequency with abdominal distension is an early warning of obstruction or toxicity.
  • 🌡️ Observations for sepsis — fever + tachycardia + a tender abdomen may mean an abscess or perforation, not "just the flare." Escalate early.
  • ⚖️ Daily weight + nutrition support — malnutrition is part of the disease; involve the dietitian, protect enteral nutrition, monitor for refeeding if severely malnourished.
  • 😖 Assess abdominal pain and distension each round; new vomiting or absent flatus = query obstruction, keep nil by mouth and call the doctor.
  • 🍑 Gentle perianal care and dignity for a young patient with an embarrassing, painful problem; watch the fistula site for increasing pain/swelling (abscess).
  • 🚭 Reinforce smoking cessation and medication counselling; support adherence — the young non-adherent patient relapses.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who charts every stool and flags the first fever is the earliest detector of the abscess and the obstruction hiding inside a Crohn's flare."

🩻 Adjunct imaging

  • CT abdomen/pelvis: quickest way to find an abscess or free air in an acutely unwell, febrile patient.
  • Abdominal X-ray: in suspected obstruction/toxicity — dilated loops, look for the complication, not the diagnosis.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why — treat-to-target

  • 📊 Symptom + biomarker trend: stool frequency, CRP and faecal calprotectin to judge whether therapy is working — the modern target is objective remission, not just "feeling better."
  • 🔬 Endoscopic re-assessment for mucosal healing — the target that predicts durable remission and fewer surgeries (treat-to-target, CALM trial logic).
  • 🧪 Drug monitoring: thiopurine → FBC/LFTs regularly; anti-TNF → drug levels & anti-drug antibodies if losing response; methotrexate → FBC/LFTs.
  • 🦴 Nutrition & bone: recheck iron/B12/folate/vitamin D; DEXA if repeated steroids; growth in adolescents.
  • ⚠️ Red flags for the complication: new obstructive symptoms (a maturing stricture), spiking fever/localised pain (abscess), or new perianal discharge (fistula) — image, do not just up the dose.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Feeling a bit better' is not remission. Chase mucosal healing and a normal calprotectin — the gap between symptomatic and endoscopic remission is where the strictures grow."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 Complications of transmural disease — and beyond the gut

🧱 Intestinal (the transmural consequences)

  • 🚧 Strictures → bowel obstruction — fibrotic narrowing (usually ileal); colicky pain, vomiting, distension. May need balloon dilatation or resection.
  • 🕳️ Fistulae — enteroenteric, enterovesical (pneumaturia/recurrent UTIs), enterocutaneous, rectovaginal, and perianal.
  • 🔥 Abscesses — intra-abdominal or perianal collections; need drainage + antibiotics, not just immunosuppression.
  • 🍑 Perianal disease — fissures, skin tags, fistulae, abscesses; a hallmark of Crohn's.
  • 🩸 Malabsorption — B12 & bile-salt malabsorption (terminal ileum), iron/folate deficiency, gallstones, oxalate renal stones, short-bowel syndrome after repeated resections.
  • 🎗️ Colorectal cancer — increased risk with long-standing colonic disease → surveillance colonoscopy.

🌍 Extra-intestinal manifestations (screen for these)

  • 🦵 Arthropathy — peripheral (parallels gut activity) and axial/sacroiliitis (independent).
  • 👁️ Uveitis / episcleritis — a red, painful eye needs urgent ophthalmology.
  • 🔴 Erythema nodosum (tender shin nodules, tracks disease activity) and pyoderma gangrenosum (painful ulcer, does NOT track activity).
  • 🟡 Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) — check LFTs; commoner with colitis, raises cholangiocarcinoma/colorectal risk.
  • 🦴 Metabolic bone disease — from inflammation, malabsorption and steroids; and VTE — IBD is a prothrombotic state.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Strictures obstruct, fistulae leak, abscesses need draining — and the disease shows on the skin, eyes, joints and liver. Examine the whole patient, not just the bowel."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🔥 Diagnosis: ileocolonic Crohn's disease with perianal involvement (A2 L1 B1p), moderately active.
  2. 🩹 Exclude/treat sepsis first: image (MRI/CT), drain any abscess, antibiotics + seton for the perianal fistula BEFORE escalating immunosuppression.
  3. 💊 Induce remission: corticosteroid (or exclusive enteral nutrition); correct anaemia and deficiencies; VTE prophylaxis if admitted.
  4. 🛡️ Complete pre-biologic screen (TB, hepatitis, TPMT) then maintain with a steroid-sparing agent — thiopurine/methotrexate and/or anti-TNF (combination for higher-risk perianal disease per SONIC).
  5. 🚭 Smoking cessation, dietitian, IBD nurse specialist, and treat-to-target follow-up on calprotectin and endoscopy. Surgery for complications only — it is not curative.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Surgery in Crohn's treats the complication, never the disease — resect a stricture and the disease can recur at the anastomosis. Preserve bowel and keep the medicine going."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Two very different Crohn's discharges

🔥 This patient (moderate, perianal, on immunosuppression)

  • Maintenance therapy + steroid taper plan; NEVER stop biologic/immunomodulator abruptly
  • Smoking cessation reinforced; contraception if on methotrexate
  • Red flags: obstruction (vomiting, distension, no flatus), fever/abscess, heavy bleeding → return
  • IBD nurse contact, calprotectin monitoring, repeat scope; vaccinations; bone protection

🟢 The mild, well-controlled patient

  • Stable on maintenance, calprotectin normal, mucosal healing
  • Annual review, deficiency & bone monitoring, cancer surveillance if long-standing colitis
  • Clear written safety-net and rapid-access flare pathway
  • Self-management education and adherence support

💎 Clinical Pearls

🦘 Mouth to anus, skippingCrohn's is patchy transmural disease anywhere in the gut — terminal ileum is classic.
🧱 Transmural = complicationsFull-thickness inflammation makes strictures, fistulae and abscesses; that word explains it all.
🍑 Two ends tell the taleExamine the mouth and the perianus — ulcers + fistulae mean Crohn's, not UC.
🧪 Calprotectin firstThe best non-invasive gate between IBS and IBD; then scope + biopsy for the tissue.
🛡️ Screen before you suppressTB, hepatitis B/C and TPMT before immunosuppression; drain pus before biologics.
💊 Induce, then maintainSteroids induce and never maintain; escalate to thiopurine/methotrexate/biologic.
🚭 Smoking cessation is treatmentSmoking worsens Crohn's (opposite of UC) — cessation is disease-modifying.
🔪 Surgery is not a cureResect the complication, preserve bowel; disease recurs at the anastomosis.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the basics

  • 🎯 Treat-to-target & tight control: the CALM trial showed that escalating therapy to biomarker targets (CRP + calprotectin) plus symptoms achieved better mucosal healing than symptom-guided care alone. Endoscopic remission, not symptom relief, is the goal.
  • 🧬 Therapeutic drug monitoring: anti-TNF loss of response is often due to sub-therapeutic drug levels or anti-drug antibodies — measure levels and optimise dose or switch, rather than blindly cycling drugs.
  • 💉 Newer targeted therapy: gut-selective anti-integrin (vedolizumab), anti-IL-12/23 (ustekinumab) and anti-IL-23 agents, plus oral JAK inhibitors and S1P modulators (more established in UC) — positioning depends on phenotype, safety and perianal involvement.
  • 🕳️ Perianal fistulising Crohn's: combined medical-surgical management — MRI mapping, seton drainage, anti-TNF (± thiopurine), and emerging local mesenchymal stem-cell therapy (darvadstrocel) for complex perianal fistulae.
  • 🚧 Stricturing disease: distinguishing inflammatory (treat medically) from fibrotic (endoscopic balloon dilatation or bowel-sparing stricturoplasty/resection) strictures — MR enterography and response to steroids help decide.
  • 🧫 Post-operative recurrence prevention: smoking cessation plus early prophylactic therapy guided by colonoscopy at 6–12 months (Rutgeerts score) reduces clinical recurrence after ileocolic resection.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Stricture Nobody Imaged"

The case: A 27-year-old man with known ileal Crohn's presented with worsening colicky central pain, vomiting and abdominal distension over two days. He had been labelled as "just a flare" at two previous visits. This time the on-call team, seeing a known Crohn's patient with pain, increased his steroids and started IV hydrocortisone without imaging. Overnight he became peritonitic. A CT — finally done at 04:00 — showed a tight fibrotic ileal stricture with proximal dilatation and a contained perforation. He went for emergency resection, spent a week in intensive care, and lost a long segment of small bowel he could not afford to lose. Separately, three months earlier he had been started on infliximab without a TB screen; he later developed reactivated pulmonary tuberculosis.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Known Crohn's = flare" — obstruction from a stricture was never considered.
🧲 NO IMAGINGVomiting + distension in Crohn's demanded a CT/MRI, not more steroids.
💊 WRONG TREATMENTSteroids do not open a fibrotic stricture — they masked the perforation.
🛡️ NO TB SCREENAnti-TNF started without screening → reactivated tuberculosis.
🚭 SMOKING IGNOREDOngoing smoking drove aggressive, stricturing, relapsing disease.

🛡️ What would have saved him: treat obstructive symptoms in Crohn's as a possible stricture and image early — a fibrotic stricture is a mechanical/surgical problem, not a steroid problem; always screen for latent TB and hepatitis before any anti-TNF; and never underestimate smoking cessation. This is exactly the discipline that protected the patient in Bed 4.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'It is just a flare' has perforated more Crohn's bowels than any stricture. Vomiting and distension earn an image, not a bigger dose of steroid."
