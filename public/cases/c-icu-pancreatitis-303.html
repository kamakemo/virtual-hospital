🧭 The diagnosis is bedside — the revised Atlanta 2-of-3

Acute pancreatitis needs two of three: (1) characteristic pain (epigastric, radiating to the back), (2) lipase or amylase > 3× the upper limit of normal, and (3) characteristic cross-sectional imaging (contrast CT/MRI/US). Our patient already meets criteria 1 and 2 — imaging is not required to diagnose, only to stage and find complications later.

⚠️ The trap of "the amylase is only mildly up"Amylase normalises fast and can be normal by presentation (especially in alcohol or hypertriglyceridaemia, where lipids can also interfere with the assay). Lipase is more sensitive and stays elevated longer — a normal amylase never excludes pancreatitis.

The medical registrar thinks out loud at 14:26:

🧠 Frame it: diagnosis is made — now it is resuscitation + severity + cause

"Pancreatitis is confirmed on pain + lipase. I am not chasing the diagnosis; I am chasing the three questions that change outcome: How sick is he (organ failure now)? How severe will he get (a severity score at 48 h)? And what caused it — because a stone obstructing the duct with cholangitis needs an urgent ERCP, and everything else is supportive."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 💧 Goal-directed fluid resuscitation — balanced crystalloid, titrated to urine output, lactate and perfusion. NOT a fixed torrent.
  2. 💨 Oxygen to keep SpO₂ ≥ 94%; watch for ARDS needing HFNO/NIV/intubation.
  3. 💉 Analgesia — titrated IV opioid; pain relief is humane and lowers catecholamine drive.
  4. 🧮 Severity score — Glasgow-Imrie + APACHE II now, CRP at 48 h; identify the organ-failure patient early.
  5. 🔎 Find the cause — US for gallstones/duct dilatation, LFTs, calcium, triglycerides, drug history; ask about cholangitis (fever, jaundice, RUQ pain).
  6. 🍽️ Plan early enteral nutrition, not prolonged nil-by-mouth. No prophylactic antibiotics.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"There is no drug that cures the pancreas. The four levers that save lives are FLUIDS, OXYGEN, ANALGESIA and treating the CAUSE — everything else is watching for complications."
🧪 Resident's self-testHe asks the nurse to keep the patient strictly nil-by-mouth for 5 days and start TPN "to rest the pancreas." Right call?

No. "Resting the gut" is an outdated dogma. Prolonged starvation and TPN worsen outcomes — the atrophic gut mucosa leaks bacteria that seed pancreatic necrosis. Early enteral nutrition (within 24–72 h), oral or NG, as tolerated, maintains gut integrity and reduces infection. Enteral beats parenteral (reflected in PYTHON and multiple meta-analyses).

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY a gland becomes a multi-organ disease (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🔓 Premature intra-acinar enzyme activation: the insult (alcohol metabolite, an impacted stone raising ductal pressure, hypertriglyceridaemia-derived free fatty acids) triggers trypsinogen → trypsin INSIDE the acinar cell, before it ever reaches the gut.
  2. 🩸 Autodigestion: activated trypsin cascades activate the other zymogens — the pancreas literally digests itself. Lipase saponifies fat (→ hypocalcaemia), elastase eats vessel walls (→ haemorrhage).
  3. 🔥 SIRS: damaged acinar cells release DAMPs and cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6) into the circulation → a systemic inflammatory response far beyond the abdomen.
  4. 💧 Capillary leak & third-spacing: inflamed endothelium leaks protein-rich fluid out of the vasculature into the retroperitoneum, gut wall and interstitium. Intravascular volume collapses → distributive + hypovolaemic shock.
  5. 🫁 Distant organ failure: the same cytokine storm and leak flood the lungs (→ ARDS), starve the kidneys of perfusion (→ AKI), and can cause DIC — the reason this sits in the ICU, not on a surgical ward.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Acute pancreatitis is not a stomach illness — it is a burn on the inside. Think of it like a major burn: massive capillary leak, huge fluid needs, SIRS and ARDS. Resuscitate the whole patient, not the pancreas."

⚖️ Contrast pair — interstitial oedematous vs necrotising (revised Atlanta)

💧 Interstitial oedematous (~80–90%)

  • Diffuse gland enlargement, no necrosis on contrast CT
  • Usually milder, resolves with supportive care
  • CT enhances uniformly

💀 Necrotising (~10–20%)

  • Non-enhancing pancreatic / peripancreatic tissue = necrosis
  • High risk of infected necrosis, organ failure, death
  • CT best after 72 h (necrosis takes days to declare)

💡 Necrosis itself is not an indication to operate; infection of that necrosis (usually week 2–4) is what triggers intervention — and even then, step-up drainage first.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The admission order set (14:30)

  • 💧 Balanced crystalloid (Hartmann's/Ringer's lactate preferred over saline), goal-directed — bolus to perfusion then titrate; reassess urine output, lactate, HR/BP every 1–2 h. Avoid fixed high-volume protocols.
  • 💨 Oxygen titrated to SpO₂ ≥ 94%; escalate to HFNO/NIV/ICU ventilation if ARDS evolves.
  • 💉 Analgesia — titrated IV opioid (e.g., morphine/fentanyl PCA); regular antiemetic.
  • 🧪 Bloods — lipase, FBC, U&E, LFTs, calcium, triglycerides, glucose, LDH, CRP (repeat at 48 h), ABG/lactate, coag, blood cultures if febrile.
  • 🩻 Urgent US abdomen for gallstones + biliary dilatation; CT with contrast only if diagnosis is unclear or to stage complications after 72 h.
  • 🍽️ Early enteral feeding as tolerated (oral/NG); do not keep strictly NBM for days; no routine TPN.
  • 🚫 NO prophylactic antibiotics. Treat only proven infection/cholangitis.
  • 📞 If cholangitis / persistent obstruction → urgent ERCP referral; if gallstone cause → cholecystectomy same admission (or after recovery in severe disease).
  • 📟 ICU monitoring, urinary catheter (hourly output), consider intra-abdominal pressure monitoring if abdomen tense.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The three orders people get WRONG in pancreatitis: too much fluid, too many antibiotics, and too little food. Get those three right and you have done most of the medicine."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ CCU priorities — the nurse runs the resuscitation safety net

  • 💧 Hourly urine output is the single most useful bedside fluid gauge — flag < 0.5 mL/kg/h AND rising output that signals you can ease off.
  • 📟 Continuous HR/BP/SpO₂; escalate desaturation early — it is the first sign of ARDS.
  • 😖 Pain scores and titrate analgesia; unrelieved pain drives tachycardia and demand. Position sitting-forward for comfort.
  • 🍽️ Support early enteral feeding — offer oral diet as nausea settles; manage the NG feed if oral is not tolerated. Do not enforce prolonged starvation.
  • 🌡️ Monitor temperature and trends: a NEW fever/deterioration around week 2 suggests infected necrosis, not the early SIRS — escalate, do not just give paracetamol.
  • 🫄 Watch the abdomen: increasing distension + tense abdomen + falling urine + rising airway pressures = possible abdominal compartment syndrome — measure bladder pressure and alert the team.
  • 🩸 Glucose monitoring (islet stress/diabetes); electrolyte replacement, especially calcium and magnesium.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In pancreatitis the urine bag talks. It tells you when you are under-filling AND when to stop — the nurse watching it hour by hour is titrating the whole resuscitation."

📈 CT / Imaging + Visual Enhancements

🖥️ Timing of imaging — read the pancreas like a movie, not a snapshot

A contrast CT on day 1 can look bland; necrosis declares over 72+ hours as non-enhancing tissue. Image for diagnosis only if uncertain — image to STAGE complications after 3 days.

🔎 The imaging toolkit — which test, when, and why

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 💧 Hourly urine output + lactate trend — the primary fluid-resuscitation targets; both under- and over-filling show here.
  • 🫁 SpO₂, RR, work of breathing, serial ABG — earliest ARDS surveillance; escalating O₂ needs = escalate care.
  • 🧮 Serial severity scoring — APACHE II daily, CRP at 48 h; the key question is whether organ failure is transient or persistent (>48 h).
  • 🫄 Intra-abdominal (bladder) pressure if the abdomen is tense — rising pressure + oliguria + high airway pressures = abdominal compartment syndrome.
  • 🌡️ Temperature & clinical trajectory over weeks — a fresh deterioration in week 2–4 signals infected necrosis; distinguish it from the early sterile SIRS fever.
  • 🩸 Glucose, calcium, magnesium; watch for GI bleeding (stress ulcer, splenic/portal vein thrombosis with varices).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The clock reframes the monitor: in week 1 you watch for organ failure and over-resuscitation; in weeks 2–4 you watch for infected necrosis. Same patient, two different vigilances."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 Local and systemic — mapped to their timeline

  • 🫁 ARDS — early respiratory failure from the systemic inflammation; commonest early organ failure.
  • 💧 AKI & distributive/hypovolaemic shock — hypoperfusion from third-spacing; may need vasopressors and RRT.
  • 💀 (Peri)pancreatic necrosis — non-viable tissue; sterile initially.
  • 🦠 Infected necrosis (usually week 2–4) — the major cause of late death; suspect with new sepsis/gas on CT → step-up drainage, delay necrosectomy.
  • 🫧 Pseudocyst (>4 weeks, encapsulated fluid) / acute collections — drain only if symptomatic/infected.
  • 🩸 Splenic / portal vein thrombosis → left-sided portal hypertension, gastric varices, GI bleed.
  • 🫄 Abdominal compartment syndrome — tense abdomen from oedema/ascites/ileus compromises kidneys, gut and lungs; a fluid-overload complication too.
  • 🍬 Endocrine/exocrine failure later — new diabetes and steatorrhoea after necrosis.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Necrosis is not an operation — INFECTED necrosis is. And even then the surgeon waits: 'delay, drain, and debride last' beats early open necrosectomy every time."

🧭 Mimics to keep in mind (the acute abdomen killers)

Perforated peptic ulcer, mesenteric ischaemia, ruptured/leaking AAA, acute cholangitis, inferior MI, and bowel obstruction can all mimic severe epigastric pain — a lipase >3× ULN with the right story clinches pancreatitis, but a soft lipase or an atypical picture earns imaging.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Diagnosing pancreatitis is only half the job — always finish the sentence: pancreatitis DUE TO ___. The cause you find (stone, triglycerides, calcium) is often the cause you can TREAT."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 💧 Resuscitate: goal-directed balanced crystalloid to urine output/lactate/perfusion; oxygen; analgesia; ICU organ support (vasopressors, ventilation, RRT as needed).
  2. 🧮 Stratify: Glasgow-Imrie 6 + persistent organ failure = severe. Admit ICU; daily reassessment.
  3. 🔎 Treat the cause: rising bilirubin + gallstones + cholangitis → urgent ERCP + sphincterotomy; plan same-admission (or post-recovery) cholecystectomy for gallstone pancreatitis to prevent recurrence.
  4. 🍽️ Feed early (enteral, oral/NG as tolerated); no prophylactic antibiotics.
  5. 🦠 If infected necrosis later: targeted antibiotics + minimally invasive step-up drainage; delay any necrosectomy until walled-off (usually >4 weeks).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Gallstone pancreatitis is not finished until the gallbladder is out. Discharging a recovered gallstone pancreatitis with the gallbladder in situ is scheduling the next admission."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Recovery discharge vs the "not-finished" trap — two different worlds

🔥 This patient (severe, gallstone)

  • Cholecystectomy done/scheduled — the definitive cure of the cause
  • Alcohol cessation support, triglyceride & diabetes management, smoking cessation
  • Monitor for late pseudocyst, new diabetes, exocrine insufficiency (steatorrhoea → enzymes)
  • Red flags: recurrent pain, fever, vomiting, jaundice → return

🟢 The mild, resolved case

  • Cause treated (e.g., cholecystectomy for gallstones same admission)
  • Early normal diet, short stay
  • Address modifiable causes (alcohol, triglycerides, drugs)
  • Clear safety-net advice on recurrence
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Discharge is not 'the pain settled' — it is 'the cause is fixed and the late complications are on the radar.' A gallbladder left in situ is a recurrence waiting to happen."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the bundle

  • 🪝 The step-up paradigm (PANTER, TENSION): for infected necrosis, endoscopic transluminal or percutaneous drainage first; escalate to video-assisted retroperitoneal debridement (VARD) or endoscopic necrosectomy only if needed. Endoscopic step-up reduces pancreatic fistula versus surgical step-up.
  • 💧 Fluid strategy nuance (WATERFALL, 2022): moderate goal-directed resuscitation with balanced crystalloid; aggressive fixed-rate protocols increase fluid overload without benefit. Dynamic targets (urine output, lactate, haematocrit/urea trend) beat static volumes.
  • 🧈 Hypertriglyceridaemic pancreatitis: insulin infusion (± heparin) activates lipoprotein lipase; plasmapheresis for TG >11.3 mmol/L with organ failure; long-term fibrates/omega-3 and cause control.
  • 🫄 Abdominal compartment syndrome: measure intra-abdominal (bladder) pressure; medical decompression (sedation, NG drainage, neuromuscular blockade, limit fluids, drain ascites) before surgical decompressive laparotomy.
  • 🪟 Revised Atlanta & determinant-based classification: collections named precisely — APFC and acute necrotic collection (<4 weeks) vs pseudocyst and walled-off necrosis (>4 weeks) — because the name dictates when/how to drain.
  • 🧬 Prognostic scores: BISAP (bedside, day 1), APACHE II, CTSI (Balthazar), persistent SIRS and haematocrit/BUN trajectory all forecast severity; none replaces serial clinical reassessment.
  • 🍽️ Nutrition evidence (PYTHON): on-demand oral feeding is as good as early routine NG in predicted-severe disease; enteral remains superior to parenteral for infection and mortality.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Antibiotics That Grew a Monster"

The case: A 58-year-old woman with gallstone pancreatitis was admitted with a Glasgow-Imrie of 4. The covering team, wanting to "be safe," started prophylactic meropenem on day 1 and kept her strictly nil-by-mouth on TPN to "rest the pancreas." Her low-grade fever and a bilirubin creeping from 40 to 110 were attributed to "the pancreatitis." No urgent US or MRCP was done; the ERCP referral sat for four days. She had an obstructing CBD stone with ascending cholangitis the whole time. By the time she was scanned she was septic; weeks later she developed infected, resistant-organism necrosis — seeded by a starved, atrophic gut and selected by the needless carbapenem. A long ICU stay and a step-up drainage followed; she survived, barely.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

💊 REFLEX ANTIBIOTICSProphylactic carbapenem in sterile disease — no benefit, selected resistant flora.
🍽️ STARVED GUTProlonged NBM + TPN atrophied the mucosa → bacterial translocation → infected necrosis.
🟡 MISSED CHOLANGITISRising bilirubin + fever blamed on "pancreatitis"; obstruction never sought.
🕰️ DELAYED ERCPReferral languished four days while an obstructing stone drove sepsis.
🧠 ANCHORINGEvery new sign folded into the original label instead of prompting a re-look.

🛡️ What would have saved her: withhold prophylactic antibiotics; feed the gut early and enterally; treat a rising bilirubin + fever as possible cholangitis and image the duct urgently; get the ERCP within hours, not days; and never fold a new deterioration into the old diagnosis without re-examining. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 3.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In pancreatitis, two well-meant reflexes kill: the antibiotic you gave for prophylaxis and the food you withheld to 'rest the gut.' And the stone whose ERCP you delayed is the third."
