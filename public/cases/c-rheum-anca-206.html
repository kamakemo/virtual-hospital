🧭 The pulmonary-renal differential you weigh at the bedside

The reflex differential for "alveolar haemorrhage + rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis" is ANCA-associated vasculitis (GPA/MPA), anti-GBM disease (Goodpasture), and lupus nephritis / other immune-complex disease. Infection (severe pneumonia with AKI) and cardiac failure are the mimics you must also exclude — but you do so while treating the vasculitis, not instead of it (see S14).

⚠️ The trap of "treat the pneumonia and review the kidney later"The CXR infiltrates here are BLOOD, not pus. Anchoring on "chest infection" and deferring the renal work-up is exactly how rapidly progressive GN reaches dialysis-dependence unnoticed. Send the vasculitis screen and involve nephrology the same hour.

🧪 Resident's self-testShe wonders whether to wait for the ANCA result before involving nephrology and planning treatment. Right call?

No — escalate now. ANCA serology can take hours to days. In a deteriorating pulmonary-renal syndrome you involve nephrology/rheumatology, arrange biopsy, and prepare induction immediately, treating on the strong clinical pattern. A negative ANCA does NOT exclude the diagnosis (~10% of AAV is ANCA-negative), and waiting risks irreversible renal loss and fatal haemorrhage.

S6

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the nose, lungs and kidneys bleed (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧫 ANCA production: autoantibodies form against neutrophil cytoplasmic antigens — proteinase-3 (PR3) in GPA, myeloperoxidase (MPO) in MPA. A trigger (infection, silica, drugs) primes neutrophils to express these antigens on their surface.
  2. 💥 Neutrophil activation: ANCA bind primed neutrophils, causing them to degranulate and release reactive oxygen species, proteases and neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) right against the small-vessel endothelium.
  3. 🩸 Small-vessel necrosis: the capillaries, venules and arterioles are destroyed. In the lung this is the alveolar capillary → blood pours into alveoli (diffuse alveolar haemorrhage). In the glomerulus the capillary tuft breaks → blood and protein leak, red cells enter the tubules and cast (red-cell casts).
  4. 🌙 Crescent formation: fibrin and macrophages fill Bowman space forming crescents — pauci-immune (little/no immune-complex staining), the histological signature of rapidly progressive GN. Untreated, crescents scar to irreversible fibrosis within days to weeks — hence the urgency.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"ANCA vasculitis is a neutrophil turned traitor against its own vessels. The crescent is a stopwatch — treat while it is cellular and reversible, before it scars to fibrous and permanent."

⚖️ Contrast pair — GPA vs MPA

🧫 GPA (granulomatosis with polyangiitis)

  • cANCA / anti-PR3
  • Granulomatous inflammation: sinus/nasal crusting, saddle-nose, lung nodules/cavities, subglottic stenosis
  • ENT + lung + kidney; higher relapse rate
  • Our patient's likely diagnosis

🩸 MPA (microscopic polyangiitis)

  • pANCA / anti-MPO
  • NO granulomas; often renal-limited or renal + alveolar haemorrhage
  • Less ENT disease; can present as isolated RPGN
  • Same induction principles

💡 The ANCA pattern predicts phenotype and relapse risk, but the emergency decision — induce immunosuppression now — is identical when the lungs and kidneys are involved.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (14:35)

  • 🧪 Vasculitis screen NOW: ANCA (PR3/MPO ELISA), anti-GBM, ANA/dsDNA, C3/C4, immunoglobulins, cryoglobulins; blood cultures to exclude endocarditis.
  • 🔬 Fresh urine microscopy for dysmorphic red cells and red-cell casts; urine protein:creatinine ratio.
  • 🩻 CT chest (alveolar haemorrhage vs infection) + CXR; FBC (falling Hb), U&E, LFTs, coagulation, CRP, group & save.
  • 🫁 Oxygen to target SpO₂ ≥ 94%; monitor for airway involvement; consider transfer factor (raised KCO) if stable.
  • 🔪 Refer nephrology for urgent renal biopsy (pauci-immune crescentic GN); ENT for nasal biopsy if needed.
  • 💊 Prepare induction: IV methylprednisolone pulses + rituximab or cyclophosphamide; screen for infection/hepatitis B before rituximab.
  • 🩸 Plasma exchange discussion for severe alveolar haemorrhage or dialysis-dependent AKI (PEXIVAS-informed).
  • 🛡️ PCP (co-trimoxazole) prophylaxis, PPI, bone protection; strict fluid balance and daily renal function.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The first orders in a pulmonary-renal syndrome are a urine microscope and an ANCA/anti-GBM screen — not a fourth antibiotic. Diagnose the bleed, then induce."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ AMU priorities — the nurse runs the safety net

  • 🫁 Continuous SpO₂ and respiratory monitoring — alveolar haemorrhage can escalate rapidly to respiratory failure; escalate early for worsening hypoxia or increasing haemoptysis.
  • 🩸 Quantify haemoptysis and monitor serial Hb — a falling haemoglobin with haemoptysis signals ongoing pulmonary bleeding.
  • 🚽 Strict fluid balance and hourly urine output via catheter; report oliguria — a driver for urgent dialysis planning.
  • 🩹 Save a fresh urine sample promptly for microscopy before it degrades; casts lyse in stale urine.
  • 💉 Support safe induction: pre-medicate and monitor rituximab infusions; watch for infection (immunosuppressed) and give PCP prophylaxis, PPI and bone protection.
  • 🧠 Explain the plan and the immunosuppression clearly — this is a frightening, unfamiliar diagnosis; adherence and infection-vigilance depend on understanding.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who saves the fresh urine and charts every millilitre of output and haemoptysis gives the team its two earliest signals — the failing kidney and the bleeding lung."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🫁 SpO₂, respiratory rate, haemoptysis volume and serial Hb — track the alveolar haemorrhage; deterioration may need ventilation ± plasma exchange.
  • 🩸 Daily creatinine, urine output and sediment — the trajectory of renal function decides dialysis timing and treatment intensity.
  • 🦠 Infection surveillance — immunosuppressed patients: fever may be sepsis, not flare; check cultures before assuming relapse.
  • 🧪 ANCA titre trend — a rising titre can precede relapse but is imperfect; correlate with clinical/organ activity.
  • 💊 Drug toxicity — FBC for cytopenias, glucose/BP for steroids, immunoglobulins on rituximab, bladder symptoms on cyclophosphamide.
  • 🦴 Bone, glucose and gastric protection maintained through the steroid course.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After induction, the monitor's question becomes 'is this a flare or an infection?' — because in the immunosuppressed AAV patient, treating the wrong one is how they die. Culture before you escalate immunosuppression."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — from the disease and from the cure

  • 🫁 Massive diffuse alveolar haemorrhage / respiratory failure — can require ventilation and plasma exchange; a leading early killer.
  • 🩸 Dialysis-dependent end-stage kidney disease — the endpoint of untreated/late RPGN; late fibrous crescents do not recover.
  • 🦠 Serious infection — the commonest cause of early death in AAV, driven by immunosuppression (PCP, bacterial sepsis, reactivation).
  • 🗣️ Subglottic/tracheal stenosis (GPA) — airway compromise; saddle-nose deformity and chronic sinus destruction.
  • 🔁 Relapse — especially PR3/GPA; requires maintenance therapy and vigilance.
  • 💊 Treatment toxicity — cyclophosphamide (infertility, cystitis, malignancy), steroids (bone, glucose, infection), rituximab (hypogammaglobulinaemia).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In AAV the disease takes the kidneys and lungs; the treatment invites infection. The art is inducing hard enough to stop the vasculitis and shielding well enough to survive the immunosuppression."

🏠 Discharge

📋 The AAV survivor discharge vs the missed-diagnosis path — two different worlds

🩸 This patient (treated early)

  • Maintenance rituximab (or azathioprine), reducing steroid taper, ± avacopan; PCP prophylaxis
  • Bone/gastric protection; monitor renal function, FBC, immunoglobulins, glucose/BP
  • Red flags: recurrent haemoptysis, new blood in urine, fevers, breathlessness → urgent review
  • Rheumatology + nephrology + ENT follow-up; vaccination and infection counselling

⚠️ The path we avoided (late diagnosis)

  • Dialysis-dependent ESKD from fibrosed crescents; permanent lung damage
  • Higher mortality from massive alveolar haemorrhage or overwhelming infection
  • The M&M story (S21) — weeks of antibiotics while the kidneys scarred
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"An AAV patient goes home knowing three things: report any new haemoptysis or blood in the urine at once, never skip your prophylaxis, and every fever is an infection until proven a flare — relapse and infection both stalk this diagnosis."

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🧬 PR3 vs MPO biology: ANCA subtype predicts phenotype and relapse better than the clinical GPA/MPA label — PR3-ANCA disease relapses more and responds particularly well to rituximab; MPO-ANCA disease is more often renal-limited with more chronic damage at presentation. Genetic associations differ (HLA-DP/PRTN3 for PR3; HLA-DQ for MPO).
  • 💊 PEXIVAS in depth: plasma exchange did not reduce death/ESKD across the whole trial, shifting practice to reserve PLEX for severe DAH, dialysis-dependent or very-high-creatinine disease and anti-GBM overlap; the reduced-dose glucocorticoid arm was non-inferior with fewer infections — a landmark for de-escalating steroid toxicity.
  • 🧪 Avacopan (ADVOCATE): oral C5a-receptor (C5aR) antagonism blocks the complement-driven neutrophil priming loop, enabling near steroid-free induction with superior sustained remission — a mechanistic advance targeting the alternative complement pathway now implicated in AAV.
  • 🔁 Maintenance strategy: RITUXVAS, MAINRITSAN and RITAZAREM established scheduled rituximab as superior to azathioprine for relapse prevention; optimal duration and ANCA/B-cell-guided dosing remain active questions.
  • 🫁 Alveolar haemorrhage management: rising KCO, serial Hb and progressively bloody BAL confirm DAH; severe cases need ventilatory support, correction of coagulopathy, and consideration of PLEX and pulse steroids while distinguishing from superadded infection.
  • 🔗 Mimics and overlap: drug-induced ANCA (levamisole-adulterated cocaine, hydralazine, propylthiouracil), infective endocarditis (ANCA-positive), and double-positive ANCA/anti-GBM disease each change management — always screen cultures, drug history and both serologies.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Chest Infection With a Bit of Blood in the Urine"

The case: A 61-year-old man saw three different clinicians over five weeks for "sinusitis," then a "chest infection." He received three courses of antibiotics. At the second visit his creatinine was 140 (baseline 80) and his urine dip showed blood ++ — filed as "probably a UTI." At the third, breathless and coughing blood, he was admitted with "pneumonia," and his creatinine was now 520. No one sent an ANCA, dipped a fresh urine under the microscope, or called nephrology. By the time a red-cell cast was finally seen, he was anuric and dialysis-dependent and bled catastrophically into both lungs. He survived on dialysis but never recovered renal function; the biopsy showed fibrous crescents — the window had closed weeks earlier.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Sinusitis then pneumonia" — infection label stuck across three visits.
🔬 URINE NOT READBlood on dipstick never examined under the microscope; casts missed.
📈 CREATININE IGNOREDA doubling then quintupling creatinine went unactioned.
🧪 SCREEN NOT SENTANCA, anti-GBM and complement never requested despite a pulmonary-renal picture.
🏥 SYSTEMNo senior/nephrology review, no joined-up view across three separate encounters.

🛡️ What would have saved his kidneys: treat "sinusitis that will not clear" plus haematuria and a rising creatinine as ANCA vasculitis; examine a fresh urine under the microscope for red-cell casts; send ANCA, anti-GBM and complement the hour a pulmonary-renal syndrome is suspected; and call nephrology for an urgent biopsy and induction while the crescents are still cellular. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 6.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'A chest infection with a bit of blood in the urine' has cost more kidneys than any rare disease. Blood in the urine plus a rising creatinine is a nephrological emergency until a microscope and an ANCA say otherwise."
