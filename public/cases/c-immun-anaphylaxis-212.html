⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Anaphylaxis = ACUTE ONSET + life-threatening Airway and/or Breathing and/or Circulation problem, usually (not always) with skin/mucosal changes. If A, B or C is threatened after an exposure — that IS anaphylaxis. Inject."

🧭 The diagnosis is CLINICAL — do not wait for a test

There is no blood test that diagnoses anaphylaxis in real time. Tryptase is drawn to confirm retrospectively, never to decide treatment. The bedside definition (acute onset + ABC compromise ± skin changes) is all you need to justify adrenaline. Hesitating for a test, a second opinion, or "to see if the antihistamine works" is how people die.

⚠️ The trap of "but there's no rash"Up to 1 in 5 anaphylaxis cases have NO skin signs — especially rapidly progressive ones and those already in shock (skin is under-perfused). Isolated collapse or isolated stridor after an exposure is still anaphylaxis. Do not let a normal-looking skin exam talk you out of the adrenaline.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The medical resident thinks out loud at 13:49:

🧠 Frame it as one reflex, not a differential

"Acute onset after a nut, threatened airway, wheeze, and shock — this is textbook anaphylaxis. My first action is not a history and not a cannula — it is IM adrenaline 500 micrograms into the anterolateral thigh, repeated after 5 minutes if she does not improve. THEN oxygen, lie her flat with legs raised, two large-bore cannulae and a fluid bolus. Antihistamine and steroid are afterthoughts, not treatments for the airway."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 💉 IM adrenaline 0.5 mg (0.5 mL of 1:1000) anterolateral thigh — repeat every 5 min if no improvement. This is step one, before anything else.
  2. 🛬 Position: lie flat with legs elevated (sit up only if breathing is the dominant problem; keep flat if hypotensive). Never let her stand or sit up suddenly.
  3. 🫁 High-flow oxygen 15 L via non-rebreathe; nebulised salbutamol/adrenaline as an adjunct for bronchospasm.
  4. 💧 IV crystalloid bolus (e.g. 500–1000 mL rapidly, titrated) for hypotension; large-bore access ×2.
  5. 📞 Call for help early: anaesthetics/ICU for the airway, and think ahead to refractory anaphylaxis (adrenaline infusion).
  6. 💊 Antihistamine and steroid ONLY after the emergency is controlled — and never instead of adrenaline.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The right first drug in anaphylaxis is adrenaline, the right route is intramuscular, and the right site is the anterolateral thigh. IV adrenaline boluses on the ward cause arrhythmias and death — save the IV route for the ICU infusion."
🧪 Resident's self-testThe intern reaches for IV adrenaline "because she's shocked." Should she give an IV bolus of 1:1000 on the resus trolley?

No — that can kill her. The out-of-hospital / ward route is intramuscular 1:1000. IV adrenaline is reserved for refractory anaphylaxis, given as a carefully titrated infusion (or tiny 20–50 microgram aliquots of 1:100,000) by someone experienced with cardiac monitoring. An IV bolus of concentrated adrenaline causes severe hypertension, myocardial ischaemia and malignant arrhythmias. IM thigh first, every time.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the crisis (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧫 Sensitisation (first exposure): the allergen (nut protein) is presented to T-helper cells → B cells class-switch and make allergen-specific IgE, which coats mast cells and basophils via high-affinity FcεRI receptors. This first hit is silent.
  2. 🔗 Re-exposure & cross-linking: the allergen bridges two adjacent IgE molecules on the mast-cell surface. This cross-linking is the trigger that flips the switch.
  3. 💥 Degranulation: mast cells explosively release histamine, tryptase, prostaglandins, leukotrienes and platelet-activating factor. Preformed mediators act in seconds; newly synthesised ones sustain and amplify.
  4. 🌊 The systemic effects: histamine & leukotrienes → vasodilation (flushing, hypotension), capillary leak (angioedema, distributive shock), bronchoconstriction (wheeze) and increased mucus. A vicious cycle: hypoperfusion → hypoxia → more mediator release → more collapse.

💡 Adrenaline reverses every arm of this at once: α1 → vasoconstriction (reverses hypotension & mucosal oedema), β1 → inotropy/chronotropy, β2 → bronchodilation AND stabilises mast cells to switch off further degranulation. No other drug touches all four.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Antihistamines block ONE mediator (histamine) at ONE receptor, arriving late. Adrenaline reverses the vasodilation, the leak, the bronchospasm AND stops the mast cell — that is why it is the drug and they are not."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Anaphylaxis vs Hereditary Angioedema

🐝 Anaphylaxis (mast cell / histamine)

  • IgE-mediated mast-cell degranulation, histamine
  • Urticaria + itch, wheeze, shock; onset in minutes
  • RESPONDS to adrenaline — that is the treatment

🧬 Hereditary Angioedema (bradykinin)

  • C1-esterase inhibitor deficiency, bradykinin
  • NO urticaria, NO itch; family history; recurrent; slower over hours
  • Does NOT respond to adrenaline/antihistamine/steroid — needs C1-INH or icatibant

💡 Same-looking swollen lip, completely different disease and drug cupboard. The presence of itchy hives and the speed point to histamine; a painless, non-itchy, recurrent, familial swelling points to bradykinin.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (13:48)

  • 💉 IM adrenaline 500 micrograms (0.5 mL of 1:1000) anterolateral thigh — NOW; repeat every 5 min if no improvement.
  • 🛬 Position: lie flat, legs elevated (sit up only if breathing dominates; do NOT stand or sit up suddenly).
  • 🫁 High-flow oxygen 15 L non-rebreathe; nebulised salbutamol ± nebulised adrenaline for bronchospasm/stridor.
  • 💧 IV crystalloid bolus 500–1000 mL stat, repeat/titrate to BP; two large-bore cannulae.
  • 📞 Call anaesthetics/ICU early for airway and for possible adrenaline infusion (refractory anaphylaxis).
  • 🩸 Serial mast-cell tryptase: at presentation (once treatment underway), at 1–2 h, and a baseline at ≥24 h (or in clinic).
  • 💊 Adjuncts AFTER the emergency: non-sedating antihistamine for skin symptoms; steroids are no longer routine (do NOT delay adrenaline for either).
  • 🧾 If NOT responding to adrenaline and NO urticaria/itch → reconsider bradykinin angioedema (HAE / ACE-inhibitor) → C1-INH concentrate or icatibant.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The order that saves the life takes ten seconds to give and needs no result to justify it: adrenaline, IM, thigh, now. Everything else on the list supports that one line."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Resus-bay priorities — the nurse guards the airway and the clock

  • 💉 Draw up and be ready to give IM adrenaline immediately; know where the auto-injector / 1:1000 ampoules live — locked-away adrenaline has killed patients.
  • 🛬 Keep her flat with legs raised; do NOT let her stand, walk to a chair, or sit up suddenly — sudden upright posture in a vasodilated patient can cause fatal "empty-ventricle" arrest.
  • ⏱️ Time-stamp every adrenaline dose and prompt the team at 5 minutes for a repeat if no improvement. Nurses run the 5-minute clock.
  • 📟 Continuous monitoring — SpO₂, ECG, non-invasive BP; watch for arrhythmia if IV adrenaline is used.
  • 🩸 Cannulate ×2, run the fluid bolus, and draw the tryptase samples at the right times (presentation, 1–2 h, baseline ≥24 h) — the timing is the whole value of the test.
  • 🧾 Keep her informed and calm; document the trigger, timings, and every intervention. Plan for the observation period — biphasic reactions can recur hours later.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who keeps the patient flat and pushes for the 5-minute repeat dose is doing the two things most likely to prevent an anaphylactic death."

🧠 How to use tryptase properly — the 3-sample ruleDraw it (1) as soon as feasible after resuscitation has begun, (2) at 1–2 hours (the peak), and (3) a baseline at ≥24 hours or in clinic. A high, unchanging baseline hints at underlying mastocytosis / hereditary alpha-tryptasaemia, a risk factor for severe reactions.

🩻 Adjunct assessment

  • Airway assessment (nasendoscopy by ENT/anaesthetics): to gauge laryngeal oedema and plan a definitive airway if needed.
  • Complement panel (C4, C1-INH level & function, C1q): the immunology workup that separates the bradykinin causes when angioedema recurs without urticaria.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🌬️ Airway & work of breathing continuously — the food-anaphylaxis killer is laryngeal oedema; a rising stridor or falling voice means the airway is closing.
  • 🩸 BP & HR every few minutes — response to adrenaline and fluids; persistent hypotension = refractory reaction → infusion + ICU.
  • 🫁 SpO₂ and RR — bronchospasm resolution; her asthma makes this the second front.
  • 📈 Continuous ECG — especially if IV adrenaline is used (ischaemia, arrhythmia).
  • ⏱️ The 5-minute clock — every IM dose triggers a reassessment at 5 min for a repeat.
  • 🕐 The observation period — plan the monitored watch for a biphasic reaction (see S13); severity of the first phase and need for repeat adrenaline guide how long.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After the first adrenaline, the monitor's question changes from 'is this anaphylaxis?' to 'is she responding, and will it come back?' — the answers are the airway, the blood pressure, and the clock."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong

  • 🌬️ Complete airway obstruction from laryngeal oedema — the commonest cause of anaphylactic death; may need emergency intubation or a surgical airway.
  • 🩸 Refractory anaphylaxis / cardiovascular collapse — not responding to ≥2 IM doses → adrenaline infusion, aggressive fluids, ICU; consider glucagon if beta-blocked.
  • 🔁 Biphasic reaction — symptoms recur (typically within 1–12 h, occasionally up to 72 h) after apparent resolution, WITHOUT re-exposure. This is why we observe.
  • 💔 Kounis syndrome — allergic acute coronary syndrome (coronary spasm/thrombosis from mast-cell mediators); and adrenaline-related myocardial ischaemia/arrhythmia.
  • 🧠 Hypoxic-ischaemic brain injury / arrest from prolonged shock or airway loss.
  • 🩹 The system complication: discharge without an adrenaline auto-injector, an allergy referral, or a plan — leaving the patient primed for a fatal repeat.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Two enemies come AFTER you think it is over: the biphasic reaction hours later, and the discharge with no auto-injector. Anticipate both, or the save unravels at home."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🐝 Diagnosis: anaphylaxis (acute onset post-nut, ABC compromise, urticaria). Treat first, confirm with serial tryptase later.
  2. 💉 IM adrenaline given and repeated at 5 min; second pre-hospital-plus-ED dose controlled the airway and BP; ready to escalate to an infusion if it recurs.
  3. 💧 O₂ + fluid bolus + flat with legs raised → BP recovers; nebulised bronchodilator for residual wheeze.
  4. 🕐 Observe for a biphasic reaction on a monitored bed; then discharge with a plan: two adrenaline auto-injectors + training, written emergency plan, allergy-clinic referral, serial tryptase arranged.

🏠 Discharge

📋 Two very different discharges — anaphylaxis vs hereditary angioedema

🐝 This patient (anaphylaxis)

  • Two adrenaline auto-injectors + hands-on training; carry at all times
  • Written personalised anaphylaxis emergency plan; allergen avoidance advice
  • Allergy-clinic referral to confirm the trigger (specific IgE/skin-prick) and baseline tryptase
  • Red flags: any airway/breathing/circulation symptom after exposure → auto-injector + 999
  • Asthma optimised (asthma worsens outcomes)

🧬 If it had been hereditary angioedema

  • NO role for auto-injector adrenaline — it does not work
  • Immunology follow-up; confirm with C4, C1-INH level & function
  • On-demand C1-INH / icatibant for attacks; consider prophylaxis
  • Avoid ACE inhibitors and oestrogens; family screening
  • Written plan for bradykinin-specific rescue therapy
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Never discharge anaphylaxis without an auto-injector, a plan, and an allergy referral — and never discharge recurrent non-itchy angioedema with an auto-injector that will fail. The right diagnosis dictates the right take-home drug."

💎 Clinical Pearls

💉 Adrenaline FIRSTIM 0.5 mg (0.5 mL 1:1000) in the thigh, repeat at 5 min. Not the antihistamine, not the steroid.
🩺 Diagnosis is clinicalAcute onset + Airway/Breathing/Circulation compromise ± skin changes. No test needed to treat.
🛬 Lie flat, legs upSit up only if breathing dominates; never stand or sit up suddenly — empty-ventricle arrest.
🧫 IgE mast-cell mechanismAllergen cross-links IgE on mast cells → histamine et al. Adrenaline reverses every arm.
🔁 Biphasic reactionsCan recur hours later without re-exposure — observe on a monitor.
🩸 Tryptase confirms, laterSerial samples (presentation, 1–2 h, baseline). Never delays treatment; can be normal.
🧬 HAE = bradykininNo urticaria, no itch, family history, recurrent; adrenaline fails → C1-INH or icatibant.
💊 Know the modifiersBeta-blocker → add glucagon; ACE inhibitor → think bradykinin angioedema, stop the drug.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the reflex

  • 🧫 Non-IgE and mixed mechanisms: direct mast-cell activation via MRGPRX2 (opioids, vancomycin "red man", quinolones, neuromuscular blockers), complement-mediated reactions, and IgG-mediated pathways — anaphylaxis without classic sensitisation.
  • 🧬 Mastocytosis & hereditary alpha-tryptasaemia: a raised BASELINE tryptase flags an expanded mast-cell burden or extra copies of the TPSAB1 gene, both predisposing to severe/idiopathic anaphylaxis and Hymenoptera-venom reactions.
  • 🐝 Venom immunotherapy & food desensitisation: subcutaneous venom immunotherapy is highly protective for sting anaphylaxis; oral immunotherapy (e.g. for peanut) and anti-IgE (omalizumab) raise reaction thresholds.
  • 🍑 Cofactors & the "summation" model: exercise, alcohol, NSAIDs, infection and menstruation lower the reaction threshold — classically food-dependent exercise-induced anaphylaxis (often omega-5-gliadin/wheat).
  • 🥩 Alpha-gal syndrome: tick-bite–acquired IgE to galactose-α-1,3-galactose causing delayed (3–6 h) anaphylaxis to mammalian meat — breaks the "minutes" rule.
  • 🧬 The bradykinin cascade in depth: C1-INH normally brakes the contact (kallikrein-kinin) system; its deficiency lets bradykinin accumulate. Newer HAE therapy targets this directly — lanadelumab (anti-kallikrein) and berotralstat (oral kallikrein inhibitor) for prophylaxis, icatibant (B2-antagonist) for attacks.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Antihistamine That Killed"

The case: A 17-year-old with a known peanut allergy ate a takeaway satay and within minutes developed hives, wheeze and a hoarse voice. In the busy ED, the plan was to "give piriton and hydrocortisone and see how she settles" — the adrenaline stayed in a locked medication cupboard while a nurse hunted for the key. Chlorphenamine and IV hydrocortisone went in; her wheeze eased briefly, so everyone relaxed. Ten minutes later her voice was gone and her SpO₂ crashed as her larynx swelled shut. By the time IM adrenaline was finally given she was peri-arrest; a difficult airway could not be secured in time. She died of hypoxic airway obstruction — a reaction that one prompt injection would almost certainly have aborted.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

💊 WRONG FIRST DRUGAntihistamine + steroid given as if they treat the airway — they do not.
🔒 ADRENALINE DELAYEDLocked away and unavailable at the critical moment; no one owned the injection.
🧠 FALSE REASSURANCETransient easing of wheeze read as "improving" — the airway was still closing.
🌬️ AIRWAY HELP LATEAnaesthetics called only once she was peri-arrest, too late for a difficult airway.
🏥 SYSTEMNo immediate-access adrenaline in resus; unclear anaphylaxis protocol.

🛡️ What would have saved her: treat any acute onset of airway/breathing/circulation compromise after an exposure as anaphylaxis and give IM adrenaline into the thigh immediately; keep adrenaline instantly accessible in every clinical area; never lead with antihistamine or steroid; call the airway team early; and never mistake a brief improvement for resolution. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 2.

🧬 …and the mirror-image errorThe other way to kill: treating a hereditary angioedema or ACE-inhibitor airway swelling as allergic anaphylaxis — piling on adrenaline, antihistamine and steroid while the non-itchy, bradykinin-driven larynx obstructs. If it does not itch, keeps recurring, runs in the family, or the patient is on an ACE inhibitor, and it is NOT responding — stop repeating adrenaline and give C1-INH concentrate or icatibant.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'We gave the antihistamine first' has killed more anaphylaxis patients than the allergen ever did. Adrenaline, intramuscular, thigh, now — and if it does not respond and does not itch, think bradykinin."
