🧬 Why THIS patient? — a sodium channel, a fever, and the night

Brugada syndrome is an inherited cardiac channelopathy, classically an autosomal-dominant loss-of-function mutation in SCN5A, the gene for the cardiac sodium channel (Naᵥ1.5). Reduced inward sodium current (↓INa) during early repolarization unmasks the transient outward potassium current (Ito) in the right-ventricular outflow-tract (RVOT) epicardium, where Ito is densest. The epicardium repolarizes faster than the endocardium → a transmural voltage gradient → the coved ST elevation on the surface ECG, and the substrate for "phase 2 re-entry": a closely-coupled premature beat that ignites polymorphic VT/VF. The substrate is genetic and lifelong; tonight three things tipped it over the edge — FEVER (sodium channels gate worse when hot), a vagotonic large meal/alcohol, and the nocturnal, high-vagal-tone small hours when Brugada events cluster.

🔥 Triggers that unmask / worsen Brugada 🌡️ Fever (the most actionable) · 💊 Sodium-channel blockers (class I antiarrhythmics, some anesthetics, tricyclics, cocaine) · 🍷 Vagotonic states — large meals, alcohol, the early hours of sleep · 🧪 Electrolyte shifts (hyper/hypokalemia, hypercalcemia) · 👨 Male sex & SE-Asian ancestry (higher prevalence and risk).

🧭 The single most important question: is the SUBSTRATE still being provoked?

Unlike a scar-related VT, the acute emergency here is not a single circuit to shock once — it is a genetically primed myocardium being inflamed by a trigger. The two pivotal questions are: (1) Is he still in / re-entering VF? If yes → defibrillate. (2) Is the trigger still active? The fever is — so aggressive antipyresis is genuine antiarrhythmic therapy. The definitive answer to "how do we stop him dying next month" is an ICD, but tonight's job is to defuse the fever, be ready to shock recurrent VF, and protect him from any provoking drug.

⚡ The decision framework: DEFIBRILLATE recurrent VF · COOL the fever · PROTECT from class-I drugsA perfusing post-ROSC Brugada patient does not need a "rate-control" or "rhythm" drug. He needs the fever brought down hard, the defibrillator at the bedside for recurrent VF, the Brugada "avoid" list enforced, and — for electrical storm — isoproterenol and quinidine, never a sodium-channel blocker.

"Young man. Structurally normal heart. Nocturnal VF arrest during a fever. Family story of sudden nocturnal death. 🎯 Immediate priorities (her actual order of operations)

  1. 🚫 Protect him from provoking drugs: NO class-I antiarrhythmics, no problem anesthetics, flag brugadadrugs.org at the bedside and on the chart.
  2. 🌡️ Aggressive fever control NOW: paracetamol, active cooling, treat the source. The fever is an active arrhythmic trigger — antipyresis is antiarrhythmic.
  3. 🔌 Defibrillator at the bedside, pads on, ready to shock recurrent VF instantly; continuous telemetry.
  4. 💉 For electrical storm: draw up isoproterenol and plan oral quinidine — the storm-specific therapy. Keep them in mind even before they're needed.
  5. 🧪 Confirm and characterize: high right-precordial leads (V1–V2 one–two intercostal spaces up) to capture the type 1 pattern; serial 12-leads as the fever falls; electrolytes; troponin (will be mildly up post-arrest, not a STEMI).
  6. 🛡️ Plan the only proven therapy: this is an aborted sudden cardiac death → a secondary-prevention ICD is the definitive answer, arranged this admission once he's stable.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Cool the fever first, keep the defibrillator within arm's reach second, and plan the ICD third — because no pill reliably stops Brugada, but a defibrillator catches what the pills can't."

🤔 "But the ST elevation — shouldn't we just treat it as a STEMI?"

"No. A STEMI gives territorial, convex ('tombstone') ST elevation with reciprocal depression and an evolving troponin/wall-motion story. Brugada gives a coved ST elevation confined to the right precordial leads in a structurally normal heart, often dynamic with fever, with no reciprocal change. Activating the cath lab here delays the real treatment and risks giving a sodium-channel-blocking sedative/antiarrhythmic. When the heart is normal and the ST is coved in V1–V2 with this story — it's Brugada."

🧪 Resident's self-test (answer before reading on) Someone suggests loading IV procainamide "to suppress any further VT." Good idea in this patient?

No — it's exactly backwards. Procainamide is a class Ia sodium-channel blocker; it is one of the agents used to provoke the Brugada pattern in diagnostic testing. In a manifesting, febrile Brugada patient it accentuates the substrate and can precipitate VF, not suppress it. For Brugada arrhythmia the mechanism-targeted drugs are quinidine (Ito blocker) and isoproterenol (for storm) — and the cornerstone is the ICD plus fever control.

🎓 Consultant Round

The on-call consultant electrophysiologist, by phone at 03:55, then at the bedside:

🗂️ Name the ECG pattern precisely — it changes the certainty

🔴 Type 1 (coved) — this patient, DIAGNOSTIC

  • Coved ST elevation ≥ 2 mm with a descending ST into a negative T wave in ≥ 1 right precordial lead (V1–V2)
  • Spontaneous type 1 (especially with symptoms) = the high-risk phenotype
  • Needs no provocation to call it Brugada
  • Aborted SCA / sustained VT → ICD

🔵 Type 2 (saddleback) — SUGGESTIVE, not diagnostic

  • Saddleback ST elevation with a positive/biphasic T wave
  • Suggestive only — needs confirmation (high leads, a fever ECG, or a sodium-channel-blocker provocation test) to convert to a diagnostic type 1
  • Asymptomatic, drug-induced-only type 1 is a lower-risk group
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Type 1 is coved and diagnostic; type 2 is saddleback and only suggestive. A coved ST in V1–V2 with syncope or an aborted arrest is the dangerous one — and it earns a defibrillator."

🌀 What is "Brugada electrical storm" — and what defuses it?

Electrical storm = ≥ 3 episodes of VF / sustained VT in 24 hours. In Brugada the storm is mechanism-specific: the epicardial action-potential dome is lost and phase-2 re-entry fires repeatedly. The playbook is not the usual antiarrhythmic ladder — it is defibrillate each episode, isoproterenol infusion (restores the calcium current and the dome, normalizing the ST segment), aggressive fever control, and quinidine (blocks Ito, the upstream culprit). Sodium-channel blockers are contraindicated. For truly refractory storm, epicardial RVOT substrate ablation can eliminate the abnormal substrate.

🧭 Consultant's three commandments for the next hour

  1. 🌡️ "Treat the fever like a loaded gun. Every degree of temperature is more Ito, more ST elevation, more VF risk. Antipyretics and cooling are first-line antiarrhythmic therapy in a febrile Brugada patient."
  2. 🔌 "He survived an arrest — that fact alone decides his future. Aborted SCA in Brugada is a Class I indication for an ICD. No drug substitutes for it; quinidine is an adjunct, not a replacement."
  3. 🚫 "Guard the chart. One reflex dose of a class-I antiarrhythmic, one wrong anesthetic agent, one missed fever — any of these can re-ignite VF. The whole team must know the brugadadrugs.org list."

📖 Teaching Points

🔬 The mechanism — WHY a sodium channel makes VF (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧬 The genotype: a loss-of-function mutation (classically SCN5A, Naᵥ1.5) reduces the inward sodium current INa that drives phase 0 and braces the early action potential.
  2. ⚖️ The unmasked current: with weaker INa, the transient outward potassium current Ito — densest in the RV outflow-tract epicardium — wins the tug-of-war and produces a deep "phase 1 notch," losing the action-potential dome in the epicardium while the endocardium keeps it.
  3. 📈 The transmural gradient = the ECG: epicardium repolarizes earlier than endocardium → a voltage gradient across the RVOT wall → the surface coved ST elevation in V1–V2.
  4. 🔁 Phase 2 re-entry: where the dome is lost in some epicardial regions but preserved in adjacent ones, the dome can propagate sideways into dome-less tissue and fire a very closely-coupled premature beat (R-on-T) — the spark for polymorphic VT / VF.
  5. 🌡️ Why fever ignites it: the mutant sodium channel gates even worse at higher temperatures — fever further reduces INa, deepening the notch, accentuating the coved ST, and tipping a stable substrate into VF. This is why a febrile night, not a treadmill, is when Brugada kills.
  6. 💥 Why it kills at night: events cluster in sleep and the early hours when vagal tone is high and heart rate slow — bradycardia and vagotonia further accentuate the substrate, so the "Bangungut"/SUNDS deaths happen in bed.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Brugada is a sodium channel that quits early, a potassium current that wins, and an epicardial dome that collapses — fever pushes it over, and only a defibrillator reliably catches the fall."

⚖️ Contrast pair #1 — Brugada vs STEMI (the lethal mimic)

📈 Brugada IS… (assume this in the right story)

  • Coved ST elevation confined to V1–V2 (right precordial)
  • Structurally normal heart, no chest pain
  • No reciprocal ST depression; pattern often dynamic with fever
  • Young patient, family history of sudden death, nocturnal arrest
  • Troponin only mildly raised (post-arrest), not an evolving infarct curve

🫀 STEMI is NOT that…

  • Territorial, convex ("tombstone") ST elevation following a coronary territory
  • Reciprocal ST depression in opposite leads
  • Ischemic chest pain, evolving troponin and wall-motion abnormality
  • Older, atherosclerotic risk profile
  • Answer is reperfusion (PCI/lysis) — not relevant to Brugada

😈 The trap: a tired team sees "ST elevation in V1–V2" and pattern-matches to "anterior STEMI." That sends the patient to the cath lab and — worse — toward sedatives/antiarrhythmics that block sodium channels and accentuate the very substrate. Coved, not convex; right precordial only, no reciprocal change; normal heart — read it as Brugada.

⚖️ Contrast pair #2 — Brugada vs ARVC (two RV diseases, different substrate)

🧬 Brugada (tonight)

  • Channelopathy — structurally normal RV
  • ECG: coved ST elevation V1–V2, fever-/drug-unmasked
  • Substrate: functional, in the RVOT epicardium (phase-2 re-entry)
  • Therapy: ICD, quinidine, fever control, avoid Na-channel blockers

🫀 ARVC

  • Cardiomyopathy — fibrofatty replacement of the RV myocardium
  • ECG: epsilon waves, T-wave inversion V1–V3, exercise-related VT (LBBB morphology)
  • Substrate: structural — seen on MRI/echo as a dilated, akinetic RV
  • Therapy: ICD, beta-blockers, exercise restriction, ablation

💡 Why it matters: both are inherited RV diseases that cause sudden death, but one is electrical (Brugada — normal imaging) and one is structural (ARVC — abnormal RV on imaging). Imaging and the precise ECG pattern separate them and steer the lifestyle and drug advice.

🔬 Fellowship Corner — Advanced Topics

  • 🧬 Genetics beyond SCN5A: SCN5A explains only ~20–30% of cases; many other loci modulate the phenotype, inheritance is variable-penetrance autosomal dominant, and a negative gene test does not exclude the disease. Family cascade screening (ECG ± fever/drug challenge) matters as much as genotyping.
  • 🧪 Provocation testing: a sodium-channel-blocker challenge (ajmaline, flecainide, procainamide) under continuous monitoring can convert a saddleback/borderline ECG to a diagnostic type 1 — done deliberately and cautiously, the mirror image of why these drugs are dangerous unsupervised.
  • 📈 High right-precordial leads: placing V1–V2 one or two intercostal spaces higher increases sensitivity for the type 1 pattern by sitting closer to the RVOT.
  • 🗺️ Epicardial substrate & ablation: abnormal late, fractionated electrograms over the RVOT epicardium mark the substrate; epicardial ablation there can normalize the ECG and reduce VF — a substrate-modifying, not just episode-treating, therapy.
  • ⚡ Risk stratification debates: the role of programmed electrical stimulation, the prognostic weight of spontaneous vs drug-induced type 1, syncope characterization, SCN5A status, and computed risk scores (e.g. Shanghai score) for the asymptomatic patient.
  • 🔌 Device choice: the subcutaneous ICD (S-ICD) is attractive in young Brugada patients with no pacing need — but vet eligibility with S-ICD screening, as the dynamic right-precordial ECG can cause sensing issues.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ EP / CCU nursing priorities — minute by minute

  • 📟 Continuous telemetry with the defibrillator at the bedside, pads attached, in UNSYNC (defib) mode. Brugada arrhythmia is VF — keep the machine charged-ready to shock.
  • 🌡️ Own the temperature. Re-check it frequently, give antipyretics on time, apply active cooling — in Brugada the fever chart is an arrhythmia chart. Escalate every spike.
  • 🛡️ Be the drug gatekeeper. Know the Brugada "avoid" list; question any class-I antiarrhythmic, certain anti-emetics/anesthetics, or tricyclics prescribed for this patient before they're given.
  • 🩸 Frequent obs with the right-precordial 12-lead repeated as the fever falls; document each strip — the EP team uses the dynamic ST changes.
  • 🧠 Neuro/consciousness checks — a patient who slumps may have re-arrested in VF; check the pulse and the monitor, don't assume a faint.
  • 📋 Document the rhythm strip before/after any event, the temperature at the time, and any drug given — fever-linked ST changes are gold for diagnosis and counseling.
  • 🔇 Calm, restful environment — but remember the paradox: Brugada events favor rest and the early hours, so vigilance must be highest overnight, not lowest.
  • 👨‍👩‍👦 Support the family who performed the CPR (see S16); their bystander resuscitation likely saved his life, and they will need to learn the fever/drug rules too.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who drives the fever down and blocks the wrong drug from reaching this patient is doing half the antiarrhythmic work in the room."

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A mildly raised troponin and ST elevation in V1–V2 after an arrest is not automatically a STEMI — in a young, structurally normal, febrile heart with a coved ST, it's Brugada, and the cath film proves it by being clean."
🧠 Why the normal coronary angiogram is the clincher, not the contradictionThe phone diagnosis was "anterior STEMI." A normal angiogram with a mildly settling troponin and a coved (not territorial) ST elevation in a structurally normal heart with this family story re-frames everything: the ST elevation is the disease's signature, not an occlusion. Now the job is fever control, an ICD, and protecting him from provoking drugs — not reperfusion.

📈 ECG / Imaging

🫀 The presenting ECG — read the right precordial leads like a detective

Right precordial leads. V1 and V2 show coved ST elevation ≥ 2 mm descending into a negative T wave — the type 1, diagnostic pattern. For contrast, a saddleback (type 2) shape is shown — only suggestive, needing confirmation. No reciprocal change, structurally normal heart.

🔎 The Brugada ECG toolkit (memorize the high-yield ones)

FeatureWhy it means Brugada (not STEMI / benign variant)
📈 Coved type 1 ST elevation≥ 2 mm in ≥ 1 right precordial lead (V1–V2), descending into a negative T wave — diagnostic for Brugada; no provocation needed.
🪑 Saddleback type 2ST elevation with a positive/biphasic T — suggestive only; confirm with high leads, a fever ECG, or a sodium-channel-blocker challenge.
📍 Right-precordial localizationChanges confined to V1–V2 (and high leads V1–V2 one–two spaces up), reflecting the RVOT epicardial substrate — not a coronary territory.
🚫 No reciprocal ST depressionUnlike STEMI; the Brugada pattern is a primary repolarization signature, not a regional ischemic injury current.
🌡️ Dynamic with fever / vagal toneThe ST elevation deepens with fever and bradycardia/vagotonia and may flatten as the fever settles — a moving target unlike a fixed infarct.
🧬 Normal structural heartNormal echo/MRI distinguishes it from STEMI (wall-motion abnormality) and ARVC (fibrofatty, dilated RV).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Coved and descending into a negative T = type 1 = Brugada, diagnostic. Saddleback = type 2 = suggestive, confirm it. And put V1–V2 a space or two higher when you suspect it — the RVOT is closer there."

🫀 Imaging & structural workup

  • 🩻 Echocardiogram: structurally normal heart — normal biventricular size and function, no wall-motion abnormality, normal RV. The normal heart is part of the diagnosis.
  • 🧲 Cardiac MRI: excludes ARVC (no fibrofatty RV, no RV dilatation/aneurysm) and other structural mimics — reinforcing that this is an electrical, not structural, disease.
  • 📈 Repeat / high-lead ECGs as the fever settles: document how dynamic the ST elevation is; capture the diagnostic type 1 at peak fever and its relative normalization when afebrile.
⚠️ The ECG's favorite lie in Brugada"ST elevation in V1–V2, so it's an anterior STEMI — to the cath lab and load an antiarrhythmic." In a young, painless, structurally normal, febrile patient with a coved ST and no reciprocal change, that misread sends him toward reperfusion he doesn't need and a sodium-channel-blocking drug that can kill him. Read the shape and the story, not just the lead.

The safest "drug" when a febrile Brugada patient destabilizes is defibrillation, cooling, isoproterenol and quinidine — and an ICD for the long run.

🏠 Long-term, disease-modifying strategy (this admission and for life)

  • 🔌 Secondary-prevention ICD — he is an aborted SCA survivor; this is the cornerstone, a Class I indication.
  • 💊 Quinidine as adjunct to reduce shocks/storm, or as the option for those who cannot/again decline an ICD.
  • 🌡️ Lifelong fever discipline: treat every fever early and aggressively, and check an ECG with any significant fever.
  • 📵 Drug avoidance: carry the brugadadrugs.org list; tell every future anesthetist, dentist and prescriber.
  • 👨‍👩‍👦 Family screening & genetic counseling — first-degree relatives need ECGs (± provocation) and the same fever/drug rules.

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📈 Continuous telemetry with the defibrillator at the bedside (UNSYNC/defib mode) until arrhythmia-free and afebrile; print strips of any ectopy or polymorphic runs.
  • 🌡️ Temperature trend — the most modifiable arrhythmia driver here. Treat aggressively and watch the V1–V2 ST segment track with it.
  • 📈 Serial right-precordial 12-leads (with high V1–V2) — document how the coved type 1 pattern deepens with fever and settles with antipyresis.
  • 🫀 Serial troponin — expect a small post-arrest rise that settles (demand/CPR), confirming this is not an evolving infarct.
  • 🧪 Electrolytes (K⁺, Ca²⁺) — keep them normal; both extremes modulate the Brugada ECG and arrhythmia risk.
  • 💉 If isoproterenol is running for storm: monitor HR, BP and for ischemia; titrate to ST normalization and VF suppression.
  • 🔋 After ICD implant: monitor for appropriate vs inappropriate detections/shocks; the dynamic right-precordial ECG can challenge S-ICD sensing — verify programming.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you stabilize a Brugada patient, the monitor's two most important readings are the temperature and the shape of V1–V2 — keep one low and the other will follow."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — and the mechanism

  • ⚡ Recurrent VF / sudden cardiac death — the great fear; the genetic substrate plus an active trigger (fever, a provoking drug) can re-fibrillate at any moment. Treatment is UNSYNC defibrillation + trigger removal.
  • 🌀 Electrical storm (≥ 3 VF/VT episodes/24 h) — phase-2 re-entry firing repeatedly; needs isoproterenol, quinidine, fever control, and sometimes RVOT ablation.
  • 🧠 Anoxic brain injury — from the arrest itself if resuscitation was delayed; bystander CPR (as here) is what preserves the brain.
  • 💊 Iatrogenic provocation — a class-I antiarrhythmic, the wrong anesthetic, or an untreated fever can cause the next VF. The therapy list and the fever chart are themselves safety devices.
  • 🔌 ICD-related issues — inappropriate shocks (the dynamic right-precordial ECG can confuse S-ICD sensing), lead/device complications, and the psychological burden of living with shocks.
  • ⚰️ Undiagnosed at-risk relatives — a "complication" of missing the diagnosis is the next family member who dies in their sleep, unscreened.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference — "The Fever Sent Home"

The case: A 38-year-old man came to a busy ED at 23:00 with two days of fever and a single syncopal episode while standing. He felt better on arrival, vitals near normal, and a junior team labelled it "a viral illness with a vasovagal faint." His ECG was filed as "non-specific anterior changes." He was discharged with paracetamol advice. That night he died in his sleep in ventricular fibrillation, his wife beside him. At the M&M the filed ECG, viewed through the right-precordial lens, showed an unmistakable coved type 1 Brugada pattern in V1–V2 — accentuated by his fever. A separate near-miss the same month: an unrecognized Brugada patient given a class-I antiarrhythmic / a sodium-channel-blocking anesthetic who promptly went into VF on the table.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Young + febrile + faint = viral/vasovagal" — syncope in this setting can be aborted VF.
📈 ECG NOT READA coved type 1 pattern in V1–V2 was on the strip and filed as "non-specific."
🌡️ FEVER UNDERRATEDThe fever was the active trigger — sent home untreated and unmonitored.
💊 LETHAL DRUGA class-I/sodium-channel-blocking agent precipitated VF in the near-miss case.
👨‍👩‍👦 FAMILY MISSEDNo one asked about sudden nocturnal deaths in the family.

🛡️ What would have saved him: apply the universal rule — a coved ST in V1–V2 with syncope and fever is Brugada; treat the fever aggressively and admit/monitor; never give sodium-channel blockers; ask about sudden nocturnal deaths; refer for an ICD after an aborted arrest. This is exactly why your team held the antiarrhythmic and cooled the fever tonight.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Just a viral faint' is the line written on the chart of patients who then die of Brugada in their sleep. A febrile syncope with a coved V1–V2 is a cardiac emergency, not a discharge."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Discharge plan & safety net

  • 💊 Medications: quinidine if started by EP (with a clear plan and side-effect counseling); a written list of drugs to avoid (brugadadrugs.org) to show every future prescriber, dentist and anesthetist.
  • 🔌 ICD in situ: wound care, device ID card, avoid strong magnetic fields, device-clinic follow-up; clear advice on what a shock feels like and what to do (one shock + feeling well = urgent review; multiple shocks = emergency).
  • 🌡️ Fever protocol: treat any fever early and aggressively with antipyretics, and seek an ECG/medical review for any significant fever — this is the single most important self-management rule.
  • 🍷 Lifestyle: avoid excess alcohol, cocaine, and very large heavy meals at night; recognize these as vagotonic triggers.
  • 🚩 Red flags → call emergency services: syncope/near-syncope, palpitations with dizziness, an ICD shock, and — for the family — any witnessed collapse or agonal night-time breathing (start CPR, call for help).
  • 👨‍👩‍👦 Family & genetics: first-degree relatives referred for ECG (± provocation) and genetic counseling; CPR/AED training offered to the household.
