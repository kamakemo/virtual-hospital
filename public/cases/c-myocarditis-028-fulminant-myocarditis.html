🧬 Why THIS patient? — virus, inflammation, oedema, stunned muscle

Fulminant myocarditis is myocardial inflammation, usually triggered by a virus (or a misdirected immune response). The infection or immune injury damages myocytes directly and ignites an inflammatory infiltrate; the muscle becomes oedematous and electrically irritable. Within days the left (± right) ventricle stops contracting properly — not because arteries are blocked, but because every fibre is sick at once. That is why the dysfunction is global and the coronaries are clean. The "fulminant" label means it arrives suddenly and crashes the haemodynamics — yet, paradoxically, the same acutely inflamed muscle that looks dead tonight often recovers completely once the inflammation settles, if you can keep her perfused through the storm.

🔥 Causes of myocarditis to keep in mind 🦠 Viral / lymphocytic (enterovirus/coxsackie, adenovirus, parvovirus B19, HHV-6, influenza, SARS-CoV-2) — commonest · 💉 Immune-checkpoint-inhibitor myocarditis (oncology patients — high mortality) · 🧟 Giant-cell (young, aggressive, arrhythmia/heart block) · 🌸 Eosinophilic (hypersensitivity/parasitic) · 🛡️ Autoimmune / systemic (sarcoid, lupus).

🤔 "But the coronaries are clean — isn't this a false alarm I can discharge?"

"No. Clean coronaries with this troponin and this ventricle is the diagnosis of myocarditis, not the exclusion of disease. The asymmetry of harm is brutal: if I admit and support a patient who turns out milder, I've lost a CCU bed-night. If I discharge a fulminant myocarditis, she arrests at home overnight. When the STEMI has clean coronaries, the answer is admit, image and support — never reassure-and-discharge."

🧪 Resident's self-test (answer before reading on) The night SHO wants to give her regular ibuprofen for the pleuritic chest pain. Good idea in this patient?

No. NSAIDs are avoided in acute myocarditis — experimentally they worsen myocardial inflammation and impair healing, and they promote fluid retention and renal injury in a patient already in shock. Treat her pain with paracetamol/opioid, support the heart, and stop the home ibuprofen. (Low-dose colchicine ± short steroid is used in the pericarditis-predominant picture, but the headline is: don't give NSAIDs to the shocked myocarditic heart.)

🔥 What does "fulminant" actually mean — and why is it (oddly) hopeful?

"Fulminant" = sudden onset (usually within 2 weeks of a viral illness) with severe haemodynamic compromise — cardiogenic shock needing inotropes or mechanical support. It looks like the worst presentation, and acutely it is. But the paradox of fulminant lymphocytic myocarditis is that, if you keep the patient alive through the inflammatory storm, most ventricles recover — frequently to near-normal function. That is why the entire strategy is bridge-to-recovery: aggressive, early mechanical support is not heroics, it is the treatment. The heart that looks dead tonight is the heart that walks out next week.

📖 Teaching Points

🔬 The mechanism — WHY inflammation stuns the whole heart (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🦠 The trigger: a virus (or a misdirected immune response — checkpoint inhibitor, autoimmune) reaches the myocardium. Direct viral injury and the immune attack against infected myocytes begin together.
  2. 🔥 Inflammation & oedema: an inflammatory infiltrate floods the interstitium. The myocardium becomes oedematous (this is what MRI's T2 signal sees), and injured myocytes leak troponin — hence a very high troponin with clean coronaries.
  3. 🫀 Global systolic failure: because every fibre is sick at once, contraction fails globally (sometimes regionally), not in a single coronary territory. The LV — and often the RV — dilates and stops emptying. Stroke volume collapses → cardiogenic shock.
  4. ⚡ Arrhythmia & block: inflamed, oedematous tissue is electrically unstable and the conduction system can be directly involved → ventricular arrhythmias and AV block (a red flag for giant-cell myocarditis).
  5. 🌱 Why fulminant can recover: the dysfunction is from reversible inflammation and oedema, not dead scarred muscle. Quell the inflammation and perfuse the body through it, and the stunned myocytes wake up — most ventricles recover. That is the whole rationale for bridge-to-recovery support.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Myocarditis is a heart drowning in inflammation, not starved by a blocked artery. Support it through the storm and the muscle comes back — that is why a pump beats a discharge letter."

⚖️ Contrast pair #1 — MYOCARDITIS vs STEMI/ACS (the lethal mimic)

🔥 MYOCARDITIS IS… (this patient)

  • Young, viral prodrome, no coronary risk factors
  • Clean coronaries on angiography
  • Global (diffuse) LV dysfunction, not one territory
  • Diffuse / saddle ST changes (often myopericarditic)
  • MRI: oedema + non-coronary, sub-epicardial/mid-wall LGE

🫀 STEMI / ACS is NOT that…

  • Older, coronary risk factors, atheroma
  • Culprit artery with an occlusive lesion
  • Territorial regional wall-motion abnormality
  • Territorial ST elevation with reciprocal change
  • LGE in a coronary, sub-endocardial/transmural pattern

😈 The trap: when the cath is clean, the brain wants to file the case as a "false-alarm STEMI" and discharge. But clean coronaries in a shocked young patient with sky-high troponin is the positive finding for myocarditis. The cost of discharging a fulminant myocarditis (overnight arrest) vastly exceeds the cost of admitting and imaging a mimic. The asymmetry of harm is why "clean coronaries → get the MRI, admit, support."

⚖️ Contrast pair #2 — The myocarditis subtypes (they need different drugs)

🦠 Lymphocytic / viral (commonest)

  • Post-viral, diffuse dysfunction
  • Usually supportive care; routine immunosuppression not proven
  • Most recover; bridge the fulminant ones with MCS
  • Specific antivirals rarely indicated

🧟🌸💉 Giant-cell / Eosinophilic / Checkpoint

  • Giant-cell: arrhythmia/block, young, fatal untreated → immunosuppression (steroids + ciclosporin) + transplant work-up; needs biopsy
  • Eosinophilic: corticosteroids + treat the cause
  • Checkpoint-inhibitor: high-dose steroids + STOP the drug

💡 Why it matters: the default for viral myocarditis is supportive care, but treating giant-cell or eosinophilic or checkpoint myocarditis as "just viral" wastes the window in which immunosuppression saves the heart (and the life). Biopsy is what separates them — which is why it is required when these subtypes are suspected.

🔬 Fellowship Corner — Advanced Topics

  • 🧲 Lake Louise criteria in detail: the 2018 update pairs a T2-based marker (oedema) with a T1-based marker (native T1, ECV or LGE) — meeting both is highly specific for acute myocarditis; LGE distribution (sub-epicardial inferolateral, mid-wall) distinguishes it from infarct.
  • 🔬 Endomyocardial biopsy & the Dallas criteria: the gold standard; sampling error limits sensitivity, so multiple samples and immunohistochemistry/viral PCR are used. It is required when giant-cell, eosinophilic or cardiac sarcoid is suspected because it changes therapy.
  • 🧟 Giant-cell myocarditis: aggressive, young patients, ventricular arrhythmia and AV block out of proportion; multinucleated giant cells on biopsy; needs combined immunosuppression and early transplant evaluation — and often an ICD.
  • 💉 Immune-checkpoint-inhibitor myocarditis: typically early after starting therapy, may overlap with myositis/myasthenia; treat with high-dose methylprednisolone, escalate to second-line immunosuppression if steroid-refractory, and permanently discontinue the ICI.
  • 🩸 MCS escalation ladder in fulminant shock: inotropes/vasopressors (a temporising bridge, not the goal) → IABP → Impella (LV unloading) → VA-ECMO (biventricular + respiratory support; add an LV vent/Impella to unload) → durable VAD or transplant if no recovery.
  • 🌱 Recovery and follow-up: serial echo/MRI tracks LV recovery; exercise restriction for 3–6 months; cardiac MRI and Holter guide return to sport; persistent dysfunction → chronic HFrEF GDMT and ICD/transplant pathways.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set, written as you'd actually write it (05:10)

  • 🚫 CANCEL the discharge — admit to CCU, shock pathway. Flag on chart and whiteboard: "fulminant myocarditis, clean coronaries — NOT a STEMI mimic to discharge."
  • 🩸 Activate the mechanical-circulatory-support pathway NOW — call cardiology + intensivist + MCS/ECMO team for early Impella/VA-ECMO as bridge-to-recovery. Do not wait for arrest.
  • 💉 Cautious inotropic support to maintain perfusion (e.g. low-dose dobutamine/milrinone ± noradrenaline) as a temporising bridge — with a low threshold to move to a pump rather than climb the dose.
  • 💧 Decongest if congested (IV furosemide guided by filling/perfusion); careful with preload in a shocked, dilated ventricle.
  • 🚫 STOP the ibuprofen — no NSAIDs. Analgesia with paracetamol/opioid. Strict bed rest, NO exercise.
  • 🧪 Bloods: serial troponin, BNP/NT-proBNP, U&E, LFTs, CK, lactate, FBC with eosinophil count, CRP/ESR, viral serology/PCR, TSH, and an oncology/drug history for checkpoint inhibitors.
  • 🧲 Cardiac MRI (Lake Louise) when haemodynamically able; meanwhile bedside echo to track LV/RV function.
  • 🔬 Endomyocardial biopsy if heart block / ventricular arrhythmia / refractory shock — to diagnose giant-cell or eosinophilic myocarditis (changes treatment).
  • ⚡ Continuous telemetry; defibrillator and temporary pacing available (myocarditis causes arrhythmia and AV block).
  • 📞 Early referral to an advanced-heart-failure / transplant centre if giant-cell suspected or no recovery on support.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ CCU nursing priorities — minute by minute

  • 📟 Continuous telemetry with the defibrillator and pacing at the bedside. The inflamed heart throws ventricular arrhythmias and AV block without warning.
  • 🩸 Know the shock pathway cold: escalating mottling, cool peripheries, falling urine output and rising lactate mean the inotropes are losing and it is time for the pump — escalate, don't wait.
  • 🩺 BP / MAP and perfusion every 5–15 min (cycle the NIBP, read the arterial line once placed). A drifting pressure means call the team now, not at the next set of obs.
  • 🩸 If on MCS (Impella/VA-ECMO): monitor cannula sites, flows, limb perfusion and bleeding; anticoagulation as prescribed; report low flows or limb ischaemia immediately.
  • 💉 Prepare inotrope/vasopressor infusions accurately on a monitored line; double-check concentrations — errors are dangerous in a shocked patient.
  • 🚫 Hold any NSAID order and query it — ibuprofen worsens myocarditis. Enforce strict bed rest / no exertion.
  • 🧠 Neuro/consciousness and urine-output checks — a patient who becomes confused or anuric is losing end-organ perfusion → escalate.
  • 🧪 Send serial troponin, lactate and the biopsy/viral samples promptly; document the rhythm and any arrhythmia for the team.
  • 👨‍👩‍👦 Keep the family updated and supported (see S16) — assign a liaison if staffing allows; for a young patient this is terrifying.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who calls 'rising lactate, falling urine' early — and who refuses the NSAID — is doing half the bridge-to-recovery work in the room."

📈 ECG / Imaging

🫀 The presenting ECG — diffuse, not territorial

Sinus tachycardia with diffuse, low-amplitude ST/T changes across many leads — not the territorial ST elevation of a single-vessel STEMI. Diffuse changes, sometimes saddle-shaped (myopericarditis), low voltages, or new conduction block all fit myocarditis.

🌐 Global hypokinesis on echoDiffuse (sometimes regional) dysfunction across the whole ventricle — not confined to one coronary territory. RV may also be involved.
🧲 Cardiac MRI — oedema (T2)Bright myocardial signal = oedema, the hallmark of acute inflammation (Lake Louise T2 criterion).
✨ Late gadolinium enhancement (non-coronary)Sub-epicardial / mid-wall LGE, classically inferolateral — a non-ischaemic pattern that distinguishes it from infarct (which is sub-endocardial/transmural in a coronary territory).
📉 Diffuse ST/T changes, low voltages, blockWidespread (often saddle) ST changes, low QRS voltages, or new AV/bundle block fit diffuse inflammation rather than a single occluded artery.
🔬 Endomyocardial biopsyThe gold standard; required when giant-cell/eosinophilic is suspected — tissue diagnosis that changes treatment.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Territorial ST elevation + a culprit artery = STEMI. Diffuse changes + global dysfunction + clean coronaries + MRI oedema/LGE = myocarditis. Read the pattern, not just the troponin."

🫀 Echo & MRI in this patient

  • 🩻 Echo: global LV hypokinesis, EF ~22%, mildly dilated and thickened (oedematous) walls, RV mildly impaired, small pericardial effusion, no regional pattern, no thrombus.
  • 🧲 Cardiac MRI (when stable): high T2 signal (oedema) and sub-epicardial/mid-wall late gadolinium enhancement in a non-coronary distribution — meets Lake Louise criteria for acute myocarditis.
  • 🔬 Endomyocardial biopsy (if indicated): sought urgently if heart block or ventricular arrhythmia raises suspicion of giant-cell myocarditis — it changes treatment to immunosuppression.
⚠️ The cath lab's favourite lie in the young chest-pain patient"Clean coronaries, so it was a false alarm — discharge." A clean cath in a shocked young patient with a huge troponin and a globally dead ventricle is the positive finding for myocarditis. Treat the clean cath as the cue to image (MRI), admit and support — never as permission to go home.

💊 Medications

💉 Acute drugs — what, why, and the traps

Drug / therapyRole tonightMechanism / caution
Inotropes (dobutamine / milrinone)Temporising support of cardiac output in cardiogenic shockA bridge, not the destination — they raise myocardial oxygen demand and can be arrhythmogenic in an inflamed heart. Low threshold to escalate to mechanical support.
Vasopressor (noradrenaline)Maintain MAP / organ perfusionUse the minimum to perfuse; excess afterload burdens the failing ventricle.
Mechanical circulatory support (Impella / VA-ECMO)The definitive bridge-to-recovery in fulminant shockImpella unloads the LV; VA-ECMO supports biventricular + respiratory failure (vent the LV). Escalate early, before multi-organ failure.
Diuretic (IV furosemide)Decongestion if volume-overloadedTitrate to filling/perfusion; careful with preload in a shocked, dilated ventricle.

Corticosteroids ± immunosuppressionFor giant-cell, eosinophilic and checkpoint-inhibitor myocarditisBiopsy-guided. Giant-cell: steroids + ciclosporin + transplant work-up. Checkpoint: high-dose steroids + STOP the drug. NOT routine for ordinary viral myocarditis.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Inotropes buy minutes; the pump buys the recovery. In fulminant myocarditis, support early and aggressively (Impella/ECMO) — and never reach for an NSAID."
🚫 The 'never' list in acute myocarditis❌ NSAIDs (ibuprofen/diclofenac) — worsen myocardial inflammation, retain fluid, harm the kidney · ❌ Exercise / exertion — risks ventricular arrhythmia and sudden death · ❌ Treating it as a "false-alarm STEMI" and discharging · ❌ Endless inotrope escalation as the endpoint instead of timely mechanical support · ❌ Missing giant-cell / eosinophilic / checkpoint-inhibitor myocarditis (each needs specific immunosuppression).

🏠 Subtype-specific & recovery therapy (this admission, not just tonight)

  • 🦠 Lymphocytic/viral: supportive care is the cornerstone; GDMT once stable; most recover.
  • 🧟 Giant-cell: corticosteroids + ciclosporin (± others) and early transplant evaluation; consider ICD.
  • 🌸 Eosinophilic: corticosteroids and treat the underlying cause.
  • 💉 Checkpoint-inhibitor: high-dose corticosteroids and permanently stop the drug; escalate immunosuppression if steroid-refractory.
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📈 Continuous telemetry with defibrillator and temporary pacing at the bedside — the inflamed heart causes ventricular arrhythmia and AV block.
  • 🩸 Invasive arterial line + perfusion markers (MAP, lactate clearance, urine output, mottling, mentation) — these tell you whether inotropes are holding or it is time for a pump.
  • 🫀 Serial troponin and NT-proBNP — track the inflammatory injury and wall stress; trends guide escalation and recovery.
  • 🩻 Daily echo (EF, LV/RV recovery, effusion) — recovering function is the cue to wean mechanical support; worsening function is the cue to escalate.
  • 🩸 If on MCS: device flows, cannula sites, anticoagulation, haemolysis (LDH/plasma-free Hb), and limb perfusion.
  • 🧪 Eosinophil count, renal/liver function and lactate repeatedly — eosinophilia can evolve; end-organ function tracks shock severity.
  • 🔬 Await biopsy / MRI results — they decide whether she needs immunosuppression (giant-cell/eosinophilic) or supportive care (viral).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In fulminant myocarditis the monitor's job is to answer two questions: 'is she perfusing?' (lactate, urine, MAP) and 'is the heart recovering?' (daily echo) — those two decide escalate vs wean."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — and the mechanism

  • 🔥 Refractory cardiogenic shock / multi-organ failure — the great fear; if support is under-dosed or delayed, hypoperfusion kills before the inflammation settles.
  • ⚡ Ventricular arrhythmia / sudden cardiac death — inflamed, oedematous myocardium is electrically unstable; exertion increases the risk (hence strict rest).
  • 🔌 High-grade AV block / bradyarrhythmia — conduction-system involvement; may need temporary pacing. Out-of-proportion block flags giant-cell myocarditis.
  • 🩸 MCS complications — bleeding, limb ischaemia, haemolysis, thrombosis, cannulation injury, infection (the price of the bridge — manage proactively).
  • 🧟 Missed giant-cell / eosinophilic / checkpoint myocarditis — treated as "just viral," it progresses to death or emergency transplant without the immunosuppression it needed.
  • 💔 Failure to recover → chronic dilated cardiomyopathy — a minority do not regain function and progress to HFrEF, ICD/transplant pathways.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference — "The Clean-Cath Discharge"

The case: A 26-year-old woman, well until a flu-like illness ten days earlier, arrived with chest pain and breathlessness, diffuse ST changes and a troponin of 22,000. A "STEMI" was called; the cath was clean. The tired night team, relieved, labelled it a "STEMI mimic — false alarm," reassured her and discharged her at 08:00. By midnight she returned in refractory cardiogenic shock, arrested in the ED, and the team could not cannulate VA-ECMO in time. She did not survive. At the M&M, the echo from the first admission — projected for all to see — showed a globally akinetic ventricle, EF 18%. It was fulminant myocarditis that should have been admitted and bridged. (Another version of this story: a giant-cell myocarditis missed for want of a biopsy → death; or NSAIDs given that worsened it.)

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 FALSE REASSURANCE"Clean coronaries = false alarm" — clean coronaries with a huge troponin and dead ventricle is the diagnosis OF myocarditis.
🩻 ECHO NOT HEEDEDA globally akinetic, EF-18% ventricle was on the screen and unread as "shock."
🩸 NO MCS PLANA fulminant shock was sent home instead of bridged with Impella/ECMO.
🔬 NO BIOPSYGiant-cell was never excluded — the subtype that needs immunosuppression went untreated.
🌙 SYSTEMNight fatigue, no senior review, no "clean-cath shock = admit & image" default.

🛡️ What would have saved her: apply the universal rule — young + viral prodrome + acute heart failure + raised troponin + clean coronaries = myocarditis until proven otherwise; admit and image (cardiac MRI), never discharge a shocked clean-cath patient; escalate early to mechanical support as a bridge-to-recovery; biopsy if giant-cell suspected; avoid NSAIDs; get senior eyes early. This is exactly why your registrar refused the discharge tonight.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Clean coronaries, false alarm' is the sentence written on the chart of patients who then die of fulminant myocarditis overnight. A clean cath is not a discharge letter."

🧭 Tonight → this week → for life

🏠 Discharge

📋 Discharge plan & safety net

  • 💊 Medications: HFrEF GDMT as tolerated during recovery (ARNI/ACEi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i), with a clear written schedule and a plan to up-titrate. Immunosuppression weaning plan if giant-cell/eosinophilic.

  • 🚫 Avoid NSAIDs — counsel specifically (she reached for ibuprofen at home). Paracetamol for pain instead.
  • 🧲 Follow-up imaging: repeat echo ± cardiac MRI to confirm sustained recovery; heart-failure and (if relevant) advanced-HF/transplant clinic appointments booked before she leaves.
  • 🚩 Red flags → seek emergency care: recurrent chest pain, breathlessness, palpitations, syncope or presyncope, or feeling faint on exertion.
  • 📚 Education: what myocarditis is in plain language, why it followed a viral illness, why rest and avoiding NSAIDs matter, and the (usually good) prognosis with recovery.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Discharge after fulminant myocarditis is not 'shock fixed, go home' — it is recovering heart-failure therapy, months of exercise restriction, no NSAIDs, follow-up imaging and a family who knows the warning signs."

💎 Clinical Pearls

🔥 Myocarditis until proven otherwiseYoung + viral prodrome + acute heart failure + raised troponin + clean coronaries = myocarditis. The asymmetry of harm makes this the safe default.
🩸 Support it, don't bury itFulminant myocarditis looks like it's dying but recovers — perfuse it through the storm with Impella/ECMO and most hearts come back.
🧟 Don't miss giant-cellYoung, ventricular arrhythmia/heart block → suspect giant-cell. It needs immunosuppression + a transplant conversation, and only biopsy makes the call.
🫀 Clean cath ≠ dischargeWhen the "STEMI" has clean coronaries, think myocarditis and get the cardiac MRI — never reassure-and-discharge a shocked ventricle.
🚫 No NSAIDsNSAIDs worsen myocardial inflammation and harm the kidney. Use paracetamol/opioid; stop the ibuprofen.
🧲 Lake Louise nails itCardiac MRI: oedema on T2 + late gadolinium enhancement in a non-coronary (sub-epicardial/mid-wall) pattern confirms acute myocarditis.
🔬 Biopsy when it changes treatmentEndomyocardial biopsy is the gold standard — required when giant-cell or eosinophilic myocarditis is suspected.
💉 Ask the oncology questionCheckpoint-inhibitor myocarditis is lethal — high-dose steroids and STOP the drug. Always check the cancer-drug history.
🌐 Global vs territorialMyocarditis = diffuse/global dysfunction + clean coronaries; STEMI = territorial wall-motion abnormality + a culprit artery.
💉 Inotropes are a bridge, not a homeEndless inotrope escalation raises demand on an inflamed heart. The pump unloads it — escalate early.
🏃 Rest the inflamed heartRestrict exercise for 3–6 months — exertion risks ventricular arrhythmia and sudden death.
🌱 Prognosis is hopefulMost fulminant lymphocytic myocarditis recovers LV function — the heart that looks dead tonight can be normal next month.
