🧭 The bedside triad that screams 'joint sepsis'

Fever + a single acutely inflamed joint + pain on the slightest passive movement / inability to weight-bear. The last is key: crystal and reactive arthritis hurt, but a truly septic joint typically refuses even a few degrees of passive movement because the whole capsule is inflamed and under pressure.

⚠️ The trap of "she has gout"Anchoring on a prior diagnosis is the classic killer here. Gout and infection can coexist in the same joint — indeed chronic joint disease is a risk factor for infection. A history of crystals never excludes sepsis; only the synovial fluid does. Aspirate FIRST, label the anchor as a bias, and act on the worst-case.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The rheumatology SpR thinks out loud at 14:52:

🧠 Frame it as "prove it is not infected," not "it is probably gout"

"Hot joint, fever, immunosuppressed host, a prosthesis on that side — this is septic arthritis until the fluid proves otherwise. My job is source-diagnosis and source-control: aspirate the knee now, send urgent Gram stain, culture, cell count and crystals, take two sets of blood cultures and a sepsis screen, then start empirical IV antibiotics per policy. Because there is a prosthesis, I call orthopaedics tonight — that joint may need surgical washout, and a native-joint aspiration technique differs from a prosthetic one."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🔬 Joint aspiration NOW — before antibiotics if achievable without delaying antibiotics in a septic patient; send synovial fluid for urgent Gram stain, culture, WCC/differential, and polarised-light crystal microscopy.
  2. 🩸 Two sets of blood cultures, FBC, CRP/ESR, U&E, glucose, lactate, urate (interpreted with caution — often low or normal during an acute flare).
  3. 💉 Empirical IV antibiotics immediately AFTER the aspirate (or immediately regardless if the patient is septic and the tap is delayed) per local policy, covering Staphylococcus aureus.
  4. 📞 Orthopaedics referral for drainage/washout — mandatory for a prosthetic joint; strongly consider for any tense pyogenic native joint.
  5. 🚫 Do NOT inject intra-articular steroid and do NOT escalate immunosuppression until infection is excluded; hold the methotrexate/biologic.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Aspirate before you inject, culture before you cover, and call orthopaedics before you admit for 'IV antibiotics and observe' — drainage is treatment, not an afterthought."
🧪 Resident's self-testThe joint is a prosthetic knee. Should she attempt the aspiration herself in the assessment bay?

Not blindly. A native hot joint should be aspirated promptly at the bedside, but a suspected prosthetic-joint infection is aspirated under strict aseptic (often image-guided, theatre-standard) conditions by orthopaedics to avoid seeding the prosthesis and to obtain reliable cultures. The principle is unchanged — get fluid before antibiotics — but the operator and setting differ. Call orthopaedics early; do not delay antibiotics in a septic patient waiting for the perfect tap.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY a septic joint destroys itself so fast (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🦠 Seeding: bacteria reach the synovium — usually haematogenously (the synovium is highly vascular and has no basement membrane), sometimes by direct inoculation (injection, trauma, surgery) or contiguous spread from adjacent osteomyelitis/cellulitis.
  2. 🧨 Explosive inflammation: bacterial products trigger a massive neutrophil influx into the closed joint space; the synovial WCC climbs into the tens of thousands, mostly neutrophils.
  3. ⚗️ Enzymatic cartilage digestion: neutrophils and bacteria release proteases, elastase, collagenase and reactive oxygen species. Cartilage is avascular and cannot repair — matrix (proteoglycan then collagen) is degraded within 24–48 hours.
  4. 💥 Pressure + ischaemia: a tense effusion raises intra-articular pressure, compromising cartilage nutrition and subchondral blood supply — a vicious cycle of pus, pressure and destruction that only drainage breaks.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Pus under pressure in a closed avascular space digests cartilage in a day or two — that is why septic arthritis is a drain-it-tonight emergency, not a start-antibiotics-and-see problem."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Septic vs Crystal arthritis (and remember: BOTH can coexist)

🦠 Septic arthritis

  • Fever, systemically unwell, refuses passive movement
  • Synovial WCC often > 50,000/µL, >90% neutrophils; Gram stain/culture may be positive
  • Emergency: aspirate, IV antibiotics, DRAIN

💎 Crystal arthritis (gout/pseudogout)

  • Recurrent, often afebrile (though a big flare can mimic sepsis)
  • Crystals on polarised light; WCC usually lower (2,000–50,000)
  • Treat inflammation — but crystals do NOT exclude coexisting infection

💡 The trap that harms patients is treating this as an "either/or." You can find urate crystals AND grow Staph aureus from the same knee. If the patient is septic, treat the sepsis regardless of crystals.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (14:55)

  • 🔬 Urgent joint aspiration — send synovial fluid for Gram stain, culture & sensitivities, cell count with differential, and polarised-light crystal microscopy. Mark samples URGENT.
  • 🩸 Two sets of blood cultures (before antibiotics), FBC, CRP, ESR, U&E, LFT, glucose/HbA1c, lactate, serum urate.
  • 💉 Empirical IV antibiotics per local policy AFTER the aspirate — e.g. IV flucloxacillin, with vancomycin/teicoplanin if MRSA risk or penicillin-allergic (see S11).
  • 🦠 If young, sexually active → add gonococcal cover and screen (urethral/cervical/pharyngeal/rectal NAAT); if prosthetic joint → orthopaedics + microbiology now, do not blind-tap.
  • 💊 Analgesia (paracetamol ± opioid); rest and elevate the joint; VTE assessment.
  • 🚫 HOLD methotrexate and the biologic; do NOT give intra-articular or high-dose systemic steroid until infection excluded.
  • 📞 Refer orthopaedics for washout/drainage (mandatory for prosthetic joint); IV fluids and sepsis-6 if septic.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Aspirate, then cover, then drain — and the aspirate goes for Gram stain, culture, cell count AND crystals every single time, because you can have both sepsis and gout."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Assessment-bay priorities — the nurse runs the safety net

  • 📟 Sepsis observations — NEWS2 every 30–60 min; escalate rising HR, temperature, RR or falling BP. A hot joint can tip into septic shock.
  • 🩸 Ensure blood cultures are drawn BEFORE antibiotics and that the synovial samples reach the lab urgently and labelled — a lost or delayed aspirate loses the diagnosis.
  • 💧 IV access ×2, fluid resuscitation as prescribed, accurate fluid balance; hourly urine output if septic.
  • 🦵 Rest, elevate and support the joint; handle gently — passive movement is agony. Position for comfort; cold packs may ease pain (do not delay definitive care).
  • 😖 Pain score regularly and pre-empt analgesia before any examination or transfer to theatre.
  • 🚫 Challenge any order for intra-articular steroid or for the patient's usual methotrexate/biologic until infection is excluded — the nurse is the last line before a harmful injection.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who makes sure the joint is tapped and cultured BEFORE the first dose of antibiotic protects the diagnosis for everyone who follows."

🔬 Aspiration / Imaging + Visual Enhancements

🔬 The synovial fluid — read it like a movie

One needle, four answers: the fluid is turbid, packed with neutrophils, AND contains urate crystals. The crystals are a red herring for stand-down — the Gram stain grew Staph. Sepsis and gout, together.

🔎 Crystal microscopy under polarised light — know these cold

CrystalShape & birefringenceMeaning
💎 Monosodium urate (gout)Needle-shaped; strongly NEGATIVELY birefringent (yellow when parallel to the compensator)Gout. May coexist with infection — never a stand-down.
🔷 Calcium pyrophosphate (pseudogout)Rhomboid/brick; weakly POSITIVELY birefringent (blue when parallel)CPPD/pseudogout — often knees/wrists in the elderly.
🦠 No crystals, purulent + organismsSheets of neutrophils, Gram stain positiveSeptic arthritis — drain and treat.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Needles that are Negative = gout; Positive rhomboids = Pseudogout. But crystals never exclude infection — always read the Gram stain and culture on the same fluid."

🩻 Imaging — supportive, never a substitute for the tap

  • Plain X-ray: often normal early; establishes a baseline, may show chondrocalcinosis (CPPD), erosions (RA/gout), effusion, or — late — joint-space loss and periarticular destruction. In a prosthesis: look for loosening/lucency suggesting infection.
  • Ultrasound: confirms an effusion and guides aspiration, especially in deep/prosthetic/difficult joints.
  • MRI: best for adjacent osteomyelitis, soft-tissue collections and for hip/spine/sacroiliac joints; do not let imaging delay the aspirate and antibiotics.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📈 Sepsis physiology (NEWS2), lactate — the joint is a source; watch for progression to septic shock in the first hours.
  • 🌡️ Temperature and the joint itself — persisting fever, re-accumulating effusion or unremitting pain after 48–72 h suggests inadequate drainage → repeat aspiration or return to theatre.
  • 🔬 Trend CRP (and clinical exam) to gauge response; falling CRP + improving joint + negative repeat cultures guide de-escalation.
  • 💊 Antibiotic levels / renal & liver function — vancomycin levels, flucloxacillin-related derangement, dosing in renal impairment.
  • 🫀 If S. aureus bacteraemia → hunt for endocarditis (echo) and other metastatic foci; ensure line-source excluded.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you start antibiotics, the monitor's question becomes 'is this joint actually being drained?' — persistent fever and re-effusion mean go back and drain, not just change the antibiotic."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong

  • 🦴 Irreversible cartilage destruction & secondary osteoarthritis — the commonest legacy of delay; ends in chronic pain, stiffness, sometimes arthrodesis.
  • 💥 Osteomyelitis / adjacent bone involvement — prolongs treatment; often needs surgery.
  • 🩸 Sepsis / septic shock / death — native-joint mortality ~10–15%, higher in polyarticular disease, RA and the elderly.
  • 🫀 Metastatic infection — S. aureus bacteraemia can seed endocarditis, spinal/psoas abscess and distant joints.
  • 🦵 Prosthetic-joint failure — may require debridement-and-retention or staged revision/removal.
  • 🩹 The system complication: the missed diagnosis — a septic joint mislabelled "gout," given steroid, no aspiration. The single most preventable disaster.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The deadliest complication of the hot joint is the diagnosis you did not make — every hour of 'let's see how the gout settles' is another hour of cartilage digesting."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🔬 Diagnosis: septic arthritis of a prosthetic knee (turbid fluid, WCC 78,000/94% neutrophils, Gram-positive cocci, later S. aureus bacteraemia) with coexisting gout crystals.
  2. 💉 Empirical IV antibiotics after aspiration, tailored to culture/sensitivities with ID + microbiology input; treat the bacteraemia; hunt for endocarditis.
  3. 🛠️ Source control: orthopaedic surgical washout/drainage (prosthetic joint → likely debridement ± staged revision); repeat aspiration if native and re-accumulating.
  4. 💎 Manage the gout separately once sepsis controlled — do not start urate-lowering during acute illness; treat the flare with agents safe in this context.
  5. 🏥 Admit under joint rheumatology/orthopaedic care; hold DMARDs/biologic; diabetes and sepsis care; physiotherapy and rehab as the joint settles.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Septic arthritis is treated by a needle, a drug and a drain — miss any one of the three and the joint (or the patient) is lost."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Recovery & the safe follow-up — two different worlds

🦠 This patient (confirmed septic arthritis)

  • Complete the tailored antibiotic course (OPAT/oral switch per ID); monitor CRP + joint
  • Physiotherapy and graded rehab; restart DMARDs/biologic only once infection cleared
  • Elective urate-lowering for gout later, with flare cover; optimise diabetes
  • Red flags: return of fever, re-swelling, increasing pain → back immediately

🟢 A genuinely non-septic crystal flare

  • Aspirate NEGATIVE for organisms on Gram/culture, crystals positive, systemically well
  • Flare treatment + safety-net; arrange urate-lowering pathway
  • Explicit "come back immediately if febrile or the joint worsens" advice
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"You only 'downgrade' a hot joint to a crystal flare once the culture is negative and the patient is well — never on the day of presentation, and never on gestalt."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🦵 Prosthetic-joint infection (PJI): classification (early/delayed/late), the role of sonication of explanted hardware and prolonged cultures for biofilm organisms (coagulase-negative staph, Cutibacterium), and strategies — DAIR (debridement, antibiotics, implant retention) for early PJI with a stable implant vs one- or two-stage revision for established infection.
  • 🦠 Gonococcal (disseminated) arthritis: the young, sexually active patient with the triad of migratory polyarthralgia, tenosynovitis and pustular skin lesions; synovial cultures are often negative — diagnose with NAAT from mucosal sites and treat with ceftriaxone; responds dramatically.
  • 🧪 Synovial biomarkers: synovial leukocyte esterase, alpha-defensin and C-reactive protein in PJI diagnosis (MSIS criteria); the limits of the classic ">50,000" threshold in prosthetic and partially-treated joints.
  • 🧬 Unusual hosts & organisms: Mycobacterium tuberculosis (chronic monoarthritis, Poncet disease), Brucella, fungal arthritis in the immunocompromised, and Salmonella joint infection in sickle-cell disease.
  • 💉 Antibiotic route & duration debate: OVIVA established early oral switch is non-inferior for bone & joint infection; shorter courses are being tested — individualise with ID.
  • 🦠 Immunosuppression & biologics: anti-TNF and JAK inhibitors raise septic-arthritis risk and blunt signs; a low threshold to aspirate and to hold therapy during infection is essential.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Gout Flare That Was Not"

The case: A 72-year-old man with known gout and diabetes attended a busy clinic with a hot, swollen, agonising right knee and a low-grade fever. Labelled "a gout flare," he was given an intra-articular corticosteroid injection and oral steroid — no aspirate was sent for culture. The redness settled for 48 hours and everyone relaxed. He re-presented on day 4 systemically septic; the knee was aspirated at last and grew Staphylococcus aureus, with urate crystals also present. Despite washout and IV antibiotics the cartilage was destroyed; he developed S. aureus bacteraemia and died of overwhelming sepsis two weeks later.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Known gout" closed the book; the fever was explained away.
🔬 NO ASPIRATEThe joint was injected but never tapped for Gram stain and culture.
💉 STEROID INTO SEPSISIntra-articular + systemic steroid suppressed inflammation and masked the infection.
💎 CRYSTALS ≠ SAFEEven had crystals been found, they would NOT have excluded coexisting Staph.
🏥 SYSTEMBusy clinic, no urgent-aspiration pathway, no orthopaedic escalation.

🛡️ What would have saved him: treat every hot joint as septic until aspirated; send Gram stain, culture, cell count AND crystals off the same needle; never inject steroid into an unaspirated joint; start empirical anti-staph antibiotics after the tap and call orthopaedics for drainage; and remember crystals and sepsis coexist. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 4.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'It settled with the steroid' has killed more joints than it ever cured. Relief is not a diagnosis — the aspirate and the culture are."
