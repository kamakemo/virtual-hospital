A referral delayed is cartilage that does not come back.

🧭 The three big patterns you separate at the bedside

Before you commit to RA, your reflex differential for "polyarthritis of the hands" is the pattern trio: rheumatoid arthritis (symmetrical, small joints, DIP-sparing, MCP/PIP/wrist), osteoarthritis (DIP/PIP + thumb base, bony, worse with use), and psoriatic arthritis (asymmetrical, DIP involvement, dactylitis, nail disease). One good hand exam triages all three (see S14).

⚠️ The trap of "the bloods are normal, so it is not RA"Up to ~20–30% of RA is seronegative (RF and anti-CCP negative), and inflammatory markers can be normal in early or mild disease. The diagnosis is clinical — persistent, objective synovitis of a typical distribution. Do NOT let a normal RF discharge a swollen, stiff hand.

🧪 Registrar's self-testThe RF is negative. Does that exclude rheumatoid arthritis?

No. RF is neither sensitive nor specific — it is positive in Sjögren, hepatitis C, endocarditis and ~5% of healthy people, and negative in a fifth of true RA. anti-CCP is far more specific (~95%) and predicts erosive disease. But the diagnosis remains clinical: persistent objective synovitis of a typical distribution IS rheumatoid arthritis even when both antibodies are negative (seronegative RA).

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the synovium turns on the joint (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🚬 Trigger + citrullination: in a genetically primed host (HLA-DRB1 "shared epitope"), smoking and mucosal/gum inflammation drive peptidyl-arginine deiminase to convert arginine to citrulline in joint proteins.
  2. 🎯 Loss of tolerance: the immune system makes anti-citrullinated peptide antibodies (anti-CCP) and rheumatoid factor (an antibody against the Fc of IgG) — often years before symptoms.
  3. 🔥 Synovitis: T cells, B cells, macrophages and fibroblast-like synoviocytes flood the synovium; TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-1 drive a self-amplifying inflammatory loop — the vicious cycle.
  4. 🌱 Pannus: the inflamed synovium proliferates into an invasive pannus that grows over and into cartilage and bone.
  5. 💀 Erosion: RANKL-driven osteoclasts plus matrix metalloproteinases resorb bone and destroy cartilage → the classic marginal erosions and, over time, deformity. This is WHY early cytokine blockade preserves the joint.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"RA is not wear-and-tear — it is an autoimmune synovitis whose pannus digests the joint. You are not treating pain, you are switching off the enzyme that eats bone."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Rheumatoid arthritis vs Osteoarthritis

🔥 Rheumatoid (inflammatory)

  • Symmetrical MCP/PIP/wrist/MTP; DIPs spared
  • Morning stiffness > 1 h, eases with use
  • Boggy warm soft-tissue swelling; systemic (fatigue, raised CRP)
  • Marginal erosions, juxta-articular osteopenia on X-ray

🦴 Osteoarthritis (mechanical)

  • DIPs (Heberden), PIPs (Bouchard), thumb base, knees
  • Stiffness < 30 min, worse WITH use, better with rest
  • Hard bony swelling; not systemic
  • Osteophytes, joint-space narrowing, subchondral sclerosis

💡 And versus psoriatic arthritis: PsA is typically asymmetrical, involves the DIPs, brings dactylitis ("sausage digit"), enthesitis, nail pitting and psoriasis — the mirror-image pattern to RA.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (first clinic visit)

  • 🧪 anti-CCP + RF; ESR & CRP; FBC, U&E, LFTs; ANA if features overlap; uric acid if crystal doubt.
  • 🧫 Pre-DMARD safety screen: hepatitis B & C serology, and — before any biologic — latent TB screening (IGRA) and VZV status.
  • 📸 X-ray both hands & feet (baseline erosions); ultrasound of hands/wrists for power-Doppler synovitis; baseline CXR (ILD, pre-MTX baseline).
  • 💊 Methotrexate once weekly (start ~10–15 mg, titrate) + folic acid on a different day; anchor DMARD.
  • 💉 Steroid bridge — short oral prednisolone taper or a single IM methylprednisolone/triamcinolone depot for rapid symptom control.
  • 🩹 NSAID + PPI for symptomatic relief (not disease-modifying); analgesia as needed.
  • 🧮 Document DAS28 baseline; book early review (weeks, not months) to titrate to target.
  • 🚫 Prescribing alert: flag the chart — never co-prescribe trimethoprim/co-trimoxazole with methotrexate.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Methotrexate weekly, folic acid on a different day, and a steroid bridge to cover the gap — start the DMARD at the first visit, because the erosions do not wait for the follow-up appointment."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ The rheumatology nurse runs the safety net and the education

  • 📚 Methotrexate counselling: weekly, not daily (the classic fatal error is daily dosing); folic acid on a separate day; alcohol moderation; reliable contraception (teratogen).
  • 💉 Teach subcutaneous methotrexate technique if switched from oral for tolerance/absorption; supply a monitoring booklet.
  • 🩸 Blood-monitoring gatekeeper: arrange FBC, U&E and LFTs every 2 weeks until stable then ~monthly-to-3-monthly; a rising MCV, falling counts or transaminitis triggers a hold and a call.
  • 🤒 Sick-day and infection rules: mouth ulcers, fever, breathlessness or a new cough = stop methotrexate and seek review (marrow suppression, pneumonitis, infection on immunosuppression).
  • 💊 Reconciliation: screen every new prescription for interactions — challenge any trimethoprim, co-trimoxazole or high-dose NSAID added elsewhere.
  • 🧠 Function and mood: signpost occupational therapy (joint protection, splints), physiotherapy, and support for fatigue and low mood — RA is a life-long systemic illness.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who says the words 'weekly, not daily, and never with trimethoprim' at every methotrexate start prevents more deaths than any biologic."

🩻 The extra-articular checklist behind the imaging

  • Lungs: interstitial lung disease (get a baseline CXR; low threshold for HRCT and PFTs if breathless), pleural effusions, rheumatoid nodules.
  • Heart/serosa: pericarditis/effusion; accelerated atherosclerosis (the biggest cause of death — see S13).
  • Neck: atlanto-axial subluxation — the anaesthetic landmine (see S13/S21).

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🧮 Disease activity (DAS28) at every visit — treat-to-target: review every 1–3 months in active disease and escalate until remission/low activity, then space out.
  • 🩸 Drug safety bloods — FBC (myelosuppression, rising MCV), LFTs (hepatotoxicity), U&E; fortnightly at start/after dose change, then monthly-to-3-monthly.
  • 🫁 Respiratory — new dyspnoea or dry cough → think MTX pneumonitis or RA-ILD; do not "wait and see."
  • ❤️ Cardiovascular — annual risk review; RA is an independent CV risk factor; control lipids, BP, and smoking hard.
  • 🦴 Bone — steroids + inflammation → osteoporosis; assess fracture risk, give bone protection where indicated.
  • 🦠 Infection surveillance — immunosuppressed: vaccinate (avoid live vaccines on biologics), have clear sick-day and infection rules.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you name 'RA,' the monitor's question changes from 'is it inflammatory?' to 'am I at target, and is the drug safe?' — and the answers live in the DAS28 and the full blood count."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — articular, extra-articular, and iatrogenic

  • 🖐️ Joint destruction & deformity — ulnar deviation, swan-neck and boutonnière fingers, Z-thumb, wrist subluxation; disability if uncontrolled.
  • 🦴 Atlanto-axial subluxation — erosion of the transverse ligament/dens; neck instability that can compress the cord at intubation — the anaesthetic emergency (get flexion/extension views pre-op).
  • 🫁 Interstitial lung disease, pleural effusions, rheumatoid nodules (skin, lung — Caplan syndrome with pneumoconiosis).
  • ❤️ Accelerated cardiovascular disease — chronic inflammation drives atherosclerosis; the leading cause of death in RA; also pericarditis/serositis.
  • 🩸 Felty syndrome — RA + splenomegaly + neutropenia (infection risk); large granular lymphocyte expansion.
  • 🔴 Rheumatoid vasculitis — nailfold infarcts, digital ischaemia, mononeuritis multiplex, skin ulcers (severe seropositive disease).
  • 👁️ Eyes: secondary Sjögren (dry eyes/mouth), episcleritis, scleritis (can threaten sight).
  • 🧬 Secondary (AA) amyloidosis — chronic inflammation → proteinuria/renal failure; anaemia of chronic disease; lymphoma risk raised.
  • 💊 Iatrogenic: methotrexate pancytopenia/pneumonitis/hepatotoxicity; steroid osteoporosis/diabetes; biologic/JAK infection, TB reactivation, VTE/MACE.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"RA patients most often die of their heart, not their hands — treat the inflammation and the cardiovascular risk as one disease. And never intubate a rheumatoid neck you have not imaged."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🖐️ Diagnosis: seropositive rheumatoid arthritis (ACR/EULAR 10/10, high anti-CCP, raised CRP, symmetric small-joint synovitis).
  2. 💊 Start methotrexate + folic acid as the anchor DMARD with a steroid bridge; NSAID/PPI for symptoms.
  3. 🎯 Treat-to-target: review every 1–3 months, titrate/optimise until DAS28 remission or low activity; add/switch to combination csDMARDs, then biologics/JAK inhibitors if the target is missed.
  4. 🫀 Whole-patient care: aggressive cardiovascular-risk and smoking-cessation intervention, bone protection, vaccination, OT/physio, mood and fatigue support.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"RA is not 'give a DMARD and discharge' — it is a target you chase: measure activity, escalate until remission, and keep chasing, because uncontrolled inflammation erodes joints AND arteries."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🧬 Pre-clinical RA & prevention: anti-CCP can be positive years before symptoms; trials (e.g. anti-CCP-positive arthralgia cohorts) explore whether early intervention can delay or prevent progression to clinical arthritis — the frontier of "treating before the joint".
  • 🎯 Treat-to-target nuance: DAS28 vs SDAI/CDAI vs Boolean remission; the limits of DAS28 (misses feet, can be inflated by tender-joint/patient-global in central sensitisation); ultrasound-defined remission and whether imaging remission should drive tapering.
  • 📉 De-escalation: in sustained remission, how to taper biologics/csDMARDs while watching for flare and radiographic progression; the trade-off between drug burden and relapse risk.
  • 🫁 RA-ILD: risk factors (male, smoking, high-titre RF/anti-CCP, UIP pattern), the debate over methotrexate and lung disease, and antifibrotic use in progressive fibrosing ILD.
  • 💉 Mechanistic sequencing: anti-TNF vs IL-6 (tocilizumab) vs T-cell co-stimulation (abatacept — more effective in high anti-CCP) vs B-cell depletion (rituximab — seropositive/nodular) vs JAK inhibition; cycling within versus switching across mechanisms after failure.
  • ⚖️ JAK-inhibitor safety: ORAL Surveillance and regulator restrictions — MACE, VTE, malignancy and zoster risk in older patients with cardiovascular risk factors; shared decision-making on route (oral) versus injectable biologics.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "Two Anti-Folates"

The case: A 58-year-old woman with well-controlled rheumatoid arthritis on weekly methotrexate 20 mg saw an out-of-hours GP for dysuria. She was prescribed trimethoprim for a presumed UTI; nobody cross-checked her regular medicines, and her methotrexate continued. Six days later she returned with a fever, a mouth full of ulcers, a sore throat and easy bruising. Bloods showed pancytopenia — white cells 0.8, platelets 22, neutrophils 0.2. She had neutropenic sepsis on a background of methotrexate-induced marrow suppression, the two anti-folates having stacked. She spent nine days in hospital on folinic-acid rescue, filgrastim and broad-spectrum antibiotics, and only just survived.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

💊 INTERACTION MISSEDTrimethoprim + methotrexate = additive anti-folate → predictable pancytopenia.
📋 NO RECONCILIATIONOut-of-hours prescriber never checked the regular medication list.
🩸 NO MONITORING TRIGGERNew symptoms (ulcers/fever) not linked to the marrow until late.
📣 PATIENT NOT WARNEDSick-day/interaction rules not embedded — she did not know to refuse trimethoprim.
🦴 SECOND NEAR-MISSA separate RA patient had a cervical cord scare when intubated without flexion/extension films.

🛡️ What would have saved her: hard-stop interaction alerts so trimethoprim/co-trimoxazole can never be co-prescribed with methotrexate; medication reconciliation at every prescribing encounter; patients drilled that methotrexate is weekly, not daily, and that mouth ulcers/fever/bruising mean stop and call; and — for the second near-miss — cervical-spine imaging before any intubation in long-standing RA. This is exactly the discipline that protected the patient in Bed 3.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Just a simple antibiotic' has hospitalised more methotrexate patients than any biologic ever has. Reconcile every prescription — the deadliest interaction is the one nobody looked for."
