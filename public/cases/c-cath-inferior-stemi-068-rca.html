🚦 Risk stratification & the indication

An inferior STEMI is a clinical + ECG diagnosis — persistent ST elevation ≥ 1 mm in ≥ 2 contiguous inferior leads (II, III, aVF). It mandates immediate reperfusion: primary PCI within 120 minutes of first medical contact (FMC), otherwise fibrinolysis then transfer (2023 ESC ACS). What makes THIS inferior STEMI dangerous is the proximal RCA culprit: it takes out the RV marginal branch (RV infarct) and the SA/AV nodal branches (bradyarrhythmia and AV block). The RV cannot generate forward flow, so the patient depends on preload — and nitrates or diuretics will drop him off a cliff.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Inferior STEMI + hypotension + clear lungs = get right-sided leads and think RV infarct. The lungs are clear because the failing chamber is the RIGHT one — fill the tank, never empty it."

📋 Pre-Procedure Workup & Planning

🧾 The 60-second pre-PCI checklist (do NOT delay reperfusion for it)

  • ➡️ Right-sided leads FIRST — record V4R (and V3R–V6R) before anything else in an inferior STEMI; V4R ST elevation confirms RV involvement and changes your whole haemodynamic plan.
  • 💧 Preload plan — RV infarct is preload-dependent: prepare fluid boluses and ban nitrates, morphine-heavy sedation, and diuretics until the RCA is open and the RV recovers.
  • 📟 Bradyarrhythmia plan — proximal RCA feeds the SA and AV nodes; have atropine drawn up and transcutaneous pads on, and a transvenous pacing kit to hand.
  • 🩸 Antithrombotic review — DAPT loaded (aspirin + ticagrelor/prasugrel); plan procedural anticoagulation (see S3).
  • 🖐️ Access assessment — Allen/Barbeau test of the radial (see S3); mark femoral pulses as back-up.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In every inferior STEMI, the first extra lead you record is V4R — one wire on the right chest tells you whether a nitrate will help the pain or stop the heart."

🩸 Vascular Access & Setup

🖐️ Radial vs femoral — and why radial still wins in STEMI

🖐️ Radial (default)

  • Fewer access-site bleeds & vascular complications
  • Lower mortality in ACS (MATRIX, RIVAL ACS subgroup)
  • Early ambulation, patient comfort
  • Confirm patency first (Barbeau/Allen)

🦵 Femoral (bail-out / support)

  • When radial fails / spasm / small vessel, or you anticipate needing a transvenous pacing wire and mechanical support in a shocky RV infarct
  • Higher bleeding — use micropuncture + ultrasound guidance
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Radial-first in ACS saves lives by preventing bleeds — but in a bradycardic, hypotensive RV infarct, plan your femoral venous access early so a transvenous pacing wire is one puncture away."

💉 Sheath, anticoagulation & the ACT concept

  • 🔧 Sheath: 6 Fr radial (hydrophilic); give an intra-arterial "radial cocktail" (verapamil ± heparin) to prevent spasm — note the nitrate component is minimised/omitted while the RV is preload-dependent and hypotensive.
  • 🩸 Procedural anticoagulation: unfractionated heparin (70–100 U/kg, titrate to an ACT ~250–300 s), or bivalirudin. The ACT (activated clotting time) is your bedside gauge that the blood is anticoagulated enough to wire a coronary without thrombus forming on the equipment.
  • 🧭 Guide catheter: for the RCA (right system) → a JR4 (or Amplatz-left / AL1 for a superior take-off); the guide gives back-up support to push equipment across the proximal occlusion.
  • 📟 Venous access: secure femoral (or internal jugular) venous access for a transvenous temporary pacing wire if high-grade AV block emerges.

S5

🔬 Lesion Assessment — Physiology & Imaging

🫀 In a STEMI, physiology waitsThe culprit is an acutely occluded proximal RCA — you do NOT need FFR to prove it needs treating. Reperfuse the culprit NOW. Physiology (FFR/iFR) belongs to the non-culprit lesions and to stable disease. Imaging (IVUS/OCT) may still guide sizing and mechanism, but in a hypotensive RV infarct keep contrast and adenosine to a minimum.

📏 FFR / iFR — the physiology of a stenosis (for stable / non-culprit disease)

  • FFR (fractional flow reserve) — the ratio of maximal flow across a stenosis to normal, measured with a pressure wire during hyperaemia (adenosine). FFR ≤ 0.80 = ischaemic → treat. Above 0.80 → defer safely.
  • iFR (instantaneous wave-free ratio) — a resting index, no adenosine. iFR ≤ 0.89 = significant. Useful when adenosine-induced vasodilation would worsen an already hypotensive patient.

🎯 FFR ≤ 0.80 → the stenosis limits flow enough to cause ischaemia → treat. > 0.80 → defer and treat medically (DEFER, FAME). In acute STEMI, stage the bystander lesion (COMPLETE).

🖥️ Intravascular imaging — IVUS vs OCT

🔵 IVUS

  • Ultrasound — deep penetration, sizes the vessel, quantifies plaque burden, sees behind calcium
  • No contrast flush needed — attractive in a hypotensive RV-infarct patient where you are limiting contrast

🟠 OCT

  • Light — ultra-high resolution; superb for edge dissection, thrombus, apposition, and plaque erosion vs rupture vs SCAD
  • Needs a contrast flush; less penetration
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Do not FFR the culprit — it is occluded; you already know. Save physiology for the bystander lesions, and in a shocky RV infarct prefer IVUS so you are not adding contrast and adenosine to a failing right heart."

🛠️ Intervention — Step-by-Step Execution

🎈 Balloon angioplasty & stent deployment. Source: Wikimedia Commons (Blausen)

🪜 The sequence — each step is a decision

  1. 🧵 Wire the RCA — a workhorse guidewire across the proximal occlusion into the distal true lumen (confirm distal position, not the RV marginal or a subintimal track).
  2. 🫧 Restore flow — cross with a small balloon; if a heavy thrombus burden is present, selective aspiration thrombectomy may be considered — but it is NOT routine (TOTAL/TASTE). A whiff of flow relieves ischaemia and lets you size.
  3. 📟 Watch the rhythm on reperfusion — opening the proximal RCA can trigger bradycardia/AV block; have atropine and the pacing wire ready as flow returns.
  4. 📏 Lesion prep & sizing — the RCA is often a large-calibre vessel; size the stent 1:1 to the reference diameter (frequently 3.5–4.0 mm) using IVUS or online QCA.
  5. 🩹 Deploy a drug-eluting stent, then post-dilate with a non-compliant balloon to full expansion/apposition (under-expansion is the #1 cause of stent thrombosis/restenosis).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The RCA is a big pipe — size to it. Under-size the stent in a 4 mm RCA and you build a future thrombosis; the proximal segment is also where reperfusion bradyarrhythmias are born, so keep atropine on the tray."

🏠 Post-Procedure Care, Debrief & Assessment

🩹 Access-site & rhythm care

  • 🖐️ Radial: patent-haemostasis compression band, monitor for radial artery occlusion & bleeding.
  • 📟 Keep a transvenous pacing wire in situ if AV block persisted; most reperfusion-related block resolves within hours-to-days as the nodal artery recovers — reassess before removing.
  • 💧 Continue judicious preload management: fluids for the RV, but watch for LV filling and pulmonary oedema once the RV recovers — reintroduce nitrates/diuretics only when haemodynamics allow.

💊 Secondary prevention (start day 1)

  • DAPT — aspirin + a P2Y₁₂ inhibitor (ticagrelor/prasugrel preferred) for 12 months (individualise by bleeding risk).
  • High-intensity statin (atorvastatin 80 mg) regardless of LDL; add ezetimibe/PCSK9i to target.
  • Beta-blocker — introduce with caution and only once the bradyarrhythmia has resolved and the patient is haemodynamically stable; ACE inhibitor and MRA if EF ≤ 40% with HF/diabetes.
  • 🏃 Cardiac rehab, smoking cessation, risk-factor control; echo at 24–48 h for LV/RV function; ICD reassessment at ≥ 40 days if EF ≤ 35%.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Do not rush the beta-blocker in an inferior/RV infarct with bradycardia — wait for the node to recover. The stent fixed the plumbing; the pills and the rehab fix the patient."

🔭 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

  • ➡️ RV infarct physiology: the non-compliant, ischaemic RV cannot generate forward flow, so cardiac output becomes preload-dependent. A vicious cycle follows — RV dilates → the interventricular septum bows into the LV → the septum's contribution to RV output is lost AND the LV is underfilled → systemic hypotension → worse coronary perfusion → more RV ischaemia. Nitrates/diuretics/aggressive PEEP break the cycle in the WRONG direction.
  • 💧 Haemodynamic support: volume loading first, but a distended RV can worsen septal shift — titrate to response, add dobutamine/inotrope if fluid-refractory, and maintain AV synchrony (atrial or dual-chamber pacing beats ventricular pacing in RV infarct because the atrial kick fills the stiff RV).
  • 📟 Bradyarrhythmia & conduction: proximal RCA supplies the SA node (~60%) and AV node (~90% in right-dominant); block is usually nodal (narrow escape, atropine-responsive, transient) — contrast with the wide, unreliable infranodal block of an anterior MI.
  • 🫧 Thrombus & distal protection: the RCA often carries a large thrombus burden; routine aspiration is not recommended (TOTAL/TASTE), but selective aspiration, deferred stenting, or intracoronary GP IIb/IIIa can be considered in massive thrombus.
  • 🫀 Mechanical support in RV shock: for refractory RV failure consider RV-specific support (Impella RP, ProtekDuo) or VA-ECMO — and beware "north-south" / LV-underfilling nuances when combining devices.
  • 🖥️ Imaging-guided PCI: IVUS is attractive in the RCA to size a large vessel and limit contrast in a hypotensive patient; OCT distinguishes plaque rupture vs erosion vs SCAD.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Nitrate That Emptied the Tank"

The case: A 61-year-old arrived with inferior ST elevation and a BP of 96/60. He was in pain, so the team gave sublingual GTN and then a small IV nitrate infusion "to settle the chest pain" — and, seeing a raised JVP, added a dose of IV furosemide for presumed heart failure. Nobody recorded right-sided leads. Within two minutes his BP collapsed to 50/30 and he lost consciousness; he was a proximal-RCA RV infarct whose entire output depended on preload. The nitrate and the diuretic emptied a tank the right ventricle could not refill. Fluids and pressors were started too late; he arrested on the way to the lab and could not be brought back.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

➡️ NO V4RRight-sided leads never recorded — the RV infarct was invisible to the team.
💨 NITRATE GIVENA preload reducer in a preload-dependent RV dropped the BP off a cliff.
💧 DIURETIC GIVENThe raised JVP was misread as LV failure; furosemide emptied the tank further.
🫁 CLEAR LUNGS IGNOREDHypotension with CLEAR lungs should have screamed RIGHT-sided failure.
⏱️ LATE RESCUEFluids/pressors and reperfusion came after the arrest, not before.

🛡️ What would have saved him: record V4R in EVERY inferior STEMI; the instant you see RV involvement, ban nitrates, morphine-heavy sedation and diuretics, run fluid to fill the RV, treat bradycardia with atropine/pacing, add an inotrope if fluid-refractory, and get the RCA open fast — the RV recovers when its artery does. This is the drill in S6.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Hypotension + clear lungs + raised JVP after an inferior MI is RV infarct until proven otherwise — fill the tank, never empty it. A nitrate here is not a treatment, it is a mechanism of death."
