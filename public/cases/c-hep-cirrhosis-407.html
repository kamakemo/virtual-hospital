🧭 The three questions you answer at the bedside first

In any decompensated cirrhotic your reflex is: (1) Is there infection? (tap, cultures, CXR, urine — SBP is present in ~10–25% of ascitic admissions and is frequently silent). (2) Is there a bleed? (melaena, drop in Hb — varices). (3) Is the kidney failing? (creatinine trend — pre-renal vs ATN vs HRS). All three are precipitants of decompensation and all three can be lethal within hours.

⚠️ The trap of "no abdominal pain, so it is not SBP"Up to a third of SBP is asymptomatic — no fever, no pain, no rebound. It may present only as worsening encephalopathy, a rising creatinine, or unexplained hypotension. That is precisely why the tap is mandatory in every admission, not just the tender ones.

"Ascites did not just 'get worse' by itself. Something tipped him over. So: diagnostic tap now, cultures, lactate, hold the diuretics, hold the beta-blocker while he is hypotensive, and give albumin because his creatinine is climbing."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🧪 Diagnostic ascitic tap NOW — cell count & differential, culture (into blood-culture bottles at the bedside), albumin/protein for SAAG. Neutrophils ≥ 250/mm³ = SBP.
  2. 🩸 Blood cultures ×2, lactate, FBC, U&E, LFTs, INR, CRP, venous gas; urine dip & culture; CXR.
  3. 💧 Stop diuretics (spironolactone + furosemide) and any nephrotoxin while creatinine is rising.
  4. 💉 Human albumin — volume expansion to protect the kidney; the cornerstone of both SBP treatment and HRS work-up.
  5. 💊 If the tap confirms (or strongly suggests) SBP → empirical IV cefotaxime without waiting for culture.
  6. 🩸 Hold carvedilol tonight — non-selective beta-blockers are dangerous in SBP/hypotension/HRS (see S11).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In cirrhosis, treat the kidney with albumin, not with more saline and never with more diuretic — the problem is effective hypovolaemia inside a total-body-fluid overload, so you refill the arterial tree, not the peritoneum."
🧪 Resident's self-testHis INR is 1.8 and platelets 68. Should she transfuse FFP/platelets before the diagnostic tap?

No. A diagnostic paracentesis is very low risk (major bleeding < 1%). Guidelines (AASLD/EASL) do not recommend routine correction of INR or platelets before a diagnostic or therapeutic tap. The conventional INR poorly reflects the "rebalanced" haemostasis of cirrhosis. Delaying or skipping the tap to "fix the clotting" causes far more harm (missed SBP) than the tap ever would.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY ascites, WHY the failing kidney (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧱 Fibrosis → portal hypertension: a scarred, nodular liver resists blood flow. Portal pressure rises (hepatic venous pressure gradient > 10 mmHg = clinically significant; > 12 mmHg = variceal/ascites risk).
  2. 🌬️ Splanchnic vasodilation: shear stress and portal hypertension drive nitric oxide release → the splanchnic arterioles dilate. Blood pools in the gut circulation.
  3. 🫗 Effective arterial hypovolaemia: despite total-body fluid overload, the arterial tree is underfilled. The body senses "low volume."
  4. 🧪 Neurohormonal storm: baroreceptors fire → RAAS + sympathetic + ADH all activate → the kidney avidly retains sodium and water and constricts its own vessels.
  5. 💧 Ascites: retained fluid + portal hypertension + low oncotic pressure (hypoalbuminaemia) push fluid into the peritoneum faster than it can be reabsorbed.
  6. 🩸 Hepatorenal syndrome: as vasodilation worsens (often tipped by SBP), renal vasoconstriction becomes extreme. The kidney is structurally normal but functionally shut down — a "kidney strangled by the circulation," not by intrinsic damage.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Ascites and hepatorenal syndrome are the SAME lie told twice: the body thinks it is empty when it is drowning. That is why the cure is to refill the artery (albumin, terlipressin), not to empty the patient (diuretics, saline)."

⚖️ Contrast pair — SBP vs Secondary bacterial peritonitis

🦠 Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis

  • Single organism (usually E. coli, Klebsiella, or pneumococcus); no surgical source
  • Neutrophils ≥ 250/mm³; protein usually low
  • Treat medically: cefotaxime + albumin

🔪 Secondary (surgical) peritonitis

  • Perforation/abscess — polymicrobial
  • Very high neutrophils, high protein, low glucose, high LDH (Runyon criteria)
  • Needs imaging + surgery — antibiotics alone will fail

💡 If SBP "does not respond" to antibiotics, or the fluid looks like secondary peritonitis, image the abdomen — you may be treating a perforation as if it were SBP.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (21:55)

  • 🧪 Diagnostic ascitic tap NOW — cell count & differential, protein + albumin (for SAAG), culture inoculated into blood-culture bottles at the bedside, Gram stain, glucose/LDH if secondary peritonitis suspected.
  • 🩸 Blood cultures ×2 (before antibiotics), lactate, FBC, U&E, LFTs, INR, CRP, venous gas, group & save.
  • 💊 IV cefotaxime 2 g (or per local resistance — piperacillin-tazobactam if nosocomial/recent quinolones) if SBP confirmed or strongly suspected.
  • 💉 Human albumin 20% — 1.5 g/kg on day 1 and 1 g/kg on day 3 for SBP (renal protection).
  • 💧 Hold spironolactone & furosemide; hold carvedilol while hypotensive/septic; stop any NSAID/nephrotoxin.
  • 🚽 Lactulose to keep bowels open (encephalopathy prophylaxis); daily weights, strict fluid balance, urinary catheter for accurate output.
  • 🍽️ No-added-salt diet (≈ 80–90 mmol Na/day); avoid fluid restriction unless Na < 125.
  • 📞 Escalate to hepatology + critical care if creatinine keeps rising or encephalopathy/airway threatened.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Cultures before antibiotics, albumin alongside the antibiotic, and diuretics OFF while the kidney is angry — three cheap decisions that decide whether he leaves on his feet."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Ward priorities — the nurse guards the fluid balance and the airway

  • ⚖️ Daily weight, strict input/output, hourly urine via catheter — the creatinine and urine output are the HRS early-warning system.
  • 🧠 Screen for encephalopathy each round — ask for the flap, track orientation and sleep pattern; a new flap or drowsiness means escalate and protect the airway.
  • 🌡️ Full observations 4-hourly; a swing back to hypotension or a new fever spike means re-culture and re-assess for progression of SBP.
  • 💉 Run albumin as prescribed and time it with the antibiotic; document the ascitic sample times and volumes precisely.
  • 🚽 Titrate lactulose to 2–3 soft stools/day; too little under-treats, too much dehydrates and worsens the kidney.
  • 🩸 Watch for melaena/haematemesis (varices) and for any bleeding at the tap site; report new confusion, reducing urine, or a rising creatinine immediately.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In cirrhosis the nurse's charts ARE the diagnosis: falling urine output and a creeping creatinine are hepatorenal syndrome announcing itself long before the registrar rounds."

🩻 Adjunct imaging

  • Abdominal ultrasound with Dopplers: confirms ascites and marks a safe tap site; assesses portal-vein patency (portal-vein thrombosis is a decompensation trigger) and screens for HCC.
  • CT abdomen: if secondary peritonitis, perforation, or HCC is suspected, or SBP fails to respond.
  • Renal ultrasound: excludes obstruction and structural kidney disease as part of the HRS work-up.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📈 Creatinine + urine output — the HRS trajectory. Daily (or 12-hourly) creatinine; hourly urine via catheter. A doubling despite albumin is type-1 HRS declaring itself.
  • 🧠 Encephalopathy grade each shift — asterixis, orientation, drowsiness; a rising grade threatens the airway and signals an untreated precipitant.
  • 🌡️ Temperature & haemodynamics — response to antibiotics; a repeat tap at 48 h should show the neutrophil count falling by > 25% (if not, think resistant organism or secondary peritonitis).
  • 🩸 Hb + signs of GI bleed — varices remain a competing decompensation trigger.
  • 🧪 Sodium & potassium — hyponatraemia (do not over-correct) and hyperkalaemia (spironolactone) both bite.
  • ⚖️ Daily weight & abdominal girth — the ascites response, and a guard against over-diuresis later.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Do a repeat diagnostic tap at 48 hours: a neutrophil count that has not fallen by a quarter means your antibiotic is failing or you are treating the wrong peritonitis."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong

  • 🩸 Type-1 hepatorenal syndrome — rapid doubling of creatinine, often triggered by SBP; without terlipressin + albumin (or transplant) it is frequently fatal.
  • 🧠 Hepatic encephalopathy — precipitated by the infection; can progress to coma and airway compromise.
  • 🩸 Variceal haemorrhage — a competing, immediately lethal decompensation.
  • 🫁 Hepatic hydrothorax / respiratory splinting from tense ascites; spontaneous bacterial empyema is the pleural analogue of SBP.
  • 💉 Post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction after large-volume taps without albumin → renal impairment and hyponatraemia.
  • 🦠 Recurrent/secondary infection & sepsis; SBP recurs in ~70% within a year without prophylaxis.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"SBP and HRS are chained together: the infection is usually the hand that pushes the kidney off the cliff — treat the SBP fast and you have half-treated the HRS."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Discharge & secondary prevention — two worlds

🫄 This patient (post-SBP, post-HRS)

  • Lifelong secondary SBP prophylaxis (norfloxacin/ciprofloxacin)
  • No-added-salt diet; lowest effective diuretic dose with home weights
  • Lactulose ± rifaximin; alcohol abstinence support
  • Variceal surveillance/banding; NSBB restarted only when haemodynamically safe
  • Transplant assessment; HCC surveillance US 6-monthly
  • Red flags: fever, confusion, black stools, less urine, rapid swelling → return immediately

🟢 The stable, compensated cirrhotic

  • Diet + diuretic-controlled ascites, no infection, stable creatinine
  • Surveillance endoscopy + 6-monthly HCC ultrasound
  • Vaccinations, avoid NSAIDs & hepatotoxins, alcohol abstinence
  • Clear "come back if…" safety-net; annual severity scoring
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"One episode of SBP earns lifelong prophylaxis and a transplant conversation — because the next SBP without them recurs in most patients within a year and often brings HRS with it."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🩸 ICA-AKI & the new HRS nomenclature: the old "HRS type 1/2" is replaced by HRS-AKI (acute, was type 1) and HRS-NAKI (non-acute, was type 2, the driver of refractory ascites). Diagnosis uses the ICA-AKI criteria (rise ≥ 26.5 µmol/L in 48 h or ≥ 1.5× baseline).
  • 🧪 Terlipressin nuance (CONFIRM trial): improves HRS reversal but carries respiratory-failure and ischaemic risk — patient selection matters, and continuous infusion may reduce adverse events versus boluses.
  • 🔧 TIPS (transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt): for refractory ascites and secondary variceal prophylaxis; decompresses portal pressure but risks precipitating/worsening encephalopathy and needs adequate liver reserve.
  • 🤖 Long-term albumin (ANSWER trial) & the "cirrhosis is systemic inflammation" paradigm (PICD/ACLF): acute-on-chronic liver failure is driven by systemic inflammation and organ failures (CLIF-C, CANONIC study); grading ACLF guides ICU/transplant decisions.
  • 🧫 Antibiotic resistance in SBP: nosocomial and quinolone-exposed SBP increasingly involves resistant/ESBL organisms — broaden empirical cover (piperacillin-tazobactam, carbapenem) per local ecology and de-escalate on cultures.
  • 🧬 Hyponatraemia & vaptans: hypervolaemic hyponatraemia of cirrhosis is a poor-prognosis marker; correct slowly (osmotic demyelination risk), and remember MELD-Na incorporates sodium for transplant prioritisation.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Tap That Waited Until Morning"

The case: A 62-year-old woman with alcohol-related cirrhosis was admitted at 23:00 with worsening ascites and mild confusion. She was afebrile and "not that tender," so the on-call team labelled it "decompensation for diuretic review" and planned a tap on the morning list. Her INR was 2.1, which the team felt "made the tap risky." Overnight her urine output fell and her creatinine rose from 90 to 190. The tap, finally done at 11:00 the next day, showed 950 neutrophils — florid, silent SBP. She was already in type-1 hepatorenal syndrome. Despite terlipressin and albumin she progressed to multi-organ failure and died four days later.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Just decompensation" label stuck; afebrile + soft abdomen falsely reassured — SBP is often silent.
🧪 TAP DEFERREDThe one mandatory test was pushed to the morning list.
🩸 INR MYTHA raised INR wrongly treated as a contraindication to a safe diagnostic tap.
💧 KIDNEY IGNOREDRising creatinine not acted on; no albumin, diuretics not stopped.
⏱️ TIME LOST12 hours between the SBP being present and being treated — long enough for HRS to become irreversible.

🛡️ What would have saved her: tap every ascitic admission on arrival regardless of fever, tenderness or clotting; treat neutrophils ≥ 250 as SBP immediately with cefotaxime and albumin; act on the rising creatinine at once (stop diuretics/nephrotoxins, give albumin); and start terlipressin the moment HRS-AKI is confirmed. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 7.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'We will tap him in the morning' has killed more cirrhotics than any bug. The needle takes twenty minutes; the SBP you miss takes a life."
