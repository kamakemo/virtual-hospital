🧭 The three questions that shape every HFrEF visit

Before any drug change, answer: (1) Is he congested? (treat volume with diuretics — they relieve symptoms but do not change mortality). (2) Is he perfusing / what's the BP, HR, K⁺, eGFR? (the headroom for pillars). (3) Which of the four pillars is he missing or below target? (the prognosis-changing work). Today: dry, warm, good numbers, three pillars missing — a green light.

⚠️ The trap of "he's compensated, don't rock the boat"Diuretics make patients feel better but do nothing for survival; the four pillars make patients live longer but are the ones most often deferred for fear of a small BP or creatinine wobble. Treating only symptoms while skipping the pillars is the central error of chronic HF care.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY each pillar works (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧬 Neurohormonal vicious cycle: a fall in cardiac output activates the RAAS (angiotensin II → vasoconstriction, aldosterone) and the sympathetic nervous system (catecholamines). Adaptive for minutes, these drive fibrosis, adverse remodelling, arrhythmia and salt/water retention for months — the heart is poisoned by its own compensations.
  2. 1️⃣ ARNI / RAAS inhibition: blocks angiotensin II and inhibits neprilysin, so protective natriuretic peptides survive longer → vasodilation, natriuresis, anti-fibrosis. ARNI beats ACEi (PARADIGM-HF) by adding the neprilysin arm.
  3. 2️⃣ Beta-blocker: shields the myocardium from chronic sympathetic overdrive → less remodelling, lower heart rate, fewer arrhythmic deaths, recovered EF over months.
  4. 3️⃣ MRA: blocks aldosterone-driven myocardial/vascular fibrosis and Na⁺ retention (and K⁺/Mg²⁺ wasting that provokes arrhythmia).
  5. 4️⃣ SGLT2 inhibitor: glucose-independent — promotes natriuresis/osmotic diuresis, improves myocardial energetics (ketone use), reduces preload/afterload and cardiac inflammation.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"HFrEF is a neurohormonal disease, so the cure is neurohormonal: block RAAS (ARNI), block sympathetics (β-blocker), block aldosterone (MRA), and unload/reprogram metabolism (SGLT2i). Four blocks, four independent lives saved."

⚖️ Contrast pair — ARNI vs ACEi, and "old triple" vs "new four-pillar"

🕰️ ACEi & "old" triple therapy

  • ACEi/ARB + beta-blocker + MRA
  • Blocks RAAS + sympathetics + aldosterone
  • Titrated slowly, one drug at a time
  • Misses neprilysin benefit and the SGLT2i pillar

🆕 ARNI & the four-pillar era

  • ARNI (or ACEi/ARB) + beta-blocker + MRA + SGLT2i
  • Adds neprilysin inhibition (more natriuretic peptide) & the metabolic pillar
  • Started early, together, low-dose → up-titrate (STRONG-HF)
  • The washout matters: never an ARNI within 36 h of an ACEi

💡 The shift is not just "one more drug" — it is start-low-and-simultaneous replacing the old slow, sequential single-drug titration, plus preferring the ARNI and adding the SGLT2i.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The optimisation order set (10:25)

  • 🍬 Start dapagliflozin 10 mg OD (no titration; counsel on sick-day rules, genital hygiene, small early eGFR "dip" that is expected).
  • 🧂 Start spironolactone 25 mg OD (K⁺ 4.3, eGFR 62 → safe); recheck U&E/K⁺ in 1–2 weeks.

  • ⏫ Up-titrate bisoprolol 1.25 → 2.5 mg now, then double every 2 weeks toward 10 mg as HR/BP allow.
  • 💧 Furosemide 40 mg PRN for congestion only (symptom control, not prognosis) — reassess dose as SGLT2i/ARNI add diuretic effect.
  • 🩸 Treat iron deficiency: IV ferric carboxymaltose (ferritin 60, TSAT 16%) — improves symptoms/QoL and reduces HF hospitalisation.
  • 🧪 Labs: U&E/K⁺/eGFR at 1–2 weeks and after each up-titration; NT-proBNP to track response; BP & HR at every visit.
  • 🗓️ Book early follow-up (~1–2 weeks) — STRONG-HF style — NOT six months. Reassess EF at ≥3 months for ICD/CRT.

S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ HF-nurse priorities — the engine of up-titration

  • ⚖️ Daily weights & a symptom diary — a 2 kg gain over 2–3 days flags early congestion before the next admission; teach the patient to self-report.
  • 🧪 Nurse-led lab checks & titration — many services run protocolised up-titration clinics; the nurse chases K⁺/eGFR/BP/HR and moves doses, which is how STRONG-HF-style intensity actually happens.
  • 💊 Medicines counselling: why four drugs and not one; expect a small BP/eGFR dip; sick-day rules for SGLT2i (hold in acute dehydration/illness, watch euglycaemic DKA); never stop the beta-blocker abruptly.
  • 🧂 MRA teaching: report muscle cramps/palpitations, avoid potassium-based salt substitutes and NSAIDs; know the K⁺ recheck schedule.
  • 🩺 ARNI washout safety: confirm the ramipril was truly stopped 36 h before the first ARNI dose; counsel to seek help for any lip/tongue swelling (angioedema).
  • 🏃 Rehab & lifestyle: cardiac rehab, fluid/salt advice, smoking/alcohol, vaccination, and psychological support — adherence is the hidden fifth pillar.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse-led up-titration clinic is where lives are actually saved in HFrEF — the prescription is only a promise until someone chases the doses to target and keeps the patient adherent."

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch during optimisation and why

  • 🧪 U&E / K⁺ / eGFR 1–2 weeks after starting/up-titrating an ARNI or MRA — the commonest, most manageable signals (hyperkalaemia, creatinine rise).
  • 🩸 BP & HR at every step — they set how far you can push the ARNI (BP) and the beta-blocker (HR); asymptomatic low-normal values are usually tolerated.
  • ⚖️ Weight & congestion — daily weights, symptom diary; adjust the loop diuretic, not the pillars, for volume.
  • 🧬 NT-proBNP trend — falling levels track successful neurohormonal blockade and reverse remodelling.
  • 🖥️ EF re-assessment at ≥3 months of optimal GDMT — decides ICD (EF ≤ 35%) and CRT (broad LBBB) eligibility.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you write the four pillars, the monitor's question is not 'is he stable?' but 'can I push each dose closer to target this week?' — the target dose is where the trials found the benefit."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — of therapy and of under-therapy

  • 🧂 Hyperkalaemia (ARNI + MRA) — recheck K⁺; manage with diet, K⁺ binders, dose-adjust — rarely a reason to abandon the pillar entirely.
  • 🩸 Symptomatic hypotension — de-prioritise/spread dosing, reduce the loop diuretic first, keep the prognostic drugs.
  • 😮‍💨 ARNI angioedema — especially if the 36-h ACEi washout is skipped; stop the drug, treat, never re-challenge.
  • 🍬 SGLT2i: euglycaemic DKA (sick-day rules), genital/urinary infection, volume depletion.
  • ⚡ Arrhythmia / sudden cardiac death — the reason EF ≤ 35% after optimal GDMT earns a primary-prevention ICD.
  • 🕳️ The silent complication — therapeutic inertia: the biggest "complication" of all is the pillar never started and the dose never up-titrated → recurrent admissions and premature death (S21).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The deadliest complication in chronic HFrEF isn't a side-effect — it's the drug that was never given because someone feared a manageable side-effect. Manage the wobble; keep the pillar."

ARNI started after 36-h washout; bisoprolol at 3.75 mg. K⁺ 4.6, eGFR 58 (expected small dip). BP 108/68 — asymptomatic, pillars kept.
Week 6
Bisoprolol 7.5 mg, ARNI 49/51 BD, spironolactone 50 mg. NYHA II, orthopnoea gone, NT-proBNP 720.

🏛️ Four pillars, early & togetherARNI + beta-blocker + MRA + SGLT2i, started low and simultaneously, then up-titrated — each an independent life saved.
⏳ Every delayed pillar is a stolen yearComprehensive therapy roughly halves mortality vs ACEi-alone and adds years of life.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the four pillars

  • 🧬 Neurohormonal mechanisms, pillar by pillar: RAAS/ARNI (angiotensin blockade + neprilysin inhibition preserving natriuretic peptides — offset by the angioedema-prone bradykinin overlap that mandates the ACEi washout); sympathetic/beta-blockade (reversing catecholamine-driven remodelling and down-regulated β-receptors); aldosterone/MRA (anti-fibrotic, anti-arrhythmic via K⁺/Mg²⁺ retention); SGLT2/natriuresis (osmotic diuresis, improved cardiac energetics via ketone utilisation, reduced epicardial inflammation) — four distinct pathways, hence additive benefit.
  • 🔀 Sequencing debate vs simultaneous low-dose strategy: the historic slow, sequential single-drug titration is being replaced by early simultaneous initiation at low doses (McMurray/Packer "accelerated" schemes) then rapid up-titration; STRONG-HF validated intensive early titration with close follow-up. Getting all four on board in weeks beats months of getting one perfect.
  • 🖥️ Device eligibility after GDMT: reassess EF at ≥3 months of optimal therapy — primary-prevention ICD if EF ≤ 35% (esp. ischaemic, NYHA II–III, >1 yr expected survival); CRT if EF ≤ 35% with LBBB and QRS ≥ 130–150 ms; CRT-D combines both. Many hearts reverse-remodel enough on GDMT to change the decision.
  • 🩸 Iron & comorbidity: IV iron (ferric carboxymaltose/derisomaltose) for iron deficiency (ferritin < 100, or 100–299 with TSAT < 20%) improves symptoms/QoL and cuts HF hospitalisation — treat AF, sleep-disordered breathing, obesity, diabetes, CKD.
  • 🩶 HFmrEF (EF 41–49%): SGLT2i recommended; the other pillars "may be considered" on weaker evidence — a genuine grey zone where trials pooled mildly-reduced and preserved EF.
  • 🚑 When to refer to advanced HF: recurrent admissions, escalating diuretics, intolerance of GDMT, persistent NYHA III–IV, low output, arrhythmia burden ("I-NEED-HELP" markers) → transplant / LVAD / palliative pathways. Refer early, before end-organ failure closes the options.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Silent Undertreatment"

The case: A man with an EF of 25% after a large anterior MI was started on ramipril alone. Over four years he passed through successive clinics and GP reviews. His ramipril was never up-titrated past a low dose. He was never started on a beta-blocker to target, never given an MRA, never trialled an SGLT2 inhibitor, and never switched to an ARNI. Each visit read the same: "stable, continue current medication, review in six months." He was admitted five times with decompensation — each time "tweaked and sent home." He died at 61 of progressive pump failure. Not one clinician had prescribed a wrong drug; the tragedy was the drugs he was never given.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🕳️ THERAPEUTIC INERTIA"Stable, continue" repeated for years — a comfortable patient mistaken for a treated one.
📉 NEVER UP-TITRATEDLow-dose ACEi and no beta-blocker to target — sub-target doses lose most of the trial benefit.
🧱 MISSING PILLARSNo MRA, no SGLT2i, never an ARNI — three of four pillars simply absent.
💧 SYMPTOMS ONLYDiuretics chased congestion; nobody touched prognosis.
🗓️ NO SYSTEMNo up-titration clinic, no lab schedule, no ownership of the plan between admissions.

🛡️ What would have saved him: read EF ≤ 40% as a mandate for all four pillars, started early and together; drive each toward target with scheduled K⁺/eGFR/BP/HR checks; add the MRA and SGLT2i that were sitting there unclaimed; switch to an ARNI (with the washout); and own the up-titration in a nurse-led clinic instead of "review in six months." Comprehensive four-pillar therapy would have roughly halved his mortality and kept him out of hospital. This is exactly the discipline applied to the patient in Bed 2.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In HFrEF the fatal error is rarely the wrong drug — it's the right drugs never given and never up-titrated. 'Stable, continue, review in six months' has killed more patients than any single prescription."
