📜 Past Medical History — why this infarct became shock

  • 🩸 Type 2 diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia, 30 pack-year smoker — the substrate that grew the plaque that ruptured.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Cardiogenic shock is not 'low blood pressure after an MI' — it is hypoperfusion of every organ despite adequate filling. The cold skin, the silent kidney and the rising lactate are the diagnosis, not the cuff."

🧬 Why THIS patient? — big infarct + late flow + multivessel disease

Cardiogenic shock complicates roughly 5–10% of acute MIs and remains the leading cause of in-hospital MI death. The arithmetic is brutal: a large anterior infarct kills enough myocardium that stroke volume collapses; cardiac output falls; the body clamps down with vasoconstriction (cold periphery) and tachycardia, but the failing ventricle cannot keep up. Low output starves every organ — including the coronaries themselves, which are perfused by that same falling pressure. Ischemia begets more ischemia. Add multivessel disease and a late, low-flow presentation, and the spiral is already turning by the time he reaches your bed.

🔥 Risk factors for cardiogenic shock after MI 🫀 Large / anterior infarct · ⏱️ Late presentation, delayed reperfusion · 🩸 Multivessel coronary disease · 👴 Older age · 💔 Prior MI / low baseline EF · 🩺 Diabetes, CKD · ⚙️ Mechanical complications (VSD, papillary muscle / free-wall rupture) · 💉 Right ventricular infarction

🧭 The single most important question: is this PUMP failure — and how deep is the spiral?

Confirm the shock is cardiogenic (cold, congested, high filling pressures) and not septic / hypovolemic / obstructive (S14). Then stage it with the SCAI shock classification: A (at risk) → B (beginning/pre-shock: hypotensive or tachycardic but still perfusing) → C (classic: hypoperfusion needing pressors/inotropes) → D (deteriorating despite initial support) → E (extremis: refractory, arresting). Mr. A.K. is hypotensive, oliguric, cold and lactate 4.6 on no support yet — SCAI Stage C, sliding toward D.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The cardiology resident arrives at 04:54. Her reasoning, out loud:

🧠 Name the physiology first, then act

"Big anterior MI, EF 25%, cold-and-wet, lactate climbing, kidney shut. That is cardiogenic shock — the pump has failed and every organ is downstream of it. The culprit is open, which is the single most important thing we can do for mortality (SHOCK trial). So the job now is to break the spiral: restore enough perfusion pressure to feed the brain, kidneys and the coronaries, give the weak ventricle inotropic help, and unload the wet lungs — without flooding him and without reaching reflexively for a balloon pump that doesn't change mortality."

🎯 Immediate priorities (her actual order of operations)

  1. 🩹 Confirm the culprit is open and STOP there: do NOT proceed to immediate multivessel PCI tonight. CULPRIT-SHOCK showed culprit-lesion-only PCI lowered death/renal-replacement vs immediate complete revascularization. Stage the other lesions later.
  2. 💉 Start norepinephrine first-line to a MAP target ~65 mmHg — fewer arrhythmias and lower mortality than dopamine in the shock subgroup (De Backer).
  3. 💪 Add an inotrope (dobutamine, or milrinone) once pressure is supported, to actually improve contractility and forward flow — the pressor alone just squeezes a failing pump.
  4. 💧 Be stingy with fluid: lungs are wet, JVP up. A small, monitored bolus only if there's evidence of fluid responsiveness; otherwise diurese/offload once a MAP is established.
  5. 🫁 Support oxygenation/ventilation: NIV or early intubation for the pulmonary edema and rising work of breathing — and to reduce the metabolic cost of breathing.
  6. 🔎 Hunt for a mechanical complication and RV shock: urgent echo for VSD, papillary muscle rupture, free-wall rupture/tamponade, and RV infarction — these have completely different rescue pathways.
  7. 🛟 Escalate support deliberately, not reflexively: if he deteriorates despite drugs, consider Impella or VA-ECMO in selected patients — NOT an automatic IABP.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Open the culprit, support the pressure with norepinephrine, help the pump with an inotrope, keep the lungs dry, and look for a rupture — in that order. Mechanical support is a decision, not a reflex."

🤔 "But shouldn't we just fix all the arteries now and pop in a balloon pump?"

"No — and both of those feel right, which is exactly why they're traps. Routine immediate multivessel PCI in shock increased death and renal failure (CULPRIT-SHOCK), so we treat only the culprit tonight and stage the rest. And the intra-aortic balloon pump, despite decades of habit, did not reduce 30-day or long-term mortality in MI shock (IABP-SHOCK II) — so it is not the answer to 'he's hypotensive, do something mechanical.' If we need mechanical support, the evidence now points to selected use of Impella (DanGer Shock) or VA-ECMO, not a default balloon."

🧪 Resident's self-test (answer before reading on) The intern asks: "He's hypotensive — let's give dopamine, it raises the pressure and supports the heart, right?" Good first pressor here?

No. In the De Backer trial, dopamine caused more arrhythmias overall, and in the cardiogenic shock subgroup it was associated with higher mortality than norepinephrine. Norepinephrine is the first-line vasopressor in cardiogenic shock. Reach for dopamine here and you risk lighting off a tachyarrhythmia in an already ischemic, failing ventricle.

🌀 What is the 'death spiral' — and where is he on it?

The spiral is a self-amplifying loop: infarct → LV pump failure → low cardiac output → systemic AND coronary hypoperfusion → worsening ischemia + neurohormonal & inflammatory activation → further pump failure → deeper hypotension. Each turn makes the next turn easier. Breaking it requires restoring coronary flow (done — culprit open) and supporting perfusion (pressor + inotrope ± mechanical support) fast enough to interrupt the loop before multi-organ failure becomes irreversible. The SCAI stage is the speedometer: he is Stage C now; if the lactate keeps climbing on support he is Stage D, and the conversation turns to mechanical circulatory support.

📖 Teaching Points

🔬 The mechanism — WHY an infarct becomes a spiral (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧱 The hit: a large infarct destroys or stuns enough contractile muscle (often > 40% of the LV) that stroke volume collapses. The pump can no longer generate forward flow.
  2. 📉 Output falls: cardiac output = stroke volume × heart rate. The body compensates with tachycardia and intense vasoconstriction — but a failing ventricle pumping against a clamped-down vasculature just works harder for less flow. Hence cold skin and a narrow pulse pressure.
  3. 🫀 The coronary betrayal: the coronaries are perfused by the very pressure that is now falling. Low output → low coronary perfusion → more ischemia → even weaker pump. The organ that needs flow most is starved by its own failure.
  4. 🔥 Neurohormonal & inflammatory amplification: catecholamines, RAAS activation and a systemic inflammatory response (a "SIRS-like" vasoplegia) layer on, raising afterload, retaining fluid (wet lungs), and adding a vasodilatory insult — the spiral now has multiple engines.
  5. 💥 Why it kills: sustained hypoperfusion → rising lactate, acute kidney injury, hepatic and gut ischemia, multi-organ failure. Past a point the loop is self-sustaining even if the artery is open. Breaking the spiral early — flow + perfusion support — is the whole game.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Cardiogenic shock is a wheel that turns faster the longer it spins. Open the culprit to stop feeding it, support the pressure and the pump to slow it, and stage everything else once the wheel is no longer accelerating."

⚖️ Contrast pair #1 — Cardiogenic vs the other shocks (don't mislabel the pump)

❄️ CARDIOGENIC (assume in this patient)

  • Cold, mottled, vasoconstricted periphery
  • Congested: raised JVP, wet lungs, high filling pressures
  • Low cardiac output, narrow pulse pressure
  • Echo: poorly contracting, often dilated LV
  • Fix: revascularize culprit, inotrope + pressor, ± MCS

🔥 SEPTIC / others (NOT this)

  • Septic: warm, vasodilated, high output, wide pulse pressure, fever source
  • Hypovolemic: cold but empty — low filling, flat JVP, responds to fluid
  • Obstructive: tamponade (muffled, pulsus paradoxus) or massive PE (RV strain)
  • Bedside echo + perfusion + filling pressures separate them

😈 The trap: treat cardiogenic as hypovolemic and you flood the lungs; treat it as septic and you chase vasoplegia you don't have. The discriminator is filling pressure: cardiogenic is cold AND congested (high filling), hypovolemic is cold AND empty (low filling). Bedside echo and the JVP settle it in seconds. Remember LV vs RV shock too — an inferior MI with clear lungs, raised JVP and hypotension is RV shock and is fluid-and-flow dependent, the opposite of the wet LV.

⚖️ Contrast pair #2 — Pump failure vs a mechanical complication

🫀 Primary pump failure (tonight)

  • Big infarct, global LV weakness, no new murmur
  • Gradual, predictable hypotension after a large MI
  • Therapy: culprit PCI, pressor + inotrope, offload, ± MCS
  • Echo: large akinetic territory, low EF

⚙️ Mechanical complication

  • New murmur + abrupt collapse (often days 2–7)
  • VSD (harsh holosystolic, step-up on right heart cath), papillary-muscle rupture (acute MR, flash edema), free-wall rupture (tamponade, PEA)
  • Therapy: surgery/device — pressors and MCS only bridge
  • Echo with Doppler is diagnostic — do it for any sudden deterioration

💡 Why it matters: pour inotrope into a ruptured papillary muscle and you treat the symptom while the patient bleeds time. Every shocked post-MI patient who deteriorates or develops a new murmur needs an echo before the next escalation.

🔬 Fellowship Corner — Advanced Topics

  • 🛟 Mechanical circulatory support menu: IABP (afterload reduction, modest — no mortality benefit), Impella (microaxial LV unloading, DanGer-Shock benefit in selected STEMI shock), VA-ECMO (full cardiopulmonary support for biventricular/refractory shock, but increases LV afterload → may need venting/Impella "ECPELLA"), TandemHeart, surgical VAD.
  • 📊 Invasive hemodynamics: the pulmonary artery catheter is back in selected shock — cardiac index, PCWP, and the cardiac power output (CPO = MAP × CO / 451) and PAPi (pulmonary artery pulsatility index for RV failure) guide escalation and weaning.
  • 🫀 RV shock & biventricular failure: inferior MI with RV infarction needs preload optimization, avoidance of excessive PEEP/vasodilators, inotropy, and sometimes RV-specific support (RVAD, ProtekDuo) — the management is the mirror image of LV shock.
  • 🏥 Shock teams & hub-and-spoke systems: protocolized multidisciplinary "cardiogenic shock teams" and early transfer to MCS-capable centers are associated with improved survival in registries (e.g. NCSI).
  • 🧪 Adjuncts & the failing rescue: lactate clearance as a resuscitation target, inotrope choice (dobutamine vs milrinone — DOREMI showed no clear difference), the limited/harmful role of routine vasopressin, and the controversies around steroids and the inflammatory ("vasoplegic") phenotype.
  • ⚙️ Mechanical complications in depth: post-MI VSD (right-heart-cath oxygen step-up, percutaneous vs surgical closure timing), acute MR from papillary-muscle rupture (emergent valve surgery), and contained free-wall rupture / pseudoaneurysm.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ CCU nursing priorities — minute by minute

  • 📟 Continuous arterial BP, ECG and SpO₂. Titrate the norepinephrine to the prescribed MAP target (≈ 65) — and call early if you're chasing the dose upward.
  • 🚽 Hourly urine output is your perfusion gauge. A kidney making < 0.5 mL/kg/h is telling you the organs are still starved — report a falling output as a deterioration, not a nuisance.
  • 🧪 Serial lactate on the prescribed schedule — a clearing lactate means the resuscitation is working; a rising one means escalate.
  • 💉 Vasoactive infusions on dedicated, clearly-labelled lines (ideally central); double-check concentrations — a norepinephrine error is itself dangerous. Never bolus or run two pressors down the same lumen unsafely.
  • 🥶 Re-examine perfusion each round: skin temperature line up the limb, mottling score, capillary refill, mentation. Map the shock visually and trend it.
  • 🫁 Watch the work of breathing: rising rate, frothy sputum, falling sats = pulmonary edema worsening — escalate to NIV/intubation, do not just turn up the oxygen.
  • 🩺 Re-auscultate for a new murmur every shift and report it immediately — it can be the first sign of a mechanical complication.
  • 💧 Be the fluid gatekeeper: question reflex large-volume boluses; these lungs are wet. Escalate any prescription that doesn't fit the cold-and-wet picture.
  • 👨‍👩‍👦 Keep the family informed and supported (see S16) — assign a liaison if staffing allows; shock is frightening and fast-moving for relatives.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who watches the urine, the lactate and the skin is reading the perfusion in real time — those three numbers tell you the shock is breaking before the blood pressure does."

Echo (bedside)EF 25%, large anteroapical akinesis, no VSD/MR seen—Confirms primary pump failure and, crucially tonight, excludes a mechanical complication — re-image if he deteriorates or a murmur appears.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Lactate and mixed-venous oxygen are the dashboard of cardiogenic shock — they tell you whether the pump is delivering oxygen to the organs, which the blood pressure alone never will."
🧠 Why the bedside echo is the most important 'investigation' tonightAn echo in the shocked post-MI patient answers three questions at once: Is the LV the problem? (global vs regional failure), Is there a mechanical complication? (VSD jet, flail leaflet with acute MR, pericardial effusion/tamponade), and Is the RV failing? (RV dilation/hypokinesis in inferior MI). Each answer routes him down a completely different pathway. Do not escalate drugs or devices on a shocked post-MI patient without putting the probe on the chest.

🫀 The presenting ECG — read it like a detective

Anterior STEMI: convex ST elevation in V1–V4 with a compensatory sinus tachycardia. A large anterior territory plus shock physiology is the classic recipe for pump-failure cardiogenic shock.

🫀 Post-PCI imaging summary

  • 🩻 Angiography: proximal LAD occlusion now stented, TIMI 3 flow; significant circumflex and RCA disease deliberately left for staged treatment.
  • 🫀 Echo: EF 25%, large akinetic anteroapical wall, high filling pressures, no VSD or significant MR tonight, RV preserved — primary LV pump failure.
  • 🫁 Lung ultrasound: diffuse B-lines (pulmonary edema) — confirms "wet," argues against large-volume fluid.

💊 Medications

💉 Acute drugs — what, why, and the traps

🚫 The 'avoid / defer' list in acute MI cardiogenic shock❌ Dopamine as first-line pressor (arrhythmia, mortality) · ❌ reflex large-volume fluid in the wet patient · ❌ negative inotropes (beta-blockers) and afterload-lowering agents until perfused · ❌ reflexive IABP as the "mechanical support" answer (IABP-SHOCK II) · ❌ immediate multivessel PCI (CULPRIT-SHOCK). When in doubt, the priorities are an open culprit, a perfusing MAP, and an echo to exclude a rupture.

🏠 Therapy once the shock is broken (this admission, not just tonight)

  • 💙 Initiate/uptitrate HFrEF GDMT (ARNI/ACEi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i) — but only once off pressors and perfusing; this large infarct will leave him with chronic HFrEF.
  • 🩹 Stage revascularization of the circumflex/RCA when stable.
  • 🔌 Reassess for an ICD / device therapy after recovery and re-evaluation of EF (post-MI waiting period applies).
  • 🛟 Wean mechanical support (if used) as cardiac power output and lactate recover; plan device removal and rehab.
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🩸 Invasive arterial MAP continuously — titrate norepinephrine to ≈ 65 mmHg; a falling MAP on a rising dose is a deterioration alarm.
  • 🚽 Hourly urine output — the simplest, most honest perfusion monitor; trending up means the kidney is being fed again.
  • 🧪 Serial lactate and ScvO₂ — clearance = resuscitation working; a stuck or rising lactate = SCAI stage progressing, escalate.
  • 🫁 SpO₂, work of breathing, lung ultrasound — track the pulmonary edema and the response to offloading/ventilation.
  • 📊 Cardiac index / PCWP / cardiac power output (if a PA catheter is placed) — objective forward-flow and filling data to guide inotrope and MCS decisions.
  • 🩺 Serial exam for a new murmur and repeat echo on any deterioration — to catch an evolving mechanical complication early.
  • 📐 Re-stage SCAI (A→E) regularly — the stage drives escalation, MCS timing and prognostic conversations with the family.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you start the pressor, the monitor's question changes from 'is he hypotensive?' to 'is he perfusing?' — and the two numbers that answer it are the urine output and the lactate."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — and the mechanism

  • 📉 Refractory shock / multi-organ failure — the spiral wins despite an open artery and drugs; lactate climbs, kidneys and liver fail. Cue for mechanical support or, if futile, ceilings of care.
  • ⚙️ Mechanical complications — VSD (new harsh murmur, oxygen step-up), papillary-muscle rupture (acute severe MR, flash edema, new murmur), free-wall rupture (tamponade, PEA arrest). Surgical/device emergencies hiding inside "pump failure."
  • 💔 Right ventricular shock — inferior MI with RV infarction: hypotension with clear lungs and a raised JVP; mismanaged by treating it like the wet LV.
  • ⚡ Malignant arrhythmias — VT/VF on ischemic, catecholamine-soaked myocardium (worsened by dopamine); and bradyarrhythmia/AV block in inferior MI.
  • 🩸 Device/procedural complications — limb ischemia, bleeding, hemolysis and access-site problems from Impella/VA-ECMO; the price of mechanical support.
  • 🧠 Hypoperfusion injury — acute kidney injury, ischemic hepatitis, gut ischemia, anoxic brain injury after arrest — the downstream toll of the spiral.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference — "Everything While We're In There"

The case: A 66-year-old man with an anterior STEMI and cardiogenic shock reached the cath lab. The culprit LAD was opened — and then, "to be complete," the team went on to stent the circumflex and RCA in the same sitting. The procedure was long, contrast-heavy, and his pressure sagged throughout. To prop him up someone chose dopamine as the first pressor, and he ran a burst of fast VT mid-case. An intra-aortic balloon pump was inserted reflexively "for support." He left the lab in deeper shock than he arrived, developed acute kidney injury requiring dialysis, and died on day 3 of refractory multi-organ failure. The M&M reviewed three avoidable choices.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🩹 OVER-PCIImmediate multivessel PCI in shock — exactly what CULPRIT-SHOCK showed harms.
💉 WRONG PRESSORDopamine first → tachyarrhythmia and higher mortality (De Backer).
🎈 REFLEX IABPBalloon pump inserted by habit — no mortality benefit (IABP-SHOCK II).
🔎 NO ECHONo bedside echo to stage the pump or exclude a mechanical complication.
🏥 NO SHOCK TEAMNo protocolized escalation or early MCS-hub decision.

🛡️ What would have saved him: open the culprit only and stage the rest (CULPRIT-SHOCK); norepinephrine first, not dopamine; an early echo to confirm the physiology and exclude a rupture; and deliberate, device-matched mechanical support (Impella/VA-ECMO) through a shock team — not a reflex balloon. This is exactly why your nurse held the extra fluid and why you stopped at the culprit tonight.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Do everything while we're in there' is the sentence written on the chart of patients pushed deeper into shock. In MI shock, less in the lab (culprit only) and the right drug (norepinephrine) save more lives than more hardware."

🔀 Differentials

🧩 The shock-after-MI differential

DiagnosisCluesWhy it matters
💔 Primary LV pump failure (this patient)Large infarct, cold & wet, low output + high filling, no new murmur, EF 25% on echoDefault after a big MI; treat with culprit PCI, norepinephrine + inotrope, offload, ± MCS.

🏠 Discharge

📋 Discharge plan & safety net

  • 💊 Medications: dual antiplatelet therapy (duration per stent/bleeding risk), high-intensity statin, and uptitrated HFrEF GDMT (ARNI/ACEi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i) with a clear written schedule and follow-up bloods (renal function, potassium).
  • 🩹 Staged revascularization: remaining RCA lesion booked; cardiology follow-up to complete the plan.
  • 🔌 Device reassessment: repeat echo at ~6–12 weeks; if EF remains ≤ 35% on optimal therapy, reassess for a primary-prevention ICD after the post-MI waiting period.
  • 💙 Cardiac rehabilitation referral, smoking cessation, diabetes and blood-pressure optimization, dietary and fluid/salt education for heart failure.
  • 🚩 Red flags → call emergency services: recurrent chest pain, breathlessness at rest or lying flat, rapid weight gain/swelling, fainting or palpitations, or feeling cold, clammy and "wrung out" again.
  • 📚 Education: what cardiogenic shock and HFrEF mean in plain language, why every medication matters, daily weights, and a clear "what to do if symptoms return" plan; CPR/AED awareness offered to the family.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Surviving cardiogenic shock is the start, not the end — discharge means staged revascularization, optimized heart-failure drugs, an EF reassessment for a device, rehab, and a family who knows the warning signs of the next decompensation."

💎 Clinical Pearls

🩹 Culprit onlyIn MI cardiogenic shock, open the culprit artery — and ONLY the culprit. Immediate multivessel PCI harmed patients (CULPRIT-SHOCK). Stage the rest.
💉 Norepinephrine firstNorepinephrine, not dopamine, is the first pressor in cardiogenic shock — fewer arrhythmias, lower mortality (De Backer).
🎈 No reflex balloonThe IABP does not save lives in MI shock (IABP-SHOCK II) — don't reach for it reflexively.
🫀 Open the artery earlyEmergent revascularization is the single biggest mortality lever (SHOCK trial). Reperfuse fast.
🩺 New murmur = ruptureA new murmur in a shocked post-MI patient is a mechanical rupture until an echo proves otherwise.
📐 Stage it (SCAI)A→E (at-risk → beginning → classic → deteriorating → extremis). The stage drives escalation and prognosis.
❄️ Cold & wetLow output (cold) + congestion (wet) is the classic, lethal MI-shock profile. Perfusion is the diagnosis, not the cuff.
🔀 Know the shock typeCold+congested = cardiogenic; cold+empty = hypovolemic; warm = septic. Echo + JVP + perfusion separate them.
💪 Pressor + inotropeSqueezing alone just raises afterload on a weak pump — add an inotrope to actually move blood forward.
💧 Stingy with fluidThe lungs are wet — reflex large-volume resuscitation worsens edema. Small, monitored challenges only.
🛟 MCS by physiologyIf escalating: Impella (selected STEMI shock, DanGer) or VA-ECMO for biventricular failure — match the device to the patient.
🚽 Watch perfusionUrine output and lactate clearance tell you the spiral is breaking before the blood pressure does.
