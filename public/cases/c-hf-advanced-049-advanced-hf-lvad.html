Missing the referral window is not a soft error; it removes a survival option that cannot be recovered.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The HF fellow thinks out loud on the morning round:

🧠 Reframe: this is not another "tune-up," it is a recognition moment

"On paper we've 'optimised' him — but the truth is his heart no longer lets us give GDMT: every uptitration drops his pressure or his kidneys. He's inotrope-dependent, cachectic, shocking, and admitted five times this year. That is textbook Stage D / advanced HF. My job today is not to find a cleverer diuretic — it's to refer him to the advanced-HF/transplant service now, while his kidneys, liver and pulmonary vasculature can still recover, and to work him up for transplant versus a durable LVAD."

🎯 The fellow's order of operations

  1. 📞 Refer to the advanced-HF centre TODAY — inotrope dependence alone is a referral trigger; don't wait for the "right" admission.
  2. 🩸 Protect end-organs: maintain perfusion (careful inotrope/afterload management), avoid nephrotoxins, treat congestion without over-diuresing a low-output kidney.
  3. 🫀 Right-heart catheter to measure filling pressures, cardiac output and — critically — pulmonary vascular resistance and its reversibility (candidacy hinges on this).
  4. 📊 Assign an INTERMACS profile to gauge urgency (he is roughly profile 3 — "stable but inotrope-dependent").
  5. 🗣️ Begin honest shared decision-making + early palliative-care involvement in parallel — supporting advanced therapies and comfort are not mutually exclusive.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Refer advanced heart failure EARLY — the door to transplant and LVAD closes once the other organs fail, and you cannot reopen it."
🧪 Resident's self-testThe consultant asks: "He responded again last night — why refer now rather than after the next admission?"

Because candidacy is a moving target that only worsens. Every week of inotrope-dependent low output nudges his pulmonary vascular resistance up and his renal/hepatic function down. Fixed pulmonary hypertension and irreversible multi-organ failure are absolute barriers to transplant and major risk factors for LVAD. Referring "after the next crash" means referring a patient who may no longer be a candidate. The safest time to refer is the moment you recognise Stage D — which is now.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY waiting closes the door (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🫀 The pump fails forward and backward: a dilated, weak LV cannot generate stroke volume (low forward output) and cannot empty (high filling pressures back up into lungs, liver, gut, kidneys).
  2. 🫘 Cardiorenal cascade: low forward flow + high venous pressure → renal hypoperfusion and congestion → rising creatinine, diuretic resistance, and inability to tolerate ACEi/ARNI/MRA (they drop BP or K⁺ worsens). The very drugs that treat HF become untakeable.
  3. 🩸 Cardiohepatic + gut injury: congestion causes congestive hepatopathy (rising bilirubin, coagulopathy) and bowel wall oedema → malabsorption + a catabolic, inflammatory state → cardiac cachexia (muscle wasting that itself predicts death and worsens surgical candidacy).
  4. 🫁 Pulmonary vascular remodelling: chronically high left-sided pressures drive pulmonary hypertension. Early it is reactive/reversible; left long enough it becomes fixed (elevated, non-vasoreactive PVR) — and a right ventricle facing a fixed high-resistance circuit will fail a transplanted heart. Fixed PH is a hard contraindication to transplant.
  5. 🚪 The closing door: once multi-organ failure and fixed PH are established, the patient is no longer a transplant or LVAD candidate. This damage is largely irreversible — so the window for a life-saving operation genuinely closes if you wait.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Advanced HF doesn't just weaken the heart — it slowly disqualifies the patient. Every month of delay converts a fixable problem into an inoperable one."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Bridge-to-Transplant vs Destination-Therapy LVAD

🌉 Bridge-to-Transplant (BTT)

  • Patient IS a transplant candidate but too sick / donor not yet available
  • LVAD keeps them alive and rehabilitates end-organs while listed
  • Also "bridge-to-candidacy/decision" — support to reverse a relative contraindication (renal, PH, nutrition) and re-assess

🏁 Destination Therapy (DT)

  • Patient is NOT a transplant candidate (age, comorbidity, fixed PH)
  • LVAD is the definitive, permanent therapy (REMATCH concept)
  • Modern HeartMate 3 DT survival now approaches earlier transplant-era figures

💡 The device may be identical — the intent (bridge vs destination) is set by candidacy, and candidacy is set by how early you referred and how well the other organs held up.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The admission & referral order set

  • 📞 Referral to the regional advanced-HF / transplant centre — today. State the referral triggers explicitly (inotrope dependence, ≥2 admissions, GDMT intolerance, cardiorenal, cachexia).
  • 🫀 Right-heart catheterisation — filling pressures, cardiac output/index, PVR and vasoreactivity testing (candidacy hinges on reversibility of PH).
  • 🩺 Echo (LVEF, RV function, MR/AR/TR, LV size), cardiopulmonary exercise test (peak VO₂) if able, and 6-minute walk for prognostication.
  • 💉 Continue inotrope (dobutamine/milrinone) as a bridge to preserve perfusion — but flag that inotrope dependence is itself the referral trigger, not a reason to stay on the ward.
  • 💧 IV loop diuretic titrated to decongest without crashing the low-output kidney; daily weights, strict fluid balance, U&E.
  • 🧪 End-organ panel: U&E, LFTs, bilirubin, INR, lactate, natriuretic peptides, iron studies, nutrition markers (albumin, prealbumin), HbA1c.
  • 🦠 Transplant/LVAD candidacy screen initiated by the centre: virology/serology, malignancy screening, dental, frailty and psychosocial assessment.
  • 🗣️ Palliative-care referral in parallel for symptom control and goals-of-care — alongside, not instead of, advanced-therapy work-up.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The single most important order in advanced HF is the phone call to the advanced-HF centre — everything else is supportive care while that referral is processed."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Advanced-HF nursing — perfusion, nutrition and the end-organs

  • 💉 Guard the inotrope line and the perfusion: never let dobutamine/milrinone run dry or bolus; watch for arrhythmia. Cool peripheries, falling urine output and rising lactate are early "output is failing" alarms.
  • 🚽 Hourly urine output & daily weights: the kidney is the real-time cardiac-output monitor. Report oliguria promptly — cardiorenal deterioration changes candidacy.
  • 🍽️ Nutrition is treatment: cachexia predicts death and worsens surgical outcomes. Dietitian input, protein-forward small meals, treat nausea from gut congestion.
  • 🩹 Skin, mouth and fall prevention in a cachectic, low-output patient; meticulous line care (future LVAD/transplant patients cannot afford bloodstream infection).
  • ⚡ Debrief ICD shocks: they are painful and frightening — psychological support matters and each shock is a referral flag to escalate.
  • 🗣️ Be the honest voice: nurses often hear the real fears. Support shared decision-making and ensure palliative and advanced-therapy conversations are happening, not deferred.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In advanced HF the nurse watches three organs at once — heart (rhythm/perfusion), kidney (urine), and the whole patient (weight, mood, will). Any one deteriorating is a reason to escalate the referral, not just the diuretic."

📈 ECG / Imaging + Visual Enhancements

〰️ Arterial waveform — low output now, continuous-flow after LVAD

The native trace shows a small, narrow-pulse-pressure waveform (low stroke volume). After a continuous-flow LVAD the arterial line flattens — pulsatility falls, a palpable pulse and reliable cuff BP may disappear, and a Doppler-derived mean arterial pressure replaces systolic/diastolic readings.

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why — end-organs are the dashboard

  • 🫀 Telemetry + device interrogation: ventricular arrhythmia and ICD shocks are frequent and each is a red flag; check CRT-D function and burden of ectopy.
  • 🚽 Urine output, daily weight, U&E: the renal trend is the most sensitive real-time index of forward output and of whether you are over- or under-diuresing.
  • 🩸 Lactate, mentation, peripheral temperature: perfusion markers — a rising lactate on inotrope means the pump is losing, escalate the referral not just the drip.
  • 🫁 Repeat right-heart haemodynamics on therapy: is PVR falling (still reversible) or fixing? This directly informs transplant candidacy timing.
  • 🍽️ Nutrition & weight trend: ongoing muscle loss is a prognostic and candidacy alarm — track it deliberately.
  • 📞 Referral status: monitor that the advanced-HF centre review is actually happening — a referral that stalls in an inbox is the same as no referral.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In advanced HF the monitor's question is not 'is he congested?' but 'are his kidneys, liver and pulmonary vessels still recoverable?' — because that, not the diuretic, decides whether surgery can still save him."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — of the disease, and of durable LVAD therapy

🫀 Of untreated advanced HF

  • ⚡ Sudden cardiac death / arrhythmic storm — recurrent VT and ICD shocks.
  • 💔 Progressive cardiogenic shock and inotrope dependence sliding to INTERMACS 1.
  • 🫘 Multi-organ failure — cardiorenal, cardiohepatic, cachexia — the very complications that remove candidacy.
  • 🫁 Fixed pulmonary hypertension — the silent, irreversible disqualifier for transplant.

🔩 Of a durable continuous-flow LVAD (know these cold)

  • 🩸 Bleeding — acquired von Willebrand syndrome (high shear cleaves vWF multimers) + GI arteriovenous malformations → recurrent GI bleeds; plus anticoagulation-related bleeding.
  • 🧱 Pump thrombosis — device clot (much rarer with HeartMate 3) → haemolysis, power spikes, heart failure.
  • 🦠 Driveline infection — the percutaneous lead is a lifelong infection portal; meticulous site care is essential.
  • 🧠 Stroke — ischaemic (thromboembolic) or haemorrhagic; a leading cause of death/disability on support.
  • 🫀 Right ventricular failure — after LV unloading increases RV preload; may need inotropes, iNO, or RV assist device.
  • 🌊 Aortic regurgitation (de novo) — continuous flow across a rarely-opening aortic valve promotes AR over time, worsening effective forward flow.
  • 🌀 Suction events / low-flow alarms — over-speed or hypovolaemia collapses the LV onto the inflow cannula, triggering arrhythmia.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The modern LVAD (HeartMate 3) is a durable therapy, not a last-ditch gamble — but it bleeds, clots and can fail the right ventricle; know its complications before you refer, so you can consent honestly."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Two very different destinations — and both start with the referral

🔩 Bridged / on a durable LVAD

  • Anticoagulation (warfarin) + antiplatelet; strict INR/mean-arterial-pressure targets
  • Driveline care, controller/battery competence, emergency plan, LVAD-coordinator contact
  • Surveillance for bleeding, thrombosis, infection, stroke, RV failure, AR
  • Continue GDMT/decongestion as tolerated; cardiac rehab; transplant follow-up if BTT

🕊️ Not a surgical candidate — palliative pathway

  • Symptom-directed care: diuretics, opioids for dyspnoea, anti-emetics, oxygen if hypoxic
  • Consider ICD deactivation discussion to avoid distressing shocks at end of life
  • Advance-care planning, community HF + palliative teams, carer support
  • Honest prognostication — quality of remaining life as the explicit goal
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Every advanced-HF patient deserves the referral — whether it ends in a transplant, a pump, or a well-planned palliative pathway. What no patient deserves is a default of endless ward tune-ups until the choice is made by default."
🗣️ Palliative care is active treatment, not defeatWhen surgery isn't appropriate, high-quality palliative care and honest shared decision-making are the therapy. It runs alongside advanced-therapy evaluation from the start, not only after everything else has failed.

💎 Clinical Pearls

🚪 The door closesRefer advanced HF EARLY — transplant/LVAD candidacy is lost once end-organs and pulmonary vessels fail, and it cannot be regained.
🆘 I NEED HELPInotropes, NYHA III–IV, End-organ dysfunction, EF very low, Defibrillator shocks, Hospitalisations, Escalating diuretics, Low BP-limited GDMT, Prognostic markers — any one triggers referral.
💊 GDMT you can't giveWhen the pressure/kidney vetoes ARNI, beta-blocker and MRA, that intolerance IS advanced HF — not a reason to keep titrating.
📊 INTERMACS 2–4The best time to operate durably is before the crash (profile 1); refer while still profile 2–4.
🫁 PVR reversibilityFixed pulmonary hypertension contraindicates transplant — measure PVR early, while it is still reactive.
🔩 HeartMate 3Fully mag-lev continuous-flow pump (MOMENTUM 3): better survival, far less pump thrombosis than older devices.
🩸 LVAD bleeds & clotsAcquired von Willebrand + GI AVMs → bleeding; also thrombosis, driveline infection, stroke, RV failure, de novo AR.
🕊️ Palliative = therapyIf not a surgical candidate, honest shared decision-making and palliative care are the treatment — offered early, not last.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the referral

  • 📊 INTERMACS profiles 1–7 in depth: 1 "crash and burn" (critical shock) → 2 "sliding on inotropes" → 3 "stable but inotrope-dependent" → 4 "resting symptoms / frequent flyer" → 5 "housebound" → 6 "walking wounded" → 7 "advanced NYHA III." Modifiers: temporary MCS (TCS), arrhythmia (A), frequent flyer (FF). Outcomes are worst in profile 1 — the argument for referring before the crash.
  • 🎯 Intent of MCS — BTT vs BTD vs DT: bridge-to-transplant (already a candidate, supported while listed); bridge-to-candidacy/decision (support to reverse a relative contraindication — renal, PH, nutrition, adherence — then re-evaluate); destination therapy (permanent, for non-transplant candidates, per REMATCH).
  • 🔩 Continuous-flow LVAD physiology: a rotary pump generates near-continuous flow → reduced pulsatility (often no palpable pulse, unreliable cuff BP; use Doppler MAP). Pump speed is set to unload the LV without causing suction events (LV collapse onto the inflow cannula → ectopy/arrhythmia). Flow is preload-dependent and afterload-sensitive; hypertension reduces flow and raises stroke risk, so MAP is tightly controlled.
  • 🫀 RV failure after LV unloading: LVAD raises RV preload and shifts the septum; a marginal RV decompensates. Predict with RV indices (TAPSE, RV:LV ratio, CVP:PCWP, severe TR); manage with volume optimisation, inotropes, pulmonary vasodilators (iNO), or an RVAD/BiVAD.
  • 🩸 Acquired von Willebrand syndrome & GI AVMs: high shear cleaves large vWF multimers; combined with reduced pulsatility promoting angiodysplasia → recurrent GI bleeding. Balanced against thrombosis risk requiring anticoagulation — a lifelong tightrope.
  • 🌊 De novo aortic regurgitation, pump thrombosis, driveline infection, stroke: the signature durable-MCS complication set to counsel and surveil for; HeartMate 3's mag-lev design (MOMENTUM 3) sharply cut pump thrombosis and stroke vs axial-flow HeartMate II.
  • 🚫 Transplant contraindications: fixed/irreversible pulmonary hypertension (high non-vasoreactive PVR), active or recent malignancy, active infection, irreversible major other-organ dysfunction (unless combined-organ transplant), severe frailty, ongoing substance misuse or non-adherence, and inadequate psychosocial support.
  • 💊 Immunosuppression basics (post-transplant): induction (e.g., basiliximab/ATG) then triple maintenance — a calcineurin inhibitor (tacrolimus/ciclosporin) + antiproliferative (mycophenolate) + corticosteroid; watch for rejection (endomyocardial biopsy surveillance), infection (CMV/PJP prophylaxis), nephrotoxicity, malignancy and cardiac allograft vasculopathy.
  • 🕊️ Palliative care & shared decision-making: integrated from diagnosis of Stage D; symptom control, advance-care planning, ICD-deactivation conversations, and explicit trade-off discussions (survival vs invasiveness vs quality of life) for every pathway — surgical or not.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Referral That Came Six Months Too Late"

The case: A 58-year-old man with non-ischaemic dilated cardiomyopathy, EF 15%, cycled through the ward every few weeks over half a year. Each admission the team escalated furosemide and gave intermittent dobutamine and "kept him going," proud that he always improved enough to go home. His creatinine crept up, his bilirubin rose, he lost weight, and his pulmonary pressures — never remeasured — quietly fixed. Only when he arrived in florid cardiogenic shock (INTERMACS 1) did anyone finally phone the transplant centre. By then he had fixed pulmonary hypertension, a failing liver and kidneys, and cardiac cachexia. He was declined for both transplant and LVAD and died a few weeks later. Referred six months earlier, the advanced-HF team agreed, he would have been an excellent candidate.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"He always responds" — improvement each admission masked a relentless downward trajectory.
📞 NO REFERRALClear I NEED HELP triggers (inotropes, recurrent admissions, cachexia) never prompted a call to the advanced-HF centre.
🫁 PH LET FIXPulmonary vascular resistance never remeasured; reversible PH allowed to become fixed and inoperable.
🫘 END-ORGANS IGNOREDRising creatinine and bilirubin read as "expected" rather than as the closing candidacy window.
🗣️ NO PLAN BNeither advanced therapies nor honest palliative planning were ever discussed — a default of endless tune-ups.

🛡️ What would have saved him: read recurrent admissions + inotrope dependence + cachexia as advanced HF and refer on the first such admission; remeasure PVR and protect end-organs while they are still recoverable; treat the referral as time-critical, not a courtesy; and run honest shared decision-making — transplant, LVAD, or palliative — from the start. This is exactly the recognition that saved the patient in Bed 7.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"He died of a disease that had a surgical answer months earlier. 'Keeping him going' on the ward without a referral is not care — it is watching the door close in slow motion."
