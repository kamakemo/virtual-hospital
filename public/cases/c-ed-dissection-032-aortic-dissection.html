🧭 The three killers you exclude at the bedside first

For "sudden severe chest pain + unwell," the immediately lethal trio is ACS, aortic dissection, and pulmonary embolism (± tension pneumothorax, oesophageal rupture). When an inferior STEMI comes with tearing/back pain, unequal pulses, or a new AR murmur → image the aorta first.

🧪 Resident's self-testShe's tempted to start GTN infusion first to bring that 190 systolic down fast. Right move?

No — beta-blocker first. A vasodilator given before rate control triggers reflex tachycardia, which raises dP/dt (the shear force of each ejection) and can propagate the tear. Blunt the heart rate and contractility with esmolol/labetalol to HR ≤ 60 FIRST, then add the vasodilator to reach SBP 100–120. Lower the RATE before the PRESSURE.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY it tears, WHY it kills (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧱 Weakened media: chronic hypertension (and connective-tissue disorders) drives cystic medial degeneration — loss of elastin and smooth muscle in the aortic wall's middle layer. The wall loses its tensile strength.
  2. 💥 Intimal tear: each systole delivers a pulse of shear stress — the dP/dt, the rate of rise of pressure — against that weakened wall. An intimal tear opens, usually where wall stress is highest (ascending aorta just above the valve, or just distal to the left subclavian).
  3. 🩸 False lumen cleaves: blood is driven under pressure into the media, splitting it into a true and a false lumen. Every subsequent heartbeat's dP/dt propagates the dissection along the vessel — antegrade and/or retrograde.
  4. 💀 The lethal complications all flow from where the flap travels: retrograde into the pericardium → tamponade; across the aortic valve → acute severe AR + heart failure; across the coronary ostium (usually RCA) → inferior STEMI; into arch vessels → stroke; down into branch arteries → limb/mesenteric/renal malperfusion; or free rupture → exsanguination.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Dissection is a plumbing catastrophe driven by shear: lower the dP/dt (the RATE and force of each beat) and you slow the tear — this is why anti-impulse therapy comes before everything except calling the surgeon."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Stanford Type A vs Type B

🔪 Type A (the surgeon)

  • Involves the ascending aorta (± arch/descending)
  • Risks tamponade, acute AR, coronary occlusion, stroke
  • Emergency surgery — medical mortality ~1–2%/hour
  • Our patient

💉 Type B (the infusion pump)

  • Begins distal to the left subclavian, ascending aorta spared
  • Uncomplicated → medical anti-impulse therapy, admit, control BP/HR
  • Complicated (malperfusion, rupture, refractory pain/HTN) → TEVAR

💡 The classification is anatomical but the message is operational: ascending involvement calls a surgeon; descending-only calls an infusion pump (and an interventionalist if it misbehaves).

🎮 Simulation — dP/dt: the Shear-Force Dial

Anti-impulse therapy exists to lower dP/dt — the shear each heartbeat delivers to the torn wall. Drag the dial (heart rate / contractility) and watch how the wall shear and propagation risk change. Aim to blunt the rate before chasing the pressure.

Set the dial to explore how rate control lowers aortic wall shear.

🎯 High rate + high contractility = high dP/dt = the tear propagates. Beta-blockade to HR ≤ 60 collapses the shear. Give a vasodilator alone (without rate control) and reflex tachycardia drives the dial back up — widening the tear.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (21:52)

  • 🚫 HOLD all further antithrombotics — no P2Y₁₂ inhibitor, no heparin, no thrombolysis — until dissection is excluded.
  • 💉 Esmolol infusion (or IV labetalol boluses) titrated to HR ≤ 60; if beta-blocker contraindicated, use a non-dihydropyridine calcium blocker (diltiazem).
  • 💊 Then add nitroprusside (or GTN) infusion to a target SBP 100–120 mmHg, measured in the higher-pressure (left) arm — never a vasodilator before the beta-blocker.
  • 😖 IV opioid analgesia generously (morphine/fentanyl) — pain relief IS anti-impulse therapy.
  • 🩻 CT angiogram aorta (ECG-gated, arch to iliacs) urgently; bedside echo now (root diameter, dissection flap, AR severity, pericardial effusion/tamponade).
  • 🩸 Group & cross-match 4–6 units, FBC, U&E, coag, troponin, lactate; two large-bore IVs; arterial line in the higher arm; urinary catheter (monitor renal malperfusion).
  • 📞 Call cardiothoracic surgery immediately and alert theatres/ICU; if hypotensive, treat cautiously — permissive lower BP, cautious fluids, and think tamponade.
  • 📈 Continuous monitoring with defib pads on; repeat 12-lead if the picture evolves.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Two infusions and one phone call save this life: esmolol to slow the beat, nitroprusside to soften the pressure, and the cardiothoracic surgeon on the line — in that order."
🚫 The 'never in a dissection' list❌ heparin · ❌ ticagrelor/prasugrel/clopidogrel loading · ❌ thrombolysis · ❌ a vasodilator before rate control. Each one either drives the shear up or turns a contained tear into fatal bleeding.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Resus-bay priorities — the nurse runs the anti-impulse safety net

  • 🩸 Cuff on the HIGHER-pressure arm and title the art-line there — titrating to the falsely-low (malperfused) arm will over-treat and under-perfuse the brain and heart. Document which arm every reading came from.
  • 💓 Titrate the beta-blocker to HR ≤ 60 first, then the vasodilator to SBP 100–120 — flag immediately if the heart rate climbs after the vasodilator starts (reflex tachycardia = under-blocked).
  • 😖 Aggressive, frequent analgesia and a calm bedside — every spike of pain or anxiety raises catecholamines, HR and dP/dt and propagates the tear.
  • 🩸 Two large-bore cannulae, send the cross-match, keep blood products moving — this patient may be in theatre within the hour and may bleed.
  • 📟 Continuous ECG + arterial waveform; watch for a narrowing pulse pressure, rising HR, muffled tone, distended neck veins — the signature of evolving tamponade.
  • 🚫 Challenge any order for heparin, a P2Y₁₂ inhibitor or thrombolysis until the aorta is cleared — the nurse is the last line against the fatal antithrombotic.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who insists the blood pressure is titrated to the RIGHT arm — the higher one — and who queries the heparin, is the one who keeps the brain perfused and the tear from spreading."

📈 ECG / Imaging + Visual Enhancements

🫀 The ECG that misleads — inferior "STEMI" from a torn RCA ostium

A Type A dissection extending to the right coronary ostium produces a genuine inferior STEMI pattern. Cathing and anticoagulating this "STEMI" without imaging the aorta is a classic fatal trap.

🩻 Imaging — what confirms the diagnosis

ModalityLook forRole
🖥️ CT angiogram (aorta)Intimal flap, true vs false lumen, entry tear, ascending involvement (= Type A), branch-vessel malperfusion, pericardial bloodFirst-line in the stable-enough patient; maps the whole aorta for the surgeon.
🫀 Transoesophageal echo (TOE)Ascending flap, AR mechanism/severity, pericardial effusion/tamponade, entry tear near the rootFor the too-unstable-to-travel patient; done in resus/theatre.
📟 Bedside TTE (POCUS)Pericardial effusion/tamponade, dilated root, AR jet, proximal flapImmediate screen while arranging CT — a pericardial effusion + this story is enough to call the surgeon.
🩻 Chest X-rayWidened mediastinum (> 8 cm), abnormal aortic contour, left pleural effusion, displaced calciumSupportive only — a normal CXR does NOT exclude dissection.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"An inferior STEMI with tearing/back pain, unequal pulses or a new AR murmur is a dissection until the aorta is imaged — a normal chest X-ray never lets you off that hook."
⚠️ Reading the CT like a surgeonThe report must answer: does the flap involve the ascending aorta (Type A → theatre) or start beyond the left subclavian (Type B)? Which branches are perfused from the true vs false lumen (malperfusion)? Is there pericardial blood (tamponade risk)? These findings, not the diagnosis alone, drive the operation.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🩸 Arterial line + cuff on the higher-pressure arm, beat-to-beat SBP targeted 100–120; re-check inter-arm differential (a changing gradient means the flap is moving).
  • 💓 Continuous HR — the anti-impulse target is HR ≤ 60; a rising HR after starting the vasodilator = under-blocked, propagation risk.
  • 🫀 Echo/tamponade surveillance — narrowing pulse pressure, rising HR, muffled heart sounds, distended neck veins, pulsus paradoxus = pericardial tamponade, a peri-arrest emergency.
  • 🧠 Serial neuro obs + limb perfusion — new focal deficit (carotid), a newly cold/pulseless limb, or falling urine output signal evolving malperfusion.
  • 🧪 Trend lactate, creatinine, Hb — rising lactate/creatinine = mesenteric/renal malperfusion; falling Hb = leak/rupture.
  • 📈 Repeat 12-lead if chest pain changes — coronary involvement can evolve.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you say 'dissection,' the monitor's questions become: is he tamponading, is he malperfusing, and is the tear still spreading? — the answers are the pulse pressure, the pulses, and the neuro exam."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can kill him — follow the flap

  • 🫀 Cardiac tamponade — retrograde rupture into the pericardium; the leading cause of death in Type A. Beware pericardiocentesis (see S15).
  • 🎵 Acute severe aortic regurgitation — the flap disrupts the valve → sudden LV volume overload, flash pulmonary oedema, cardiogenic shock.
  • 🩸 Coronary malperfusion — usually RCA ostium → inferior STEMI, arrhythmia, pump failure.
  • 🧠 Stroke / syncope — arch-vessel (carotid) involvement → focal deficit or global hypoperfusion.
  • 🦵 Branch malperfusion — mesenteric (bowel ischaemia, rising lactate), renal (AKI), limb (cold pulseless leg). Dynamic (flap flutters over the ostium) vs static (flap fixed into the branch).
  • 💥 Free rupture / exsanguination — into pericardium, pleura (usually left), or mediastinum → instant arrest.
  • 🩺 The system complication: the misdiagnosis — treated as ACS and anticoagulated, or discharged as "musculoskeletal back pain."
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Every complication of Type A dissection is 'the flap arrived somewhere' — pericardium, valve, coronary, brain, gut, kidney or limb. Examine for all of them, because any one can be the thing that kills in the next ten minutes."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Post-repair follow-up vs the low-risk chest pain safely sent home — two different worlds

🫀 This patient (post Type A repair)

  • Lifelong strict BP control (β-blocker ± ARB), HR/impulse control, smoking cessation
  • Surveillance imaging (CT/MR) of the whole aorta — the residual/downstream aorta can re-dissect or aneurysm
  • Genetics referral (Marfan/Loeys-Dietz/familial thoracic aortic disease) + first-degree relative screening
  • Avoid heavy isometric straining; red flags (recurrent tearing pain, syncope, limb/neuro symptoms) → 999

🟢 The genuinely low-risk chest pain

  • ADD-RS 0 + negative D-dimer + reassuring alternative diagnosis
  • Documented reasoning, written safety-net, explicit "come back if the pain tears, moves, or you faint"
  • No antithrombotic error made "just in case"
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Surviving the dissection is chapter one; the residual aorta is chapter two — lifelong BP control, surveillance imaging and family screening are what stop the sequel."

💎 Clinical Pearls

💥 Tearing + maximal at onsetIschaemia crescendos; a tear is worst from the first second and migrates. Language matters.
🩸 Both arms, all pulsesInter-arm BP differential + pulse deficit + new AR murmur = dissection until the CT says otherwise.
🩺 Rule out the aorta firstClear the aorta BEFORE you anticoagulate the coronary — an inferior STEMI can be a torn RCA ostium.
💓 Rate before pressureEsmolol to HR ≤ 60 first, THEN nitroprusside to SBP 100–120; a vasodilator alone widens the tear.
📏 Titrate to the higher armTarget SBP in the higher-pressure arm — the low arm is malperfused and will mislead you.
🔪 Type A = surgeon, Type B = pumpAscending involvement goes to theatre; descending-only gets medical anti-impulse ± TEVAR.
🫀 Fear the pericardiumTamponade is the leading killer; drain only a tiny controlled volume as a bridge to theatre.
🧮 Score, don't guessADD-RS ≥ 2 → straight to CT; a normal D-dimer only helps when ADD-RS ≤ 1.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🩸 The acute aortic syndrome spectrum: classic dissection sits alongside intramural haematoma (IMH) — bleeding in the media with no visible intimal flap (may progress to dissection or regress) — and penetrating atherosclerotic ulcer (PAU) — an ulcerated plaque eroding into the media. Ascending (Type A) IMH/PAU is generally managed like Type A dissection (surgery); descending like Type B.
  • 🦵 Dynamic vs static malperfusion: in dynamic obstruction the mobile flap intermittently covers a branch ostium (often relieved by treating the proximal aorta/true-lumen pressure); in static obstruction the flap propagates into the branch and fixes it (needs branch-vessel stenting/fenestration). Distinguishing them changes whether central repair alone suffices.
  • 🧬 Heritable aortopathies: Marfan (FBN1, fibrillin-1), Loeys-Dietz (TGFβ-pathway, more aggressive/earlier dissection at smaller diameters), vascular Ehlers-Danlos (COL3A1), bicuspid aortic valve and familial thoracic aortic aneurysm/dissection. These drive earlier surgical thresholds and mandate family screening.
  • 🖥️ TEVAR for Type B: uncomplicated Type B is medical (anti-impulse, BP control); complicated Type B (malperfusion, rupture, refractory pain/hypertension, rapid expansion) gets thoracic endovascular aortic repair. Trials (INSTEAD-XL, ADSORB) suggest early TEVAR may improve aortic remodelling in selected sub-acute Type B.
  • 🧠 Cerebral protection in arch surgery: when the arch is involved, repair uses deep/moderate hypothermic circulatory arrest with antegrade or retrograde cerebral perfusion to protect the brain while the surgeon works in a bloodless field.
  • 📏 Surgical thresholds & prevention: elective root/ascending replacement is considered at ~5.5 cm (lower in Marfan/Loeys-Dietz/bicuspid or with rapid growth/family history) — the population from which acute dissections emerge, which is why the incidentally 'dilated aorta' letter should never be ignored.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The STEMI That Was a Tear"

The case: A 58-year-old hypertensive man arrived with sudden severe chest pain and an ECG showing inferior ST elevation. He was pre-alerted as an inferior STEMI, loaded with aspirin, ticagrelor and heparin, and rushed to the cath lab. Nobody recorded a blood pressure in both arms; nobody noted the new early-diastolic murmur or the fact the pain had ripped through to his back. At angiography the coronaries were near-normal — the "occlusion" was a Type A dissection flap over the RCA ostium. Minutes later he became hypotensive and bradycardic: haemopericardium and tamponade, accelerated by the antithrombotics. He arrested on the table and could not be saved.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Inferior STEMI" label locked in; the tearing/back-radiating history was never revisited.
🩸 EXAM SKIPPEDNo bilateral BP; the new AR murmur and pulse deficit were never sought.
💉 ANTITHROMBOTIC REFLEXAspirin + ticagrelor + heparin given before the aorta was cleared.
🖥️ NO AORTIC IMAGINGStraight to cath without a CT aorta despite dissection features.
🫀 TAMPONADEAnticoagulation turned a contained tear into fatal haemopericardium.

🛡️ What would have saved him: take the blood pressure in both arms and feel all the pulses on every chest pain; listen for a new aortic-regurgitation murmur; treat "tearing, maximal-at-onset, migrating-to-the-back" as aortic language; and rule out the aorta BEFORE anticoagulating a coronary — especially an inferior STEMI with any dissection feature. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 2.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"An inferior STEMI plus tearing/back pain, unequal pulses or a new AR murmur is a dissection until the aorta is imaged — anticoagulate it and you finish the tear the aorta started."
