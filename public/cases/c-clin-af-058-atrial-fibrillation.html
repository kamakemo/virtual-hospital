🔥 Why AF is the quiet giant of the clinicAF is the commonest sustained arrhythmia (lifetime risk roughly 1 in 3) and it multiplies stroke risk about five-fold. The entire clinic visit exists to stop that clot before it forms.

🧭 The three questions that frame every new AF patient

Before any prescription, structure the encounter as the integrated ABC / CARE pathway (2020 ESC ABC → 2024 ESC CARE): Comorbidity & risk-factor management, Avoid stroke (anticoagulate), Reduce symptoms (rate/rhythm), Evaluate & re-evaluate. Ask: (1) Is this really AF, and is it secondary to something reversible? (2) What is the stroke risk, and what is the net benefit of anticoagulation? (3) How symptomatic is he, and does he want rate or rhythm control?

⚠️ The trap of "the pulse is only 96 — he's fine"A controlled rate does not mean a controlled disease. Stroke risk is independent of rate and of whether AF is paroxysmal, persistent or permanent. A comfortable rate can lull you into skipping the anticoagulation decision — the decision that actually saves the life.

🎯 Her order of operations

  2. 💊 Start a DOAC (e.g. apixaban) after checking renal/hepatic function and interactions; stop the aspirin — it adds bleeding risk without stroke protection.
  3. 🎚️ Rate control with a beta-blocker; book echo and discuss early rhythm control / catheter ablation, especially as he's symptomatic and paroxysmal.
  4. 🛠️ Attack the comorbidities — tighten BP, weight-loss referral, alcohol reduction, sleep-study for OSA.
  5. 🗓️ Structured follow-up — the "E" of CARE: re-evaluate stroke risk (it rises with age), symptoms, and renal function over time.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A DOAC is first choice for almost every AF patient — warfarin is reserved for mechanical valves and moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis. Everyone else, reach for the DOAC."

No. Aspirin is not effective stroke prevention in AF and is no longer recommended for it. In AVERROES, apixaban roughly halved stroke versus aspirin with similar major-bleeding rates — so aspirin gives you the bleeding of an antithrombotic with almost none of the protection. 🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY AF harms (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. ⚡ Disorganised atrial activity: multiple re-entrant wavelets / rapid ectopic firing (often from pulmonary vein sleeves) replace coordinated atrial contraction. The atria quiver at 350–600/min; the AV node filters this into an irregularly irregular ventricular response.
  2. 🌀 Stasis → thrombus: the non-contracting left atrium — especially the left atrial appendage — becomes a stagnant pool. Virchow's triad (stasis + endothelial change + hypercoagulability) forms a clot there.
  3. 🧠 Cardioembolic stroke: a whole clot embolises to the brain → a large, disabling stroke (a full MCA territory, not a small lacune). This is the harm the whole visit exists to prevent.
  4. 💔 Tachycardia-mediated cardiomyopathy: a persistently fast, irregular ventricular rate impairs filling and, over weeks–months, weakens the ventricle — a reversible cardiomyopathy if the rate/rhythm is controlled.
  5. 🫀 Loss of atrial kick: ~20–30% of ventricular filling is lost, which is why some patients feel breathless and fatigued even at a "normal" rate.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The atrium doesn't kill by beating fast — it kills by standing still. Stasis in the left atrial appendage makes the clot; anticoagulation, not rate control, is what prevents the stroke."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Rate control vs (early) Rhythm control

🐢 Rate control

  • Accept AF, slow the ventricle: beta-blocker or non-DHP CCB (diltiazem/verapamil)
  • Avoid non-DHP CCBs in HFrEF; digoxin as add-on/if sedentary
  • Lenient target < 110/min if asymptomatic (RACE II); stricter if symptomatic/LV dysfunction
  • Simple, safe — but does not restore atrial kick or reduce symptoms as much

🎯 (Early) Rhythm control

  • Restore/maintain sinus rhythm: antiarrhythmics (flecainide if no structural disease; amiodarone/sotalol) or catheter ablation (PVI)
  • EAST-AFNET 4: early rhythm control improves outcomes
  • Ablation can be first-line for symptomatic paroxysmal AF; improves outcomes in AF+HF (CASTLE-AF)
  • Best for symptomatic, younger, HF, or recent-onset AF

💡 Remember: rhythm vs rate control changes symptoms and outcomes — it does NOT change the anticoagulation decision. Even after successful ablation, stroke prophylaxis continues based on CHA₂DS₂-VASc.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The clinic order set (10:40)

  • 📈 12-lead ECG (confirm AF, assess rate, look for pre-excitation/LVH/prior MI); baseline rhythm strip.
  • 🧪 Bloods: FBC, U&E + eGFR (DOAC dosing), LFTs, TSH, HbA1c, lipids, coagulation, calcium/magnesium.
  • 🫀 Transthoracic echo — LV function, LA size, valve assessment (exclude significant mitral stenosis before committing to a DOAC), screen for structural heart disease.
  • 💊 Start a DOAC (e.g. apixaban 5 mg BD; reduce to 2.5 mg BD if ≥2 of: age ≥80, weight ≤60 kg, creatinine ≥133 µmol/L) — and STOP the aspirin.
  • 🎚️ Rate control: bisoprolol 2.5–5 mg OD (or diltiazem if beta-blocker not tolerated and no HFrEF); target symptom relief, resting HR < 110 as a starting goal.
  • 🩺 Comorbidity work-up: home BP monitoring, weight-management referral, alcohol brief intervention, sleep study for OSA.
  • 🗓️ Refer to AF nurse-led integrated clinic; flag for rhythm-control / ablation discussion given symptomatic paroxysmal AF.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Two orders do the heavy lifting in new AF: start the DOAC and stop the aspirin. Everything else — rate, rhythm, comorbidities — is important, but the anticoagulant is what prevents the funeral."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ AF nurse-led clinic priorities — the nurse runs integrated care

  • 💓 Manual pulse + 12-lead confirmation — automated BP cuffs mis-measure in AF; palpate and confirm rhythm on ECG.
  • 💊 DOAC adherence counselling — a missed DOAC dose leaves the patient unprotected within a day (short half-life); stress not stopping without advice, and same-time daily dosing.
  • 🩸 Bleeding safety-net — teach the patient to recognise and report black stools, unusual bruising, or any head injury; provide an anticoagulation alert card.
  • 🍷 Risk-factor coaching — alcohol reduction, weight loss, smoking, medication review for NSAIDs/interactions.
  • 😮‍💨 Symptom diary / EHRA class — record palpitations, breathlessness, effort tolerance to inform the rate-vs-rhythm decision at review.
  • 🚫 Confirm the aspirin is stopped — reconcile the medication list; combining aspirin with a DOAC adds bleeding without benefit unless there is a separate vascular indication.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The most dangerous day on a DOAC is the day it's forgotten — a short half-life means protection disappears fast. Adherence teaching by the AF nurse is stroke prevention."

📈 ECG / Imaging + Visual Enhancements

🫀 The AF ECG — read it like a fingerprint

The hallmarks: absent P waves with a wavering fibrillatory baseline, and irregularly irregular QRS spacing. Narrow QRS unless there is bundle branch block or aberrancy.

🔎 Patterns and mimics you must know cold

PatternLook forMeaning / action
💠 Typical AFNo P waves, fibrillatory baseline, irregularly irregular narrow QRSConfirm the diagnosis on a 12-lead; stratify with CHA₂DS₂-VASc.
🔺 Atrial flutterSawtooth F waves (~300/min), often 2:1 → regular ~150Same thromboembolic risk as AF; anticoagulate identically; very ablatable (cavotricuspid isthmus).
⚡ AF with pre-excitation (WPW)Irregular, broad, very fast, varying QRS🚨 EMERGENCY — avoid AV-nodal blockers (digoxin, verapamil, adenosine, beta-blockers); they can accelerate to VF. Use procainamide / DC cardioversion.
🌀 Multifocal atrial tachycardia≥3 distinct P-wave morphologies, irregular — but P waves ARE presentNot AF; usually pulmonary disease — treat the lung, correct hypoxia/electrolytes.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Irregular, broad and very fast? Think AF with WPW — and never give an AV-nodal blocker, or you can tip it into ventricular fibrillation."

🩻 Adjunct imaging & monitoring

  • Transthoracic echo: LV function, LA size, valves (the DOAC-vs-warfarin decision).
  • Transoesophageal echo (TOE): visualises the left atrial appendage for thrombus before early cardioversion when AF duration is uncertain/≥48 h and the patient isn't already anticoagulated.
  • Ambulatory / prolonged ECG monitoring: quantifies AF burden and correlates symptoms with rhythm; wearables and implantable loop recorders increasingly detect subclinical AF.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why (over months, in clinic)

  • 🩸 Renal function — recheck eGFR at least annually (more often if CrCl <60, elderly, or unwell); a falling eGFR changes DOAC dose or eligibility. No routine INR needed on a DOAC.
  • 💊 Adherence & bleeding — review at each visit; ask about bruising, GI bleeding, and any missed doses.
  • 💓 Rate & symptoms — resting HR, effort tolerance, EHRA symptom class; up-titrate rate control or escalate to rhythm control if symptomatic.
  • 🎛️ Ambulatory ECG — to assess rate control across the day and AF burden, and to correlate symptoms.
  • 🧮 Re-score CHA₂DS₂-VASc — it rises with age and new comorbidities; a patient who "didn't need" anticoagulation last year may now.
  • 🛠️ Comorbidities — BP, weight, HbA1c, alcohol, OSA treatment adherence (CPAP).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"AF care doesn't end at the first prescription — it's a movie. Re-check the kidney, re-score the stroke risk, and re-ask about symptoms at every visit ('E' for Evaluate)."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — of the disease and of the treatment

  • 🧠 Cardioembolic stroke / systemic embolism — the big one; large, disabling, often fatal. The reason anticoagulation exists.
  • 💔 Tachycardia-mediated cardiomyopathy & heart failure — from chronic uncontrolled rate; reversible with rate/rhythm control.
  • 🩸 Bleeding from anticoagulation — GI and (less commonly with DOACs) intracranial; the trade-off you actively manage with HAS-BLED modification.
  • ⚗️ Drug interactions / dosing errors — DOAC under-dosing (loss of protection) or over-dosing (bleeding); interactions with azoles, rifampicin, some anticonvulsants, and P-gp inhibitors.
  • 🎯 Rhythm-control harms — proarrhythmia (flecainide, sotalol/QT), amiodarone organ toxicity.
  • 🔧 Ablation complications — rare but serious: cardiac tamponade, pulmonary vein stenosis, atrio-oesophageal fistula, phrenic nerve palsy, vascular access issues.
  • 🩹 The system complication: under-anticoagulation — the patient left on aspirin or nothing "to be safe," who then has the preventable stroke.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The commonest serious complication of AF isn't a drug side-effect — it's the stroke you didn't prevent because you were afraid of the bleed you could have managed."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The integrated ABC / CARE plan

  1. 🩸 A — Avoid stroke: CHA₂DS₂-VASc 3 → start a DOAC (apixaban 5 mg BD), stop aspirin, modify (not withhold for) HAS-BLED risks.
  2. 🎚️ B — Better symptoms: rate control with bisoprolol now; refer for early rhythm control / catheter ablation given symptomatic paroxysmal AF (EAST-AFNET 4, EARLY-AF).
  3. 🛠️ C — Comorbidities: tighten BP, weight-loss programme, alcohol reduction, treat OSA, optimise diabetes.
  4. 🗓️ E — Evaluate: AF nurse-led follow-up; re-check renal function, re-score stroke risk, reassess symptoms and adherence.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Early rhythm control and catheter ablation are no longer last resorts — they improve outcomes (EAST-AFNET 4, CASTLE-AF). A symptomatic patient deserves the ablation conversation on day one, not after years of tablets."

💎 Clinical Pearls

🚫 Aspirin ≠ stroke preventionIn AF, aspirin doesn't prevent strokes — anticoagulate by CHA₂DS₂-VASc with a DOAC, or don't anticoagulate at all.
🧮 Score, don't guessMen ≥1, women ≥2 on CHA₂DS₂-VASc → anticoagulate. The sex point never anticoagulates a woman alone.
🩸 HAS-BLED fixes, not forbidsUse it to modify bleeding risk (BP, alcohol), never as an excuse to withhold the anticoagulant.
💊 DOAC firstDOAC for almost everyone — warfarin only for mechanical valves & moderate–severe mitral stenosis.
🎯 Rhythm control earlyEAST-AFNET 4 & CASTLE-AF: early rhythm control and ablation improve outcomes — not last resorts.
🌀 Stasis, not speedThe stroke comes from the stagnant left atrial appendage — anticoagulation, not rate control, prevents it.
🛠️ Treat the whole patientBP, obesity, OSA, alcohol, diabetes — risk-factor control reduces AF burden ('C'/CARE).

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🗂️ AF subtypes & burden: paroxysmal (self-terminating <7 days), persistent (>7 days / needs cardioversion), long-standing persistent (>1 year), permanent (accepted). Stroke risk is driven by CHA₂DS₂-VASc, not by subtype — but higher AF burden (time in AF) associates with higher risk, blurring the old categories.
  • 📟 Device-detected subclinical AF: brief atrial high-rate episodes on pacemakers/loop recorders. NOAH-AFNET 6 and ARTESIA showed anticoagulation gives only a modest stroke reduction at the cost of more bleeding — so short subclinical episodes are a nuanced, individualised decision, not an automatic DOAC (unlike clinical ECG-documented AF).
  • 🎯 Catheter ablation: pulmonary vein isolation (PVI) is the cornerstone (the PVs trigger most paroxysmal AF). Pulsed-field ablation (PFA) — non-thermal, tissue-selective electroporation — reduces oesophageal/phrenic injury and speeds procedures. Indications: symptomatic paroxysmal AF (can be first-line, EARLY-AF/STOP-AF), drug-refractory AF, and AF with HFrEF (CASTLE-AF improves survival/HF hospitalisation). Anticoagulation continues per CHA₂DS₂-VASc regardless of success.
  • 🔒 Left atrial appendage occlusion (LAAO): for patients with a genuine long-term contraindication to anticoagulation (e.g. recurrent life-threatening bleeding). Devices (Watchman/Amulet) close the appendage — the source of >90% of AF thrombi — an alternative only when a DOAC truly can't be used, not a convenience substitute.
  • ⚗️ DOAC dosing pitfalls: renal-based dose reductions (dabigatran/edoxaban most renally cleared); edoxaban loses efficacy at very high CrCl >95; avoid strong P-gp/CYP3A4 interactions; inappropriate under-dosing is a common, dangerous error that leaves patients under-protected. Contraindicated in mechanical valves and moderate–severe mitral stenosis → use warfarin.
  • 🆘 Reversal agents: idarucizumab for dabigatran; andexanet alfa (or PCC where unavailable) for factor Xa inhibitors; vitamin K + PCC for warfarin — know these before major bleeding or urgent surgery.
  • 🤝 Integrated AF care: nurse-led, guideline-based ABC/CARE management reduces death and hospitalisation. Aggressive risk-factor modification (the LEGACY/CARDIO-FIT weight-loss data) meaningfully reduces AF burden and improves ablation success — comorbidity control is disease-modifying, not adjunctive.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Aspirin That Wasn't a Blood Thinner"

The case: A 76-year-old woman with atrial fibrillation and a CHA₂DS₂-VASc of 5 (age, hypertension, diabetes, female) was seen after an incidental AF diagnosis. Her GP worried a DOAC "might cause a bleed," so she was started on aspirin 75 mg "to thin the blood a bit." She felt well and was never re-scored. Eight months later she collapsed at home with a dense right hemiplegia and global aphasia — a massive left middle-cerebral-artery embolic stroke from left atrial appendage thrombus. She died two weeks later. Aspirin had given her the bleeding risk of an antithrombotic with almost none of the stroke protection — a DOAC would very likely have prevented the event.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

💊 WRONG DRUGAspirin used as 'stroke prevention' — ineffective in AF (AVERROES/ACTIVE-W).
🩸 FEAR OF BLEEDINGBleeding worry withheld the effective drug — DOACs bleed less intracranially than even warfarin.
🧮 SCORE IGNOREDCHA₂DS₂-VASc 5 mandated anticoagulation; the number was never acted on.
🔁 NEVER RE-SCOREDNo structured follow-up; the 'E' of CARE never happened.
🤫 FALSE REASSURANCE"She feels fine" — but AF stroke risk is silent and independent of symptoms.

🛡️ What would have saved her: calculate CHA₂DS₂-VASc and act on it; start a DOAC (preferred over warfarin, far less intracranial bleeding); understand that aspirin is not stroke prevention in AF; use HAS-BLED to fix bleeding risks (BP, alcohol, NSAIDs), not to withhold anticoagulation; and re-score at structured follow-up. This is exactly the discipline that protected the patient in Bed 4.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'I gave aspirin to be safe' has caused more AF strokes than it has ever prevented. In atrial fibrillation, aspirin is the illusion of protection — anticoagulate with a DOAC, or make a documented decision not to."
