🧮 The three questions that structure every prevention visit

Instead of the ED's "three killers," the prevention clinic runs on three questions: (1) How high is her calculated 10-year (and lifetime) risk? (2) Are there risk-enhancers or subclinical disease (CAC) that reclassify her? (3) What therapy does that risk justify — lifestyle for all, a statin and BP treatment for the high-risk, and (almost never) aspirin? Answer those three and the plan writes itself (see S14).

⚠️ The trap of "she feels well"Feeling well tells you nothing about atherosclerotic burden — plaque is silent for decades. The counterpart trap is over-reacting to one alarming number (a single high LDL) without calculating the composite risk. Quantify, then act — neither reassurance nor panic is a plan.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The cardiology resident thinks out loud at 09:52:

🧠 Frame it as a risk-matching problem, not a "should I give a pill"

"Asymptomatic, so this is primary prevention. My job is to quantify her risk with a validated calculator — SCORE2 here in Europe, or the Pooled Cohort Equations / newer PREVENT equations in the US — then apply lifestyle to everyone and add drugs by risk band. She looks borderline/intermediate on the base score, so I'll use her risk-enhancers (family history, recent smoking, central adiposity) or a coronary artery calcium score to break the tie. I am not reaching for aspirin — she has no established disease."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 📊 Calculate 10-year risk (SCORE2 / Pooled-Cohort/PREVENT) using age, sex, smoking, systolic BP and cholesterol; consider lifetime risk for communication.
  2. 🥗 Prescribe lifestyle for all — smoking cessation support, diet, exercise, weight, alcohol — regardless of the number.
  3. 🫀 Refine borderline/intermediate risk with risk-enhancing factors or a CAC score (a zero de-escalates; a high score reclassifies upward).
  4. 💊 Match drug therapy to risk — a statin (and BP treatment to target) once risk crosses the threshold.
  5. 🚫 Do NOT start routine aspirin — reserve antiplatelets for secondary prevention.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Calculate before you medicate: the risk score decides who needs a statin, and a calcium score decides the borderline cases the equation can't."
🧪 Resident's self-testThe patient asks for "a baby aspirin to be safe." Start it?

No. In primary prevention the ASPREE, ASCEND and ARRIVE trials showed the small reduction in ischaemic events is outweighed by major (GI and intracranial) bleeding — net harm in most, and clear harm in healthy older adults. Aspirin is reserved for secondary prevention (established disease). Treat her risk with lifestyle and, where indicated, a statin and BP therapy instead.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY risk-based prevention works (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧱 Atherosclerosis is lifelong & silent: LDL particles cross and are retained in the arterial intima, oxidise, and drive foam-cell and plaque formation over decades before any symptom.
  2. ➗ Risk is multiplicative: smoking, hypertension and cholesterol don't add — they multiply. A "borderline" level of each combines into substantial composite risk, which is why we calculate rather than eyeball.
  3. 🧪 Lower LDL for longer = less plaque: the arterial wall integrates cumulative LDL exposure. Lowering LDL early and durably (statins) slows and stabilises plaque — the biological basis of the CTT ~22%-per-mmol/L benefit.
  4. 🩸 Aspirin's ledger flips without established disease: with no ruptured plaque to guard against, its antithrombotic benefit shrinks while its bleeding cost stays fixed — so in primary prevention the balance tips to harm.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"When the risk is borderline, let the coronary calcium score break the tie — a zero is powerful reassurance, a high score is a call to treat."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Primary vs Secondary prevention

🟢 Primary prevention (this patient)

  • No established ASCVD — decision is risk-based
  • Lifestyle for all; statin + BP treatment for the high-risk
  • No routine aspirin (net harm — ASPREE)
  • Refine borderline cases with CAC / risk-enhancers

🔴 Secondary prevention

  • Established disease (prior MI, stroke, PAD, revascularisation)
  • High-intensity statin AND aspirin, aggressive LDL/BP targets
  • Antiplatelet benefit now clearly outweighs bleeding
  • No risk calculator needed — the event is the risk

💡 A raised LDL or a family history is not a diagnosis — it is a risk input. Ask: what is the composite risk, and does it cross the threshold to treat?

📋 Orders

🖊️ The risk-assessment order set (10:10)

  • 🧪 Fasting lipid profile (or non-fasting non-HDL/apoB), HbA1c/fasting glucose, U&E + eGFR, LFTs (statin baseline), TSH.
  • 🧬 Lipoprotein(a) once in a lifetime, plus hs-CRP where it will change management (JUPITER-style risk-enhancement).
  • 📊 Calculate 10-year risk (SCORE2 / Pooled-Cohort/PREVENT); record lifetime risk for communication.
  • 🩸 Confirm BP with home/ambulatory readings; screen for target-organ damage if hypertension is established.
  • 🫀 Coronary artery calcium (CAC) score for borderline/intermediate risk where the statin decision is uncertain.
  • 📈 Resting 12-lead ECG (baseline; look for LVH, prior silent infarct).
  • 🚫 No routine aspirin; no reflexive statin before the risk is quantified in a genuinely low-risk person.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The cheapest, highest-yield 'orders' in prevention are a validated risk score and a real conversation about lifestyle — the labs and the calcium score refine, they don't replace them."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Clinic-nurse priorities — the engine room of prevention

  • 🩸 Measure BP properly — rested, correct cuff, both arms, averaged repeats; arrange home/ambulatory readings. A mismeasured BP mis-risk-stratifies the whole visit.
  • 🚬 Deliver smoking-cessation support — very-brief advice, pharmacotherapy (NRT/varenicline) and referral; the single highest-value lifestyle intervention.
  • 🥗 Lifestyle counselling — Mediterranean-style diet, ≥150 min/week moderate activity, weight and alcohol goals; set specific, achievable targets.
  • ⚖️ Anthropometry — weight, BMI, waist circumference; track over time as an adherence/response marker.
  • 💊 Adherence & beliefs — explore statin fears and the "aspirin myth" openly; concordance is what turns a prescription into prevention.
  • 📞 Safety-net & recall — book lipid re-check and follow-up; ensure results and the CAC are actioned, not lost.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who gets someone to actually stop smoking has prevented more heart attacks than most drugs on the chart."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to follow and why

  • 🧪 Lipid response — recheck LDL/non-HDL ~6–12 weeks after starting/changing a statin; confirm the expected ≥40–50% LDL fall on high-intensity therapy.
  • 🩸 Blood pressure to target — track home readings; escalate agents until controlled (generally <130/80 in most treated patients, individualised).
  • 🧬 Adherence & tolerability — ask specifically about statin myalgia; rechallenge/switch rather than abandon; concordance is everything.
  • 🩺 LFTs / glucose — baseline and if symptomatic; no need for intensive routine liver monitoring.
  • 🔁 Reassess risk periodically — recalculate every few years or when factors change; a CAC need not be repeated frequently.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In prevention the 'monitor' is the follow-up clinic: an LDL that actually fell, a BP that actually reached target, and a patient still taking the tablet a year later."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — of the disease and of the treatment

  • 🩸 Inappropriate aspirin → major bleeding — GI and intracranial haemorrhage in primary prevention; the avoidable harm at the heart of this case.
  • 💪 Statin-associated muscle symptoms — common complaint, true myopathy rare, rhabdomyolysis very rare; usually manageable by dose/switch/rechallenge (much is nocebo).
  • 🩸 Small excess of new-onset diabetes with statins — real but clinically outweighed by CVD reduction; not a reason to withhold.
  • 🚫 Under-treatment — the genuinely high-risk patient never risk-assessed or statined who returns with an avoidable MI (the mirror-image error).
  • 😟 Over-treatment / medicalising the low-risk — polypharmacy, anxiety and side-effects in a young, low-risk person who needed only lifestyle.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Prevention has two failure modes that mirror each other: treating the low-risk into harm, and never treating the high-risk at all — the risk score is your defence against both."

📋 Primary vs secondary prevention plans — two different worlds

🟢 This patient (primary prevention)

  • Risk-matched statin + BP treatment to target; lifestyle for all
  • No aspirin — explained clearly
  • Lipid/BP recheck at 6–12 weeks; periodic risk reassessment

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • ⏳ 10-year vs lifetime risk & communication: young patients with high lifetime but low 10-year risk are under-served by decade-based thresholds; PREVENT's 30-year risk and "risk age"/decision aids help frame shared decisions and prevent both under- and over-treatment.
  • 🧬 Risk-enhancing factors: premature family history, chronic inflammatory disease (RA, SLE, psoriasis, HIV), CKD, adverse ethnicity/social deprivation, raised Lp(a) and high hs-CRP each shift a borderline patient — codified in the 2019 ACC/AHA guideline and 2021 ESC framework.
  • 🫀 CAC to reclassify: the "power of zero" (very low event rates with CAC 0 allowing statin deferral) vs a high score prompting treatment; caveats — CAC 0 does not exclude soft plaque, and it is a poor tool in the already clearly high- or low-risk.
  • 💊 Statin thresholds & shared decision-making: reconciling ESC (SCORE2 age-specific bands, LDL goals by risk category) with ACC/AHA (7.5%/20% thresholds, "risk discussion" 5–20%) — the numbers differ, the philosophy converges.
  • 🩸 Blood-pressure targets in prevention: evidence (SPRINT) and guideline movement toward lower targets (~<130/80 in many), balanced against age, frailty and polypharmacy.
  • 🚫 The ended era of routine primary-prevention aspirin: ASPREE/ASCEND/ARRIVE reversed decades of practice; residual nuance in very-high-risk younger patients after careful bleeding assessment, but the default is now "no."
  • 💠 Polypill concepts: fixed-dose statin + antihypertensives (± aspirin in secondary prevention) improve adherence and event rates in population strategies (TIPS-3, PolyIran) — a systems answer to a compliance problem.
  • 🔁 When primary becomes secondary: the moment subclinical disease becomes clinical (an event, or overt obstructive disease), the whole framework — including aspirin and aggressive targets — changes.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Baby Aspirin That Bled"

The case: A fit, independent 74-year-old woman with no cardiovascular disease mentioned at a routine check that she'd "heard aspirin is good for the heart." Without any risk assessment, she was started on daily low-dose aspirin "for prevention." Eight months later she presented pale and collapsed with a major gastrointestinal haemorrhage (an intracranial bleed is the other feared outcome), requiring transfusion and critical care. She had nothing to prevent — no established disease — and the antiplatelet gave her only its bleeding risk. The ASPREE trial had already shown precisely this: routine aspirin in healthy older adults causes net harm in primary prevention.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 OUTDATED REFLEX"Aspirin is good for the heart" applied without established disease.
📊 NO RISK ASSESSMENTNo SCORE2/PCE, no framing of benefit vs bleeding risk.
📚 EVIDENCE IGNOREDASPREE/ASCEND/ARRIVE already showed net harm in primary prevention.
🩸 BLEEDING RISK MISSEDAge itself raises GI/intracranial bleeding — the harm side of the ledger.
💬 NO SHARED DECISIONStarted reflexively, not discussed as a risk–benefit trade-off.

🛡️ What she actually needed: a proper risk assessment, lifestyle advice, and — if her calculated risk warranted it — a statin and blood-pressure treatment, not an antiplatelet with nothing to prevent. Contrast her with the mirror-image failure: a genuinely high-risk patient who is never risk-assessed or statined and returns with an avoidable MI. Both errors are prevented by the same discipline — calculate the risk, then match the therapy to it, exactly as we did in Bed 8.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'A baby aspirin for prevention' has bled more healthy people than it has ever saved — without established disease, its harm outweighs its benefit. Calculate the risk and reach for a statin, not an antiplatelet."
