🔥 Why a fast heart can be a dying heartA rate of 190 is not just uncomfortable — at very high rates diastole (the filling phase) collapses. The ventricle cannot fill, stroke volume falls, and coronary perfusion (which happens in diastole) drops just as demand soars. The result is a self-reinforcing spiral: less output → hypotension and ischaemia → more sympathetic drive → faster rate → even less filling. The rate itself becomes the killer.

🧭 The single fork that organises everything

Every tachyarrhythmia collapses to one decision tree. STABLE patients buy you time to characterise the rhythm (narrow vs wide, regular vs irregular) and choose a drug. UNSTABLE patients — any of the 4 S's — jump straight to synchronized DC cardioversion, whatever the rhythm turns out to be, because instability itself is the emergency. This patient has three adverse features (shock, presyncope, ischaemia) plus early failure. She is unmistakably unstable.

🚫 The trap of "let me just try a drug first"Reaching for adenosine or a rate-control drug in a shocked patient wastes the minutes she does not have — and if this broad irregular rhythm is pre-excited AF, an AV-nodal blocker can accelerate conduction down the accessory pathway and tip her into VF. In the unstable patient, the safe, universal answer is a synchronized shock.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY fast is dangerous, and WHY a shock fixes it

  1. ⏱️ Diastole is where filling happens: as heart rate climbs, systole barely shortens but diastole collapses. At 190/min there is almost no time for the ventricle to fill.
  2. 📉 Falling stroke volume: an under-filled ventricle ejects less per beat. Even with a huge rate, cardiac output (rate × stroke volume) falls once filling is critically impaired.
  3. 🫀 Coronary starvation: the left ventricle is perfused in diastole. Short diastole + high demand = subendocardial ischaemia → chest pain and ST changes, weakening contraction further.
  4. 🌀 The instability spiral: low output → hypotension → reflex sympathetic surge → faster rate → even less filling. Left alone it ends in shock, pulmonary oedema, or VF.
  5. ⚡ Why electricity works: a synchronized shock depolarises the whole myocardium at once, letting the sinus node re-seize control — instantly restoring an organised rhythm with proper filling. Drugs are slower and, in the wrong rhythm, dangerous.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A heart beating too fast to fill is a heart failing to pump — restore the rhythm and you restore the output; that is why the unstable patient gets a shock, not a strategy."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Synchronized cardioversion vs Unsynchronised defibrillation

🔌 Synchronized cardioversion

  • For an organised rhythm WITH a pulse (unstable VT with pulse, SVT, AF/flutter)
  • Shock is timed to the R wave to avoid the T-wave "R-on-T" and iatrogenic VF
  • Sedate the conscious patient if time allows
  • Machine reverts to unsync after each shock — re-arm SYNC every time

⚡ Unsynchronised defibrillation

  • For pulseless VT and VF, and chaotic polymorphic VT where the machine can't sync
  • Shock delivered immediately, any point in the cycle
  • Part of the cardiac-arrest algorithm with CPR
  • If your "SVT" degenerates and loses its pulse → switch to defib + CPR

💡 Same machine, two modes. The pulse and the organisation of the rhythm tell you which button to press.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (02:49)

  • 📟 Defib pads on, continuous 3-/12-lead monitoring, print a rhythm strip and a 12-lead if seconds allow.
  • ⚡ Prepare synchronized cardioversion — SYNC engaged, biphasic energy set and escalating protocol ready.
  • 💉 Procedural sedation / analgesia drawn up (e.g. ketamine or midazolam ± fentanyl) for the conscious patient — but do not delay the shock in peri-arrest.
  • 🩸 Two large-bore IV lines; bloods: U&E (K⁺, Mg²⁺), gas, glucose, FBC, troponin, TFTs; correct electrolytes.
  • 🚫 Do NOT give AV-nodal blockers (adenosine, verapamil, diltiazem, beta-blockers, digoxin) to a broad irregular rhythm — possible pre-excited AF.
  • 🫁 O₂ to keep SpO₂ ≥ 94%; sit up if pulmonary oedema; small cautious fluid only if clearly under-filled.
  • 📞 Call senior EM + cardiology early; if pulseless or degenerates → switch to the cardiac-arrest algorithm (unsync defib + CPR).

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Resus-bay priorities — the nurse runs the shock safely

  • 📟 Confirm pads are correctly placed and SYNC is on; check the monitor is marking each R wave before every shock and call "re-sync" after each one.
  • 💉 Draw up and titrate sedation; watch airway and SpO₂ through the procedure; have suction and BVM ready.
  • ⚡ Safety choreography: "charging… oxygen away… I'm clear, you're clear, everyone clear… shocking." Nobody touching the patient or bed.
  • 🩸 Cycle BP every 1–3 min; feel a central pulse after each shock — a lost pulse means switch to defib + CPR immediately.
  • 🧪 Send electrolytes fast and keep K⁺ 4.0–5.0 and Mg²⁺ replete — hypokalaemia/hypomagnesaemia lower the shock's success and invite recurrence/torsades.
  • 🧾 Time-stamp every shock, energy used, rhythm before/after, and sedation given; keep the frightened, conscious patient informed.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After every shock the defibrillator forgets — the nurse who calls 'SYNC is back on' before the next shock prevents an accidental defibrillation into the T wave."

🩻 Adjunct imaging

  • Bedside echo (POCUS): gauge LV function and filling, look for pericardial effusion or gross structural disease that changes management.
  • CXR (after stabilisation): pulmonary oedema, cardiomegaly, or a precipitant such as pneumonia.
  • Post-conversion 12-lead: the real diagnostic ECG — look for a delta wave (WPW), old infarct/scar (VT substrate), or a long QT.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📟 Continuous telemetry with defib pads on — the rhythm can recur or degenerate to VF at any moment; be ready to switch to unsync defib.
  • 🩸 BP every 1–3 min and a central pulse check after each shock — recovery of BP confirms restored output; a lost pulse means arrest.
  • 💓 Rate and rhythm before/after each shock — document conversion, recurrence, and QRS width/regularity changes.
  • 🫁 SpO₂ and respiratory rate through sedation and for evolving/receding pulmonary oedema.
  • 🧪 Serial K⁺/Mg²⁺ and a post-conversion 12-lead (delta wave? long QT? ischaemia?) to guide the definitive plan.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you convert her, the monitor's question changes from 'is she perfusing?' to 'will it come back?' — keep the pads on and the potassium topped up."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong

  • ⚡ Degeneration to VF / pulseless VT — from the arrhythmia itself, from an AV-nodal blocker in pre-excited AF, or from an unsynchronised shock landing on the T wave.
  • 💔 Cardiogenic shock / pulmonary oedema — sustained high rates that outrun the failing pump.
  • 🧠 Syncope with injury or aspiration from cerebral hypoperfusion.
  • 🫀 Post-cardioversion complications — bradyarrhythmia/asystole (especially with sick sinus), or thromboembolic stroke if AF > 48 h is cardioverted without anticoagulation (a calculated risk when the patient is unstable).
  • 💉 Sedation-related hypotension or airway compromise.
  • 🔁 Recurrence — untreated triggers (electrolytes, thyrotoxicosis, stimulants, the pathway itself) bring the rhythm straight back.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The two ways a cardioversion goes wrong are shocking off-sync into VF, and forgetting the pulse check — organised-with-a-pulse gets SYNC, no-pulse gets defib and CPR."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The disposition plan

  1. ⚡ Immediate: unstable tachyarrhythmia → synchronized DC cardioversion under sedation; convert to sinus, BP and symptoms recover.
  2. 🧪 Stabilise & correct: replace K⁺/Mg²⁺, treat precipitants (stimulants, thyroid), keep pads on for recurrence.

🏠 Discharge

📋 Two very different tachycardia dispositions

⚡ This patient (WPW / pre-excited AF)

  • Admitted, cardioverted, then ablation of the accessory pathway — definitive cure
  • Documented allergy-style alert: "avoid AV-nodal blockers" until ablated
  • Avoid stimulants/decongestants; correct electrolytes; treat thyroid if abnormal
  • Red flags: recurrent fast palpitations, syncope, chest pain → 999

🟢 The stable, self-terminating SVT

  • Converted in ED (vagal/adenosine), well and pain-free, normal post-conversion ECG
  • Teach vagal manoeuvres; discuss caffeine/alcohol/sleep triggers
  • Outpatient cardiology for consideration of ablation vs prophylaxis

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the algorithm

  • 🟥 VT vs SVT-with-aberrancy: features that argue strongly for VT — AV dissociation, capture and fusion beats, very wide QRS, extreme axis, and precordial concordance. Structured tools (Brugada algorithm, Vereckei aVR algorithm) formalise this, but the safest bedside default remains the "treat wide-and-regular as VT" rule — misclassifying VT as SVT and giving a nodal blocker is far more dangerous than the reverse.
  • ⚡ Pre-excited AF physiology: the accessory pathway lacks the AV node's decremental "brake," so it can conduct AF at rates > 250/min. Block the node and you funnel all conduction down that fast pathway → VF. Procainamide (or ibutilide) slows the pathway; cardioversion resets it.
  • 💉 Sedation for cardioversion: balancing amnesia/analgesia against haemodynamics in a shocked patient — ketamine (haemodynamically favourable), midazolam, propofol (watch hypotension), etomidate, ± short-acting opioid. Pre-oxygenate, prepare the airway, and accept lighter sedation if peri-arrest.
  • 🌪️ Polymorphic VT / electrical storm: distinguish long-QT torsades (→ magnesium, correct QT drivers, overdrive pacing, isoproterenol) from ischaemic polymorphic VT (→ revascularisation, beta-blockade). Electrical storm (≥3 episodes/24 h) may need deep sedation, IV beta-blockade (esmolol), amiodarone, stellate ganglion block, or mechanical support.
  • 🔧 Drug-refractory / recurrent arrhythmia: when repeated shocks fail or the rhythm recurs — hunt reversible drivers (ischaemia, electrolytes, hypoxia, drugs), consider antiarrhythmic loading, and escalate to EP for ablation or device therapy.
  • 🫀 Cardioversion & thromboembolism: in AF > 48 h, elective cardioversion needs anticoagulation or a TOE to exclude atrial thrombus — but in the unstable patient the shock proceeds now and anticoagulation follows, a justified acceptance of stroke risk to save a life.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Verapamil That Started a VF Arrest"

The case: A 24-year-old man arrived with palpitations and a rate of ~210. The monitor showed a broad, irregular tachycardia, but he was chatting and his BP was 105/68 — so the team called it "SVT with aberrancy" and pushed IV verapamil to slow it down. Within a minute the ventricular rate accelerated, the QRS became chaotic, he lost consciousness, and the rhythm degenerated into ventricular fibrillation. He was a routine cardioversion candidate; instead the room ran a full arrest. He survived after defibrillation and CPR — but only just, and with an avoidable ICU stay. His post-arrest ECG showed a delta wave: this had been pre-excited AF in WPW all along.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 MISREAD RHYTHMBroad + irregular called "SVT with aberrancy" instead of pre-excited AF.
💊 NODAL BLOCKERVerapamil blocked the AV node and unleashed the accessory pathway → VF.
📈 ECG NOT SOUGHTThe old WPW ECG in his record was never checked before treating.
⚖️ WRONG FRAME"He looks stable" led to reaching for a drug when the safe universal tool was a shock.
🚫 RULE IGNOREDThe "no AV-nodal blockers in wide-and-irregular" rule wasn't applied.

🛡️ What would have saved the near-miss: read the two axes — wide + irregular = think pre-excited AF and keep hands off the AV node; if unstable, synchronized cardioversion; if stable, procainamide or elective cardioversion, never verapamil/adenosine/diltiazem/beta-blocker/digoxin. And the mirror-image error — giving an unstable VT escalating drug infusions while the patient circles the drain instead of simply delivering a synchronized shock — kills the same way, by chemistry when the patient needed electricity. That discipline is exactly what saved the patient in Bed 7.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'It looked like SVT' has arrested more WPW patients than the arrhythmia itself — respect wide-and-irregular, keep off the AV node, and when in doubt, shock."
