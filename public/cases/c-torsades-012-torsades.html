🧬 Why THIS patient? — stacked QT drugs + low K⁺/Mg²⁺ + bradycardia

Torsades needs a long QT, and she has every reason to have one: two or more QT-prolonging drugs layered together, profound hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia, under-treated hypothyroidism, and a slow baseline rate. Prolonged repolarization lengthens phase 2/3 of the action potential, allowing the L-type calcium current to reactivate and fire an early afterdepolarization (EAD). Across a ventricle with heterogeneous repolarization, that triggered beat finds windows of recovery and re-entry to wander through — producing the twisting polymorphic VT. Her bradycardia and pauses make it pause-dependent: a PVC, then a compensatory pause, then a beat with an even longer QT that fires the next EAD. Several insults, one twisting storm.

🔥 Risk factors for acquired (drug-induced) long QT / Torsades 💊 QT-prolonging drugs — and especially several together · ⚡ ↓K⁺, ↓Mg²⁺, ↓Ca²⁺ · 😴 Bradycardia / AV block / pauses · 👵 Older age, female sex · 🩺 Hypothyroidism · 🫀 Heart failure / LVH · 🧠 Acute intracranial events · 🍽️ Starvation / anorexia · 🧬 Subclinical congenital LQTS unmasked by any of the above

"No. Naming the rhythm precisely changes the drug. Monomorphic VT (one fixed shape, usually scar) tolerates amiodarone. 🌀 Acquired vs congenital long QT — why the caveat matters tonight

This is acquired (drug- and electrolyte-induced) long QT, and it is pause-dependent — so isoproterenol (to raise the rate and abolish the pauses) is a legitimate bridge until transvenous pacing is in. In congenital long-QT syndrome, the mainstay is beta-blockade and you avoid isoproterenol/catecholamines, which can provoke the rhythm. Read the cause before you choose: catecholamines help the acquired pause-dependent form and harm the congenital adrenergic form.

📖 Teaching Points

🔬 The mechanism — WHY a long QT twists (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 📏 Prolonged repolarization: QT-prolonging drugs (mostly hERG/I_Kr potassium-channel blockers) plus low K⁺/Mg²⁺ lengthen phase 2/3 of the action potential. The cell takes too long to repolarize → a long QT.
  2. ⚡ Early afterdepolarizations (EADs): during that prolonged plateau, the L-type calcium current reactivates, producing a secondary upstroke — an EAD. If it reaches threshold, it fires a triggered beat. This is the spark.
  3. 🌀 Heterogeneous repolarization → twisting re-entry: repolarization is uneven across the ventricular wall (transmural dispersion). The triggered beat from an EAD lands in tissue that has recovered unevenly, so functional re-entry wanders and the QRS axis twists around the baseline — polymorphic VT.
  4. 😴 Pause-dependence (the long–short sequence): a PVC → a compensatory pause → the next sinus beat has an even longer QT → the EAD on that beat fires Torsades. Bradycardia and pauses are therefore the trigger, which is why raising the rate (pacing/isoproterenol) abolishes them.
  5. 💥 Why it kills: most runs self-terminate, but each is a near-miss; a run can sustain or degenerate into VF — disorganized, pulseless, fatal in minutes. Magnesium suppresses the EADs; a faster rate shortens the QT and removes the pauses; stopping the drugs and refilling K⁺ removes the substrate.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Torsades is a long action potential that reignites itself: a long QT lets calcium current reactivate (the EAD), and a ventricle that recovers unevenly lets the spark twist. Magnesium quiets the spark; speed shortens the fuse."

⚖️ Contrast pair #1 — Torsades vs polymorphic VT with NORMAL QT (the triage that changes everything)

🌀 Torsades — long QT (tonight)

  • QT markedly prolonged; QRS twists around the baseline
  • Trigger: QT drugs, ↓K⁺/↓Mg²⁺, bradycardia/pauses, congenital LQTS
  • Therapy: IV magnesium, K⁺ to high-normal, stop QT drugs, pace/isoproterenol
  • Avoid amiodarone (worsens the QT)

❤️‍🔥 Polymorphic VT — normal QT

  • QRS varies but the QT is normal
  • Trigger: acute ischemia / infarction
  • Therapy: revascularize (cath lab); standard antiarrhythmics/beta-blocker acceptable
  • Magnesium is not the headline; fix the coronary

😈 The trap: both look like a chaotic, twisting VT on the monitor. If you do not measure the QT you cannot tell them apart — and the treatments diverge completely (magnesium-and-pace vs cath lab). The QT interval is the fork in the road.

⚖️ Contrast pair #2 — Acquired vs Congenital long QT (the isoproterenol caveat)

💊 Acquired long QT (tonight)

  • Cause: drugs + ↓K⁺/↓Mg²⁺ + bradycardia; often pause-dependent
  • Isoproterenol and overdrive pacing are OK — they raise the rate and abolish the pauses
  • Remove the offending drugs and correct electrolytes

🧬 Congenital long QT (LQTS)

  • Cause: inherited channelopathy (LQT1/2/3); adrenergically driven
  • Mainstay: beta-blockers; AVOID isoproterenol/catecholamines
  • Consider ICD, left cardiac sympathetic denervation

💡 Why it matters: the same drug (isoproterenol) that rescues acquired pause-dependent Torsades can provoke the congenital adrenergic form. Establish whether the long QT is acquired before you give a catecholamine.

🔬 Fellowship Corner — Advanced Topics

  • 🧬 Congenital LQTS subtypes: LQT1 (I_Ks, exertion/swimming-triggered, beta-blocker-responsive), LQT2 (I_Kr/hERG, auditory/startle and postpartum triggers), LQT3 (SCN5A late sodium current, rest/sleep events, mexiletine may help). Genotype guides triggers and therapy.
  • 📏 QT measurement pitfalls: use Bazett vs Fridericia at extremes of rate, measure in lead II/V5, "tangent method" for the T-wave end, beware U waves merging into the T, and beware QT measured immediately after a pause (it looks longest there — which is the point).
  • 💊 Mechanism of drug-induced LQT: most culprits block the rapid delayed-rectifier K⁺ current (I_Kr / hERG); risk multiplies with hypokalemia (which itself reduces I_Kr), hypomagnesemia, bradycardia, hepatic/renal impairment and CYP interactions that raise drug levels.
  • 😴 Pause-dependent vs adrenergic-dependent Torsades: acquired/bradycardia-related is pause-dependent (pacing/isoproterenol help); congenital adrenergic LQTS is catecholamine-dependent (beta-blockade, avoid isoproterenol). The cause dictates whether you raise or block sympathetic tone.
  • 🔌 Temporary pacing options: transvenous overdrive pacing to ~90–110 is definitive for pause-dependence; transcutaneous pacing bridges; atrial pacing preserves AV synchrony. Isoproterenol is a pharmacologic bridge in acquired LQT only.
  • 🧪 Short-QT and other repolarization syndromes: contrast with short-QT syndrome and Brugada — high-yield channelopathy patterns that also cause polymorphic VT but with the opposite or normal QT.

🛏️ Telemetry nursing priorities — minute by minute

  • 📟 Continuous telemetry with the defibrillator at the bedside, pads attached and set to UNSYNCHRONIZED. Capture every run on a printed strip — the QT and the onset pattern are gold for the EP team.
  • 🧪 Have magnesium drawn up and ready; give the 2 g as prescribed and prepare repeat doses — magnesium is the first drug to reach for, not the defibrillator (unless pulseless).
  • ⚡ Run potassium repletion as prescribed on a monitored line; double-check concentrations and rates — potassium errors are themselves arrhythmogenic and lethal.
  • 🚫 Guard the drug chart: ensure the QT-prolonging drugs stay stopped; question any new order for ondansetron, macrolides, fluoroquinolones, antipsychotics or other QT-prolongers.
  • 😴 Watch the rate and the pauses: bradycardia and pauses precede the next run. Set up for transvenous/transcutaneous pacing and have isoproterenol ready as ordered.
  • 🧠 Neuro/consciousness checks — she drops out during each run; a patient who slumps may have lost the pulse → check it, don't assume, and be ready to defibrillate.
  • 📋 Document the QTc, the rhythm strip and the time before and after each intervention, plus the energy delivered for any shock.
  • 👨‍👩‍👦 Keep the family updated and supported (see S16) — repeated collapses are terrifying to witness.

🫀 Post-stabilization 12-lead & echo

  • 📈 Post-treatment 12-lead: after magnesium, potassium repletion and pacing to ~95/min, the QTc shortens toward 440 ms and the runs stop — the rate-dependence confirmed in front of you.
  • 🩻 Echo: structurally near-normal heart, preserved EF, no regional wall-motion abnormality — consistent with a repolarization problem, not scar VT or ischemia.

🏠 Substrate-modifying therapy (this admission, not just tonight)

  • 📋 Permanent medication review: document a hard allergy/avoid list of QT-prolongers; cross-check every future drug against a credible QT-drug list (e.g. CredibleMeds).
  • 💧 Revisit the diuretics that drove chronic hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia; add replacement or a potassium-sparing strategy and a monitoring plan.
  • 🩺 Optimize thyroid replacement — correcting hypothyroidism helps normalize the QT and rate.
  • 🧬 If the QT stays long after everything is corrected → evaluate for congenital LQTS (beta-blocker, possible ICD, family screening).

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📏 QTc on every 12-lead — the single most important number; track it down toward < 440 ms as you correct electrolytes, stop drugs and pace. A QTc that won't shorten suggests a congenital component.
  • 📈 Continuous telemetry with the defibrillator at the bedside (UNSYNCHRONIZED-ready) until well controlled; print every run and note its onset (long–short?).
  • ⚡ Serial K⁺, Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺ (e.g. q2–4h initially) — the most modifiable drivers; keep K⁺ 4.5–5.0, Mg²⁺ > 1.0.
  • 😴 Heart rate and pauses — watch for the bradycardia/pauses that precede a run; confirm the pacing rate is holding ~90–110 if pacing.
  • 💊 Medication reconciliation — actively monitor that no QT-prolonging drug is restarted anywhere on the chart.
  • 🩺 Cause monitoring — TSH, renal function (drug clearance), treatment of the infection/dehydration that started the cascade.
  • 🔌 If a temporary pacing wire is in: monitor for capture, sensing and the rate set to suppress pauses.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you break Torsades, the monitor's job is to answer two questions: is the QT shortening, and are the pauses gone? Those two — QTc and rate — tell you whether the rhythm is coming back."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — and the mechanism

  • ⚡ Degeneration to VF / cardiac arrest — the great fear; a run that doesn't self-terminate organizes into sustained Torsades then VF. Treatment pivots to UNSYNC defibrillation + CPR + magnesium.
  • 🌀 Incessant Torsades — back-to-back runs, often provoked by giving a QT-prolonging antiarrhythmic (amiodarone) or by failing to remove the offending drug.
  • 🧠 Anoxic injury / trauma from recurrent syncope — repeated collapses cause falls, aspiration and cerebral hypoperfusion.
  • 😴 Bradycardia/pause complications — the slow rate that drives the rhythm; over-aggressive AV-nodal slowing or further bradycardia worsens it.
  • 💊 Iatrogenic QT prolongation — the very drug reached for ("it's VT") lengthens the QT and worsens the Torsades; electrolyte errors during repletion are also arrhythmogenic.
  • 🫀 Recurrence — if the offending drugs aren't stopped and K⁺/Mg²⁺ aren't corrected (and the pauses not paced out), Torsades returns.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference — "The QT Nobody Measured"

The case: A 72-year-old woman, admitted with vomiting and a chest infection, was on ondansetron and a macrolide with a potassium of 2.7 and an un-checked magnesium. She had recurrent self-terminating polymorphic VT and recurrent syncope. A tired night team called it "VT" and gave IV amiodarone 300 mg — without measuring the QT or stopping the offending drugs. The QT lengthened further, the Torsades became incessant, and she arrested. A prolonged resuscitation followed. She did not survive. The pre-treatment strip, projected at the M&M, showed a textbook long QT with a long–short, pause-dependent onset.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

📏 QT NEVER MEASUREDThe long QT — the whole diagnosis — was on the strip and unread.
💊 DRUGS NOT STOPPEDOndansetron + macrolide + diuretics kept the QT long throughout.
🧪 LETHAL DRUGIV amiodarone prolonged the QT → incessant Torsades → arrest.
⚡ ELECTROLYTES IGNOREDK⁺ 2.7 and unchecked Mg²⁺ — the fuse left burning.
🌙 SYSTEMNight fatigue, no senior review, no "measure the QT first" default.

🛡️ What would have saved her: measure the QT on every polymorphic VT; give magnesium first (even at a normal level); stop every QT-prolonging drug; correct K⁺/Mg²⁺; raise the rate to abolish pauses; and never give a QT-prolonging antiarrhythmic to a long-QT rhythm. This is exactly why your nurse held the amiodarone tonight.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'It's just VT, load amiodarone' is the sentence written on the chart of patients who then die of Torsades. Measure the QT before you name the rhythm."
