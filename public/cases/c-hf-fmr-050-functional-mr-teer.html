🧭 The three questions that frame every MR

For any significant MR ask: (1) Primary or secondary? (valve disease vs ventricle/atrium disease); (2) Acute or chronic? (a new flail/papillary rupture with flash oedema behaves very differently from years of remodelling); (3) How severe, and is it proportionate to the ventricle? These three answers decide whether the next move is a diuretic, an ACE-up-titration, a surgeon, or an interventional Heart Team.

⚠️ The trap of "severe MR = operate"In primary MR, severe + symptomatic (or with LV/atrial consequences) usually means fix the valve. In secondary MR, "severe" is often a consequence of the failing ventricle — and unloading the ventricle can drop the MR grade without any device. Never let the word "severe" bypass the primary-vs-secondary question.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The cardiology resident thinks out loud on the round:

🧠 Frame it as ventricle-first, valve-second

"Structurally normal leaflets, big dilated LV, EF 30%, tethered coaptation, dilated annulus — this is secondary (functional) MR, Carpentier type IIIb. The valve is the victim, not the culprit. My job isn't to book a MitraClip; it's to make sure the ventricle is fully treated first: the four pillars of GDMT at target and CRT for his LBBB. Then re-image. If severe symptomatic MR persists on genuinely optimised therapy, then the Heart Team considers TEER."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 💊 Optimise four-pillar GDMT — ARNI (or ACEi/ARB), beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2 inhibitor — up-titrated to target/maximally tolerated.
  2. 🔌 Assess CRT — LBBB with QRS 148 ms + EF ≤35% + symptoms → strong CRT indication; resynchronisation itself reduces functional MR.
  3. 💧 Decongest — loop diuretic to euvolaemia; treat the current congestion.
  4. 🔁 Re-image at 3–6 months on optimised therapy — quantify residual MR (EROA, RegVol) and grade proportionate vs disproportionate.
  5. 👥 Heart Team referral only for persistent severe symptomatic secondary MR meeting COAPT-type criteria.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Optimise the medicine before you offer the metal. A MitraClip in a patient who was never on four-pillar therapy is a MITRA-FR failure waiting to happen."
🧪 Resident's self-testThe referral says "severe MR — book the MitraClip now." He is on ramipril alone, no CRT. Proceed straight to TEER?

No. He has never had optimised GDMT (single-agent ACEi only) or CRT. Guidelines and the COAPT experience require maximally tolerated GDMT first; unloading the ventricle may itself reduce the MR. Proceeding now risks a procedure with the outcome of MITRA-FR — cost, risk, and an untreated ventricle. Optimise, resynchronise, re-assess, then decide.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the valve leaks when the leaflets are normal

  1. 🧱 LV injury & remodelling: after MI (or in dilated cardiomyopathy) the LV dilates and becomes spherical instead of elliptical.
  2. 📍 Papillary muscle displacement: the remodelled ventricle drags the papillary muscles apically and laterally, away from the annulus.
  3. 🪢 Leaflet tethering (tenting): the chordae pull the otherwise-normal leaflets down into the ventricle — they can't rise to meet in the annular plane. This is Carpentier type IIIb (restricted systolic motion).
  4. ⭕ Annular dilatation: the mitral annulus stretches and flattens, so the coaptation surface shrinks further.
  5. 💧 Loss of coaptation → regurgitation: the leaflets don't close → systolic backflow into the left atrium → volume overload → more LV dilatation → more MR. A vicious cycle: MR begets MR.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Functional MR is a leak of geometry, not of tissue — the leaflets are normal but the ventricle has moved the goalposts. Move the ventricle back (unload it) and the leak shrinks."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Primary (degenerative) vs Secondary (functional) MR

🩹 Primary / degenerative MR

  • The valve apparatus is diseased: prolapse, flail, ruptured chord, myxomatous leaflets
  • Carpentier type II (excess motion)
  • Treatment = fix the valve — surgical repair (or replacement), or TEER if high surgical risk

🎈 Secondary / functional MR

  • The valve is normal; the ventricle (or atrium) is diseased
  • Carpentier type I (annular, atrial) or IIIb (tethering, ventricular)
  • Treatment = fix the ventricle first — GDMT + CRT — then TEER in selected persistent severe MR

💡 The single most consequential distinction in all of mitral disease: whose fault is the leak — the valve's or the ventricle's? It changes everything downstream.

🎮 Simulation — LV Size vs Regurgitant Orifice (the COAPT vs MITRA-FR Dial)

Drag the effective regurgitant orifice area (EROA). Imagine the LV is only moderately dilated (COAPT-like). Watch how the balance of a big orifice against a not-huge ventricle — disproportionate MR on optimised GDMT — is exactly where TEER helps, while a small orifice in a giant end-stage ventricle (proportionate MR) does not benefit.

Set the dial to explore proportionate vs disproportionate MR.

🎯 A big EROA relative to a not-hugely-dilated LV, on optimised GDMT = disproportionate MR = the COAPT sweet-spot where TEER improves outcomes. A modest EROA in a massively dilated end-stage LV = proportionate MR = MITRA-FR territory, where TEER does not help.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The admission order set

  • 💧 IV loop diuretic to decongest to euvolaemia; daily weights, strict fluid balance, restrict salt.
  • 💊 Start / up-titrate four-pillar GDMT: ARNI (or ACEi/ARB), beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2 inhibitor — increase as BP/renal function/potassium allow.
  • 🫀 Transthoracic echo with full MR quantification (EROA, regurgitant volume/fraction, vena contracta, LV volumes, tenting) — repeat after optimisation.
  • 🔌 CRT work-up: confirm LBBB QRS ≥130 ms, EF ≤35%, sinus rhythm → device clinic referral.
  • 🧪 Bloods: NT-proBNP, U&E, eGFR, FBC, iron studies (treat iron deficiency), TFT, HbA1c, lipids.
  • 📈 12-lead ECG; consider coronary re-assessment for viable ischaemic territory.
  • 👥 Do NOT refer for TEER/surgery yet — reassess MR after GDMT ± CRT optimisation; Heart Team only for persistent severe symptomatic secondary MR.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The first order in severe functional MR is a prescription, not a procedure — decongest, up-titrate the four pillars, resynchronise, then re-image."

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Heart-failure ward priorities — the nurse drives decongestion & titration safety

  • ⚖️ Daily weights, strict fluid balance, and symptom checks — the truest measure of whether decongestion is working.
  • 💊 Support GDMT up-titration: monitor BP, heart rate, potassium and renal function so the four pillars can be pushed to target safely.
  • 🫁 Watch respiratory status, orthopnoea and SpO₂; sit upright, oxygen only if hypoxic.
  • 🩸 Monitor for over-diuresis (hypotension, rising creatinine, hypokalaemia) — the balance point of congestion vs perfusion.
  • 🧾 Educate: salt/fluid awareness, medication adherence, daily weight with a "call if +2 kg in 3 days" safety-net.
  • 👥 Prepare the patient and family for the plan: optimise medicines, consider a CRT device, then re-scan — a device for the valve is a later, conditional step.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who gets a functional-MR patient to true euvolaemia on four-pillar therapy has already treated most of the MR before any interventionist is called."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • ⚖️ Weight, fluid balance, congestion signs daily — the front-line marker of decongestion and diuretic dose.
  • 🩸 U&E, eGFR, potassium during GDMT up-titration — the limiting factors for reaching target doses.
  • 🫀 Serial echo — re-quantify MR (EROA, RegVol) and LV volumes/EF after optimisation ± CRT; this is the decision point for TEER.
  • 🧪 NT-proBNP trend and functional class (NYHA, 6-minute walk) to gauge response.
  • 📈 Rhythm monitoring — new AF worsens functional MR and may drive an atrial component; rate/rhythm control matters.
  • 🔌 Post-CRT: check biventricular pacing %, response, and re-assess MR before any valve decision.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In functional MR the monitor of record is the follow-up echo on optimised therapy — the MR grade you treat is the one that persists AFTER the ventricle has been fully unloaded."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — disease and procedure

  • 💔 Progressive HF & recurrent hospitalisation — the natural history of untreated severe secondary MR; MR begets MR.
  • 🫁 Pulmonary hypertension & right heart failure — chronic LA volume overload transmitted backward.
  • ⚡ Atrial fibrillation — worsens MR (annular/atrial), reduces filling, raises stroke risk.
  • 💀 Sudden cardiac death — the HFrEF substrate; consider ICD/CRT-D indications.
  • 🔧 TEER-specific: residual/recurrent MR, single-leaflet device attachment, leaflet injury, device embolisation, iatrogenic mitral stenosis (raised gradient), transseptal/vascular complications, tamponade.
  • 🩹 The system complication: clipping too early (before GDMT) — a MITRA-FR outcome: risk without benefit, ventricle untreated.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The worst complication of functional MR isn't on the echo — it's a device deployed into a ventricle that was never optimised. You can't clip your way out of untreated heart failure."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🎈 Diagnosis: severe secondary (functional) MR, ischaemic cardiomyopathy, EF 30%, NYHA III — Carpentier IIIb tethering.
  2. 💊 Optimise the ventricle first: decongest to euvolaemia; up-titrate all four GDMT pillars to target/maximally tolerated.
  3. 🔌 Resynchronise: CRT for LBBB 148 ms + EF ≤35% + symptoms — reduces functional MR in itself.
  4. 🔁 Re-assess at 3–6 months on optimised therapy — repeat echo; grade residual MR and proportionality.
  5. 👥 Heart Team: if severe symptomatic secondary MR persists and criteria are met → transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER/MitraClip); surgery for selected patients (esp. if revascularisation needed).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Secondary MR is a Heart-Team decision made twice: first the whole team optimises the ventricle, and only if the leak survives that does the team decide on a clip."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Two very different discharge worlds

🎈 This patient (secondary MR, post-TEER)

  • Continue four-pillar GDMT at target; CRT-D optimised
  • Post-TEER antithrombotics per protocol; echo surveillance of clip result & gradient
  • HF nurse follow-up, weights, fluid/salt advice, cardiac rehab

  • Not a "GDMT-first" pathway — the valve was the disease

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🪢 Mechanism in depth: LV remodelling → spherical geometry → apical/lateral papillary-muscle displacement → chordal leaflet tethering (tenting height/area) + mitral annular dilatation & flattening → loss of coaptation. Carpentier IIIb (restricted systolic motion). Regional wall-motion (basal-inferior after RCA/circumflex MI) drives asymmetric tethering.
  • 📐 Proportionate vs disproportionate MR: the Grayburn/Packer framework reconciling COAPT and MITRA-FR. When EROA is small relative to a very large LVEDV, the MR is "proportionate" to the failing ventricle (MITRA-FR) — treat the ventricle. When EROA is large relative to a not-hugely-dilated LV, the MR is "disproportionate" (COAPT) — an extra driver of decompensation that TEER can correct on top of optimised GDMT.
  • 🫧 Atrial functional MR: AF and HFpEF cause left atrial and mitral annular dilatation with normal LV size/function and normal leaflet motion (Carpentier type I). Distinct from ventricular FMR; managed by treating AF/HFpEF (rhythm/rate, decongestion); the role and best technique of transcatheter therapy are still being defined.
  • 🔧 TEER technique & complications: transseptal, transcatheter edge-to-edge leaflet grasping (MitraClip/PASCAL) creating a double-orifice valve. Watch for residual/recurrent MR, single-leaflet device attachment, leaflet injury, device embolisation, iatrogenic mitral stenosis (mean gradient), and access/tamponade complications. Success needs adequate coaptation length, limited tenting, and a mid-leaflet (A2–P2) jet.
  • 🔪 Surgery in secondary MR: restrictive (undersized) annuloplasty has high MR recurrence; the CTSN trial showed replacement is more durable than repair for severe ischaemic MR, with no clear survival difference. Isolated mitral surgery has never shown a survival benefit in secondary MR — hence GDMT-first and TEER for selected patients. Consider combined surgery when CABG is otherwise indicated.
  • 👥 The Heart Team: HF cardiologist, imaging cardiologist, interventional cardiologist and cardiac surgeon jointly confirm optimised GDMT, mechanism, proportionality, anatomy, frailty and goals before committing to any valve intervention.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Clip Before the Cure"

The case: A 71-year-old man with non-ischaemic dilated cardiomyopathy, EF 28% and a loud pansystolic murmur was fast-tracked to a MitraClip for "severe MR." At the time he was on ramipril monotherapy — no beta-blocker, no MRA, no SGLT2 inhibitor — and had never been assessed for CRT despite a broad LBBB. His echo showed a massively dilated LV (LVEDVi ~150 mL/m²) with an EROA of only ~0.22 cm² — proportionate MR in an end-stage ventricle: a textbook MITRA-FR patient. The clip was placed. It reduced the MR one grade, carried a real procedural risk, and changed nothing about his outcome — his untreated ventricular disease marched on and he was re-admitted twice more before dying of progressive heart failure.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🏷️ WRONG FRAMESecondary MR treated as if it were primary — fixing the valve instead of the ventricle.
💊 GDMT SKIPPEDClipped on ACEi monotherapy; the four pillars and CRT were never delivered.
📐 NO PROPORTIONALITYSmall EROA in a huge LV = proportionate MR — the MITRA-FR phenotype that doesn't benefit.
👥 NO HEART TEAMFast-tracked on a report, without a team confirming selection criteria.
🔪 VENTRICLE UNTREATEDThe device addressed the leak but not the failing ventricle underneath.

🛡️ What would have helped: name the mechanism (secondary MR → fix the ventricle); deliver optimised four-pillar GDMT and CRT first; re-image and quantify proportionality; and let the Heart Team apply COAPT-type criteria before any clip. Had his MR remained severe and disproportionate on optimised therapy, he'd have been the right patient to clip — like the COAPT-type man in Bed 8, who was optimised first and then thrived. Same operation, opposite outcomes — because the patients were different.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A clip cannot cure a ventricle. Optimise and resynchronise first; if severe disproportionate MR survives that, the right patient is standing in front of you — and only then does the device earn its place."
