🔎 Why comorbidities dominate the HF storyModern HFrEF care has four pillars (ARNI/ACEi-ARB, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i) that transform prognosis — but two-thirds of HF patients carry three or more comorbidities, and those comorbidities drive most hospitalisations, symptoms and deaths. Iron deficiency alone is present in roughly half of HF patients. The pump is often the easy part; the comorbidities are the difference between a life reclaimed and a revolving door.

🧭 The comorbidity checklist you run at every HF review

Every HFrEF review deserves a systematic sweep of the outcome-deciders: iron studies (ferritin + TSAT), rhythm & rate (AF control), glycaemia (SGLT2i status), renal function & potassium (are the RAASi/MRA at target?), and sleep-disordered breathing. Name the comorbidity, then treat it.

🧪 Resident's self-testShe's tempted to prescribe oral ferrous sulfate "because it's simpler and cheaper." Correct in HF?

No. Oral iron does not work in heart failure — the IRONOUT-HF trial showed high-dose oral iron failed to improve exercise capacity or replete iron stores. The inflammatory HF state raises hepcidin, which blocks gut iron absorption and iron release from stores. Correct HF iron deficiency with intravenous iron (ferric carboxymaltose/derisomaltose).

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY iron deficiency saps a heart-failure patient (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. ⚙️ Iron is the engine's spark: iron is a cofactor in mitochondrial electron-transport enzymes and myoglobin. Cardiac and skeletal muscle are iron-hungry, high-energy tissues.
  2. 🔋 Deficiency starves the mitochondria: without adequate iron, oxidative phosphorylation falters — muscles fatigue early, contractile and skeletal-muscle energetics fall, exercise capacity drops. This happens before anaemia.
  3. 🩸 Absolute vs functional: absolute = empty stores (low ferritin). Functional = adequate stores but iron locked away and unavailable (normal-ish ferritin, low TSAT) — driven by inflammation.
  4. 🚪 Why oral fails: HF is an inflammatory state → high hepcidin → ferroportin degraded → gut absorption blocked and stored iron trapped in macrophages. Swallowed iron simply cannot get in. IV iron bypasses the gate.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Iron runs the mitochondria of the failing heart and its muscles — deficiency drains the patient even with a normal haemoglobin, and only intravenous iron can refill the tank."

⚖️ Contrast pair — IV iron vs oral iron in HF

🚫 Oral iron (ineffective in HF)

  • Blocked by hepcidin in the inflammatory HF state
  • IRONOUT-HF: no gain in exercise capacity or iron stores
  • GI side-effects, poor adherence

✅ IV iron (works)

  • Ferric carboxymaltose / derisomaltose bypasses gut absorption
  • FAIR-HF/CONFIRM-HF: better symptoms, 6MWT, QoL
  • AFFIRM-AHF/IRONMAN: fewer HF hospitalisations

💡 In heart failure, the route is the therapy. "Iron" written on a chart means nothing unless it goes in a vein.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The comorbidity order set (10:20)

  • 🧲 IV iron — ferric carboxymaltose (or derisomaltose), dose by weight/Hb deficit; recheck iron studies before repeat dosing. Not oral iron.
  • 💢 AF rate/rhythm: optimise bisoprolol; cardiology referral for AF ablation assessment (EF 32%). Do NOT prescribe verapamil/diltiazem. Digoxin only if needed and cautiously.
  • 🩸 SGLT2 inhibitor (dapagliflozin 10 mg or empagliflozin 10 mg) — for HF and diabetes; counsel on sick-day rules/euglycaemic DKA.
  • 🧪 Restart the MRA (spironolactone/eplerenone) with a potassium binder (patiromer or sodium zirconium cyclosilicate); repeat U&E in 3–7 days.
  • 😴 Refer for sleep study; CPAP if obstructive. No adaptive servo-ventilation if central sleep apnoea + reduced EF (SERVE-HF).
  • 🧾 Confirm the four HFrEF pillars are all present and up-titrated to maximum tolerated; continue apixaban for AF.
  • 📞 HF nurse follow-up; screen for depression, frailty and polypharmacy.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The cheapest wins in heart failure are often an iron infusion, an SGLT2 tablet, and a potassium binder that keeps the MRA on board — not another loop-diuretic dose."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ HF-ward priorities — the nurse runs the comorbidity safety net

  • 🧲 Administer IV iron safely: observe for infusion reactions, watch the cannula site (extravasation of ferric carboxymaltose causes brown skin staining), have anaphylaxis kit to hand, document the dose and next-due.
  • 💢 Apical–radial pulse for AF: count the apical rate (a radial-only count misses the pulse deficit) and report rates persistently >110 or symptomatic palpitations.
  • 🧪 Track potassium and renal function around MRA restart + binder; flag K⁺ trends before they force a drug stop.
  • 🩸 SGLT2i counselling: genital hygiene, sick-day rules, recognise euglycaemic DKA symptoms; a small early dip in eGFR is expected and not a reason to stop.
  • 🚶 Functional baseline: record a 6-minute-walk / how-far-can-you-go before IV iron so improvement is measurable.
  • 😴 Take a sleep history from patient and bed-partner (snoring, apnoeas, somnolence); support CPAP set-up if prescribed.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who counts the apical rate, watches the potassium, and measures how far he can walk before and after his iron is tracking the comorbidities that actually decide his year."

📈 ECG / Imaging + Visual Enhancements

🫀 The rhythm strip — atrial fibrillation, and why it hurts a failing heart

Atrial fibrillation: absent P waves, an irregularly irregular R–R, and a fine fibrillatory baseline. Lose the atrial kick and let the rate run fast and irregular, and a stiff failing ventricle fills and ejects even less.

🔎 The AF–HF vicious cycle you must know cold

MechanismEffect on the failing heartImplication
💔 Loss of atrial kickUp to ~20% of ventricular filling lost — worse in a stiff/failing ventricle dependent on late diastolic fillingRhythm control may recover meaningful output.
🏃 Fast, irregular rateShortened diastole cuts filling and coronary perfusion; irregularity itself impairs stroke volumeRate-control target, but not with negative inotropes.
🔁 Tachycardia-mediated cardiomyopathyChronic uncontrolled rates can themselves depress EF — sometimes reversibleRestoring rhythm/rate can improve EF.
🩸 ThromboembolismStasis in fibrillating atria → stroke riskAnticoagulate per CHA₂DS₂-VASc (he is on apixaban).
🚫 Non-DHP CCB trapVerapamil/diltiazem add negative inotropy to a failing ventricleContraindicated in HFrEF — use beta-blockers ± cautious digoxin.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Atrial fibrillation and heart failure feed each other — control the rhythm or rate wisely, consider ablation when the EF is low, and never rate-control with verapamil or diltiazem."

🩻 Adjunct imaging

  • Echocardiogram: EF 32%, dilated LV, moderate functional MR, LA enlargement (the substrate for AF) — no new regional change or effusion.
  • CXR: cardiomegaly, no acute pulmonary oedema — consistent with euvolaemia and chronic disease.

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🧲 Iron studies — recheck ferritin/TSAT before repeat IV dosing (typically re-evaluate at ~8–12 weeks); track symptoms and 6-minute-walk for response.
  • 🧪 Potassium & renal function — after MRA restart + binder and after SGLT2i start; a small eGFR dip with SGLT2i is expected, not a stop signal.
  • 💢 Heart rate/rhythm — apical rate in AF; assess for symptomatic tachycardia and response to beta-blocker/ablation.
  • 🩸 Glucose/HbA1c and sick-day education for the SGLT2 inhibitor.
  • 😴 Sleep-study result — obstructive (CPAP) vs central (avoid ASV); daytime somnolence and overnight oximetry as follow-up markers.
  • 📉 NT-proBNP & weight — trend for decompensation; screen mood and frailty at each review.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you treat the comorbidities, the monitor's question becomes: is his iron staying up, his potassium safe on the MRA, his rate controlled, and his walk getting longer?"
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — comorbidity edition

  • 💥 Decompensation from a non-DHP CCB — verapamil/diltiazem given for AF rate control tip a failing ventricle into pulmonary oedema (the M&M case).
  • 🫁 Excess mortality from ASV in central sleep apnoea + reduced EF (SERVE-HF) — a "treatment" that harms.
  • 📉 GDMT withdrawal spiral — stopping RAASi/MRA for a mild potassium rise loses prognostic benefit; the patient deteriorates from under-treatment.
  • 🧲 Untreated iron deficiency — persistent fatigue, poor exercise capacity, avoidable HF hospitalisations.
  • 🩸 Hyperkalaemia from RAASi/MRA in CKD — real, but manageable with binders, diet and monitoring rather than drug abandonment.
  • 💊 SGLT2i-related euglycaemic DKA and genital infections; polypharmacy interactions and adherence failure as the silent system complication.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The deadliest complications here are iatrogenic — the wrong AF drug, the wrong sleep device, and the life-saving drug stopped for a number that a binder would have fixed."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🧲 Iron deficiency — definition & mechanism: absolute (depleted stores, low ferritin) vs functional (adequate stores but iron sequestered, normalish ferritin + low TSAT). Iron is central to mitochondrial electron transport, myoglobin and aconitase in cardiac and skeletal muscle — deficiency impairs oxidative energetics and exercise capacity before anaemia, which is why symptoms and 6MWT improve with repletion independent of Hb.
  • 🚪 Why IV not oral: HF is a chronic inflammatory state → IL-6 drives hepatic hepcidin → ferroportin internalised → enteral absorption and macrophage iron release blocked. Oral iron cannot overcome this (IRONOUT-HF). IV iron delivers directly to transferrin/stores. Watch FCM-induced hypophosphataemia (FGF23 effect).
  • 💢 AF–HF interplay: rate vs rhythm control; AF ablation in HFrEF (CASTLE-AF mortality/HF-hospitalisation benefit; CASTLE-HTx in advanced HF/transplant candidates). Avoid non-DHP CCBs (negative inotropy); be cautious with digoxin (narrow therapeutic index, observational mortality signals) — reserve as add-on. Rule out tachycardia-mediated cardiomyopathy.
  • 🩸 SGLT2 inhibitors across the spectrum: benefit in HFrEF and HFpEF, in diabetes and CKD (DAPA-CKD/EMPA-KIDNEY), and post-acute HF (SOLOIST/EMPULSE). Mechanisms beyond glucose: natriuresis, improved cardiac energetics, reduced preload/afterload.
  • 🧪 Hyperkalaemia management to preserve GDMT: the MRA and RAASi are prognostic — abandoning them for a mild K⁺ rise costs lives. Potassium binders (patiromer, sodium zirconium cyclosilicate) plus dietary counselling and loop diuretics enable up-titration (OPAL-HK, DIAMOND). Distinguish spurious/haemolysed samples.
  • 😴 Sleep-disordered breathing: obstructive (upper-airway collapse → CPAP helps) vs central/Cheyne–Stokes (unstable ventilatory control in HF). ASV is harmful in predominant central SA with reduced EF (SERVE-HF increased mortality) — do not use. Optimise HF therapy first.
  • 🧩 The rest of the comorbidity burden: depression (worsens adherence/outcomes — screen and treat), frailty and sarcopenia (cardiac rehab, nutrition), and polypharmacy (deprescribe, check interactions) — each independently shapes prognosis.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Rate-Control That Drowned Him"

The case: A 66-year-old man with ischaemic HFrEF (EF 30%) and permanent atrial fibrillation attended clinic exhausted, with a rate in the 100s. To "control the rate," he was started on verapamil. Within days his failing ventricle — now burdened by the drug's negative inotropy — decompensated, and he was admitted in frank pulmonary oedema. On review: his iron had never been checked (ferritin 22, severely deficient), and his ACE inhibitor had been quietly down-titrated then stopped months earlier for a potassium of 5.4 — a level a potassium binder would have solved. Three correctable comorbidities — iron, the wrong rate-control drug, and hyperkalaemia-driven GDMT withdrawal — had together undone him.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🚫 WRONG DRUGVerapamil (non-DHP CCB) for AF in HFrEF — negative inotropy floods the lungs. Contraindicated.
🧲 IRON IGNOREDSevere iron deficiency never checked — untreated fatigue and vulnerability.
🧪 GDMT ABANDONEDACE inhibitor stopped for K⁺ 5.4 instead of adding a binder — prognostic drug lost.
🩸 NO SGLT2iAn easy pillar with proven benefit never started.
🧠 PUMP-ONLY THINKINGEverything blamed on "the heart failure"; the comorbidities went unexamined.

🛡️ What would have saved him: rate-control AF in HFrEF with a beta-blocker (never verapamil/diltiazem); check iron in every HF patient and give it IV; treat a mild potassium rise with a binder and keep the RAASi/MRA; and start an SGLT2 inhibitor. This is exactly the discipline that turned the patient in Bed 12 around.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Never slow atrial fibrillation in a failing heart with verapamil, never let a bindable potassium rob a patient of their ACE inhibitor, and never forget to check the iron — the comorbidities decide the outcome."
