🧬 Why THIS patient? — an inherited channel that won't let the cell reset

The QT interval is the time the ventricle spends depolarized and then repolarizing. Repolarization is driven mostly by potassium leaving the cell through IKs and IKr, while a small late sodium current (INa) tries to keep it depolarized. Congenital LQTS is a set of inherited mutations that tip this balance toward prolonged repolarization — either too little outward K⁺ current (LQT1 = ↓IKs; LQT2 = ↓IKr) or too much inward late Na⁺ current (LQT3 = ↑late INa). A longer action potential lets a stray inward calcium current re-fire the membrane before it has fully recovered — an early afterdepolarization (EAD). An EAD that captures, in a ventricle whose repolarization is wildly heterogeneous, launches Torsades de Pointes → syncope, or if sustained, VF and sudden death. The substrate was written in her DNA at conception; today an adrenergic surge (exercise + the cold-water dive reflex) pulled the trigger.

🔥 What raises event risk in congenital LQTS 📐 Longer QTc (risk climbs steeply > 500 ms) · 😵 Prior syncope or aborted arrest (the strongest predictor) · 👦 Genotype (LQT1/2/3, and certain mutations) · 🏃 Adrenergic triggers (exercise, emotion, startle — or rest in LQT3) · 🧪 Hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia · 💊 QT-prolonging drugs · 👶 Younger age & male in childhood, female after puberty · 👂 Jervell–Lange-Nielsen (deaf, very high risk).

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The cardiology resident arrives at 16:46. Her reasoning, out loud:

🧠 Pattern recognition first, then physiology

"Young. Structurally normal heart. Arrest during swimming. Two prior syncopes — one exertional, one with a startle. A young uncle dead in his sleep. And a QTc of 490 with notched T waves. That is congenital long QT syndrome until proven otherwise, and the swimming trigger points me straight at LQT1. The dangerous instinct is to call her first two faints 'vasovagal' again — but a vasovagal faint doesn't happen during a sprint, and it doesn't run in a family that buries its young."

🎯 Immediate priorities (her actual order of operations)

  1. 🚫 Kill the catecholamine pathway: NO isoprenaline, NO sympathomimetics, NO unnecessary adrenaline. Sinus brady at 58 in LQTS is expected, not a problem to treat. Say it out loud and take the infusion down.
  2. 💊 Start a beta-blocker now: nadolol (preferred) or propranolol — the first-line, mortality-reducing therapy that blunts the adrenergic surges that trigger her events.
  3. 🧪 Correct electrolytes to high-normal: K⁺ toward 4.5–5.0, Mg²⁺ > 1.0; a low K⁺/Mg²⁺ further prolongs repolarization and invites Torsades.
  4. 🧹 Strip QT-prolonging drugs: review every chart and med — no macrolides, no fluoroquinolones, no ondansetron, no haloperidol, no QT-prolonging antiemetics or antifungals.
  5. 🔌 Plan the secondary-prevention ICD: she has had an aborted cardiac arrest — this is a Class I indication, alongside the beta-blocker (not instead of it).
  6. 🧬 Confirm and cascade: formal QTc measurement (repeat, Bazett), Schwartz score, genetic testing, and screen all first-degree relatives with an ECG.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Protect first, provoke never: start the beta-blocker, refill the potassium, bin the QT drugs — and remember that in congenital long QT a slow heart is the disease, not an emergency to override with catecholamines."

🤔 "Her heart rate is only 58 — shouldn't I speed it up?"

"No. Resting sinus bradycardia is a feature of LQTS, and beta-blockade will slow it further — that's fine, even reassuring, as long as she's perfusing. The myth that you must 'fix' the rate with isoprenaline comes from acquired, pause-dependent Torsades, where bradycardia and pauses precipitate the arrhythmia. Congenital LQTS is adrenergically triggered — the opposite physiology. If beta-blockade ever causes genuinely symptomatic bradycardia, the answer is pacing (often as part of the ICD), never an adrenaline drip."

🧪 Resident's self-test (answer before reading on) A colleague suggests amiodarone "to settle her rhythm." Good idea in this patient?

No. Amiodarone is a class III antiarrhythmic that prolongs the QT — exactly the interval that is already dangerously long. Giving it can lengthen the QT further and provoke Torsades. The antiarrhythmic strategy in congenital LQTS is sympatholysis (beta-blockers), plus mexiletine (a late-Na blocker that shortens the QT) specifically in LQT3 — not QT-prolonging agents.

📖 Teaching Points

🔬 The mechanism — WHY a long QT twists into Torsades (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧬 The inherited defect: a mutation cripples a repolarizing potassium current (LQT1 ↓IKs, LQT2 ↓IKr) or strengthens a depolarizing late sodium current (LQT3 ↑late INa). Either way the action potential gets longer.
  2. 📐 The longer plateau: while the cell is still depolarized, L-type calcium channels can recover and re-open. A second little hump of inward current — an early afterdepolarization (EAD) — rides on the plateau.
  3. ⚡ The trigger beat: if an EAD reaches threshold, it fires a premature ventricular beat — the spark that lands on the vulnerable, heterogeneously-repolarizing ventricle.
  4. 🌀 Re-entry that wanders: because different regions repolarize at different times (transmural dispersion of repolarization), the wavefront spirals and its axis rotates — the QRS appears to twist around the baseline = Torsades de Pointes.
  5. 💥 Why it kills: Torsades usually self-terminates (→ syncope), but it can sustain and degenerate into VF — disorganized, pulseless, fatal in minutes. That is what happened in the pool today.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Long QT is a cell that won't reset; an adrenergic surge lights an early afterdepolarization, and a heart that repolarizes unevenly lets that spark twist into Torsades. Beta-blockade lowers the spark; the ICD catches the fire."

⚖️ Contrast pair #1 — Congenital LQTS vs Acquired Torsades (opposite reflexes)

🧬 CONGENITAL LQTS (this patient)

  • Inherited channel mutation; young, family history
  • Adrenergically triggered (exercise/emotion/startle)
  • Treat with beta-blockers; ICD for aborted SCA
  • AVOID isoprenaline / sympathomimetics — they are the trigger

💊 ACQUIRED TORSADES

  • Caused by drugs, ↓K⁺/↓Mg²⁺, bradycardia, pauses
  • Often pause-dependent (long-short initiating sequence)
  • Treat by removing the cause + IV magnesium + correct K⁺
  • Pacing / isoprenaline are OK to suppress pauses

😈 The trap: reflexively reaching for isoprenaline/pacing in a young arrest because "that's how you treat Torsades." It is — for the acquired, pause-dependent kind. In congenital LQTS the same drug feeds the catecholamine surge that triggers events. The asymmetry of harm is why you must name which Torsades you're treating before you reach for any drip.

⚖️ Contrast pair #2 — LQT1 vs LQT2 vs LQT3 (trigger / T wave / therapy)

🏊 LQT1 (this patient's likely type)

  • KCNQ1, ↓IKs; exercise/swimming triggers
  • Broad-based T waves
  • Beta-blockers most protective; restrict competitive sport & unsupervised swimming

🔔🌙 LQT2 & LQT3

  • LQT2: KCNH2/hERG, ↓IKr; emotion/auditory (alarm clock) triggers; low-amplitude notched/bifid T
  • LQT3: SCN5A, ↑late INa; events at rest/sleep; long isoelectric ST then late T; mexiletine shortens QT

💡 Why it matters: the genotype predicts when she's at risk and what protects her — beta-blockers shine in LQT1/2, while LQT3 needs a late-Na blocker (mexiletine) and a lower ICD threshold. Her swimming trigger and broad T waves point to LQT1.

🔬 Fellowship Corner — Advanced Topics

  • 📐 Concealed & borderline long QT: ~25–35% of gene-positive patients have a near-normal resting QTc. Use exercise/recovery QT (failure of QT to shorten, or paradoxical lengthening at 4 min recovery — esp. LQT1), epinephrine challenge, and Holter to unmask it.
  • 🧮 Schwartz score & QTc pitfalls: Bazett overcorrects at high rates and undercorrects at low rates; measure in lead II/V5, longest QT, tangent method, and beware sinus arrhythmia and U waves. Schwartz score integrates ECG, history and family data.
  • 🧬 Genotype–phenotype & risk stratification: KCNQ1-A341V, certain hERG pore mutations, and JLN carry higher risk; QTc > 500 ms, prior arrest/syncope, and very young/very specific genotypes raise the stakes and lower the ICD threshold.
  • 💉 LCSD technique & role: video-assisted thoracoscopic left stellate-sparing sympathectomy; powerful in refractory LQTS and CPVT, and a bridge or adjunct to ICD in those with frequent appropriate shocks.
  • 👶 Special situations: the postpartum window (LQT2 events cluster here), drug-gene interactions, and Andersen-Tawil (LQT7, periodic paralysis, dysmorphism) and Timothy syndrome (LQT8, CACNA1C, syndactyly) as multisystem variants.
  • 🧪 CPVT — the catecholaminergic mimic: RyR2/CASQ2 mutations cause exercise/emotion-induced bidirectional/polymorphic VT with a normal resting QT — same beta-blocker (often nadolol) ± flecainide ± LCSD logic, but the ECG at rest is clean.

  • 🛟 Lifestyle counselling started: no unsupervised swimming, restrict competitive sport (LQT1), avoid sudden auditory triggers/alarm clocks (LQT2), carry a QT-drug alert card.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Ward / EP nursing priorities — protecting a structurally normal but electrically fragile heart

  • 📟 Continuous telemetry with the defibrillator at the bedside, pads attached. Know that the rhythm to fear is a polymorphic, twisting VT (Torsades) that can degenerate to VF.
  • 🚫 Guard the catecholamine line: question any prescription for isoprenaline, adrenaline infusions or unnecessary sympathomimetics — in congenital LQTS these feed the trigger. Resting bradycardia at 58 is expected and not treated with stimulants.
  • 🧪 Keep K⁺ and Mg²⁺ high-normal: run replacement as prescribed, double-check rates, and recheck — a low K⁺/Mg²⁺ lengthens the QT she is already fighting.
  • 💊 Screen every drug against the QT list before giving it (antiemetics, antibiotics, antipsychotics): flag macrolides, fluoroquinolones, ondansetron, haloperidol. One wrong dose can precipitate Torsades.
  • 🔔 Quiet the environment (especially LQT2): minimize sudden loud noises and alarms; warn before startling her; this is genuinely antiarrhythmic in the auditory-trigger genotype.
  • 🧠 Watch for syncope/seizure-like activity — a brief Torsades run can look like a faint or a fit; if she slumps, check the pulse and the monitor immediately.
  • 📋 Document any rhythm strip, the QTc trend, and every drug given — the morphology of a captured Torsades run is gold for the EP team.
  • 👨‍👩‍👧 Support a frightened teenager and her parents (see S16): an aborted arrest in a healthy-looking child is traumatic; assign a liaison if staffing allows.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who keeps the potassium high, the QT-drugs off the chart and the catecholamines out of the room is doing the antiarrhythmic work the beta-blocker can't do alone."

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"First exclude the acquired causes you can fix in an hour — low K⁺, low Mg²⁺, low Ca²⁺, QT drugs, hypothyroidism — then, in a young patient with the right story, call the residual long QT congenital and treat it as such."
🧠 How to actually measure and read the QTMeasure in lead II or V5, from QRS onset to the end of the T wave (tangent method), take the longest interval, and correct for rate (Bazett: QTc = QT/√RR). Beware U waves, sinus arrhythmia, and rate extremes that fool Bazett. Then read the shape: broad-based T (LQT1), low-amplitude notched/bifid T (LQT2), or a long isoelectric ST with a late T (LQT3). The morphology hints at the genotype before the gene panel returns.

📈 ECG / Imaging

🫀 The resting 12-lead — read the interval AND the T-wave shape

Sinus rhythm with a prolonged QT and low-amplitude, notched T waves, then a short polymorphic run whose axis twists around the baseline — Torsades de Pointes. The long QT is the substrate; an early afterdepolarization is the spark.

🔎 The long-QT toolkit (memorize the high-yield ones)

FeatureWhy it matters
📐 Prolonged QTc> 480 ms repeatedly (or > 460 ms with symptoms) is diagnostic. The interval reflects abnormally long repolarization — the core defect.
🏊 Broad-based T wavesSuggest LQT1 (↓IKs) — the exercise/swimming genotype.
🔔 Low-amplitude / notched / bifid T wavesSuggest LQT2 (↓IKr) — the emotion/auditory-startle genotype (this patient's T-wave clue).
🌙 Long isoelectric ST then a late TSuggests LQT3 (↑late INa) — the rest/sleep genotype; mexiletine shortens the QT.
🌀 Torsades de PointesPolymorphic VT whose QRS axis twists around the baseline — the malignant arrhythmia of a long QT; may self-terminate (syncope) or sustain (VF).
🏃 Exercise/recovery QT & epinephrine challengeUnmask concealed LQTS: failure of the QT to shorten with rate, or paradoxical lengthening in recovery (esp. LQT1). Useful when the resting QTc is borderline.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Read the QT for the diagnosis and the T-wave shape for the genotype: broad T swims (LQT1), notched T startles (LQT2), late T sleeps (LQT3)."

🩻 Other imaging & tests

  • 📈 Repeat 12-leads & Holter: serial QTc, T-wave alternans (a marker of high electrical instability), and capture of any spontaneous Torsades runs.
  • 🩻 Echocardiogram: structurally normal — confirms primary electrical disease and excludes cardiomyopathy/ischemia as the cause of the arrest.
  • 🏃 Exercise testing (later, supervised): assesses QT behaviour with rate and the response to beta-blockade — done carefully given her risk.
⚠️ The ECG's favorite lie in long QT"A single normal-looking QTc rules it out." It does not: up to a third of gene-positive patients have a near-normal resting QTc (concealed LQTS). Repeat the ECG, use the exercise/recovery QT and epinephrine challenge, weigh the family history, and never let one reassuring strip override a textbook story.

💊 Medications

💉 Therapy — what, why, and the traps

DrugRoleMechanism / caution
Nadolol (preferred)First-line long-term protection; best LQTS evidenceNon-selective, long-acting beta-blocker; blunts the adrenergic surges that trigger events (best in LQT1/2). Uptitrate to maximally tolerated dose; expect/accept bradycardia.
PropranololAlternative first-line beta-blockerNon-selective; also reduces events. Multiple daily doses; same antiadrenergic principle.
Magnesium sulfate 2 g IVDrug of choice for active TorsadesStabilizes the membrane and suppresses early afterdepolarizations — give regardless of the magnesium level if she twists. Also replete to > 1.0 prophylactically.

🚫 The 'never' list in congenital long QT — QT-prolonging drugs to avoid❌ Macrolides (azithromycin, erythromycin, clarithromycin) · ❌ Fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin) · ❌ Ondansetron & other QT-prolonging antiemetics · ❌ Haloperidol / many antipsychotics & antidepressants · ❌ Methadone · ❌ Some antifungals (fluconazole) & antimalarials · ❌ Class IA/III antiarrhythmics (incl. amiodarone, sotalol) · ❌ Drugs causing ↓K⁺/↓Mg²⁺. Check crediblemeds.org before every new prescription.

🏠 Definitive & long-term therapy (this admission and for life)

  • 🔌 Secondary-prevention ICD — Class I after her aborted cardiac arrest, in addition to the beta-blocker (never instead of it).
  • 💊 Lifelong beta-blocker at maximally tolerated dose, with adherence support — stopping it is a classic cause of breakthrough events.
  • 🧬 Genotype-guided add-ons: mexiletine for LQT3; high-normal K⁺ strategy for LQT2.
  • 💉 Left cardiac sympathetic denervation (LCSD) for breakthrough events on beta-blockade or frequent ICD shocks.
  • 🛟 Lifestyle prescription: avoid QT drugs, no unsupervised swimming, restrict competitive sport (LQT1), remove sudden auditory triggers/alarm clocks (LQT2), correct electrolytes during illness.
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📈 Continuous telemetry with the defibrillator at the bedside until established on beta-blockade and arrhythmia-free; print any ectopy or polymorphic runs.
  • 📐 Serial QTc on every 12-lead — the headline number; watch that nothing on the chart (a sneaky antiemetic, a low K⁺) is lengthening it further.
  • 🧪 Serial K⁺ and Mg²⁺ kept high-normal (K⁺ 4.5–5.0, Mg²⁺ > 1.0) — the most modifiable QT drivers.
  • 💓 Heart rate on beta-blockade — expect and tolerate sinus bradycardia; only act if genuinely symptomatic, and then with pacing, not catecholamines.
  • 🌀 Watch for T-wave alternans and runs of polymorphic VT — early markers of electrical instability that should prompt escalation.
  • 🔋 Once an ICD is placed: monitor for appropriate vs inappropriate detections/shocks and ensure the bradycardia-pacing function supports beta-blockade.
  • 🧬 Track the genetic result and the family-screening ECGs as they return — they change long-term therapy and counselling.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After the arrest, the monitor's two jobs are: 'is the QT creeping longer?' and 'is she throwing polymorphic beats?' — and the two numbers that answer the first are potassium and the medication list."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — and the mechanism

  • 🌀 Recurrent Torsades / VF arrest — the central danger; an adrenergic surge or a QT-prolonging drug relights early afterdepolarizations. Treatment: defibrillate, magnesium, beta-blockade (NOT isoprenaline).
  • 💊 Iatrogenic QT prolongation — a single wrong drug (macrolide, ondansetron) or a dropped K⁺/Mg²⁺ can precipitate Torsades. Entirely preventable.
  • 🧠 Anoxic brain injury / drowning sequelae — from a prolonged or unwitnessed arrest (here mitigated by fast bystander CPR and early AED).
  • 🔌 ICD-related problems — inappropriate shocks (very distressing in a teenager), lead issues, infection, and the psychological burden of living with a device.
  • 💔 Breakthrough events on beta-blockade — especially with non-adherence or in LQT3; may need mexiletine, LCSD or ICD therapy.
  • 🧬 Undiagnosed affected relatives — the family is at risk until screened; a missed cascade can mean the next sudden death is preventable but unprevented.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference — "The Faint That Was a Warning"

The case: A 16-year-old midfielder collapsed mid-match, recovered spontaneously within a minute, and was seen in a busy clinic. He was labelled "vasovagal — dehydrated, hot day" and cleared to return to sport with no ECG. His mother mentioned that her brother had "died young in his sleep," but it wasn't recorded. Three weeks later he collapsed during a match and could not be resuscitated. A sibling's screening ECG, done after the funeral, showed a QTc of 500 ms — congenital long QT syndrome. The diagnosis had been one 12-lead away.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Young, fit, hot day = vasovagal" — but syncope DURING exertion is red-flag, not benign.
📈 NO ECGA single resting 12-lead would likely have shown the long QT.
🧬 FAMILY HISTORY IGNORED"Uncle died young in his sleep" is a channelopathy clue, unrecorded.
🏃 CLEARED FOR SPORTReturned to the exact adrenergic trigger that kills LQT1.
🩺 SYSTEMNo 'ECG for young exertional syncope' rule; no family-screening pathway.

🛡️ What would have saved him: a 12-lead ECG for every young person with exertional or emotional syncope; taking the family history of premature/sleep sudden death seriously; not clearing for sport until cardiac causes are excluded; and a cascade-screening pathway for relatives. This is exactly why your patient's nurse held the isoprenaline and why we are screening her whole family tonight.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Just a vasovagal faint' is the sentence written on the chart of young people who later die of long QT. Exertional or familial syncope earns an ECG — always."

🔀 Differentials

🧩 The young-person syncope / aborted-SCA differential

DiagnosisCluesWhy it matters
🧬 Congenital long QT syndrome (this patient)Long QTc, exertional/emotional/startle syncope, family sudden death, notched/broad T waves, structurally normal heartDefault in young exertional syncope with a long QT; beta-blocker + ICD; AVOID catecholamines & QT drugs.
💊 Acquired (drug/electrolyte) TorsadesQT-prolonging drug, ↓K⁺/↓Mg²⁺/↓Ca²⁺, bradycardia/pauses; often older or sickerMirror image therapy — remove the cause, magnesium, and pacing/isoprenaline are OK. Must exclude before calling it congenital.
🏃 CPVT (catecholaminergic polymorphic VT)Exercise/emotion-induced bidirectional/polymorphic VT with a NORMAL resting QT; RyR2/CASQ2Same beta-blocker (nadolol) ± flecainide ± LCSD logic, but a clean resting ECG — the great LQTS mimic.
💔 Hypertrophic cardiomyopathyExertional syncope, murmur, LVH on ECG, asymmetric septal hypertrophy on echoStructural cause of young SCD — distinguished by the echo (here normal).
🫀 Anomalous coronary / ARVC / Brugada / WPWExertional chest pain or arrest (anomalous coronary), epsilon waves (ARVC), coved ST V1–2 (Brugada), delta wave (WPW)Other young-SCD causes; each has its own ECG/imaging signature and treatment.

🏠 Discharge

📋 Discharge plan & safety net

  • 💊 Medications: lifelong beta-blocker (nadolol) at the maximally tolerated dose with a written schedule and strong adherence counselling; genotype-guided add-ons (mexiletine if LQT3) per EP. Stopping the beta-blocker is a classic cause of breakthrough events.
  • 🔌 ICD in situ: wound care, device ID card, device-clinic follow-up, and clear instructions on what a shock feels like and what to do after one.
  • 📇 QT-drug alert card and a documented allergy/alert band: no macrolides, fluoroquinolones, ondansetron, QT-prolonging antiemetics/antipsychotics; check crediblemeds.org before any new prescription.
  • 🛟 Lifestyle: no unsupervised swimming, restrict competitive sport (LQT1), avoid sudden auditory triggers/alarm clocks (LQT2), keep electrolytes up during vomiting/diarrhoea illness.
  • 👨‍👩‍👧 Family cascade screening & genetic counselling booked for all first-degree relatives; her mother (newly diagnosed) referred for treatment.
  • 🚩 Red flags → call emergency services: syncope or near-syncope, palpitations, an ICD shock (one shock + feeling well = urgent review; multiple shocks = emergency), and any new QT-prolonging drug prescribed elsewhere.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Discharge after an LQTS arrest is not 'rhythm normal, go home' — it is a lifelong beta-blocker, an ICD, a QT-drug card, a lifestyle plan, and a family screened for the same hidden spark."

📐 Measure the QTcExertional or emotional syncope in a young person with a family history of sudden death is long QT until the ECG says otherwise.
🛡️ Beta-blocker = seatbeltNadolol (or propranolol) is first-line, mortality-reducing therapy that blunts the adrenergic surges that trigger events.
🪂 Isoproterenol = cliffIn congenital LQTS, catecholamines are the trigger — the exact reverse of acquired, pause-dependent Torsades where pacing/isoprenaline are OK.
🏊🔔🌙 LQT1/2/3LQT1 swims (broad T), LQT2 startles (notched T), LQT3 sleeps (late T). The trigger and the T wave name the gene.
🔌 ICD after aborted SCAAn aborted cardiac arrest is a Class I secondary-prevention indication — in addition to the beta-blocker, never instead of it.
