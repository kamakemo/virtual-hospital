🧬 Why THIS patient? — the substrate

Dissection needs two things: a weak wall and a force that tears it. The wall weakens via cystic medial degeneration — fragmentation of elastin and loss of smooth muscle in the media. The tearing force is dP/dt — how hard and how fast each pulse wave slams into the aortic wall. Eleven years of untreated hypertension supplied both: pressure remodeled the media, and every one of his ~100,000 daily heartbeats hammered the same spot above the right sinus of Valsalva. Tonight, while lifting boxes off his truck (Valsalva → BP surge), the intima finally gave way.

🔥 Risk factor checklist for dissection 🔺 Hypertension (76% of cases, by far #1) · 🧬 Marfan, Loeys-Dietz, Ehlers-Danlos vascular type · 🫀 Bicuspid aortic valve (9%) · 💉 Cocaine/amphetamines (catecholamine surge) · 🤰 Pregnancy (third trimester) · 🏋️ Heavy lifting/Valsalva · 🩻 Prior cardiac surgery or aortic instrumentation · 🦠 Aortitis (syphilis, Takayasu, giant cell)

🤲 The pulse exam — 30 seconds that beats any score

Palpate both radials, both femorals, both carotids simultaneously. A pulse deficit is present in only ~30% of dissections — but when present it carries a likelihood ratio of ~5.7, the strongest single bedside finding. Absence of a deficit never rules it out.

⚠️ Which arm tells the truth?The HIGHER reading (left, 196/104) is the patient's real central pressure. The right arm reads falsely low because the flap is obstructing the innominate artery. Treat the high number — if you chase the falsely low right-arm reading with fluids and pressors, you will accelerate the tear. Always manage BP from the higher arm, and put the arterial line there.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The cardiology resident arrives at 02:55. Her thought process, out loud:

🧠 Pattern recognition first, then physiology

"This is not an ACS pattern. Maximal-at-onset tearing pain, migration, syncope, pulse deficit, new AR murmur — that cluster has exactly one home: acute aortic syndrome. The inferior ST depression is a trap; worse, if the flap shaves the right coronary ostium we may see inferior ST elevation, and someone will want to lyse him. Over my dead body — or rather, over his."

🎯 Immediate priorities (her actual order of operations)

  1. 🚫 Block the ACS pathway: no aspirin, no heparin, no ticagrelor, no lytics. Physically tell the nurse and write it on the board: "NO ANTITHROMBOTICS — ?DISSECTION."
  2. 📞 Parallel processing: call CT to clear the scanner while the porter is being found, while anti-impulse therapy is being drawn up. In dissection, tasks run in parallel or the patient dies in series.
  3. 💉 Anti-impulse therapy: esmolol infusion first (rate control lowers dP/dt), THEN add a vasodilator if SBP still > 120. Targets: HR ≤ 60, SBP 100–120 — using the LEFT arm.
  4. 🩻 Bedside TTE while waiting for CT: looking for pericardial effusion, AR, a flap in the root. Takes 3 minutes, changes everything.
  5. 📟 Early warning to cardiothoracic surgery: "probable type A" — don't wait for the CT to make the first call. Surgeons need lead time to open a theatre at 3 AM.
  6. 🩸 Crossmatch 4 units, baseline labs, analgesia generously — pain itself drives catecholamines, HR and BP, which drive the tear. Fentanyl is anti-impulse therapy too.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Lower the RATE before the PRESSURE. A vasodilator without a beta-blocker causes reflex tachycardia — and reflex tachycardia rips the aorta wider."

🤔 Why not fluids for the 84/52 right arm?

"Because the right arm is lying. His true central pressure is 196 systolic. Volume-loading him against a hypertensive, tearing aorta is like over-inflating a tire that is already splitting at the sidewall. The exception — if he develops true hypotension (tamponade, rupture) — then cautious small boluses while running to theatre."

🧪 Resident's self-test (answer before reading on) The patient's pain suddenly improves to 3/10 without analgesia. Reassuring?

No — be MORE afraid. Spontaneous pain relief in dissection can mean the false lumen has decompressed — sometimes by rupturing into the pericardium or pleura, sometimes by re-entering the true lumen. Falling pain + rising JVP + falling BP = tamponade. Re-examine immediately.

🎓 Consultant Round

The on-call consultant cardiologist, by phone at 03:05, then at the bedside:

🗂️ Classification decides everything

🔴 Stanford Type A (this patient)

  • Involves the ascending aorta (± arch, ± descending), regardless of where the tear started
  • ~60–65% of dissections
  • Kills via: tamponade, acute AR, coronary ostial occlusion (usually RCA), stroke, rupture
  • Treatment: EMERGENCY SURGERY. Medical therapy alone ≈ 50–60% mortality in the first week
  • DeBakey I (ascending → beyond arch) or II (ascending only)

🔵 Stanford Type B

  • Confined to distal to the left subclavian (descending aorta)
  • ~35% of dissections
  • Kills via: malperfusion (gut, kidney, spinal cord, limb), rupture, refractory pain/HTN
  • Treatment: medical anti-impulse therapy first; TEVAR (endovascular stent-graft) if complicated
  • DeBakey III
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Type A goes to the SURGEON. Type B goes to the INFUSION PUMP. Confusing the two kills patients in both directions."

🔪 What the surgeons will actually do

  • Goal: replace the segment containing the entry tear and prevent the three killers — tamponade, AR, coronary occlusion. This is a life-saving operation, not a total aortic repair.
  • Supracoronary interposition graft if the root and valve are salvageable; valve resuspension often fixes the AR because the leaflets are normal — they were merely unhinged.
  • Bentall procedure (composite valve-graft + coronary reimplantation) if the root is destroyed or the valve irreparable; valve-sparing root replacement (David procedure) in younger patients/connective tissue disease.
  • Hemiarch or total arch ± frozen elephant trunk if the arch is torn — performed under deep hypothermic circulatory arrest with antegrade cerebral perfusion.
  • Operative mortality ~15–25% — terrifying, until you remember non-operative mortality approaches 60%.

🧭 Consultant's three commandments for the next hour

  1. ⏱️ "Nothing — not the troponin, not a second opinion, not the morning shift — delays this CT or this referral. Time-to-theatre is the only modifiable mortality factor."
  2. 💉 "Esmolol titrated to HR ≤ 60 BEFORE nitroprusside touches him. If he were asthmatic or in acute severe AR-driven heart failure, we'd discuss labetalol vs careful titration — but the rate-first principle stands."
  3. 🫀 "If he arrests or his pressure collapses: think tamponade. Pericardiocentesis here is a bridge of last resort — aspirate the minimum volume to restore a pressure (5–10 mL aliquots), because abrupt decompression re-pressurizes the false lumen and re-opens the bleed. The definitive drain is the operating theatre."

📖 Teaching Points

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the aorta tears (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧱 The wall weakens: chronic hypertension → medial smooth muscle apoptosis + elastin fragmentation + mucoid (cystic) degeneration. The media — the load-bearing layer — becomes laminated cardboard instead of steel-belted rubber.
  2. ⚡ The intima tears: usually within centimeters of the sinotubular junction (highest wall stress, by Laplace: tension = pressure × radius) or just distal to the left subclavian (tethered by the ligamentum arteriosum — a fixed point that whips with each pulse).
  3. 🌊 Blood dissects the media: pulsatile flow enters the tear and cleaves the media into a true lumen and a false lumen separated by the intimomedial flap. The false lumen propagates ante- and retrograde at every systole — driven by dP/dt, the steepness of the pressure upstroke.
  4. 💥 Death finds the path of least resistance: retrograde into the pericardium (tamponade — the #1 killer), into the aortic root (acute AR), across the coronary ostia (MI — usually RCA, hence inferior changes), into branch vessels (stroke, paraplegia, dead gut, dead kidney, dead leg), or transmural rupture (exsanguination).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Anti-impulse therapy doesn't lower the pressure for its own sake — it flattens dP/dt, the WHIP-CRACK of each systole. Beta-blockade flattens the whip; vasodilators alone just make it crack faster."

⚖️ Contrast pair #1 — Dissection vs STEMI (the deadly mimic)

🩸 Dissection IS…

  • Maximal at onset, tearing, migrating
  • Radiates THROUGH to the back
  • Pulse deficits / inter-arm BP gap > 20 mmHg
  • New diastolic (AR) murmur
  • Pain + neuro deficit / syncope
  • ECG often normal or nonspecific (only ~15% have ST elevation, usually inferior from RCA ostial involvement)
  • Treatment: β-blockade, surgery. Anticoagulation = death

🫀 STEMI is NOT that…

  • Crescendo pressure pain over minutes
  • Radiates to arm/jaw, not through to the back
  • Pulses symmetric
  • S4 maybe; no new AR murmur
  • Neuro deficits rare at presentation
  • ECG: territorial ST elevation with reciprocal changes
  • Treatment: DAPT + heparin + PCI. Delay = death

😈 The trap: ~1–2% of "STEMIs" are actually dissections involving a coronary. The cost of lysing a dissection is ~70% mortality. One screening question before every lytic/anticoagulant: "Does the pain tear to the back, and are the pulses equal?"

⚖️ Contrast pair #2 — Acute AR vs Chronic AR

⚡ Acute AR (tonight)

  • Small, non-adapted LV → regurgitant volume slams diastolic pressure up instantly
  • SHORT, quiet murmur (pressures equalize fast)
  • Tachycardia, pulmonary edema, shock
  • NO wide pulse pressure, NO classic eponymous signs
  • Surgical emergency

🐢 Chronic AR

  • Years of remodeling → huge compliant LV
  • Long decrescendo murmur, displaced apex
  • Wide pulse pressure, Corrigan, de Musset, Quincke signs
  • Tolerated for decades
  • Elective surgery by symptoms/LV criteria

💡 Why so different? Compliance. The chronic LV had years to dilate and swallow the regurgitant volume; the acute LV is a stiff small chamber suddenly force-fed an extra 50 mL per beat — diastolic LV pressure skyrockets, the mitral valve closes early, the lungs flood.

🔬 Fellowship Corner — Advanced Topics

  • 🧩 Acute aortic syndrome spectrum: classic dissection ↔ intramural hematoma (IMH) (vasa vasorum rupture, no flap — treat type A IMH like type A dissection) ↔ penetrating atherosclerotic ulcer (PAU). Same emergency, different anatomy.
  • 🌀 Dynamic vs static malperfusion: dynamic = flap prolapses over the branch ostium with each systole (fixable by restoring true lumen flow / fenestration); static = dissection extends into the branch itself (needs branch stenting). On CTA, look at whether the branch fills from true or false lumen.
  • 🧬 Genetics: FBN1 (Marfan), TGFBR1/2 + SMAD3 (Loeys-Dietz — dissects at smaller diameters, operate earlier), COL3A1 (vascular Ehlers-Danlos — tissue like wet paper, surgery itself is hazardous), ACTA2/MYH11 (familial TAAD). Every dissection < 60 years deserves a genetics referral and first-degree-relative screening echo/CT.
  • 🥶 Cerebral protection in arch surgery: deep hypothermic circulatory arrest vs moderate hypothermia + antegrade cerebral perfusion via axillary cannulation — modern series favor the latter for longer safe arrest times.
  • 🦵 Spinal cord protection (extensive repairs/TEVAR): the artery of Adamkiewicz (typically T8–L1); CSF drains lower the "compartment pressure" of the cord — perfusion = MAP − CSF pressure.
  • 🩻 False lumen fate predicts the future: a patent, pressurized false lumen drives late aneurysmal degeneration; complete thrombosis is favorable; partial thrombosis is paradoxically the worst (it seals the exit, pressurizing the sac).

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Resus-bay nursing priorities — minute by minute

  • 📟 Continuous cardiac monitoring + invasive arterial trace once line placed; obs q5–15 min, documented from the LEFT arm / arterial line.
  • ↔️ Repeat both-arm NIBP every 15 min — a widening differential means the flap is propagating. Chart it as a pair, e.g. "L 142/80 | R 78/50."
  • 🧠 Neuro obs q15 min (GCS, limb power, speech) — the flap can take a carotid at any moment; new deficit = call the doctor, not a wait-and-see.
  • 🦵 All four limb pulses + color/warmth hourly — mark pulses with a skin pen; "the right foot has gone cold" is a CTA-level finding.
  • 😖 Pain scores q15 min; titrate prescribed fentanyl — escalating pain = extending dissection. Document where the pain is now (migration matters).
  • 💧 Hourly urine output (target > 0.5 mL/kg/h); falling output = renal malperfusion or shock.
  • 🔇 Environment as therapy: calm voice, dim lights, no chaos at the bedside. Fear → catecholamines → dP/dt → tear. A quiet nurse is hemodynamic management.
  • 🚫 Confirm NO antithrombotics are charted; challenge any new prescription against the whiteboard flag.
  • 🧳 Pre-op prep in parallel: ID bands, consent witnessed, dentures out, jewelry off, crossmatch chased, theatre checklist started — assume he goes to surgery within the hour.
  • 👨‍👩‍👦 Keep the family informed every 20–30 min (see S16 journey module) — assign one nurse as family liaison if staffing allows.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who recharts both arms and all four pulses every hour is running the most sensitive dissection-extension monitor in the hospital."
