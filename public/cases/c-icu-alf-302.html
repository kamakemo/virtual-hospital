🧪 Resident's self-testThe night team suggests giving FFP "to bring the INR down before it gets dangerous." Do it?

No — not routinely. In ALF the raised INR reflects failing hepatic synthesis and is the single most important prognostic marker (it drives the King's College Criteria). Correcting it with FFP masks the trend the transplant team relies on, and these patients are often not truly bleeding despite the numbers (balanced deficits of pro- and anticoagulant factors). Give products only for active bleeding or to cover an invasive procedure — and tell the transplant centre if you do.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY paracetamol kills the liver, WHY the brain swells (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 💊 Paracetamol metabolism: most is conjugated safely, but a fraction becomes the toxic metabolite NAPQI via CYP2E1. Normally glutathione mops NAPQI up.
  2. 🪫 Glutathione depletion: in overdose (or fasting/enzyme-induced states) glutathione runs out; free NAPQI binds hepatocyte proteins → centrilobular necrosis. NAC replenishes glutathione — that is exactly why it works, and why earlier is better.
  3. 🏭 Loss of synthetic function: the dying liver stops making clotting factors → rising INR (coagulopathy); stops storing/releasing glucose → hypoglycaemia; stops clearing toxins.
  4. 🧠 Hyperammonaemia → cerebral oedema: the failing liver cannot detoxify ammonia. Ammonia crosses into the brain, astrocytes convert it to glutamine, which draws water in osmotically → astrocyte swelling → cerebral oedema and raised ICP — the leading cause of death in hyperacute ALF.
  5. 🔥 SIRS / multi-organ spiral: necrotic liver releases inflammatory mediators → distributive shock, AKI, lactic acidosis, functional immunosuppression and sepsis — the vicious cycle that closes the transplant window.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"NAC replaces the glutathione the liver ran out of — so give it early, give it late, and give it even when the cause is not paracetamol. It is safe; the missed dose is not."

⚖️ Contrast pair — acute liver failure vs decompensated chronic liver disease

🫗 Acute liver failure (this patient)

  • Previously NORMAL liver; encephalopathy + INR ≥ 1.5
  • Cerebral OEDEMA / raised ICP is the killer
  • NAC, neuroprotection, King's criteria, EMERGENCY transplant

🍷 Decompensated cirrhosis

  • Pre-existing chronic liver disease; stigmata, varices, ascites
  • Cerebral oedema is RARE; encephalopathy driven by precipitants
  • Treat precipitant, lactulose, manage portal hypertension

💡 Same word "encephalopathy", completely different danger. In ALF the brain physically swells and cones; in cirrhosis it usually does not. Do not manage one as the other.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Critical-care priorities — the nurse guards the brain and the sugar

  • 🍬 Hourly glucose; a falling glucose in an already-confused liver patient can look like "worsening encephalopathy" — always check the sugar first and treat it.
  • 🧠 Neuro obs & encephalopathy grade; escalate the moment she becomes less rousable — deepening encephalopathy heralds cerebral oedema and the need for airway protection.
  • 🛌 Nurse head-up ~30°, head midline; minimise suctioning/agitation that spike ICP; cluster care; keep her normothermic.
  • 🩸 Do not chase the INR with products; report bleeding, but understand the coagulopathy is a prognostic sign, not a target — challenge any unplanned FFP.
  • 🧫 Strict asepsis with lines; watch for fever/hypotension (sepsis) and for falling urine output (AKI).
  • 📞 Keep the transplant referral, timings and repeat bloods visible on the chart — the trend is the story the liver unit needs.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In the confused liver-failure patient, the nurse's glucometer is a neurological instrument — check the sugar before you blame the encephalopathy."

📈 Imaging / EEG + Visual Enhancements

🩸 The INR trend — the prognostic curve you must NOT flatten

The true INR climbs toward the King's threshold — the signal the transplant team needs. Give FFP and the line falls artificially, hiding the prognosis. The number is a message; do not delete it.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Cerebral oedema is the number-one killer in hyperacute liver failure — treat the brain as the second failing organ from the moment encephalopathy appears: head-up, control sodium/CO₂, hypertonic saline or mannitol for surges."

🖼️ Neuroprotection at a glance

  • Raise the head (~30°, midline) to aid cerebral venous drainage.
  • Avoid ICP spikes: minimise coughing/suctioning/agitation, treat fever and seizures, keep sodium high-normal (145–150).
  • Surges: hypertonic saline or mannitol; controlled ventilation to target CO₂ once intubated.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🧠 Encephalopathy grade & pupils — deepening grade or a fixed/sluggish pupil signals cerebral oedema/coning — a neuro emergency.
  • 🍬 Hourly glucose — hypoglycaemia is common, mimics/worsens encephalopathy, and is instantly treatable.
  • 🩸 Serial INR & bilirubin — the prognostic trend for King's; report to the transplant centre. Do not flatten it with products.
  • 🧪 Arterial pH & lactate — a persistent acidosis after resuscitation is an adverse King's marker in paracetamol ALF.
  • 🫘 Urine output / creatinine — AKI is part of the King's triad and a driver of outcome; consider renal replacement early for acidosis/ammonia/fluid.
  • 🧫 Temperature, cultures, inflammatory markers — infection is frequent and closes the transplant window.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In ALF you are monitoring FIVE failing organs at once — brain, liver, kidney, circulation and immune system — and every one of them can be the reason a transplant is offered, or too late."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — the organ threats that kill

  • 🧠 Cerebral oedema & raised ICP / coning — the leading cause of death in hyperacute ALF; driven by hyperammonaemia. Herald: deepening encephalopathy, hypertension + bradycardia (Cushing), pupillary change.
  • 🩸 Coagulopathy & bleeding — real bleeding risk exists, but the numbers overstate it (balanced factor deficits); manage bleeding, not the INR.
  • 🍬 Hypoglycaemia — recurrent and dangerous; a reversible cause of coma.
  • 🧫 Infection / SIRS / sepsis — functional immunosuppression; sepsis worsens encephalopathy and can make a patient untransplantable.
  • 🫘 Acute kidney injury — common (especially paracetamol); may need renal replacement, also clears ammonia/acid.
  • 🧪 Lactic acidosis & multi-organ failure — the final common pathway; a persistently rising lactate is ominous.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The liver is the first domino; the brain, kidney, circulation and immune system fall next. Anticipate each one — and if a transplant can interrupt the cascade, refer BEFORE the later dominoes fall."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🫗 Diagnosis: paracetamol-induced acute liver failure with grade II encephalopathy, rising INR, AKI and acidosis. NAC running; glucose and neuroprotection managed in ICU.
  2. 👑 Apply King's College Criteria and refer the regional transplant centre NOW — pH < 7.3, or the INR>6.5 + Cr>300 + grade III/IV triad. She is trending toward criteria; the referral is a phone call, not a wait.
  3. 🧠 Neuroprotection escalated as encephalopathy deepens: intubate for airway protection at grade III–IV, control CO₂/sodium, hypertonic saline/mannitol for ICP surges.
  4. 🩸 Coagulopathy left uncorrected unless bleeding/procedure; kidney supported (consider RRT for acidosis/ammonia); infection screened and treated early.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The transplant referral is a treatment, not a formality — and it is time-critical. Make the call when you DIAGNOSE acute liver failure, not when the patient is dying."
🧠 Managing the brain — the ICP bundle (why the ICU team leads)
  • Head-up 30°, midline; avoid ICP spikes (coughing, suction, agitation); treat fever and seizures.
  • Sodium 145–150 with hypertonic saline; mannitol for acute surges; controlled ventilation to a target CO₂ once intubated.
  • Intubate early for grade III–IV encephalopathy to protect the airway and control CO₂ — do not wait for aspiration.
  • ICP monitoring only in selected transplant candidates after weighing the bleeding risk with the transplant/neuro team.

  • Red flags: fever, graft dysfunction, jaundice, confusion → urgent review

🟢 Recovered ALF without transplant

  • Some paracetamol ALF regenerate if they survive the acute phase — hepatology follow-up to confirm recovery
  • Same mental-health/safeguarding pathway; overdose prevention
  • Counsel on hidden paracetamol in OTC/compound analgesics
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Surviving the liver is only half the job — a paracetamol ALF discharge without a mental-health and safeguarding plan sends the patient home to the same overdose. Treat the cause, not just the organ."

💎 Clinical Pearls

🫗 The definitionEncephalopathy + coagulopathy (INR ≥ 1.5) in a liver WITHOUT pre-existing disease = acute liver failure.
💧 NAC — early, late, and even non-paracetamolIt replaces glutathione; give it in doubt, it is safe.
🩸 Don't 'fix' the INRIt is the key transplant prognostic marker — products only for bleeding/procedures.
🧠 Cerebral oedema is the killerHyperammonaemia swells astrocytes; head-up, sodium 145–150, hypertonic saline/mannitol.
🍬 Always check the glucoseHypoglycaemia mimics and worsens encephalopathy and is instantly reversible.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the bundle

  • ⏱️ Classifying by jaundice-to-encephalopathy interval: hyperacute (< 7 days — paracetamol/viral; highest cerebral-oedema risk but best spontaneous recovery), acute (8–28 days), subacute (5–12 weeks — worst spontaneous survival despite less oedema). The tempo predicts both ICP risk and transplant urgency.
  • 🩸 Extracorporeal & liver-support therapies: high-volume plasma exchange improves transplant-free survival in ALF (Larsen); albumin dialysis (MARS) and other liver-support devices are bridges, not cures; continuous renal replacement clears ammonia and controls acidosis/sodium.
  • 🧠 Ammonia & ICP science: arterial ammonia > ~150–200 µmol/L predicts intracranial hypertension; management targets ammonia production/clearance (CRRT), astrocyte osmotic load (hypertonic saline), and cerebral metabolic rate. Indomethacin and therapeutic hypothermia are salvage options in refractory ICP.
  • 🍄 Aetiology-specific antidotes: silibinin/penicillin + NAC for Amanita phalloides; aciclovir for HSV hepatitis; steroids for autoimmune; chelation/urgent transplant for Wilsonian crisis (haemolysis + low ALP:bilirubin ratio); delivery for pregnancy-related (HELLP/AFLP).
  • 🧪 Prognostic refinements: arterial lactate and phosphate add to King's criteria in paracetamol ALF; dynamic models (e.g. incorporating lactate, or the ALFSG index) aim to improve on the original 1989 rules — but early referral still beats any score used late.
  • 🩺 Coagulation nuance: ALF produces a rebalanced haemostasis (parallel falls in pro- and anticoagulant factors); conventional INR overestimates bleeding risk — viscoelastic testing (TEG/ROTEM) guides real transfusion decisions better than the INR, which stays reserved as a prognostic marker.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The INR We 'Corrected' to Death"

The case: A 34-year-old man presented with a staggered paracetamol overdose, jaundice and early confusion. NAC was started — good. But overnight his INR rose to 7.2, and the covering team, worried he would "bleed", gave three units of FFP, dropping the recorded INR to 2.4. On the morning ward round the INR "looked fine", he was not obviously encephalopathic between doses of sedation, and the plan became "continue NAC, review need for transplant referral later". No one phoned the liver unit. Twenty-four hours on, he developed fixed dilated pupils from cerebral oedema; by the time the transplant centre was called he was coning and no longer a candidate. He died.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🩸 MASKED PROGNOSISFFP given to "correct" the INR erased the key King's marker.
👑 CRITERIA HIDDENWith a false INR, King's criteria appeared unmet — referral deemed unnecessary.
📞 NO EARLY REFERRALThe transplant centre was never called while she was still transferable.
💤 SEDATION MASKED BRAINSedatives clouded the encephalopathy exam, delaying recognition of ICP.
🧠 OEDEMA MISSEDCerebral oedema — the number-one killer — was not anticipated or pre-empted.

🛡️ What would have saved him: treat the INR as a prognostic marker, not a target — do NOT give FFP unless bleeding/procedure; apply King's College Criteria on the RAW INR and refer the transplant centre EARLY, before criteria are fully met; anticipate and neuroprotect against cerebral oedema from the first sign of encephalopathy; and avoid sedatives that hide the neuro exam. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 2.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In acute liver failure, correcting the INR can kill — you delete the very number that earns the patient a transplant. Trend it, refer on it, and keep your FFP in the fridge unless there is blood on the floor."
