🧬 Why THIS patient? — a proximal RCA occlusion that took the right ventricle with it

An inferior STEMI is usually a right coronary artery (RCA) story. When the occlusion is proximal — above the right ventricular (acute) marginal branches — the clot starves not only the inferior wall of the left ventricle but the entire right ventricle as well. The stunned, acutely non-compliant RV cannot push enough blood across the lungs to fill the left heart. The left ventricle is therefore underfilled: stroke volume falls, the blood pressure falls — but because the failing chamber sits upstream of the lungs, the lungs stay dry while systemic venous pressure backs up and the JVP rises. The same RCA also feeds the SA and AV nodes and triggers the Bezold–Jarisch reflex, so bradycardia and AV block ride along with the infarct. One artery, four findings.

🔥 Who gets RV infarction? 🫀 Up to ~40% of inferior STEMIs have some RV involvement (clinically significant in a smaller subset) · ➡️ Driven by proximal RCA occlusion · 💧 Manifests as preload-dependent hypotension · 🫁 With clear lung fields · 📐 And a raised JVP / Kussmaul's sign · 🐢 Frequently with bradyarrhythmia or AV block.

The cardiology resident arrives at 14:24. Her reasoning, out loud:

🧠 Pattern recognition first, then physiology

"Inferior ST elevation, hypotensive, clear lungs, neck veins up, and he's slow. That pentad is right ventricular infarction — I don't need to wait for the troponin. The dangerous instinct is to see 'STEMI + chest pain + low pressure' and reflexively reach for a nitrate and an opioid. In this patient that is removing the preload that is keeping him alive. The right ventricle is stunned and stiff; it runs on filling pressure. Take that away and the left heart has nothing to pump."

🎯 Immediate priorities (her actual order of operations)

  1. 🚫 Stop the preload reducers: no nitroglycerin, no morphine, no diuretic. Say it out loud and take the GTN and furosemide off the trolley.
  2. ➡️ Record V4R (right-sided leads) NOW — confirm the diagnosis with the single most useful sign, ST elevation in V4R, plus posterior leads.
  3. 💧 Cautious IV fluid bolus (250–500 mL crystalloid) to fill the RV, reassessing pressure and lungs after each — the RV is preload-dependent.
  4. 🫀 Reperfuse: activate primary PCI of the RCA without delay — opening the artery is the definitive treatment for the RV too.
  5. 🐢 Treat the bradycardia/AV block: atropine for symptomatic bradycardia; have transcutaneous/temporary pacing ready. Preserve AV synchrony — the stiff RV depends on the atrial kick.
  6. 💪 If still hypotensive despite filling → inotropic support (dobutamine) rather than more and more fluid; over-filling overloads the RV.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In RV infarction the order is: refuse the nitrate, get the V4R, give fluid, open the artery, and keep him in sinus rhythm — because the atrial kick is half his cardiac output."

🤔 "But he has chest pain — surely he needs a nitrate?"

"His pain will be treated by opening the artery, not by a venodilator that empties his ventricle. Nitrates relieve ischemic pain by reducing preload and wall stress — exactly the physiology that an RV infarct cannot survive. If you must give analgesia, give it cautiously and watch the pressure like a hawk; morphine is itself a venodilator. The chest pain is a symptom of an occluded RCA — reperfusion is the analgesic that doesn't kill him."

🧪 Resident's self-test (answer before reading on) Someone suggests "a small dose of IV furosemide because the JVP is so high — he's clearly congested." Good idea in this patient?

No — dangerously wrong. The raised JVP here is not volume overload of the lungs; it is venous return backing up behind a stiff, infarcted RV that the underfilled LV needs more preload, not less. Diuresing him strips the very filling pressure keeping the left heart full and will collapse his blood pressure. The high JVP with clear lungs is the signature of RV infarction, not a reason to offload. Fluids, not furosemide.

🔴 RV-infarct hypotension (this patient)

  • The left ventricle itself fails to pump forward
  • WET lungs — crackles, pulmonary edema, hypoxia
  • Usually large anterior (LAD) infarct
  • Treatment: avoid fluid bolus, support/offload — inotropes, vasopressors, careful diuresis, mechanical support (IABP/Impella)
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"RV-infarct shock you FILL; LV pump-failure shock you SUPPORT and OFFLOAD. The lungs tell you which: clear → fluids, wet → no fluids. Same low pressure, opposite treatment."

🐢 "Inferior MI loves to block" — watch the conduction system

The RCA supplies the SA node in ~60% of people and the AV node in ~90%, and an inferior infarct also fires the Bezold–Jarisch reflex (vagally mediated bradycardia and hypotension). So sinus bradycardia, first-degree and Mobitz I (Wenckebach) AV block, and even complete heart block are common — and usually at the nodal level, so they are atropine-responsive and often transient with reperfusion. Because the stiff RV leans on the atrial kick, losing AV synchrony (e.g. junctional rhythm or complete block) can drop the pressure dramatically — restore synchrony (atropine, AV-sequential temporary pacing) and the pressure often follows.

🧭 Consultant's three commandments for the next hour

  1. ➡️ "Get the V4R on the chart before anyone touches a syringe. ST elevation in V4R is the diagnosis. Every inferior STEMI in this unit gets right-sided and posterior leads — no exceptions."
  2. 💧 "Fill, then reperfuse. Cautious boluses to support the RV, then the artery open as fast as possible. If he's still hypotensive after sensible filling, add dobutamine — do not chase the pressure with endless saline and overload the RV."
  3. 🚫 "Ban the preload reducers on the drug chart — nitroglycerin, morphine, diuretics — and keep him in sinus rhythm. The single commonest way to kill an RV infarct is a 'routine' nitrate for chest pain."

📖 Teaching Points

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the RV infarct makes a preload-dependent, clear-lunged shock (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🩸 The artery: a proximal RCA occlusion (above the RV marginal branches) infarcts the inferior LV and the right ventricle. A distal RCA or a dominant circumflex lesion spares the RV — location is everything.
  2. 🧱 The stunned RV: the acutely infarcted right ventricle becomes dilated, hypocontractile and stiff. It can neither contract well nor relax to accept venous return.
  3. ⬇️ Underfilled left heart: if the RV can't push blood through the lungs, the left atrium and LV receive less inflow. LV preload falls → stroke volume falls → blood pressure falls. The problem is delivery to the LV, not the LV itself.
  4. 🫁 Why the lungs stay clear: the bottleneck sits upstream of the pulmonary capillaries. Pressure backs up into the systemic veins (high JVP), not the lungs — so no crackles, no pulmonary edema. That dissociation (hypotension with clear lungs) is the fingerprint.
  5. 💧 Why fluids help and nitrates kill: a stiff RV sits on the steep part of its filling curve — a little extra volume meaningfully raises RV output and thus LV filling. A nitrate (or morphine, or a diuretic) removes venous return, drops RV preload, and the underfilled LV has even less to pump → profound hypotension/arrest.
  6. 🐢 Why it blocks and why AV synchrony matters: the RCA feeds the SA/AV nodes and the Bezold–Jarisch reflex adds vagal tone → bradycardia/AV block. The stiff RV depends heavily on a timed atrial contraction (the atrial kick) to fill — lose AV synchrony and output falls sharply.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The RV infarct is a heart begging for volume: fill it, open its artery, keep it in sinus rhythm — and never take away the preload it is living on."

⚖️ Contrast pair #1 — RV-infarct hypotension vs LV pump-failure shock (the lethal mimic)

💧 RV infarct → FILL

  • Inferior STEMI, proximal RCA
  • CLEAR lungs, raised JVP, Kussmaul's sign
  • Preload-DEPENDENT — fluids raise the pressure
  • Bradycardia/AV block common
  • AVOID nitrates, morphine, diuretics

💦 LV pump failure → DON'T FILL

  • Usually large anterior (LAD) STEMI
  • WET lungs — crackles, edema, hypoxia
  • Forward failure of the LV itself
  • Fluid bolus worsens the edema
  • Inotropes/pressors, careful diuresis, mechanical support

😈 The trap: both present as a hypotensive STEMI. The brain pattern-matches "low pressure in an MI = cardiogenic shock = avoid fluid / give a nitrate for pain" and does exactly the wrong thing. The lungs break the tie: clear lungs with a high JVP send you toward fluids; wet lungs send you away from them.

⚖️ Contrast pair #2 — Nitrate-helps vs Nitrate-harms

✅ Nitrate HELPS

  • Anterior STEMI/ACS with pulmonary edema and adequate/high BP
  • Reducing preload & afterload unloads a congested LV
  • Relieves ischemic pain and breathlessness

🚫 Nitrate HARMS

  • RV infarction (and any preload-dependent state)
  • Removing preload empties the underfilled LV → collapse
  • Also avoid in hypotension and recent PDE5-inhibitor use

💡 Why it matters: the same little spray that rescues a wet, hypertensive anterior STEMI can arrest a dry, hypotensive RV infarct. Check the lungs and the JVP before you reach for the GTN.

🔬 Fellowship Corner — Advanced Topics

  • 🫀 Coronary anatomy & dominance: the RV marginal (acute marginal) branches arise from the proximal-to-mid RCA; occlusion proximal to them defines RV-involving infarcts. Left dominance (circumflex-supplied PDA) makes isolated RV infarction far less likely.
  • 📈 The full right-sided/posterior workup: V4R is the headline; V3R–V6R and posterior leads V7–V9 detect posterolateral extension. ST elevation III > II and ST depression in I/aVL also point to an RCA culprit.
  • 🩻 Echo/hemodynamic signatures: dilated hypokinetic RV, tricuspid regurgitation, right-to-left interatrial shunt through a stretched PFO (refractory hypoxemia), equalization of diastolic pressures, and a noncompliant RV mimicking constriction (Kussmaul's sign).
  • 💉 When fluids aren't enough: add dobutamine for RV inotropy; consider pulmonary vasodilators to offload the RV; refractory cases may need an RV assist device or VA-ECMO. Avoid over-resuscitation — RV overdistension bows the septum left and chokes LV filling.
  • 🐢 Bradyarrhythmia nuances: inferior-MI complete heart block is usually high (nodal) with a stable junctional escape and good prognosis once reperfused — contrast with the unstable infranodal block of anterior MI. AV-sequential pacing beats ventricular-only pacing because it preserves the atrial kick.
  • 🫁 The PFO trap: rising right atrial pressure can open a PFO and cause right-to-left shunting → hypoxemia that worsens with the very PEEP/pressors used to treat it — a classic RV-infarct gotcha.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set, written as you'd actually write it (14:30)

  • 🚨 NO nitroglycerin, NO morphine, NO diuretic while preload-dependent/hypotensive — flag on chart and whiteboard.
  • ➡️ Right-sided 12-lead (V4R) + posterior leads (V7–V9) NOW — document ST elevation in V4R.
  • 💧 IV fluid bolus 250–500 mL crystalloid, reassess BP, JVP and lungs after each; repeat cautiously to support the RV (avoid over-filling).
  • 🫀 Activate primary PCI of the culprit RCA immediately; dual antiplatelet (aspirin + a P2Y₁₂ inhibitor) + anticoagulation per protocol.
  • 🐢 Atropine 0.5–1 mg IV for symptomatic bradycardia/AV block; set up transcutaneous pacing and prepare AV-sequential temporary pacing if high-grade block.
  • 💪 Dobutamine infusion if hypotension persists despite adequate filling (RV inotropic support) — not endless saline.
  • 🫁 O₂ only if SpO₂ < 94%; continuous monitoring; second large-bore IV; serial 12-leads.
  • 🩸 Bloods: troponin, U&E, glucose, CBC, coagulation; cautious analgesia (small titrated dose) if pain severe, watching the pressure closely.
  • 🔌 Keep defibrillator and pacing pads attached — anticipate brady-/tachyarrhythmia and reperfusion arrhythmia.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ CCU nursing priorities — minute by minute

  • 🚫 Guard the drug chart. Know that this patient must NOT receive nitroglycerin, morphine or a diuretic while preload-dependent — query any such prescription before giving it.
  • 💧 Run fluid boluses as prescribed and reassess in between — BP, JVP and lung sounds after each bolus. Tell the doctor if the lungs start to crackle (sign you are reaching the RV's ceiling).
  • 🩸 BP every 2–5 min (cycle the NIBP, or read the arterial line once placed). A falling pressure means escalate now, not at the next set of obs.
  • 🐢 Watch the rhythm for bradycardia and AV block — have atropine drawn and the pacing pads attached; a sudden junctional rhythm or complete block can drop the pressure fast.
  • 📟 Keep continuous telemetry with defibrillator/pacing ready at the bedside; pads on.
  • 🫀 Prepare for emergent PCI: consent support, groin/radial prep, antiplatelet/anticoagulant administration, transfer readiness.
  • 🧠 Neuro/perfusion checks — a patient who becomes confused or cold is losing output; recheck the pressure and the rhythm.
  • 📋 Document the V4R strip and every bolus/response, the rate and any pacing — gold for the cath-lab and CCU team.
  • 👨‍👩‍👦 Keep the family updated and supported (see S16) — assign a liaison if staffing allows; a low pressure with a "normal chest X-ray" is confusing and frightening to relatives.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who refuses the routine nitrate and keeps the right ventricle full is doing half the resuscitation in the room."

🐢 Bradycardia / AV block on the stripThe RCA feeds the SA/AV nodes; conduction disease accompanies inferior/RV infarction and is usually nodal and atropine-responsive.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Find ST elevation in V4R and the argument is over — it's an RV infarct. But you must look right-sided to see it: every inferior STEMI gets a V4R."

🫀 Post-reperfusion ECG & echo

  • 📈 Post-PCI 12-lead: resolving inferior ST elevation, recovering rate as nodal block lifts with reperfusion; the V4R elevation settles as the RV is reperfused.
  • 🩻 Echo: dilated, hypokinetic RV with tricuspid regurgitation and preserved LV systolic function; look for a right-to-left interatrial shunt (PFO) if hypoxemia is out of proportion to clear lungs.
  • 🧲 Cardiac MRI (later): confirms and quantifies RV infarction/edema and excludes other RV pathology; informs prognosis.
⚠️ The ECG's favorite lie in inferior STEMI"The standard 12-lead only shows an inferior STEMI, so the RV is fine." The conventional leads do not face the right ventricle — RV infarction is silent on a normal 12-lead. The only way to see it is a right-sided tracing. Skip the V4R and you can miss the very thing that makes a nitrate lethal.

💊 Medications

💉 Acute drugs — what, why, and the traps

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Nitrates, morphine and diuretics all empty the heart. Give any of them to an RV infarct and you remove the only thing filling the left ventricle. In RV infarction the treatment is volume, not venodilation."
🚫 The 'never' list in right ventricular infarction❌ Nitroglycerin (sublingual or IV) · ❌ routine morphine/opioids early · ❌ diuretics · ❌ aggressive over-diuresis or "treating" the high JVP · ❌ losing AV synchrony without correcting it. When the pressure is low and the lungs are clear, the safest "drug" is a cautious bag of fluid and an open RCA.

🏠 Secondary-prevention therapy (this admission, not just today)

  • 💙 Standard post-MI therapy once stable and reperfused: dual antiplatelet, high-intensity statin, ACE inhibitor and beta-blocker (introduced carefully — beta-blockers can worsen the bradycardia/AV block early in RV infarction, so start once rate and pressure allow).
  • 🫀 Reperfusion follow-through — complete revascularization as indicated, cardiac rehab.
  • 🚭 Risk-factor modification — smoking cessation, diabetes and lipid control, blood-pressure management.
  • 📈 Re-image RV function — RV stunning often recovers substantially over days to weeks after reperfusion.
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🩸 Invasive arterial line for beat-to-beat BP through fluid boluses, PCI and any inotrope titration — a preload-dependent circulation can change fast.
  • 💧 Volume response after every bolus — track BP, JVP and lung sounds; the appearance of crackles signals you have reached the RV's filling ceiling.
  • 📈 Continuous telemetry with defibrillator/pacing at the bedside — for bradycardia, AV block, loss of AV synchrony and reperfusion arrhythmias.
  • 🐢 Rate and rhythm — a new junctional rhythm or complete block in a stiff RV can drop the pressure; be ready to pace AV-sequentially.
  • 🫁 SpO₂ and work of breathing — sudden refractory hypoxemia with clear lungs suggests right-to-left shunting across a PFO.
  • 🫀 Serial 12-leads and V4R — track ST resolution after reperfusion; recovering rate often signals the nodal block lifting.
  • 🧪 Serial troponin, lactate, renal function — trend infarct size, perfusion and contrast effect on the kidneys.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you reperfuse the RV, the monitor's job changes from 'is he filled enough?' to 'have I over-filled him?' — and the two numbers that answer it are the blood pressure and the lung exam."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — and the mechanism

  • 💉 Preload-reducer–induced collapse — the great fear; a nitrate, opioid or diuretic strips the preload keeping the LV filled → profound hypotension/arrest.
  • 🐢 Bradycardia / high-grade AV block — RCA feeds the nodes; loss of rate or AV synchrony crashes a stiff, preload-dependent RV.
  • 🧠 Cardiogenic shock from RV failure — refractory low output despite filling; needs inotropes, sometimes RV mechanical support/VA-ECMO.
  • 🫁 Refractory hypoxemia via right-to-left PFO shunt — high right atrial pressure opens a PFO; hypoxemia paradoxically worsens with pressors/PEEP.
  • 💧 Iatrogenic RV overload — over-aggressive fluids distend the RV, bow the septum into the LV and reduce output (the opposite error).
  • 🫀 Mechanical & arrhythmic complications — reperfusion arrhythmias, tricuspid regurgitation, and (in extensive infarcts) RV free-wall issues; recurrence if the artery re-occludes.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference — "The Nitrate That Emptied the Heart"

The case: A 64-year-old man arrived with two hours of inferior chest pain; the 12-lead showed an inferior STEMI and his BP was 96/60. He had ongoing pain, so the busy team gave sublingual then IV nitroglycerin "to settle the ischemia." Within ninety seconds his pressure collapsed to unrecordable and he arrested. He had clear lungs and a raised JVP that no one had connected. A V4R was never recorded. The retrospective right-sided strip, projected at the M&M, showed unmistakable ST elevation in V4R — a right ventricular infarction whose only therapy he could not survive was the one he was given.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

➡️ NO V4RRight-sided leads were never recorded — the RV infarct was invisible on the standard 12-lead.
🫁 CLUES IGNOREDClear lungs + raised JVP + hypotension is the RV-infarct triad, unread at the bedside.
💉 LETHAL DRUGNitroglycerin removed the preload the underfilled LV depended on → collapse.
🧠 REFLEX"STEMI + pain = give a nitrate" applied without checking the physiology.
🌙 SYSTEMBusy department, no "every inferior STEMI gets a V4R" default.

🛡️ What would have saved him: apply the universal rule — inferior STEMI + hypotension + clear lungs + raised JVP = RV infarction; record a V4R on every inferior STEMI; never give nitrates/morphine/diuretics to a preload-dependent RV; treat with cautious fluids and reperfusion. This is exactly why your paramedic crew held the GTN today.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'STEMI with chest pain, give a nitrate' is the sentence written on the chart of patients who then arrest from an unrecognised RV infarct. Check the lungs and the V4R first."

💎 Clinical Pearls

💧 Fluids, not nitratesInferior STEMI + hypotension + clear lungs + raised JVP = RV infarction. Give fluids, NOT nitrates. The triad is the diagnosis.
🚫 Never nitrate an RV infarctNitroglycerin removes the only thing filling the heart. The same is true of morphine and diuretics — all empty the preload-dependent LV.
➡️ Every inferior STEMI gets a V4RST elevation in V4R is the most useful single sign of RV infarction — and the standard 12-lead can't see the RV.
🫁 The lungs break the tieHypotension with CLEAR lungs → fill the RV. Hypotension with WET lungs → LV pump failure, don't fill. Same low BP, opposite treatment.
🐢 Inferior MI loves to blockThe RCA feeds the AV node — watch for bradycardia and heart block. Usually nodal and atropine-responsive.
💓 Atrial kick mattersThe stiff RV depends on AV synchrony. Lose it (junctional/complete block) and the pressure drops — pace AV-sequentially.
🫀 Open the arteryPrimary PCI of the proximal RCA is the definitive treatment — reperfusion recovers the stunned RV and lifts the block.
💪 Fill, then inotropeIf sensible fluids don't lift the pressure, add dobutamine — don't chase the pressure with endless saline and overload the RV.
📐 Raised JVP ≠ wet lungsThe high JVP is venous return behind a stiff RV, not pulmonary congestion. Don't reach for furosemide.
🩸 Proximal RCA is the culpritThe lesion must be proximal to the RV marginal branches to take the right ventricle with it.
🫁 Beware the PFO shuntHigh right atrial pressure can open a PFO → refractory hypoxemia that worsens with pressors/PEEP.
📈 The RV recoversRV stunning usually improves substantially over days to weeks once the artery is open — support it through the storm.
