🧭 The sequence that never changes

The device pathway is a fixed order: (1) optimise all four pillars of GDMT for ≥3 months → (2) reassess EF → (3) if EF still ≤35%, assess QRS width/morphology and rhythm → (4) offer CRT for wide LBBB, and an ICD for primary prevention where indicated (usually combined as CRT-D). Skip step 1 and you may implant hardware into a ventricle that would have recovered.

⚠️ The trap of implanting too early — and of the narrow QRSTwo mirror-image errors: rushing a device before an adequate GDMT trial (some ventricles recover EF to >35% and no longer need it), and offering CRT to a patient with a narrow QRS <130 ms — precisely the group the Echo-CRT trial showed to be harmed. Both come from treating the diagnosis label rather than the physiology.

The cardiology resident presents to the device MDT:

🧠 Frame it as two separate decisions

"This isn't one question, it's two. Question one — does he need a defibrillator? EF is 28% on optimal therapy with reasonable life expectancy, so he meets primary-prevention ICD criteria — though he's non-ischaemic, so the DANISH nuance applies. Question two — does he need resynchronisation? He's in sinus rhythm with an LBBB at 160 ms and still symptomatic, which is the strongest CRT indication we have. Both are true here, so the natural answer is a combined CRT-D."

🎯 The resident's order of operations

  1. ✅ Confirm the GDMT trial was adequate — all four pillars, ≥3 months, up-titrated. Documented.
  2. 🫀 Confirm persistent EF ≤35% on a good-quality echo (or CMR) after that trial.
  3. 📈 Characterise the QRS — LBBB morphology and width ≥150 ms → high CRT-response probability.
  4. ⚡ Assess ICD candidacy — EF ≤35%, NYHA II–III, expected survival >1 yr; weigh ischaemic vs non-ischaemic aetiology (DANISH).
  5. 🤝 Shared decision-making — device type (CRT-P vs CRT-D), procedural risks, infection, and end-of-life deactivation discussed BEFORE implant.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"An ICD prevents sudden death; CRT makes the heart pump better. Decide each on its own merits, then combine them into a CRT-D only when both are truly indicated."
🧪 Resident's self-testA colleague argues: "He's symptomatic HFrEF — just put in a CRT, the QRS doesn't matter much." Correct?

Wrong — the QRS is everything. CRT benefit tracks tightly with QRS width and morphology: greatest with LBBB and QRS ≥150 ms, intermediate at 130–149 ms, and in narrow QRS <130 ms the Echo-CRT trial showed no benefit and a signal of harm. Here the LBBB at 160 ms is exactly why CRT is a good idea — but the same device in a narrow-QRS patient would be a mistake.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY resynchronisation works, WHY the defibrillator is needed

  1. 🔌 Normal conduction: the His–Purkinje system fires both ventricles almost simultaneously → a coordinated, efficient squeeze and a narrow QRS.
  2. 📈 Left bundle branch block: the left ventricle is now activated late and slowly, cell-to-cell across working myocardium. The septum contracts early while the lateral wall is still relaxed, then the lateral wall contracts against an already-emptying septum — mechanical dyssynchrony that wastes stroke work and worsens mitral regurgitation. The wider the QRS, the worse the wasted work.
  3. 🔋 CRT (biventricular pacing): a lead in the coronary sinus paces the LV lateral wall, timed with the RV, so the walls contract together again. Stroke volume rises, MR falls, and over months the ventricle reverse-remodels — smaller volumes, higher EF, fewer HF admissions, longer survival.
  4. ⚡ The parallel danger — sudden death: a scarred, dilated ventricle with EF ≤35% is an arrhythmic substrate. Re-entrant VT/VF can strike without warning. An ICD does nothing for the pump but stands ready to defibrillate a lethal rhythm — a safety net, not a treatment.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"CRT fixes a TIMING problem (dyssynchrony), the ICD guards against an ELECTRICAL catastrophe (VT/VF). One improves how the heart pumps; the other stops it from stopping."

⚖️ Contrast pair — ICD vs CRT

⚡ ICD — the safety net

  • Prevents sudden ARRHYTHMIC death (VT/VF)
  • Does nothing for symptoms or EF
  • Benefit strongest in ISCHAEMIC cardiomyopathy (MADIT-II, SCD-HeFT); attenuated in non-ischaemic DCM (DANISH)
  • Prophylactic — a shock delivered only when needed

🔌 CRT — the treatment

  • Improves PUMP function by resynchronising the LV
  • Raises EF, cuts symptoms & HF admissions, improves survival
  • Benefit is ALL about the QRS: LBBB, wide ≥150 ms
  • A therapy working continuously, beat to beat

💡 Most patients meeting both criteria receive a combined CRT-D — resynchronisation for the pump plus a defibrillator for the safety net.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The device work-up order set

  • 📈 12-lead ECG — confirm rhythm (sinus vs AF), QRS width and morphology (true LBBB criteria), PR interval.
  • 🫀 Transthoracic echo — confirm EF ≤35%, LV volumes (baseline for reverse-remodelling), MR severity, RV function; consider CMR for scar burden/aetiology.
  • 💊 Document the GDMT trial — all four pillars (ARNI/ACEi/ARB, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i), doses, duration ≥3 months, adherence.
  • 🩸 Bloods — U&E (K⁺, renal function), FBC, CRP/procalcitonin if any infection concern (implant safety), thyroid, iron studies.
  • 🤝 Shared decision-making consult — CRT-P vs CRT-D, primary-prevention rationale, procedural & infection risk, future deactivation discussion.
  • 🦷 Pre-implant sepsis screen — no active infection, dental/skin check; hold implant if febrile or bacteraemic (device infection is catastrophic).
  • 🩻 Refer to the device MDT / electrophysiology for CRT-D implant planning (venous anatomy, coronary sinus target vein).
  • 📅 Plan re-assessment of EF if any doubt the GDMT trial was adequate — some ventricles are still recovering.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Before the leads go in, prove three things: the drugs were given a fair trial, the EF is genuinely ≤35%, and the QRS earns a CRT. Skip any one and you may implant hardware nobody needed."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Peri-implant nursing priorities

  • 💉 Pre-implant: confirm no active infection (temperature, wound/skin check, recent bacteraemia), IV antibiotic prophylaxis on schedule, group & save, coagulation checked; withhold/adjust anticoagulation per protocol.
  • 📟 Post-implant monitoring: telemetry for lead displacement or arrhythmia, wound site for haematoma or swelling, and observe for the early complications of implant (see S13).
  • 🫁 Watch for pneumothorax after subclavian access — new breathlessness, low SpO₂, chest pain; escalate for a CXR.
  • 🩹 Pocket care: inspect for haematoma, erythema, discharge; strict asepsis — device-pocket infection can seed the leads and demands full system extraction.
  • 🧾 Patient education: arm-movement restrictions on the implant side (lead stability), wound care, device-ID card, and when to seek help; explain what a shock feels like and what to do after one.
  • 🤝 Emotional support & expectations: reassure about CRT benefit timeline (symptom/EF improvement over weeks–months), and sensitively introduce that the ICD is a safety net, including future deactivation conversations.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who spots the red, swollen device pocket early saves the whole system — a seeded lead means the entire device comes out."

🩻 Adjunct imaging

  • Echocardiography: confirms EF ≤35%, quantifies LV volumes and functional MR, and provides the baseline for tracking reverse-remodelling (a ≥15% fall in LV end-systolic volume defines an echo responder).
  • Cardiac MRI: defines aetiology and, crucially, scar burden — extensive posterolateral scar predicts CRT non-response (the lead paces dead tissue) and higher arrhythmic risk.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch after CRT-D implant — and at follow-up

  • 🔌 Biventricular pacing percentage — CRT benefit needs >98% BiV pacing. Loss of capture, AF with rapid conduction, or frequent ectopy that inhibits pacing all steal the benefit; chase and fix them.
  • 📈 Device interrogation — lead thresholds/impedances, appropriate vs inappropriate shocks, arrhythmia burden; remote monitoring flags problems early.
  • 🫀 Response assessment at 3–6 months — repeat echo for EF and LV volumes (reverse-remodelling), symptoms/NYHA, functional capacity. Optimise the AV/VV timing in non-responders.
  • 🩸 Electrolytes & renal function — K⁺/Mg²⁺ to minimise arrhythmia; continue up-titrating GDMT (the device does not replace it).
  • ⚡ Shock review & wellbeing — every shock is documented and interrogated; screen for the anxiety/PTSD that follows shocks and address it.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"CRT only works if it's actually pacing — chase every point below ~98% biventricular pacing, because a resynchronisation device that isn't resynchronising is just an expensive ICD."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 Device complications — procedural and long-term

  • 🫁 Peri-procedural: pneumothorax/haemothorax (subclavian access), cardiac perforation/tamponade, coronary sinus dissection during LV lead placement, pocket haematoma.
  • 🔌 Lead problems: dislodgement (esp. the LV lead), rising thresholds/loss of capture, phrenic nerve stimulation (diaphragmatic twitch), lead fracture or insulation failure over years.
  • 🦠 Device infection: pocket infection or lead endocarditis — a feared complication; usually mandates complete system extraction plus antibiotics. Prevented by asepsis, prophylaxis, and never implanting into an infected/bacteraemic patient.
  • ⚡ Inappropriate shocks: from AF/SVT, T-wave oversensing, or lead noise — painful, harmful, and psychologically scarring; minimised by careful programming and higher detection zones.
  • 🫀 CRT non-response (~30%): no symptomatic/echo improvement — from non-LBBB morphology, scar over the LV lead, suboptimal lead position, low pacing %, or AF.
  • 🧠 Psychological: shock-related anxiety/PTSD; and the harder issue of deactivation at end of life to avoid distressing shocks while dying.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The worst device complication is the one you implanted needlessly — an infection, a fracture, or an inappropriate shock in a patient who was never going to benefit (narrow QRS, recovered EF, or terminal comorbidity)."

  • Shared decision-making, written safety-net, timely outpatient testing if needed
  • Explicit "come back immediately if…" advice
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A safe chest-pain discharge is an active decision with a score, a negative troponin, a clean ECG and a written safety-net — never a silent 'looked fine.'"

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🩻 The "Occlusion MI (OMI)" paradigm: the STEMI/NSTEMI dichotomy misses acute coronary occlusion without ST-elevation criteria. OMI-vs-NOMI thinking (subtle occlusion patterns, hyperacute T waves, modified-Sgarbossa, AI-ECG) identifies patients who benefit from emergent reperfusion despite "non-STEMI" labels.
  • 🧪 hs-troponin nuance: sex-specific 99th-percentile cut-offs; the value of very low single values (< limit of detection) for immediate rule-out; assay-specific 0/1-h and 0/2-h absolute delta thresholds (ESC 2023).
  • 🫀 MINOCA (MI with non-obstructive coronaries): work up plaque disruption (intravascular imaging — OCT/IVUS), coronary spasm (provocation), spontaneous coronary artery dissection (SCAD — think young women, peripartum), microvascular disease, and cardiac MRI to distinguish true MI from myocarditis/Takotsubo.
  • 🧬 Antiplatelet timing debate: routine pre-treatment with P2Y₁₂ before angiography in NSTE-ACS is no longer recommended (no ischaemic benefit, more bleeding — ISAR-REACT 5 favoured prasugrel over ticagrelor with a deferred-loading strategy).
  • 🖥️ Coronary CT angiography in low–intermediate-risk ED chest pain: high NPV for ruling out CAD (ROMICAT-II, PROMISE), useful when troponin/ECG are equivocal and pre-test probability is modest.
  • 🩸 Cardiogenic-shock ACS: culprit-only PCI (CULPRIT-SHOCK), early revascularisation (SHOCK), judicious use of mechanical support.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Antacid That Killed"

The case: A 61-year-old diabetic man came to a crowded ED at 02:00 with "burning" epigastric/chest discomfort and one vomit. Triaged as "dyspepsia." He was given an antacid "GI cocktail," felt "a bit better," and — with the department full — was moved to the corridor to await review. The single ECG taken at triage showed tall R waves and ST depression in V1–V3; no one recorded posterior leads and no one repeated it. Ninety minutes later he was found unresponsive in the corridor chair — a posterior STEMI that had extended, then arrested in VF. Resuscitation failed.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Dyspepsia" label stuck; the GI cocktail "response" falsely reassured.
📈 ECG NOT READPosterior STEMI pattern (ST↓ V1–V3) unrecognised; posterior leads never done.
🔁 NO SERIAL ECGOne snapshot, never repeated despite ongoing symptoms.
🏥 SYSTEMOvercrowding → corridor care → no monitoring, no defib to hand.
🩺 RISK IGNOREDDiabetic with atypical presentation treated as low-risk.

🛡️ What would have saved him: treat every diabetic with chest/epigastric pain as high-risk; read ST depression in V1–V3 as a possible posterior STEMI and record V7–V9; repeat the ECG on any ongoing symptom; keep suspected ACS monitored with defib to hand — never in an unmonitored corridor; and never let a "GI cocktail response" close the door. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 1.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Responded to antacid' has killed more patients than it has ever diagnosed. Symptom relief is not a diagnosis — the ECG and the troponin are."
