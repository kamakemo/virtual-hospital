📜 History of the presenting problem — a relentless, painless march

  • 💪 12 months of progressive weakness starting in the right hand (dropping tools, weak grip), then the right leg (tripping, foot-drop). Painless, purely motor.
  • 🌊 Fasciculations — visible muscle twitching, worse when tired; wife noticed them months ago. Plus wasting and cramps.

🔥 Why this is easy to mislabel for monthsEarly MND masquerades as carpal tunnel, a "trapped nerve", cervical spondylosis, or even a stroke. The mean diagnostic delay is ~10–16 months. The tragedy is that the delay eats into the short window where riluzole, NIV, and multidisciplinary care make the most difference. The pattern — mixed UMN + LMN, no sensory signs — is what shortens that delay.

🧭 Demonstrating mixed UMN + LMN — the exam that clinches it

⬇️ Lower motor neurone signs

  • Muscle wasting (thenar, first dorsal interosseous)
  • Fasciculations (limbs + tongue)
  • Reduced tone, cramps

⬆️ Upper motor neurone signs

  • Brisk reflexes in wasted limbs (a striking combination)
  • Extensor plantars (Babinski)
  • Spasticity, spastic dysarthria, brisk jaw jerk
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A brisk reflex in a wasted, fasciculating muscle is almost pathognomonic of MND — it is the meeting of upper and lower motor neurone damage in one place."
⚠️ The negatives that protect the diagnosisNo sensory level, no numbness, normal eye movements, and continent bladder/bowel. If any of these are present, actively hunt for a mimic — a cord compression, a neuropathy, myasthenia, or a brainstem lesion — before settling on MND.

The neurology resident thinks out loud at 10:28:

🧠 Frame it as pattern + exclusion + protect the patient

"This is mixed UMN and LMN across multiple regions — hand, leg, and now bulbar — with sparing of sensation, sphincters and eye movements. That pattern is MND. My job is threefold: confirm the pattern with EMG, exclude the treatable mimics (cervical myelopathy, myasthenia, GBS, Kennedy's), and — most urgently — assess and protect his breathing and swallow. Respiratory failure is what will kill him, not his grip."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🫁 Respiratory assessment now — sitting AND supine FVC, sniff nasal inspiratory pressure (SNIP), morning symptoms; refer for NIV assessment early.
  2. 🍽️ Swallow assessment (SALT) — nil-by-mouth if unsafe; discuss PEG feeding before he becomes malnourished or respiratory-compromised.
  3. ⚡ EMG + nerve conduction studies — look for widespread active + chronic denervation with normal sensory conduction.
  4. 🧲 MRI brain + whole spine — exclude cervical myelopathy/structural mimic; bloods (including for mimics).
  5. 🤝 Refer to the specialist MND multidisciplinary team — neurologist, respiratory, SALT, dietitian, physio/OT, specialist nurse, palliative care.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Diagnose the pattern, exclude the mimics, but never let the work-up delay the two interventions that change survival — NIV and swallow safety."
🧪 Resident's self-testHis SpO₂ is 94% and he seems comfortable at rest. Is his breathing fine to leave for the clinic in 6 weeks?

No. In neuromuscular respiratory failure, SpO₂ stays normal until very late — the problem is ventilation (rising CO₂), not oxygenation. Orthopnoea, morning headaches, disturbed sleep and a falling FVC/SNIP are the early warnings. Assess respiratory function urgently and refer for NIV — do NOT be reassured by a normal saturation.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY mixed signs, WHY relentless (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. ⬇️ Lower motor neurones die — the anterior horn cells of the spinal cord and the motor cranial nuclei of the brainstem degenerate. Denervated muscle wastes, twitches (fasciculations) and weakens: the LMN signs.
  2. ⬆️ Upper motor neurones die too — the corticospinal (pyramidal) tracts and Betz cells degenerate. Loss of descending inhibition leaves brisk reflexes, spasticity and extensor plantars: the UMN signs. Both levels failing together in one body is the fingerprint of MND.
  3. 🚫 Why sensation, eyes and sphincters are spared: the disease targets the motor system. Sensory pathways, the oculomotor nuclei (relatively resistant), and Onuf's nucleus (sphincter control) are characteristically preserved — which is exactly why their involvement should make you doubt the diagnosis.
  4. 🧪 Molecular drivers: TDP-43 protein aggregation, glutamate excitotoxicity (the target of riluzole), oxidative stress, and mitochondrial dysfunction; ~10% familial (e.g. C9orf72 repeat expansion, SOD1).
  5. 🫁 Why it kills: as motor neurones to the diaphragm and bulbar muscles die, ventilation fails and the airway can no longer be protected — respiratory failure and aspiration, not the limb weakness, end life.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"MND is a pure motor catastrophe — up and down the motor pathway at once — that spares the senses, the eyes and the bladder, and eventually takes the breath."

⚖️ Contrast pair — MND vs its great mimic (myasthenia gravis)

🧠 Motor neurone disease

  • Mixed UMN + LMN, wasting, fasciculations
  • Progressive, not fatigable; asymmetric onset
  • EMG: denervation; sensation normal
  • Relentless, incurable

🔋 Myasthenia gravis

  • Pure fatigable weakness, ptosis, diplopia (eyes involved!)
  • No wasting/fasciculation early; reflexes normal
  • AChR/MuSK antibodies; decrement on repetitive stimulation
  • Treatable — do not miss it

💡 The eyes are the giveaway: prominent ptosis/diplopia points to myasthenia, not MND, where eye movements are classically spared.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Bedside priorities — protect breathing, swallow and dignity

  • 🫁 Position upright, monitor respiratory rate and work of breathing; escalate early signs of hypoventilation (rising drowsiness, headache, confusion = CO₂ retention).
  • 🍽️ Swallow safety — NBM until SALT clears; supervise meals, suction to hand, thickened fluids as advised; watch for coughing/wet voice = aspiration.
  • 💧 Secretion management — suction, antimuscarinics for drooling, positioning; a weak cough cannot clear secretions.
  • 🛌 Pressure area & mobility care — immobile, wasted patient; falls precautions; support communication (weak, dysarthric speech — allow time, use aids/boards).
  • 🧠 Emotional support — this is a devastating diagnosis; involve family, specialist nurse and palliative team; support advance care planning conversations.
  • 😢 Watch for emotional lability (pseudobulbar affect) and distinguish it from depression.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who spots the wet voice, the weak cough and the morning headache is the early-warning system for the two things that kill in MND — aspiration and respiratory failure."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🫁 Respiratory function (FVC + SNIP/MIP) — the survival-critical trend; falling values and orthopnoea/morning headaches trigger NIV. Do NOT rely on SpO₂.
  • 🍽️ Swallow & weight — regular SALT/dietitian review; falling weight or unsafe swallow prompts PEG before respiratory decline makes it high-risk.
  • 📉 ALSFRS-R functional score — tracks progression across limb, bulbar and respiratory domains.
  • 😴 Nocturnal hypoventilation — sleep quality, morning headache, daytime somnolence; overnight oximetry/capnography.
  • 💧 Secretions, spasticity, mood, communication — reassess symptom control at every visit.
  • 🤝 Advance care plan — revisit wishes, ceiling of care, preferred place of death as disease progresses.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Monitor the breathing and the swallow relentlessly — in MND the FVC is the vital sign that predicts survival, and PEG timing hinges on getting there before the lungs are too weak."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What goes wrong — and what kills

  • 🫁 Respiratory failure — progressive ventilatory (type 2) failure; the commonest cause of death. NIV is the key intervention.
  • 🥄 Aspiration pneumonia — from bulbar weakness and unsafe swallow; a frequent terminal event and a preventable crisis.
  • 📉 Malnutrition & weight loss — dysphagia; late PEG (after FVC falls) is riskier — hence early timing.
  • 🚶 Immobility complications — falls, contractures, pressure sores, VTE.
  • 🗣️ Communication loss & social isolation — anarthria; needs augmentative communication.
  • 🧠 Cognitive/behavioural change — up to ~15% develop frontotemporal dementia (ALS-FTD); pseudobulbar affect distresses patient and family.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The two complications that end life in MND — respiratory failure and aspiration — are the two you can most anticipate and soften with early NIV, swallow assessment and planning."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the diagnosis

  • 🧬 ALS-FTD spectrum & genetics: up to ~50% have some cognitive/behavioural change and ~15% frank frontotemporal dementia; the C9orf72 hexanucleotide repeat expansion links familial ALS and FTD, alongside SOD1, TARDBP and FUS mutations. TDP-43 pathology unites most cases.
  • ⚡ Diagnostic criteria evolution: El Escorial → revised Airlie House → Awaji (giving fasciculation potentials the weight of fibrillations, improving early sensitivity) → the newer Gold Coast criteria (progressive motor impairment with UMN and/or LMN dysfunction in ≥1 region, plus exclusion of mimics) which further simplify and sensitise diagnosis.
  • 🫁 Respiratory science: NIV timing guided by symptomatic hypoventilation, FVC (<~50% predicted), SNIP/MIP, and nocturnal desaturation/hypercapnia; diaphragm pacing has not shown benefit and may harm. Bulbar-predominant patients tolerate NIV less well — a key nuance.
  • 💊 Emerging therapy: tofersen (an antisense oligonucleotide) for SOD1-ALS lowers neurofilament and is approved in some settings; sodium phenylbutyrate–taurursodiol and other agents have mixed/withdrawn evidence — a fast-moving field.
  • 🩺 Neurofilament light (NfL): emerging blood/CSF biomarker of axonal degeneration for diagnosis, prognosis and trial monitoring.
  • 📜 Ethics of ventilation: the difference between NIV (revocable comfort/survival benefit) and tracheostomy ventilation (prolongs life but into the locked-in stages) makes early, honest advance care planning essential — decisions must be made before the crisis.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Breathing Nobody Watched"

The case: A 62-year-old man with bulbar-onset MND was managed in a general clinic. Over three months he reported orthopnoea, disrupted sleep and morning headaches — repeatedly recorded as "anxiety" — and his wife mentioned he was coughing on meals. His swallow was never formally assessed by SALT, no NIV referral was made, and his FVC was never measured supine (the sitting value "looked okay"). No advance care planning conversation had ever taken place. One night he was rushed in with aspiration pneumonia and type 2 respiratory failure. With no NIV in place, no documented ceiling of care, and a family who had never been prepared, he was intubated in extremis and died in ICU days later after a distressing, uninformed escalation — exactly the ending anticipatory care exists to prevent.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🫁 RESPIRATORY MISSEDOrthopnoea + morning headaches labelled "anxiety"; ventilation failing unnoticed.
📉 FVC LIEDSitting FVC "okay"; supine FVC + SNIP never measured; normal SpO₂ falsely reassured.
🍽️ NO SWALLOW ASSESSMENTChoking ignored; no SALT, no PEG plan — aspiration set up.
🩺 NO NIVNo respiratory referral; the biggest survival/quality intervention never offered.
📜 NO PLANNINGNo advance care plan; crisis decisions made in extremis at 3 am.

🛡️ What would have saved him from this ending: treat orthopnoea and morning headaches in MND as respiratory failure until proven otherwise; measure supine FVC and SNIP and never trust a normal SpO₂; refer early for NIV and formal swallow assessment; time a PEG before respiratory reserve is lost; and have honest advance care planning conversations early, so a respiratory crisis is met by an agreed plan rather than a panicked escalation. This is exactly the anticipatory care that helped the patient in Bed 8.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In MND, the limb weakness is what you see, but the breathing and the swallow are what kill — watch them relentlessly, plan for them early, and never be reassured by a normal oxygen saturation."
