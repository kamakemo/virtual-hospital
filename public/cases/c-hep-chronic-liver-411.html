🔥 Why the clinic is where curable liver disease is lostMASLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, the modern name for NAFLD) affects ~25-30% of adults and is the commonest liver disease worldwide — so "fatty liver" is nearly always statistically correct. That very commonness is the trap: it anchors the clinician, the screen is skipped, and the ~1 in 20 with a coexisting or alternative treatable cause (autoimmune hepatitis, haemochromatosis, Wilson disease) progresses silently to cirrhosis.

🧭 The reflex framework — pattern the LFTs first

Before any label, decide the pattern: is this a hepatocellular picture (ALT/AST-dominant — MASLD, viral, autoimmune, drugs, Wilson, haemochromatosis) or a cholestatic one (ALP/GGT-dominant — PBC, PSC, obstruction, drugs)? Then screen along the correct branch. Our patient is hepatocellular (ALT 96, ALP mildly up), so the hepatocellular screen leads — but you still send the cholestatic markers, because patterns overlap.

⚠️ The trap of "AST > ALT means alcohol"An AST:ALT ratio > 2 classically suggests alcohol, but a ratio that climbs above 1 in a MASLD patient is also a marker of advancing fibrosis — the enzymes can even fall as the liver burns out. Do not read falling transaminases as "getting better." Read the platelets and the fibrosis score.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The hepatology registrar thinks out loud:

🧠 Frame it as two parallel questions

"There are two jobs here, not one. Question 1: what is the aetiology? — and I refuse to answer 'MASLD' until the full non-invasive screen is back negative for everything else. Question 2: how much fibrosis is there already? — because that, not the enzyme number, decides prognosis and follow-up. I will send the complete chronic liver disease screen today and calculate FIB-4 now, then arrange transient elastography."

🎯 The order of operations

  1. 🧫 Send the full aetiology screen in one hit (S9) — viral, autoimmune, iron, copper, alpha-1-antitrypsin, coeliac, thyroid. Do not drip-feed one test per visit.
  2. 🧮 Calculate FIB-4 today (age, AST, ALT, platelets) — free, instant, and it triages who needs urgent fibrosis assessment.
  3. 📳 Arrange transient elastography (FibroScan) ± ELF blood test for anyone in the indeterminate/high FIB-4 zone.
  4. ⚖️ Start the intervention that works regardless of cause — metabolic control and weight loss — while the screen returns.
  5. 👁️ Slit-lamp + caeruloplasmin in a patient this age; never assume the young cannot have Wilson.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Do not answer 'what caused it' until you have excluded the treatable causes, and do not answer 'is it serious' until you have staged the fibrosis. Aetiology and fibrosis are two separate questions — answer both."
🧪 Registrar self-testThe transaminases are only mildly raised — can we just recheck in a year and skip the screen?

No. The height of the ALT correlates poorly with the severity of liver disease — burnt-out cirrhosis can have near-normal enzymes, and Wilson or autoimmune hepatitis can present with only modest rises. A FIB-4 of 2.6 already mandates a fibrosis assessment now, and a persistent unexplained derangement always earns the full screen. "Recheck in a year" is how curable disease becomes cirrhosis.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY fat becomes fibrosis (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🍔 Steatosis: insulin resistance and caloric excess flood hepatocytes with free fatty acids; triglyceride droplets accumulate. This alone (simple steatosis) is largely benign.
  2. 🔥 Lipotoxicity → steatohepatitis (NASH/MASH): excess free fatty acids overwhelm mitochondria → oxidative stress, ER stress and inflammasome activation → hepatocyte ballooning and inflammation. Fat is now hurting the cell.
  3. 🕸️ Fibrogenesis: injured hepatocytes and Kupffer cells signal hepatic stellate cells to transform into collagen-secreting myofibroblasts. Scar is laid down around the portal tracts.
  4. 🧱 Cirrhosis: bridging fibrosis distorts the architecture; regenerative nodules form; portal pressure rises (→ low platelets, splenomegaly, varices) and the risk of hepatocellular carcinoma appears.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Steatosis is a bystander; steatohepatitis is the fire; fibrosis is the scar that kills. The whole aim of assessment is to find who has crossed from fat to scar."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Simple steatosis vs Steatohepatitis with fibrosis

🟢 Simple steatosis (MASL)

  • Fat only, minimal inflammation
  • Low FIB-4, normal elastography
  • Excellent prognosis; metabolic risk management in primary care

🔴 Steatohepatitis + fibrosis (MASH)

  • Inflammation, ballooning, progressive scar
  • Rising FIB-4, raised liver stiffness
  • Progresses to cirrhosis / HCC; needs staging, surveillance, and possibly drug therapy

💡 Two patients with identical "fatty liver" scans can sit at opposite ends of this spectrum. Only fibrosis assessment tells them apart — the ultrasound cannot.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The clinic order set

  • 🧫 Full chronic liver disease screen (one blood draw): hepatitis B surface antigen & hepatitis C antibody; ANA, anti-smooth-muscle antibody, anti-LKM1, AMA, immunoglobulins (IgG, IgM); ferritin + transferrin saturation; caeruloplasmin (+ 24-h urinary copper if low); alpha-1-antitrypsin level; anti-TTG (coeliac); TSH.
  • 🧮 Calculate FIB-4 and NAFLD Fibrosis Score from today.
  • 📳 Transient elastography (FibroScan) ± ELF blood test given the FIB-4.
  • 🩸 Repeat LFTs, full blood count, coagulation, albumin, HbA1c, fasting lipids, renal function.
  • 👁️ Slit-lamp examination for Kayser-Fleischer rings.
  • ⚖️ Structured lifestyle intervention: dietitian referral, ≥7–10% weight-loss target, alcohol minimisation, exercise; optimise diabetes and blood pressure.
  • 💉 Check hepatitis A & B immunity and vaccinate the non-immune (protect the vulnerable liver).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Send the whole screen in one draw, not one test per visit. Drip-feeding the aetiology work-up wastes months during which a treatable disease scars the liver."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Clinic & day-unit priorities — the nurse anchors the lifestyle programme

  • 📳 Prepare and perform transient elastography correctly — the patient fasts ≥3 h; obesity may need the XL probe; flag unreliable scans (high IQR/median ratio) rather than reporting a false-reassuring number.
  • ⚖️ Own the weight-loss conversation — set a concrete ≥7–10% target, arrange dietitian and structured exercise, and review at each visit; this is the single most disease-modifying intervention.
  • 🍷 Take an honest, non-judgemental alcohol history and deliver brief intervention; document units accurately (it changes the diagnosis between MASLD, MetALD and ALD).
  • 🩸 Draw the full screen in one sitting, label copper/caeruloplasmin correctly (avoid contamination), and chase results so nothing is lost.
  • 💉 Check and complete hepatitis A/B vaccination; reinforce medication and diabetes adherence.
  • 🧾 Educate: "fatty liver" is not trivial — explain fibrosis, red-flag symptoms (jaundice, swelling, confusion, black stools) and the plan, so the patient partners in surveillance.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who secures a reliable FibroScan and a real weight-loss plan does more for this liver than any drug currently on the shelf."

🔬 When biopsy still earns its place

  • Suspected autoimmune hepatitis — interface hepatitis confirms and guides immunosuppression.
  • Discordant non-invasive tests, or a mixed/atypical picture (possible overlap syndrome).
  • To stage when the result will change management and non-invasive tools are inconclusive.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch once the diagnosis is set

  • 🧮 Serial FIB-4 / elastography — track fibrosis trajectory; response to weight loss or drug therapy.
  • 🖼️ 6-monthly ultrasound ± AFP once cirrhosis (F4) is established — HCC surveillance (see the sister case, HCC).
  • 🔭 Endoscopy for varices when cirrhosis is confirmed or platelets low / stiffness high (Baveno criteria decide who can skip it).
  • 🩸 LFTs, platelets, albumin, INR, bilirubin to detect decompensation early; recompute a MELD/Child-Pugh if cirrhotic.
  • ❤️ Aggressive cardiovascular risk monitoring — lipids, HbA1c, blood pressure — because that is what most often kills the MASLD patient.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Once a liver is cirrhotic from any cause, two surveillance programmes start for life: 6-monthly ultrasound for HCC and endoscopic assessment for varices. Diagnosing cirrhosis without starting surveillance is half a diagnosis."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 Where chronic liver disease goes if the screen and staging are missed

  • 🧱 Progression to cirrhosis — silent until decompensation; the endpoint every screen tries to prevent.
  • 🎯 Hepatocellular carcinoma — the reason cirrhotics enter 6-monthly surveillance (see the HCC case).
  • 🩸 Portal hypertension — variceal bleeding, ascites, spontaneous bacterial peritonitis.
  • 🧠 Hepatic encephalopathy — precipitated by infection, constipation, GI bleed, sedatives.
  • 🌀 Missed treatable aetiology — the "complication" of anchoring: an untreated autoimmune hepatitis or Wilson disease cirrhosing while labelled fatty liver.
  • ❤️ Cardiovascular events — the commonest cause of death in MASLD; the liver diagnosis must not eclipse the heart.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The worst complication of 'just a fatty liver' is not written in the liver — it is the treatable disease you never screened for, quietly becoming irreversible."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🫁 Diagnosis: MASH with advanced (F3) fibrosis — full screen negative for other aetiologies; FIB-4 2.6, FibroScan 12.8 kPa, low platelets, borderline splenomegaly.
  2. ⚖️ Disease-modifying core: structured ≥7–10% weight loss, dietitian, exercise, GLP-1 agonist if appropriate; tight diabetes, lipid and blood-pressure control; consider resmetirom for MASH+fibrosis.
  3. 🧮 Stage and surveil: confirm fibrosis stage; if cirrhotic, start 6-monthly ultrasound ± AFP and assess for varices (Baveno).
  4. 💉 Vaccinate against hepatitis A & B; minimise alcohol; review hepatotoxic medications.
  5. 🩺 Follow-up with serial non-invasive fibrosis testing to track response; re-screen if the picture changes.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Weight loss of 7–10% is not soft advice — it is the most effective disease-modifying therapy in MASH, capable of resolving steatohepatitis and regressing fibrosis. Prescribe it like a drug, with a target and follow-up."

🏠 Discharge / Follow-up

📋 Two very different destinations

🔴 This patient (MASH + advanced fibrosis)

  • Stays in hepatology follow-up; serial non-invasive fibrosis testing
  • Weight-loss programme with a real target; GLP-1/resmetirom as indicated; statin continued
  • If cirrhotic: 6-monthly HCC surveillance + varices assessment
  • Red flags to return: jaundice, abdominal swelling, black stools, confusion

🟢 The genuinely low-risk fatty liver

  • FIB-4 < 1.3, normal elastography, full screen negative
  • Managed in primary care: metabolic risk, weight, alcohol, cardiovascular prevention
  • Recheck FIB-4 in 2–3 years; safety-net advice
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A safe 'fatty liver' discharge is an active decision backed by a negative screen, a low FIB-4 and a reassuring elastography — never a silent 'probably NAFLD.'"

🟢 Wilson hidesCaeruloplasmin can be normal — in the young, add urine copper and a slit-lamp before excluding it.
⚖️ Weight loss is a drug≥7–10% body-weight loss resolves NASH and regresses fibrosis — prescribe it with a target.
❤️ The heart kills firstThe commonest cause of death in MASLD is cardiovascular — treat the metabolic syndrome, keep the statin.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the screen

  • 🏷️ The MASLD nomenclature change (2023): NAFLD was renamed MASLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease), requiring ≥1 cardiometabolic risk factor; "MetALD" names the overlap with significant alcohol, and NASH becomes MASH. The change is not cosmetic — it makes the metabolic driver part of the definition.
  • 💊 Emerging pharmacotherapy: resmetirom (THR-β agonist, first approved for MASH+fibrosis, MAESTRO-NASH); GLP-1 and dual/triple incretin agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide) for weight, glycaemia and NASH resolution; FGF21 analogues and pan-PPAR agonists in trials.
  • 🧬 Genetic modifiers: PNPLA3 (I148M) and TM6SF2 variants strongly influence steatosis, fibrosis and HCC risk in MASLD — the future of individual risk stratification.
  • 🔀 Autoimmune overlap syndromes: AIH-PBC and AIH-PSC overlaps need biopsy and combined immunosuppression + UDCA; IgG4-related sclerosing cholangitis is a steroid-responsive PSC mimic — do not miss it.
  • 🩸 Compensated advanced chronic liver disease (cACLD) & Baveno VII: liver stiffness <15 kPa with platelets >150 rules out clinically significant portal hypertension and lets patients safely avoid endoscopy; the "rule of five" for stiffness thresholds.
  • 🟢 Wilson disease scoring: the Leipzig criteria combine caeruloplasmin, urinary/hepatic copper, KF rings, haemolysis and ATP7B genetics — because no single test is sufficient; acute Wilsonian liver failure with Coombs-negative haemolysis and low ALP:bilirubin ratio is a transplant emergency.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Fatty Liver That Was Wilson Disease"

The case: A 28-year-old presented at 24 with mildly deranged LFTs on a routine check. An ultrasound showed "fatty liver," and — with a BMI of 27 — the label MASLD/NAFLD was applied and repeated at every annual visit for four years. Weight-loss advice was given; no aetiology screen was ever sent. At 28 they presented with jaundice, a tremor, subtle personality change and Coombs-negative haemolytic anaemia. A caeruloplasmin was finally checked and was low; 24-h urinary copper was grossly elevated and Kayser-Fleischer rings were present. The diagnosis was Wilson disease, now with established cirrhosis. Chelation was started, but the liver was already decompensating and the patient was listed for transplant.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Fatty liver" label stuck for four years; the commonest cause eclipsed the treatable one.
🧫 NO SCREENThe chronic liver disease screen — including caeruloplasmin — was never sent.
👶 AGE IGNOREDYoung age is exactly when Wilson and autoimmune hepatitis must be actively excluded.
🧮 FIBROSIS UNSTAGEDNo FIB-4, no elastography — silent progression to cirrhosis went undetected.
👁️ NO SLIT-LAMPKayser-Fleischer rings were never looked for until decompensation.

🛡️ What would have saved them: treat every set of unexplained deranged LFTs as earning a full aetiology screen, especially in the young; never let "fatty liver" close the book without excluding autoimmune hepatitis, haemochromatosis and Wilson disease; send caeruloplasmin, urine copper and a slit-lamp in anyone under 40; and stage the fibrosis with FIB-4 and elastography rather than reassuring on a normal-looking ultrasound. This is exactly the discipline that protected the patient in Bed 11.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Probably just fatty liver' has cirrhosed more treatable disease than any toxin. In a young patient with deranged LFTs, the screen you skip is the diagnosis you miss."
