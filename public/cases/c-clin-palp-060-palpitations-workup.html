The entire skill is a structured triage plus capturing the culprit rhythm, not a reassuring "you feel fine, so you are fine."

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The cardiology registrar thinks out loud at 10:34:

🧠 Frame it as benign-vs-dangerous, then capture the rhythm

"Two jobs. First, is this dangerous? He has three amber-to-red features — presyncope, one exertional onset, and a family history of unexplained sudden death — plus a resting ECG with pre-excitation. That is emphatically NOT a 'reassure and discharge' patient. Second, capture the culprit rhythm: episodes every 2–4 weeks mean a 24-hour Holter is almost useless — I need a monitor that matches his frequency."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 📈 Read the resting 12-lead first — confirm the short PR + delta wave (pre-excitation); measure the QT; look for Brugada morphology and structural clues (LVH, Q waves).
  2. 🚩 Risk-stratify — red flags (syncope, exertional, structural, family SCD, abnormal ECG) push toward urgent, specialist pathway.
  3. 🫀 Transthoracic echo — exclude structural heart disease / cardiomyopathy given the exertional symptom and family history.
  4. 📟 Match the monitor to frequency — every-few-weeks episodes → a 7–14-day patch or an external event/loop recorder, NOT a single 24-h Holter.
  5. ⚡ Refer to electrophysiology — documented pre-excitation warrants accessory-pathway risk stratification (see S5).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Match the monitor to the frequency: a 24-hour Holter for episodes that come every few weeks will simply miss them — you need a longer or implantable recorder."
🧪 Resident's self-testShe is tempted to just book "a Holter" because it's the default. Given episodes every 2–4 weeks, is a 24-hour Holter the right first monitor?

No. A 24–48-hour Holter only captures a rhythm that occurs most days. For episodes every few weeks the yield is close to zero — he will almost certainly have no attack during the recording. The correct tool is a 7–14-day patch or an external event/loop recorder (or, for rarer serious events, an implantable loop recorder). Choosing the wrong-duration monitor is the single commonest reason a dangerous rhythm is 'missed'.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the racing, WHY the danger (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🔁 Re-entry: most sudden regular SVT is a self-perpetuating circuit. In AVNRT the circuit is within the AV node (fast + slow pathways); in AVRT it uses an accessory pathway (as in WPW) that bypasses the AV node.
  2. ⚡ Pre-excitation: the accessory pathway lets the atrial impulse reach the ventricle early, pre-exciting it → short PR + delta wave (slurred QRS upstroke) on the resting ECG. This is the fingerprint sitting there between episodes.
  3. 🥁 The palpitation itself: during the tachycardia the ventricle fires ~150–250/min; the patient feels a sudden, regular, pounding rush — abrupt on and abrupt off, because the circuit either runs or it doesn't.
  4. 💀 The lethal scenario: if atrial fibrillation develops in a WPW patient, the accessory pathway can conduct the fast, irregular atrial impulses to the ventricle without the AV node's protective delay → pre-excited AF that can degenerate into ventricular fibrillation. Rare, but this is why pre-excitation earns respect.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A delta wave means WPW — risk-stratify and refer, because pre-excited AF can conduct at a lethal rate and degenerate to VF."

⚖️ Contrast pair — benign palpitations vs dangerous

🟢 Benign awareness of the heartbeat

  • Isolated "skipped beats" / thumps at rest, often when lying quietly
  • Normal resting ECG, no red flags, no syncope
  • Ectopy (PVCs/PACs), sinus tachycardia, anxiety
  • → Reassure, explain, minimal or no monitoring

🔴 Dangerous arrhythmia until proven otherwise

  • Syncope/presyncope, exertional onset, structural heart disease, family SCD
  • Abnormal resting ECG: pre-excitation, long QT, Brugada
  • Sudden regular fast palpitations (re-entrant SVT)
  • → Investigate urgently: echo + right monitor + EP referral

📋 Orders

🖊️ The clinic order set (10:55)

  • 📈 Resting 12-lead ECG — reviewed carefully: confirm short PR + delta wave; measure QTc; assess for Brugada morphology, LVH, Q waves.
  • 🧪 Bloods: FBC (anaemia), TFTs (thyrotoxicosis), U&E + Mg²⁺/Ca²⁺ (electrolyte drivers of arrhythmia), glucose. Reversible non-cardiac causes screened.
  • 🫀 Transthoracic echocardiogram — structural heart disease / cardiomyopathy, given exertional symptom + family SCD.
  • 📟 Ambulatory monitor matched to frequency: for every-2–4-week episodes → a 7–14-day patch or external event/loop recorder (NOT a 24-h Holter).
  • ⌚ Patient-triggered capture: teach him to record a smartwatch/handheld single-lead ECG during an episode and to activate the event recorder.
  • ⚡ EP referral for pre-excitation risk stratification (exercise test ± EP study) and ablation assessment.
  • 🩺 Safety advice pending work-up: vagal manoeuvre teaching; when to attend ED (sustained palpitations with chest pain, breathlessness, or syncope).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A resting 12-lead and the right-duration monitor cost almost nothing; the expensive mistake is a single Holter fired at infrequent symptoms and a delta wave left unread."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🩹 Clinic & monitoring-nurse priorities — the ambulatory safety net

  • 📟 Fit the ambulatory monitor correctly — good skin prep and electrode adhesion; explain wear time and that it must stay on to capture an episode.
  • 📓 Teach the symptom diary — patient logs the exact time, duration, and what he was doing when palpitations strike; the diary is matched to the recording to correlate rhythm with symptoms.
  • 🔘 Train the event-recorder trigger / smartwatch capture — show him how to press the button (or record a single-lead ECG) the moment an episode starts; a captured symptomatic rhythm is the diagnostic prize.
  • 💓 Teach pulse self-assessment — regular vs irregular, roughly how fast; and safe vagal manoeuvres (Valsalva, modified Valsalva) to try to terminate an SVT.
  • 🚨 Red-flag safety-netting — attend ED urgently if an episode comes with chest pain, severe breathlessness, or blackout, or if palpitations do not stop.
  • 🧾 Reinforce that "feeling fine in clinic" does not mean the work-up is over — the monitor and the diary do the real work at home.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The patient who reliably presses the event button and keeps an accurate diary is the most sensitive rhythm monitor you have — a captured tracing during symptoms beats a hundred normal recordings on quiet days."

📟 The monitoring toolbox — pick by symptom frequency

  • 24–48h Holter: daily symptoms — captures rhythm only if it happens most days.
  • Patch monitor (7–14 day): every few days to two weeks — longer wear, higher yield, patient-friendly.
  • External event / loop recorder: weekly-ish — patient triggers a capture during symptoms.
  • Smartwatch / handheld single-lead ECG: any frequency — patient records their own symptomatic tracing (confirm with 12-lead).
  • Implantable loop recorder (ILR): rare but serious events, especially with unexplained syncope — records for up to ~3 years.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to record and why — frequency drives the choice

  • 📆 Establish the symptom frequency first — it is the single variable that selects the monitor. Daily → Holter; weekly → patch/event; monthly-or-rarer → implantable loop recorder.
  • 📓 Correlate rhythm with a symptom diary — a captured tachycardia that coincides with logged symptoms is diagnostic; an incidental rhythm on an asymptomatic day is weaker evidence.
  • ⌚ Empower patient-triggered capture — event-recorder button, smartwatch single-lead ECG; the patient is often the only one present when the rhythm occurs.
  • 😵 Syncope changes the calculus — unexplained syncope with suspected arrhythmia favours an implantable loop recorder for its long recording window.
  • ⚡ Pre-excitation adds an exercise/ambulatory question — abrupt loss of the delta wave with rate suggests a lower-risk pathway; persistent pre-excitation prompts EP study.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Ask one number before you order any monitor: how often does it happen? The answer picks the device. Everything else is detail."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — the palpitations that turn dangerous

  • ⚡ Pre-excited AF → VF / sudden cardiac death — the feared WPW complication; the accessory pathway conducts AF at a lethal ventricular rate.
  • 😵 Syncope & injury — arrhythmic syncope during exertion (driving, sport, heights) can be catastrophic.
  • 🫀 PVC-induced cardiomyopathy — a very high PVC burden (often >10–20%) can cause a reversible fall in EF; "benign" ectopy that isn't monitored can quietly harm.
  • 🧬 Missed channelopathy / cardiomyopathy — long QT, Brugada, HCM, ARVC presenting as "palpitations" and dismissed as anxiety.
  • 🔁 Incessant / frequent SVT — tachycardia-mediated cardiomyopathy if left uncontrolled.
  • 🩹 The system complication: the wrong-tool work-up — a single normal Holter labelled "reassuring," closing the case on a dangerous rhythm never captured.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The deadliest complication of palpitations is not in the heart — it's the falsely reassuring normal monitor that ended the work-up before the rhythm was ever caught."

💎 Clinical Pearls

📈 Always a resting 12-leadThe diagnosis (delta wave, long QT, Brugada) often sits on the ECG between episodes — read it, don't glance.
📆 Match monitor to frequencyDaily → Holter; weekly → patch/event; rare/serious → implantable loop recorder.
🥁 Regular + sudden = SVTAbrupt-on/off, too-fast-to-tap, regular palpitations suggest re-entrant SVT — refer for ablation.
⚡ Delta wave = WPWRisk-stratify (SPERRI/exercise/EP) and refer — pre-excited AF can degenerate to VF.
😵 Red flags upgrade urgencySyncope, exertional onset, structural disease, family SCD, abnormal ECG → echo + specialist.
🩹 Normal ≠ reassuringA monitor with no symptoms recorded is uninformative — capture the rhythm during symptoms.
🚫 Beware AV-nodal blockersIn pre-excited AF, digoxin/verapamil/diltiazem/adenosine can be lethal — cardiovert, then ablate.
🫀 Watch the PVC burdenBenign ectopy usually, but a very high PVC burden can cause reversible cardiomyopathy.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🧩 The full differential of palpitations: ectopy (PVCs/PACs), SVT (AVNRT, AVRT, atrial tachycardia), atrial fibrillation/flutter, VT, physiological sinus tachycardia, and non-cardiac drivers (anxiety/panic, thyrotoxicosis, anaemia, phaeochromocytoma, caffeine/stimulants/salbutamol). The history's regularity, onset/offset, and associated features sort them faster than any test.
  • ⚡ Pre-excitation & accessory-pathway risk: manifest vs concealed pathways; SPERRI ≤ 250 ms and short antegrade ERP mark high risk; abrupt loss of pre-excitation on exertion suggests a lower-risk pathway; asymptomatic WPW in high-risk occupations/athletes may still warrant EP study (2019 ESC SVT / 2020 ESC sports).
  • 📟 Monitoring technologies: adhesive patch monitors (extended continuous recording), external and implantable loop recorders, and consumer single-lead ECG / photoplethysmography wearables — high patient-captured yield but requiring confirmatory 12-lead and mindful of false positives.
  • 🔥 Catheter ablation of SVT: slow-pathway modification for AVNRT and accessory-pathway ablation for AVRT/WPW have high success and low complication rates — first-line curative therapy for symptomatic re-entrant SVT rather than lifelong drugs.
  • 🫀 PVC burden — benign vs harmful: most ventricular ectopy is benign, but a high burden (commonly >10–20% of beats) can cause PVC-induced cardiomyopathy, which is reversible with suppression or ablation; monitor burden and LV function.
  • 🧬 When palpitations signal a channelopathy or cardiomyopathy: long QT, Brugada, catecholaminergic polymorphic VT, HCM and ARVC can all present as "palpitations." Exertional symptoms, syncope, and family history of premature SCD should trigger extended ECG interpretation, echo/CMR, exercise testing, and genetic/family evaluation.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Holter That Missed"

The case: A 24-year-old man was referred with occasional racing-heart episodes "every few weeks." In a busy clinic he was booked for the default single 24-hour Holter. He happened to have no episode that day, so the Holter was reported normal and he was reassured and discharged as "probably anxiety." His resting 12-lead had been filed but never carefully read — it showed a short PR and a delta wave (WPW). Months later he developed an episode of pre-excited atrial fibrillation; the accessory pathway conducted the irregular atrial impulses to the ventricle at a lethal rate, degenerated to ventricular fibrillation, and he had a cardiac arrest. The diagnosis had been on the first ECG all along, and the right monitor for his infrequent episodes would have caught the SVT that pointed to WPW.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

📈 ECG NOT READThe delta wave on the resting 12-lead was filed but never interpreted.
📟 WRONG MONITORA 24-h Holter for every-few-weeks symptoms — near-zero yield for his frequency.
🧠 ANCHORING"Young + well = anxiety" label stuck; red flags not weighted.
🩹 FALSE REASSURANCEA normal (asymptomatic-day) Holter was read as "all clear," closing the case.
⚡ NO EP REFERRALPre-excitation never risk-stratified, so pre-excited-AF danger was never addressed.

🛡️ What would have saved him: read every resting 12-lead in a palpitations patient and act on a delta wave; match the monitor to the symptom frequency (a patch or event recorder, not a single Holter); never let a normal asymptomatic-day recording end the work-up; weight red flags (exertional onset, presyncope, family history); and refer documented pre-excitation to electrophysiology for risk stratification and ablation. This is exactly the discipline that cured the patient in Bed 6.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A normal 24-hour Holter on a symptom-free day has falsely reassured more patients than it has ever diagnosed. The diagnosis was on the first ECG — and the monitor was simply the wrong tool for the frequency."
