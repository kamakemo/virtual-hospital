🚦 Why stent thrombosis is a category-1 emergency

Stent thrombosis (ST) is an abrupt thrombotic occlusion inside a previously stented segment. Unlike native plaque rupture, the whole stented segment thromboses at once, there are no collaterals in the acute setting, and the territory is often the SAME (or larger) as the index infarct. The clinical consequence is severe: ST presents as STEMI or sudden cardiac death in the majority, with in-hospital mortality of roughly 10–20% and very high reinfarction. This is not "restenosis" — restenosis is a gradual re-narrowing (angina over months); stent thrombosis is a sudden clot (a second heart attack in minutes).

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Recurrent ST elevation in the territory you just stented is stent thrombosis until proven otherwise — activate the lab, and plan to IMAGE the stent, not just re-balloon it blindly."

📋 Pre-Procedure Workup & Planning

🗂️ ARC definitions — say them out loud before you scrub inThe Academic Research Consortium (ARC) framework classifies stent thrombosis by certainty and by timing. Knowing the timing category instantly reframes the likely mechanism.

🎯 ARC — certainty categories

CategoryDefinition
✅ DefiniteAngiographic or pathologic confirmation of thrombus in/adjacent to the stent + an acute ischaemic clinical event
🟠 ProbableAny unexplained death within 30 days, OR any target-vessel MI without angiographic confirmation
🟡 PossibleAny unexplained death beyond 30 days after stent implantation

🕐 ARC — timing categories (this drives the mechanism)

TimingWindowDominant mechanism
⚡ Acute< 24 hMechanical: under-expansion, malapposition, edge dissection, uncovered struts
🔥 Subacute24 h – 30 daysMechanical + biological (DAPT gap, high platelet reactivity)
🕰️ Late30 days – 1 yearDAPT interruption, delayed healing, uncovered struts
🌙 Very late> 1 yearNeoatherosclerosis, malapposition, hypersensitivity (esp. 1st-gen DES)
🧭 Where does OUR patient sit?Day 5 = subacute stent thrombosis (24 h – 30 d). But the pain has been building over the last 24–48 h — an acute-on-subacute picture. He has BOTH a biological trigger (stopped ticagrelor) AND a possible mechanical substrate (was the index stent fully expanded?). Do not stop at the obvious biological cause — the mechanical cause hides on imaging.

🧾 The 60-second pre-PCI checklist (do NOT delay reperfusion for it)

  • 💊 Reload potent antiplatelets NOW — aspirin + potent P2Y₁₂ (ticagrelor 180 mg or prasugrel 60 mg). Consider IV cangrelor if he cannot swallow / gut is not absorbing.
  • 🧪 Pull the index procedure report — stent size/length, deployment pressures, any post-dilation, any residual dissection or under-expansion noted.
  • 🩸 Antithrombotic plan — heparin/bivalirudin + a plan for a GP IIb/IIIa inhibitor if heavy thrombus burden.
  • 🫘 Bleeding risk quick-check — you are about to intensify antithrombotics; note recent surgery, anaemia, prior bleeds.
  • 🖐️ Access assessment — Allen/Barbeau of the radial; femoral back-up for large-bore support if he decompensates.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Timing tells you the mechanism: acute/subacute ST screams mechanical (image it), late/very-late whispers biology and healing (DAPT and neoatherosclerosis) — but a DAPT gap can trigger any of them."

🩸 Vascular Access & Setup

🖐️ Radial vs femoral — bleeding matters even more here

You are about to run this patient on intensified antithrombotics — reloaded potent P2Y₁₂, therapeutic anticoagulation, and quite possibly a GP IIb/IIIa inhibitor. Every one of those raises bleeding risk, and a bleed after PCI roughly doubles mortality. This is exactly the patient in whom radial-first pays off — but keep a femoral plan because he is hypotensive and may need large-bore mechanical support.

🖐️ Radial (default)

  • Fewer access-site bleeds — critical when you will layer on a GP IIb/IIIa inhibitor
  • Lower mortality in ACS (MATRIX, RIVAL ACS subgroup)
  • Confirm patency first (Barbeau/Allen)

🦵 Femoral (bail-out)

  • When radial fails, or he crashes and needs Impella/IABP/ECMO for cardiogenic shock
  • Higher bleeding — micropuncture + ultrasound guidance, avoid a "high" or "low" stick
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"You are about to make his blood as thin as it will ever be — reach for the wrist. But if he is crashing into shock, have the groin prepped for support in parallel."

💉 Sheath, anticoagulation & the antithrombotic escalation

  • 🔧 Sheath: 6 Fr radial (hydrophilic); intra-arterial "radial cocktail" (verapamil ± nitrate ± heparin) to prevent spasm.
  • 🩸 Procedural anticoagulation: unfractionated heparin (70–100 U/kg, titrate to an ACT ~250–300 s), or bivalirudin. The ACT (activated clotting time) is your bedside gauge that the blood is anticoagulated enough to work in a freshly thrombosing vessel.
  • 💥 Potent platelet blockade: stent thrombosis is a platelet-rich clot on a foreign surface — this is where a GP IIb/IIIa inhibitor (abciximab/eptifibatide/tirofiban) or IV cangrelor earns its place, especially with heavy thrombus and a patient who was under-treated.
  • 🧭 Guide catheter: for the LAD (left system) → an EBU/XB left guide for the back-up support you will need to cross fresh thrombus and post-dilate a stiff, under-expanded stent.

🔬 Lesion Assessment — Physiology & Imaging

🔬 In stent thrombosis, IMAGING is mandatory — physiology waitsDo NOT FFR a thrombosed stent — it is occluded, you already know it needs treating. The critical question is WHY did the stent clot? The answer is under the thrombus, and only IVUS or OCT can reveal it. Re-ballooning blindly and walking away is how you send the same patient back a third time. In acute/subacute ST, a mechanical cause is present in the majority of cases when you look.

🧨 The vicious cycle — the mechanisms of stent thrombosis

🔧 Mechanical (find on imaging)

  • Under-expansion — the single most common finding; a stent squeezed by unprepped calcium never opened
  • Malapposition — struts floating off the wall create a nidus for clot
  • Edge dissection — a flap at the stent margin catches flow and thromboses
  • Uncovered / protruding struts — bare metal in the bloodstream
  • Geographic miss / residual disease at the edges

🧬 Biological & procedural

  • DAPT interruption — the dominant biological trigger (our patient stopped ticagrelor)
  • Clopidogrel resistance / CYP2C19 loss-of-function — high on-treatment platelet reactivity
  • High platelet reactivity from diabetes, ACS milieu, inflammation
  • Procedural: long stents, bifurcations, small vessels, thrombus-laden STEMI substrate
🔁 Why it is a VICIOUS CYCLEA poorly expanded or malapposed stent (mechanical) disturbs flow → activates platelets → forms thrombus. A DAPT gap (biological) removes the only brake on those platelets. In our patient, BOTH are firing at once — the biology exposed a mechanical flaw that a well-treated patient might have tolerated. Fix only one and the artery will thrombose again.

📏 A word on physiology (for non-culprit / stable disease later)

  • FFR ≤ 0.80 or iFR ≤ 0.89 flag an ischaemia-producing stenosis — but that is for the bystander lesions once the acute crisis is over, never for the thrombosed culprit.

🎮 Simulation — The OCT Stent-Expansion Dial

You run OCT through the freshly reopened stent. Drag the minimum stent expansion (minimal stent area vs the reference lumen) and decide: is under-expansion the cause you must fix?

Set the dial to explore expansion targets.

🎯 Aim for > 90% expansion (and an adequate absolute MSA). < 80% is significant under-expansion — the leading mechanical cause of stent thrombosis. Fix it with high-pressure non-compliant post-dilation.

🖥️ Intravascular imaging — IVUS vs OCT for stent thrombosis

🔵 IVUS

  • Ultrasound — deep penetration, quantifies vessel size and plaque behind the stent; great for sizing the post-dilation balloon and seeing under-expansion against a calcified wall
  • No contrast flush needed (useful in the crashing patient)

🟠 OCT

  • Light — ultra-high resolution; the best tool to see malapposition, edge dissection, uncovered/protruding struts, and residual thrombus
  • Directly shows the mechanical cause; needs a contrast flush
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Do not FFR the clot — image it. IVUS or OCT turns 'the stent thrombosed' into 'the stent was under-expanded at the proximal edge' — and only the second sentence tells you what to do."

🛠️ Intervention — Step-by-Step Execution

🎈 Balloon angioplasty & stent optimisation. Source: Wikimedia Commons (Blausen)

🪜 The sequence — treat the clot, then treat the CAUSE

  1. 🧵 Wire the stented segment — a workhorse guidewire across the thrombosed stent into the distal true lumen (confirm distal position in two views).
  2. 💥 Aggressive antithrombotics — give a GP IIb/IIIa inhibitor or cangrelor for this platelet-rich clot; ensure the P2Y₁₂ reload is on board.
  3. 🫧 Thrombectomy / restore flow — aspiration thrombectomy for heavy thrombus burden, then a small balloon to restore a whiff of flow and relieve ischaemia.
  4. 🔬 IMAGE the stent (mandatory) — OCT/IVUS to find the mechanical cause: under-expansion, malapposition, edge dissection, uncovered struts, or geographic miss. Do NOT skip this.
  5. 🎈 Correct the cause — high-pressure non-compliant balloon to fix under-expansion; an additional stent to cover an edge dissection or a gap; re-appose malapposed struts.
  6. ✅ Re-image to confirm — repeat OCT/IVUS to prove full expansion and apposition before you leave.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The clot is the symptom; under-expansion is the disease. Aspirate the thrombus, then post-dilate the metal to the vessel — a beautiful post-thrombectomy angiogram over an under-expanded stent is the next thrombosis waiting to happen."

S10

🏠 Post-Procedure Care, Debrief & Assessment

🩹 Access-site & early monitoring

  • 🖐️ Radial: patent-haemostasis band; watch for bleeding given intensified antithrombotics.
  • 🦵 Femoral: closure device/manual pressure; watch for haematoma, pseudoaneurysm, retroperitoneal bleed.
  • 📟 Telemetry & serial ECG — recurrent stent thrombosis can re-occur in the first hours; a GP IIb/IIIa infusion may bridge the reload.

💊 The antiplatelet reset — this is the whole point

  • Potent, uninterrupted DAPT — aspirin + ticagrelor or prasugrel (preferred over clopidogrel), for at least 12 months; individualise duration but bias toward NOT interrupting after a stent thrombosis.
  • Address the biology of why he clotted — if he was on clopidogrel, switch to a potent agent (his event plus diabetes flags high platelet reactivity); consider platelet-function/CYP2C19 testing to guide de-escalation later, not now.
  • Fix adherence, not just the artery — 30-day pill supply confirmed, no-cost/continuous-supply plan, teach-back on "never stop this without a cardiologist," family engaged, pharmacy flagged.
  • Secondary prevention — high-intensity statin (± ezetimibe/PCSK9i), beta-blocker, ACE inhibitor (anterior MI/LV dysfunction), MRA if EF ≤ 40% with HF/diabetes; cardiac rehab; smoking cessation; glucose control.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The stent was reopened in minutes; the reason he stopped his tablets takes a conversation. Never discharge a stent-thrombosis patient without a potent P2Y₁₂ AND a concrete plan for how he will actually keep taking it."

🔭 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

  • 🧬 High platelet reactivity & genetics: CYP2C19 loss-of-function carriers under-metabolise clopidogrel → high on-treatment platelet reactivity → stent thrombosis; potent agents (ticagrelor/prasugrel) bypass this. VerifyNow/PRU and genotyping guide de-escalation (TROPICAL-ACS, POPular Genetics).
  • 🔬 OCT phenotyping of ST: distinguishes under-expansion, malapposition, uncovered struts, edge dissection, and — in very-late ST — neoatherosclerosis (in-stent plaque rupture) and stent-strut hypersensitivity. The phenotype dictates the fix.
  • 🕰️ Very-late ST & 1st-gen DES: durable-polymer sirolimus/paclitaxel stents caused delayed healing and hypersensitivity; newer thin-strut, biocompatible/bioresorbable-polymer DES and abandonment of bioresorbable scaffolds (high late ST, e.g. ABSORB) reduced very-late events.
  • 💊 DAPT duration debates: shortened DAPT (1–3 mo then P2Y₁₂ monotherapy — TWILIGHT, STOPDAPT-2, MASTER-DAPT) lowers bleeding in high-bleeding-risk patients; extended DAPT (DAPT trial, PEGASUS) lowers ischaemia — a stent-thrombosis survivor sits on the ischaemic side of that trade-off.
  • 🛠️ Refractory under-expansion: when a non-compliant balloon will not open the stent, escalate — intravascular lithotripsy (Shockwave) through the struts, high-pressure/OPN balloons, or rotational atherectomy of under-treated calcium (rare, high-risk through an existing stent).
  • 🔁 Recurrent ST: hunt relentlessly for the mechanical cause on imaging; confirm true P2Y₁₂ effect (compliance, absorption, cangrelor bridge); rule out a hypercoagulable state and mechanical malapposition before blaming "resistance."

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Hernia Repair That Killed Him"

The case: A 64-year-old, six weeks after a drug-eluting stent to the LAD, was booked for an elective inguinal hernia repair. The surgeon, worried about bleeding, told him to "stop both blood thinners a week before" — and no one called cardiology. Aspirin AND the P2Y₁₂ inhibitor were held. On the morning of surgery, still in the pre-op bay, he developed crushing chest pain and arrested. The angiogram showed acute stent thrombosis of the LAD. Despite emergent PCI he never regained a perfusing rhythm. He died from an operation he never had, for a hernia that was never urgent.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🗓️ ELECTIVE, TOO EARLYNon-urgent surgery scheduled inside the high-risk DAPT window — it should have waited.
💊 BOTH AGENTS STOPPEDAspirin should almost always be continued; stopping both is the highest-risk move.
📞 NO CARDIOLOGY CALLNo multidisciplinary discussion of the ischaemic vs bleeding trade-off.
🌉 NO BRIDGING PLANNo consideration of a short-acting bridge (e.g. cangrelor) if interruption was truly needed.
🧠 "BLOOD THINNER" THINKINGDAPT treated like warfarin — an interchangeable "thinner" to pause, not a stent lifeline.

🛡️ What would have saved him: defer elective non-cardiac surgery beyond the high-risk DAPT window (ideally ≥ 6 months for elective, minimum 1 month if truly needed); continue aspirin through most procedures; if the P2Y₁₂ must be held, hold it for the shortest possible time and consider a peri-operative bridge; and NEVER interrupt DAPT after stenting without an explicit cardiology–surgery conversation. This is the exact error our index patient made on his own — he just did it by "running out" instead of by a surgeon's order.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Premature DAPT discontinuation is the deadliest prescribable error after a stent. Continue aspirin, keep the P2Y₁₂ unless a cardiologist agrees to stop it, and let no elective knife rush the timeline."

💎 Clinical Pearls

🔁 ST ≠ restenosisStent thrombosis is a sudden clot (STEMI/death); restenosis is gradual re-narrowing (angina over months).
🕐 Timing tells mechanismAcute <24h, subacute 24h–30d, late 30d–1y, very late >1y — early = mechanical, late = biology/healing.
🗂️ ARC certaintyDefinite (imaging/path + event), probable (TV-MI or death <30d), possible (unexplained death >30d).
🔬 Image, do not guessOCT/IVUS is mandatory — find under-expansion, malapposition, edge dissection, uncovered struts.
