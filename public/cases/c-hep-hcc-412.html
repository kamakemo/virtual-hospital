🧭 The three questions that will decide his fate

Before treatment, three axes must be answered — they are the whole of HCC management: (1) What is the tumour burden? (size, number, vascular invasion, spread) · (2) How good is the liver? (Child-Pugh / portal hypertension) · (3) How well is the patient? (performance status). These three feed directly into the Barcelona (BCLC) system in S5.

⚠️ The trap of "it is probably benign"In a NON-cirrhotic liver, most solid lesions are benign (haemangioma, FNH). In a CIRRHOTIC liver, that prior probability flips: a new nodule > 1 cm is HCC until multiphase imaging says otherwise. Applying "general population" reassurance to a cirrhotic is how curable cancers get watched until they are incurable.

"Cirrhotic, new 3.5 cm nodule, AFP climbing — this is HCC until proven otherwise. My job is NOT to stick a needle in it. 🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🩻 Multiphase (four-phase) CT or contrast MRI of the liver — the diagnostic test. Look for arterial hyperenhancement + venous/delayed washout (S10).
  2. 🎯 Assign a LI-RADS category; LR-5 in a cirrhotic is diagnostic of HCC with NO biopsy required.
  3. 🧮 Stage: CT chest for lung mets, review for vascular invasion / extrahepatic spread; recompute Child-Pugh and performance status.
  4. 🧪 Trend the AFP (baseline for response), check liver function, platelets, varices status.
  5. 🧑‍⚕️ Refer to the HCC MDT / tumour board — resection, transplant, ablation, TACE and systemic therapy are decided together.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Image before you needle. In a cirrhotic, diagnostic imaging can confirm HCC without a biopsy — and an unnecessary biopsy of a resectable tumour risks needle-track seeding and bleeding in a coagulopathic liver."
🧪 Resident's self-testShe wonders whether to biopsy the nodule "to be certain" before referral. Should she?

Not routinely. If multiphase imaging in a cirrhotic shows the diagnostic pattern (LR-5: arterial hyperenhancement with washout ± capsule/threshold growth), HCC is diagnosed WITHOUT biopsy. Biopsy is reserved for indeterminate lesions or a non-cirrhotic liver. Biopsy carries bleeding risk (coagulopathy/varices, thrombocytopenia) and a small but real risk of needle-track tumour seeding (~1–3%), which can turn a curable tumour into an incurable one.

🧮 The Barcelona (BCLC) system — burden + liver + patient → therapy

BCLC is the framework that links tumour burden, liver function and performance status to a specific treatment. Learn it as a staircase from curable to terminal.

Our patient: single 3.5 cm, Child-Pugh A, ECOG 0, no vascular invasion or spread → BCLC A (early) → curative intent. He is a candidate for resection, ablation, or transplant.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"You cannot stage HCC by the tumour alone. A 2 cm cancer in a Child-Pugh C liver and a 2 cm cancer in a Child-Pugh A liver are DIFFERENT diseases — the liver reserve decides what you are allowed to do."
🫁 Milan criteria — the transplant gateA cirrhotic with HCC can be cured by liver transplant (it removes the cancer AND the diseased liver) if within the Milan criteria: a single tumour ≤5 cm, OR up to 3 nodules each ≤3 cm, with no macrovascular invasion and no extrahepatic spread. Within Milan, post-transplant 5-year survival approaches 70–75%. Our patient (single 3.5 cm) is comfortably within Milan.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY cirrhosis breeds cancer (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🔥 Chronic injury: a persistent insult — HBV, HCV, alcohol, MASLD (fatty liver), haemochromatosis, aflatoxin — keeps damaging hepatocytes for decades.
  2. ♻️ Regeneration under fire: the liver responds with relentless cycles of cell death and regrowth. Every round of division is a fresh chance to copy DNA wrong.
  3. 🧬 Accumulating mutations: in the inflamed, fibrotic microenvironment, cells acquire genetic hits (TERT promoter, TP53, CTNNB1/β-catenin). Regenerative nodules become dysplastic nodules.
  4. 🩸 Neo-arterialisation → malignancy: as a dysplastic nodule progresses to HCC, it loses its normal portal-vein blood supply and grows a new arterial supply. This vascular switch is exactly what multiphase imaging detects (arterial hyperenhancement + washout). Chronic injury → regeneration → dysplasia → cancer.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"HCC is the price the liver pays for surviving chronic injury. Regeneration IS the risk — every scar-and-heal cycle is a roll of the mutational dice, which is why cirrhosis (any cause) is the soil of the cancer."

⚖️ Contrast pair — HCC WITH cirrhosis vs HBV WITHOUT cirrhosis

🫗 The usual story (≈80–90%)

  • HCC arises in an established CIRRHOTIC liver
  • Any cause: HBV, HCV, alcohol, MASLD, haemochromatosis
  • Surveillance targets this population
  • Our patient sits here

🦠 The exception — HBV

  • Hepatitis B is directly oncogenic (viral DNA integrates into the host genome)
  • Can cause HCC even in a NON-cirrhotic liver
  • So HBV carriers are offered surveillance even without cirrhosis (esp. with family history, older age, high viral load)

💡 The mechanism explains the exception: for most causes you need cirrhosis first, but HBV can drive cancer directly through genomic integration — which is why the surveillance rules single it out.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (10:05)

  • 🩻 Multiphase (four-phase) CT liver OR contrast-enhanced MRI — non-contrast, arterial, portal-venous and delayed phases. The diagnostic test for the nodule.
  • 🫁 CT chest (± bone imaging if symptomatic) for staging — lung is the commonest metastatic site.
  • 🧪 AFP (trend and prognostic baseline), LFTs, bilirubin, albumin, INR (Child-Pugh/MELD), CBC, U&E, viral serology, ferritin/transferrin saturation.
  • 🫗 Recompute Child-Pugh and MELD; document ECOG performance status.
  • 🩸 Confirm varices surveillance status and continue non-selective beta-blocker; check platelets/coagulation before any procedure.
  • 🚫 Do NOT biopsy first — defer any biopsy until imaging is reviewed; a diagnostic LR-5 needs no needle.
  • 🧑‍⚕️ Refer to the HCC multidisciplinary tumour board; involve transplant hepatology early given curative potential.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The first order in a cirrhotic with a new nodule is a multiphase scan and an MDT referral — not a biopsy request. Get the picture before you get the needle."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Clinic & day-unit priorities — the nurse holds the pathway together

  • 📅 Protect the surveillance safety net: confirm the 6-monthly ultrasound recall is active — the single most survival-changing task in a cirrhosis clinic.
  • 🩸 Watch for decompensation: new confusion (encephalopathy), abdominal swelling (ascites), black stools/haematemesis (variceal bleed), or deepening jaundice — escalate immediately.
  • 💉 Contrast safety: check renal function and allergies before the multiphase scan; ensure IV access for the timed contrast bolus (phase timing IS the diagnosis).
  • 🧾 Coagulation check before any procedure: in a cirrhotic (low platelets, raised INR, varices) never let a biopsy or ablation proceed without documented clotting and consent.
  • 🧠 Communication: he is frightened and thinking "cancer = death." Reinforce the message that early HCC is often CURABLE, and keep the MDT plan clear.
  • 🍽️ Nutrition & frailty: flag weight loss and sarcopenia — nutritional status affects candidacy for resection/transplant.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who guards the surveillance recall list saves more HCC lives than any oncologist — cancers caught at 2 cm are curable; cancers caught at 12 cm are not."

🩻 Multiphase Imaging / Biopsy + Visual Enhancements

🫗 The radiological hallmark — arterial hyperenhancement, then WASHOUT

The HCC lesion lights up brighter than background liver in the arterial phase (new tumour arteries), then washes out to become darker than liver in the portal-venous/delayed phase. That arterial-in / venous-out signature in a cirrhotic IS the diagnosis.

🔎 LI-RADS — turning the picture into a decision (cirrhotic livers)

CategoryWhat it meansAction
🟢 LR-1 / LR-2Definitely / probably benign (cyst, haemangioma)Routine surveillance.
🟡 LR-3Intermediate probability of malignancyShort-interval follow-up imaging.
🟠 LR-4Probably HCCMDT review; often repeat imaging or biopsy.
🔴 LR-5Definitely HCC: arterial hyperenhancement PLUS washout, enhancing capsule and/or threshold growth in a size-defined lesionDiagnostic — treat as HCC with NO biopsy needed.
⚫ LR-M / LR-TIVMalignant but not HCC-specific / tumour in veinBiopsy (LR-M) or upstages to advanced disease (tumour-in-vein).

Our patient scored LR-5: a 3.5 cm arterially-enhancing lesion with definite washout and a capsule. HCC is diagnosed — no biopsy required.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Arterial hyperenhancement + washout in a cirrhotic = HCC. That imaging signature (LR-5) lets you diagnose and treat WITHOUT a biopsy — and avoids the bleeding and needle-track seeding a biopsy would cost."

🪡 When (and when NOT) to biopsy

  • Biopsy INDICATED: indeterminate lesion (LR-3/4 that will not declare), lesion in a NON-cirrhotic liver, or suspicion of a non-HCC malignancy (LR-M, e.g. cholangiocarcinoma) needing tissue.
  • Biopsy AVOIDED: a diagnostic LR-5 in a cirrhotic — needling it adds bleeding risk (coagulopathy, varices, thrombocytopenia) and ~1–3% needle-track seeding, with no benefit when imaging is already diagnostic.

🚫 The 'wrong-stage therapy' trap❌ Starting sorafenib/atezolizumab in a patient with a curable single 3.5 cm tumour · ❌ TACE-ing a decompensated Child-Pugh C liver · ❌ Resecting in significant portal hypertension. Giving palliative systemic therapy to a curable patient — or curative-intent surgery to a liver that cannot survive it — is how staging errors kill.
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📅 Continued surveillance: the whole population of cirrhotics gets 6-monthly ultrasound ± AFP; on treatment, follow with multiphase CT/MRI (mRECIST) to assess response.
  • 🫗 Liver function trajectory: serial Child-Pugh/MELD — decompensation can move a patient out of curative candidacy or into transplant priority.
  • 📈 AFP trend: falling AFP after therapy suggests response; a rising AFP signals recurrence or progression.
  • 🩸 Portal hypertension: variceal surveillance and beta-blockade; watch platelets and bleeding, especially before bevacizumab or procedures.
  • 💊 Systemic-therapy toxicity (if applicable): immune-related adverse events (colitis, hepatitis, thyroiditis) with immunotherapy; hypertension/proteinuria with bevacizumab; hand-foot reaction with TKIs.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After the diagnosis, monitoring answers a moving question: is the liver still good enough for the plan, and is the tumour responding? Both the AFP and the Child-Pugh score can quietly change the treatment."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — tumour, liver, and treatment

  • 🩸 Tumour rupture & haemoperitoneum — a subcapsular HCC can bleed catastrophically; sudden abdominal pain + shock in a known HCC.
  • 🧵 Portal-vein tumour thrombosis — vascular invasion upstages to BCLC C and can precipitate decompensation.
  • 🫗 Decompensation — new ascites, encephalopathy, jaundice, or variceal bleed; can be triggered by tumour progression or by treatment (post-TACE liver injury).
  • 🔥 Post-embolisation syndrome (after TACE) — fever, pain, nausea; and the risk of liver failure if reserve is marginal.
  • 🪡 Procedure complications — biopsy/ablation bleeding in a coagulopathic liver, and needle-track tumour seeding when a resectable HCC is biopsied unnecessarily.
  • 🌍 Metastatic spread — lung, bone, lymph nodes; paraneoplastic hypoglycaemia/hypercalcaemia in advanced disease.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The deadliest 'complication' of HCC is a staging or diagnostic error made at the very start — an unnecessary biopsy that seeds a curable tumour, or a missed surveillance scan that lets it grow beyond cure."

🏠 Discharge / Follow-up

📋 Two very different journeys — early vs advanced HCC

🫗 This patient (early, curative)

  • Post-transplant (or post-resection/ablation): immunosuppression, surveillance imaging + AFP for recurrence
  • Manage the underlying liver disease drivers (alcohol abstinence, treat/monitor viral hepatitis)
  • Red flags: new pain, jaundice, weight loss, GI bleeding → urgent review
  • Lifelong hepatology + transplant follow-up

🌍 Advanced HCC (BCLC C, palliative)

  • Systemic therapy (atezolizumab-bevacizumab first-line) with toxicity monitoring
  • Early palliative-care involvement; symptom and liver-function support
  • Manage portal hypertension, pain, and nutrition
  • Honest prognostic conversations and advance-care planning
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The gap between these two columns was decided at the first nodule. Surveillance, correct imaging, and a fast MDT referral are what keep patients in the LEFT column."

🎯 Stage by BCLCTumour burden + liver function + performance status → therapy: curative, TACE, systemic, or supportive.
🫁 Milan criteriaTransplant cures if single ≤5 cm OR ≤3 nodules each ≤3 cm, no vascular invasion or spread.
📈 AFP is a trendUp to a third of HCCs are AFP-negative; use AFP for trend, prognosis and response — never as a stand-alone test.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🫁 Expanding the transplant gate: beyond Milan, the UCSF criteria and "up-to-seven", plus down-staging protocols (TACE/ablation to shrink tumours back within Milan) extend curative transplant to selected patients. AFP thresholds (e.g. French AFP model) refine listing and predict recurrence.
  • 🌊 MASLD/MASH-driven HCC: the fastest-rising cause in the West, and uniquely it can arise in NON-cirrhotic fatty liver — challenging the "surveillance only if cirrhotic" rule and driving research into risk-based (rather than cirrhosis-based) surveillance.
  • 🧬 Molecular subclasses: proliferative (TP53, FGF19/CCND1) vs non-proliferative (CTNNB1/β-catenin, TERT) HCC differ in behaviour and immune milieu, informing emerging biomarker-driven therapy.
  • 💉 Immunotherapy era: IMbrave150 (atezolizumab-bevacizumab) and HIMALAYA (durvalumab-tremelimumab, the STRIDE regimen) reset first-line advanced HCC; adjuvant atezolizumab-bevacizumab after resection/ablation (IMbrave050) explores cutting recurrence.
  • ☢️ Radioembolisation & SBRT: Y-90 selective internal radiation and stereotactic body radiotherapy add options for intermediate/locally advanced disease and as bridging/down-staging tools.
  • 📉 mRECIST response: HCC response is judged by loss of arterial enhancement (viable tumour), not just size — a shrinking-but-still-enhancing tumour may be under-treated, while a stable-size necrotic tumour is a success.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "Discharged From Surveillance"

The case: A 58-year-old man with hepatitis C cirrhosis achieved viral cure with direct-acting antivirals. His clinic letter read "SVR achieved — discharged back to GP." Nobody arranged ongoing surveillance; the 6-monthly ultrasound recall was cancelled with the hepatitis-C clinic. Two years later he re-presented jaundiced, cachectic and with new ascites. Imaging showed a 14 cm HCC invading the portal vein with lung metastases — BCLC C, far beyond any curative option. When a smaller 4 cm HCC had actually been visible in retrospect on a scan done for another reason 14 months earlier, it had been reported but never actioned. He died within four months on best supportive care. In a separate near-miss the same month, a resectable 3 cm HCC was biopsied "to be sure" despite a diagnostic LR-5 scan — and recurred along the needle track in the abdominal wall after resection.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 "VIRUS GONE, JOB DONE"Cure of HCV was mistaken for cure of cirrhosis; the cancer risk (and surveillance need) persisted.
📅 SURVEILLANCE DROPPEDThe 6-monthly ultrasound recall was cancelled with the clinic discharge — no safety net remained.
🩻 INCIDENTAL LESION IGNOREDAn earlier 4 cm lesion was reported but never actioned or referred to the MDT.
🪡 NEEDLE-TRACK SEEDINGA diagnostic LR-5 tumour was biopsied unnecessarily, seeding a curable cancer along the track.
🏥 SYSTEM / HANDOVERNo clear ownership of the cirrhotic after his viral clinic closed; results loop not closed.

🛡️ What would have saved them: keep EVERY cirrhotic in lifelong 6-monthly surveillance even after viral cure; treat a new nodule in a cirrhotic as HCC until proven otherwise; action and refer every incidental liver lesion; and NEVER biopsy a diagnostic LR-5 that is already resectable — image, stage, and take it to the MDT. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 7.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Curing the virus does not discharge the patient. The cirrhotic liver keeps making cancer — so surveillance is for life, and a diagnostic scan is worth more than a dangerous needle."
