🧭 The three questions this visit must answer

Before anything else, frame it: (1) Is the infection ACTIVE? (HBV DNA level and phase; HCV RNA positive or spontaneously cleared). (2) How much LIVER DAMAGE is already there? (fibrosis stage — cirrhosis changes everything). (3) Who else is at risk / who needs prophylaxis? (contacts, and the patient's own future immunosuppression).

⚠️ The trap of "a normal ALT means no disease"ALT fluctuates and can be normal in significant HBV — especially in the immune-tolerant and inactive-carrier phases, and even in some with advanced fibrosis. Never let a single normal ALT stop you from checking HBV DNA and staging fibrosis.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The hepatology resident thinks out loud:

🧠 Frame it as "which infections, which phase, how much damage"

"He is HBsAg-positive AND anti-HCV-positive — a dual exposure. My job is not to 'label him with hepatitis' but to characterise each virus. For HBV I need the full serological panel plus HBV DNA to place his phase; for HCV the antibody only tells me exposure, so I need HCV RNA (PCR) to prove active infection. Then I stage fibrosis non-invasively and decide who to treat. And I vaccinate the household and screen contacts today."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🧬 Full HBV panel: HBsAg, anti-HBs, HBeAg, anti-HBe, anti-HBc (IgM & IgG), and HBV DNA (viral load) — to define acute vs chronic vs phase (S10).
  2. 🧪 HCV RNA (PCR) to confirm active infection; if positive, HCV genotype is largely superseded but fibrosis staging is essential.
  3. 📐 Non-invasive fibrosis: transient elastography (FibroScan) ± APRI/FIB-4; ultrasound liver.
  4. 🅰️ Check HAV & other immunity, HIV co-infection, and hepatitis D (delta) in this HBsAg-positive patient.
  5. 🏠 Test and vaccinate household/sexual contacts (HBV vaccine); harm-reduction advice; enrol in HCC surveillance if cirrhotic.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In any HBsAg-positive patient, always test for hepatitis D — delta only infects alongside HBV, and it drives the fastest progression to cirrhosis of all the viral hepatitides."
🧪 Resident's self-testHis anti-HCV is positive. Can she start direct-acting antivirals now?

No. A positive anti-HCV proves exposure, not current infection — around 25% clear HCV spontaneously and keep the antibody for life. You must confirm HCV RNA positive before treating. Treating an antibody-positive, RNA-negative patient exposes them to drugs for an infection they no longer have.

S6

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the virus scars, WHY the danger (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧬 HBV is a DNA virus (hepadnavirus) that is not directly cytopathic. It parks a stable template (cccDNA) in the hepatocyte nucleus — which is why we can suppress but not eradicate it, and why it can reactivate.
  2. 🛡️ The immune response does the damage: cytotoxic T cells attacking infected hepatocytes cause the inflammation (raised ALT). Too weak → tolerance and high viral load; too strong → flares. This immune tug-of-war defines the natural-history phases.
  3. 🧵 HCV is an RNA virus (flavivirus) with high mutation → no effective vaccine, and it establishes chronic infection in ~75% because immune escape outpaces clearance.
  4. 🩹 Chronic injury → fibrosis → cirrhosis → HCC: repeated inflammation drives stellate-cell activation and collagen deposition; over 20–40 years the architecture distorts to cirrhosis, and the regenerating, mutation-prone liver becomes fertile ground for hepatocellular carcinoma.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"HBV is a DNA virus you SUPPRESS for life (cccDNA never leaves); HCV is an RNA virus you CURE in weeks. Same organ, opposite endgames."

📈 The natural-history phases of chronic HBV (why timing of treatment matters)

PhaseHBeAgHBV DNAALTTreat?
1️⃣ Immune-tolerant+Very highNormalUsually monitor (esp. if young, no fibrosis).
2️⃣ Immune-active (HBeAg+)+HighRaisedYes — active hepatitis, fibrosis risk.

4️⃣ HBeAg-negative active–Fluctuating highRaisedYes — precore/basal-core mutants, progressive.

⚖️ Contrast pair — Hepatitis B vs Hepatitis C

🧬 Hepatitis B

  • DNA virus; cccDNA reservoir → lifelong, can reactivate
  • Vertical/blood/sexual; vaccine-preventable
  • Treatment SUPPRESSES (tenofovir/entecavir), rarely cures
  • Can cause HCC even WITHOUT cirrhosis

🧵 Hepatitis C

  • RNA virus; no latent reservoir
  • Blood-borne; no vaccine yet
  • Direct-acting antivirals CURE >95% (SVR)
  • HCC almost always requires established cirrhosis first

💡 Antibody-positive HCV is only "exposure." HBsAg-positive is "infected now." Read the label before you read the risk.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The work-up order set (first visit)

  • 🧬 Full HBV panel: HBsAg, anti-HBs, HBeAg, anti-HBe, anti-HBc IgM & IgG, and quantitative HBV DNA.
  • 🧪 HCV RNA (PCR) to confirm active infection (± genotype); baseline HCV antibody already positive.
  • 🦠 Co-infection screen: hepatitis D (anti-HDV, in this HBsAg+ patient), HIV, hepatitis A immunity.
  • 🧾 Liver panel & synthetic function: ALT/AST, ALP, GGT, bilirubin, albumin, INR, platelets; alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) baseline.
  • 📐 Fibrosis staging: transient elastography (FibroScan) + APRI/FIB-4; liver ultrasound.
  • 💉 Vaccinate against hepatitis A (and household against HBV); test & vaccinate contacts.
  • 📅 Enrol in 6-monthly HCC surveillance (US ± AFP) if cirrhotic or high-risk HBV.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The first hepatitis visit is a package, not a single test: confirm active virus (DNA/RNA), stage fibrosis, screen co-infections and contacts, vaccinate, and book surveillance."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Clinic & public-health priorities — the nurse runs the safety net

  • 🩸 Standard precautions with every sample — HBV survives on surfaces and is highly transmissible; label blood-borne-virus samples correctly.
  • 💉 Deliver & document vaccines: hepatitis A for the patient; hepatitis B vaccination for non-immune household/sexual contacts.
  • 🧾 Contact tracing & partner notification — arrange testing for household members and sexual partners; sensitive, confidential counselling.
  • 💊 Adherence coaching: HBV nucleos(t)ide analogues are lifelong and must not be stopped abruptly (flare/decompensation risk); HCV DAAs are a short course where completing every dose secures the cure.
  • 🍺 Harm reduction: alcohol minimisation, avoid sharing razors/toothbrushes/needles, safe-sex advice; never share injecting equipment.
  • 🤰 Flag any pregnancy in an HBsAg-positive woman — third-trimester tenofovir + neonatal vaccine + HBIG prevents vertical transmission (see S13/S21).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Treating the patient treats one liver; tracing and vaccinating their contacts stops the next ten infections. Viral hepatitis care is public-health care."

🔬 Serology / Imaging + Visual Enhancements

🧬 The acute HBV serology "movie" — read it in time

HBsAg rises first; anti-HBc (with HBeAg + high DNA) marks active infection; recovery brings anti-HBs. If HBsAg persists >6 months → chronic. In the window period only anti-HBc may be positive.

🔎 The differentials of viral hepatitis A–E you must know cold

VirusType / routeKey point
🅰️ Hepatitis ARNA · faeco-oralAcute, self-limiting, no chronic phase; vaccine-preventable; travel/food outbreaks.
🅱️ Hepatitis BDNA · vertical/blood/sexualChronic in 90% if perinatal; suppress with tenofovir/entecavir; HCC even without cirrhosis.
🅲 Hepatitis CRNA · blood-borneOften asymptomatic → chronic; confirm with RNA; CURE with DAAs (>95% SVR).
🅳 Hepatitis D (delta)Defective RNA · needs HBsAgOnly co-infects with HBV; co-infection or superinfection accelerates cirrhosis fastest.
🅴 Hepatitis ERNA · faeco-oralUsually self-limiting BUT dangerous in pregnancy (high mortality); chronic in the immunosuppressed.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A and E come and go by the faeco-oral route (E kills in pregnancy); B, C and D linger in the blood. Delta cannot exist without B — always test for it when HBsAg is positive."

🩻 Imaging & staging

  • Transient elastography (FibroScan): non-invasive liver stiffness → fibrosis stage; the pivot between "monitor" and "cirrhosis pathway."
  • Ultrasound liver: coarse/nodular echotexture, splenomegaly, and the surveillance modality for HCC (6-monthly in cirrhosis).
  • Liver biopsy: now reserved for uncertain cases or discordant non-invasive tests — histology grades inflammation and stages fibrosis when it will change management.

💊 Medications

💉 The antiviral drug map — what, why, and the traps

DrugRoleMechanism / caution
Tenofovir (TDF/TAF)First-line HBV suppressionNucleotide analogue inhibits HBV polymerase; high resistance barrier. TDF: monitor renal function/bone; TAF gentler. Preferred in pregnancy.
EntecavirFirst-line HBV suppression (non-pregnant)Potent, high barrier. Avoid if prior lamivudine resistance (cross-resistance).
Pegylated interferon-αFinite-course HBV option (selected)Immune-mediated; chance of HBeAg/HBsAg loss but poorly tolerated; avoid in cirrhosis/pregnancy/autoimmune disease.
DAAs (e.g. sofosbuvir/velpatasvir, glecaprevir/pibrentasvir)HCV CURE — 8–12 weeks oralTarget NS5A/NS5B/NS3-4A; >95% SVR. Check drug interactions; screen HBV first (co-infection reactivation risk).

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"HBV: tenofovir or entecavir, usually for life. HCV: a short oral DAA course that cures. Before ANY DAA, check HBV status — treating HCV can unmask and reactivate co-existing hepatitis B."
🚫 The 'never stop abruptly' & 'never miss the screen' list❌ Do NOT stop HBV nucleos(t)ide analogues abruptly (rebound flare/decompensation). ❌ Do NOT start immunosuppression/chemo in an HBsAg+ or anti-HBc+ patient without antiviral prophylaxis. ❌ Do NOT give DAAs for HCV without first checking HBV serology.
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🧬 HBV DNA & ALT every 3–6 months on treatment — confirm suppression; a rising DNA on therapy suggests non-adherence or resistance.
  • 🧪 HCV: SVR12 — undetectable RNA 12 weeks after finishing DAAs confirms cure; then no routine RNA unless re-exposed.
  • 🎯 HCC surveillance: 6-monthly ultrasound ± AFP in cirrhosis and in high-risk HBV (family history, older age, high viral load) — the reason we catch tumours while curable.
  • 🦴 Tenofovir safety: renal function & phosphate (TDF); consider bone density with long use; switch to TAF if concern.
  • 📐 Re-stage fibrosis periodically (elastography) — successful suppression/cure can regress fibrosis over years.
  • 🔁 HBeAg seroconversion & HBsAg loss — the functional-cure milestones that may allow considered treatment stopping.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Even a cured (HCV) or well-suppressed (HBV) patient who is CIRRHOTIC still needs lifelong 6-monthly HCC surveillance — cure removes the virus, not the scarred, cancer-prone liver."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — hepatic and beyond

  • 🩹 Cirrhosis & decompensation — ascites, variceal bleeding, encephalopathy, jaundice; the endpoint of untreated chronic hepatitis.
  • 🎗️ Hepatocellular carcinoma — HBV can cause it even WITHOUT cirrhosis (integration, direct oncogenesis); HCV-related HCC usually needs cirrhosis first.
  • 🔥 HBV reactivation — under immunosuppression/chemo (esp. rituximab, steroids), a "resolved" or inactive HBV can flare to fulminant failure (S21).
  • 🩸 Extra-hepatic HCV: mixed cryoglobulinaemia (vasculitic rash, arthralgia, neuropathy, membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis), porphyria cutanea tarda, lymphoma.
  • 🧫 Extra-hepatic HBV: polyarteritis nodosa, membranous nephropathy, serum-sickness-like arthritis.
  • 🅳 Delta superinfection — accelerates an HBsAg+ patient to cirrhosis fastest of all; and hepatitis E in pregnancy carries high maternal mortality.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Viral hepatitis is not only a liver disease — cryoglobulinaemia, glomerulonephritis, vasculitis and lymphoma can be the presenting face of a silent hepatitis C or B."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🧬 Diagnosis: HBeAg-positive, immune-active chronic hepatitis B (HBV DNA 6.4 log, ALT 118); HCV antibody-positive but RNA-cleared (no DAAs); no delta/HIV.
  2. 💊 Start tenofovir (or entecavir) — active-phase disease with fibrosis risk; counsel on lifelong adherence.
  3. 📐 Stage & surveil: FibroScan result guides fibrosis stage; enrol in 6-monthly ultrasound ± AFP given high-risk HBV.
  4. 🏠 Public health: test & vaccinate household/sexual contacts; vaccinate patient against hepatitis A; harm-reduction counselling.
  5. 🛡️ Flag forever: any future immunosuppression/chemo needs continued antiviral cover — this patient must never be de-suppressed unprotected.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"HBV treatment is a marathon (lifelong suppression, never stop unprotected); HCV treatment is a sprint (a short course that cures). Prescribe for the right race."

S17

🏠 Discharge / Ongoing Care

📋 Two very different destinations — HBV suppression vs HCV cure

🧬 Chronic HBV (this patient)

  • Lifelong tenofovir/entecavir; never stop unprotected; 3–6-monthly DNA/ALT
  • 6-monthly HCC surveillance (US ± AFP)
  • Contacts vaccinated; hepatitis A immunised; alcohol minimised
  • Red flags: jaundice, abdominal swelling, confusion, GI bleed → urgent review
  • Any future immunosuppression → keep antiviral cover

🧵 Chronic HCV (the curable pathway)

  • Confirm RNA+, stage fibrosis, 8–12 weeks of DAAs
  • Confirm cure with SVR12 (undetectable RNA 12 weeks after therapy)
  • If cirrhotic: continue lifelong HCC surveillance despite cure
  • Reinfection is possible — harm reduction, retest if re-exposed
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Cure the virus, but never forget the scar: a cirrhotic patient stays in HCC surveillance for life, even after the last DAA tablet or with fully suppressed HBV DNA."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🧬 The cccDNA problem & "functional cure": HBV covalently-closed-circular DNA is a stable nuclear reservoir that current drugs cannot clear — hence lifelong suppression. HBsAg loss (functional cure) is the trial endpoint for next-generation agents.
  • 💉 Novel HBV therapeutics: siRNA (bepirovirsen and others), capsid-assembly modulators, entry inhibitors (bulevirtide — licensed for hepatitis D), and therapeutic vaccines aiming for finite-duration cure.
  • 🅳 Hepatitis D deep-dive: the most severe chronic viral hepatitis; bulevirtide (entry inhibitor blocking the NTCP receptor) ± interferon is transforming a previously untreatable disease.
  • 🎯 HBV & direct oncogenesis: viral DNA integration into the host genome and HBx protein drive hepatocarcinogenesis independent of cirrhosis — why HBV surveillance can begin before cirrhosis in high-risk groups (PAGE-B/mPAGE-B risk scores).
  • 🧪 HCV in special populations: pangenotypic DAAs simplify treatment; treat in decompensated cirrhosis (avoid protease inhibitors), in pregnancy (emerging data), and as "treatment-as-prevention" to interrupt transmission — the WHO 2030 elimination target.
  • 🔬 Occult HBV: HBsAg-negative, anti-HBc-positive with detectable HBV DNA — relevant to blood safety, transplant, and reactivation under immunosuppression.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Reactivation Nobody Screened For"

The case: A 58-year-old woman with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma started R-CHOP. Her pre-chemo bloods included LFTs but no hepatitis B screen. She was in fact anti-HBc-positive, HBsAg-negative — a "resolved" hepatitis B from decades earlier. After the third rituximab cycle her immune control collapsed; HBV DNA surged and HBsAg reappeared. She presented with jaundice and a soaring bilirubin, then fulminant hepatic failure. Tenofovir was started too late; she died awaiting transplant. Separately that month, a needle-stick junior doctor and a baby born to an untested HBsAg-positive mother both missed prophylaxis — one seroconverted, one became a chronic carrier.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧪 NO SCREENHBsAg and anti-HBc never checked before immunosuppression.
💊 NO PROPHYLAXISAnti-HBc-positive patient given rituximab with no antiviral cover.
🕵️ "RESOLVED" ≠ SAFECleared HBV still carries cccDNA that reactivates when immunity falls.
👶 VERTICAL MISSHBsAg+ mother untested → neonate not given vaccine + HBIG in time.
💉 NEEDLE-STICK GAPPost-exposure HBIG/vaccine protocol not followed for the injured doctor.

🛡️ What would have saved them: screen HBsAg AND anti-HBc before ANY immunosuppression and cover the positive with entecavir/tenofovir; remember "resolved" HBV can still reactivate; give every neonate of an HBsAg-positive mother vaccine + HBIG within 24 hours (plus third-trimester maternal tenofovir if high DNA); and follow the needle-stick protocol precisely. This is exactly the discipline that protected the patient in Bed 4 when his own chemo arrived.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Before you immunosuppress anyone, ask 'have I checked their hepatitis B?' A one-line screen and a cheap antiviral prevent a fulminant, sometimes fatal, reactivation."
