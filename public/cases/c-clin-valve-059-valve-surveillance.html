🧭 The two questions that run the whole clinic

For any severe valve lesion, two questions decide everything: (1) Is the patient genuinely asymptomatic? and (2) Is the LV still performing (size and function preserved)? If BOTH answers are reassuring → watch, with a defined surveillance interval. If EITHER fails — symptoms (overt or exercise-unmasked), a falling LVEF, or a dilating LV — the answer flips to act (see S6, S14).

⚠️ The trap of "he denies symptoms"Elderly patients unconsciously self-limit — they stop climbing the hill, take the lift, garden less — and attribute it to age. A meticulous functional history (and an exercise test where indicated) is the only way to separate the genuinely asymptomatic from the pseudo-asymptomatic.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The cardiology registrar reasons it through at 10:35:

🧠 Frame it as "watch vs act", not "reassure vs refer"

"Echo confirms severe AS with a preserved EF. On paper that's a 'watch' patient — but his history isn't clean. He's quietly dropped his exercise tolerance and blamed age. I won't take 'asymptomatic' at face value: I'll exercise-test him to unmask covert symptoms or an abnormal blood-pressure response. If the test is truly normal, he enters structured surveillance with a defined echo interval and clear safety-netting. If it's positive — or any class-I trigger appears — he goes to the Heart Team."

🎯 The registrar's order of operations

  1. 🗣️ Re-take the functional history with the spouse present — quantify what he's stopped doing.
  2. 📈 Confirm severity on the echo (velocity, mean gradient, valve area, flow state, EF, LV dimensions, valve calcification).
  3. 🏃 Exercise (treadmill) test — appropriate ONLY because he is (apparently) asymptomatic; look for symptoms, a fall or failure of BP to rise, or significant ECG changes.
  4. 🗓️ If negative → surveillance with a severity-appropriate interval + written symptom safety-net.
  5. 🤝 If positive / any trigger → Heart-Team referral for SAVR vs TAVI on age/anatomy/risk.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Exercise-testing is FOR the asymptomatic severe valve and AGAINST the symptomatic one — in a patient with symptoms, severe AS is already an indication to intervene, and stressing them is dangerous."
🧪 Registrar's self-testHe wonders whether to start a vasodilator (e.g. an ACE inhibitor or GTN) for the patient's "occasional chest tightness." Do it?

No — dangerous. In severe AS the cardiac output is fixed by the stenotic valve. A vasodilator (nitrate, high-dose ACE inhibitor, GTN) drops systemic vascular resistance but the heart cannot increase output to compensate → profound hypotension, syncope, even arrest. Exertional chest tightness in severe AS is a symptom to act on (Heart-Team), not a symptom to vasodilate.

S6

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY it stays silent, then WHY it kills

  1. 🧱 Fixed obstruction: calcific degeneration stiffens and narrows the valve. To eject through a small orifice the LV must generate a large pressure gradient.
  2. 💪 Compensatory hypertrophy: the LV responds with concentric hypertrophy, normalising wall stress and maintaining stroke volume — so the patient stays asymptomatic for years.
  3. ⚠️ Decompensation: eventually the hypertrophied, stiff LV cannot keep up. Three failure modes generate the ominous triad:
    • 🫁 Exertional dyspnoea / heart failure — rising LV filling pressures.
    • 💔 Angina — supply–demand mismatch: a thick LV with high oxygen demand and compressed sub-endocardial perfusion, often without epicardial coronary disease.
    • 🕳️ Exertional syncope — fixed output can't rise to meet peripheral vasodilatation on exertion → cerebral hypoperfusion.
  4. 💀 The cliff: once any of these appears, survival falls off a cliff and sudden-death risk rises — because the obstruction is fixed and mechanical, medicine cannot fix it. Only relieving the valve changes the trajectory.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In severe aortic stenosis the LV writes cheques the fixed valve can't cash — the instant the account overdraws (a symptom, a falling EF), you must replace the valve."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Watch vs Act, and Fixed vs Regurgitant

👁️ WATCH — asymptomatic, LV preserved

  • Genuinely asymptomatic (confirmed, and exercise-tested if in doubt)
  • Preserved LVEF, normal LV size, normal exercise BP response
  • Structured surveillance with a defined echo interval + safety-net

🔧 ACT — a trigger has appeared

  • Symptoms (overt or exercise-unmasked)
  • Falling LVEF (< 50–55%) or progressive LV dilatation
  • Very severe / rapidly progressive; another cardiac surgery planned → Heart-Team AVR/TAVI

🚫 AS — a FIXED obstruction

  • Output is fixed; the LV cannot compensate for vasodilatation
  • Never give GTN / nitrates / high-dose vasodilators for "chest pain" — risk of catastrophic hypotension

🌊 Regurgitant lesions (AR / MR)

  • Volume overload; same "symptoms + LV size/function" logic drives timing
  • Vasodilators tolerated and sometimes helpful — a very different haemodynamic world

📋 Orders

🖊️ The clinic order set (10:40)

  • 📈 Transthoracic echo review / repeat — confirm velocity, mean gradient, valve area, flow state, LVEF, LV dimensions (LVESD/LVEDD), valve calcification, aortic root/ascending aorta.
  • 🏃 Symptom-limited exercise (treadmill) test — because he is apparently asymptomatic; halt for symptoms, an abnormal BP response (fall or failure to rise ≥ 20 mmHg), or significant ST change/arrhythmia.
  • 🩸 Bloods: FBC, U&E, glucose/HbA1c, lipids, TFTs; BNP/NT-proBNP (a rising natriuretic peptide flags early decompensation).
  • 📉 12-lead ECG — LVH, strain, conduction disease (relevant pre-TAVI/SAVR).
  • 🖥️ If low-flow/low-gradient or discordant numbers → dobutamine stress echo and/or CT aortic-valve calcium scoring (see S20).
  • 🩻 CT for pre-procedural planning (annulus sizing, access, coronary height) if intervention is on the horizon; coronary assessment (angiography/CT) before AVR.
  • 🗓️ Book structured surveillance (6-monthly for severe) with an explicit written symptom safety-net; Heart-Team referral triggered by any class-I indication.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"An echo alone doesn't manage severe AS — a functional test, a follow-up interval and a written 'come back if…' plan do. Discharge without a surveillance plan is the error."
🚫 The 'never in severe AS' order❌ GTN / nitrates for chest pain · ❌ high-dose vasodilators / aggressive diuresis as a knee-jerk · ❌ competitive/high-intensity exertion advice. Fixed obstruction punishes anything that drops preload/afterload without warning.
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Valve-clinic nursing — the safety net and the truth-finder

  • 🗣️ Take the "real" history: patients disclose covert symptoms to the nurse they hide from the doctor — "I've stopped the stairs," "I get puffed hanging washing." Document and flag these.
  • 🏃 Supervise the exercise test: continuous BP and rhythm; stop immediately for symptoms, a falling/flat BP, or arrhythmia — a positive test in severe AS ends the test, it does not push through it.
  • 📞 Safety-net education: teach the patient and family the three alarm symptoms — breathlessness on exertion, chest tightness, blackouts/dizziness — and to seek review the same week, not at the next routine appointment.
  • 🗓️ Own the recall system: ensure the surveillance echo and clinic are actually booked and tracked; a patient lost to follow-up is a patient un-monitored.
  • 💊 Medication review: reconcile antihypertensives cautiously (avoid over-vasodilating), promote statin/BP control of comorbidities, and confirm no one has started a nitrate.
  • 🦷 Endocarditis awareness: reinforce dental/skin hygiene; explain who does and does not need antibiotic prophylaxis (see S20).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who asks 'what have you stopped doing?' finds the symptom the doctor's 'any chest pain?' missed — that question times the valve replacement."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What "monitoring" means for an outpatient valve

  • 🗓️ Scheduled surveillance echo — severe AS every ~6 months; track velocity, gradient, valve area, LVEF and LV dimensions for trends, not single snapshots.
  • 📉 LVEF trajectory — a fall toward or below 50% is a class-I trigger even in the "asymptomatic" patient.
  • 🎈 LV dimensions — progressive dilatation (especially relevant in AR/MR) flags decompensation.
  • 🩸 Serial natriuretic peptides — a rising BNP/NT-proBNP suggests the LV is beginning to fail and warrants earlier reassessment.
  • 🏃 Repeat exercise testing — reassess functional status if the clinical picture is borderline or the patient minimises symptoms.
  • 🗣️ Symptom surveillance — the most important monitor of all is a diligent, spouse-corroborated functional history at every visit.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Surveillance is trend-watching, not snapshot-collecting — the valve area barely moves between visits, but the symptom history and the EF trajectory are what actually cross the intervention line."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — of the disease, and of watching it

  • 💔 Symptom onset → the cliff: exertional dyspnoea/HF, angina, syncope — once present, survival collapses without valve replacement.
  • ⚡ Sudden cardiac death: the feared event; risk rises once symptoms appear, and is the reason "watchful waiting" must be tightly governed.
  • 🫀 Progressive LV dysfunction / dilatation: if intervention is delayed past the LVEF/dimension thresholds, some LV impairment may not fully recover.
  • 🦠 Infective endocarditis: a diseased valve is a substrate; new fever + murmur change = think endocarditis.
  • 🩸 Atrial fibrillation / conduction disease: new AF or heart block alters symptoms, prognosis and procedural planning.
  • 🩹 The system complication — being lost to follow-up: the deadliest "complication" of an asymptomatic severe valve is a missed recall and an un-elicited symptom (the M&M in S21).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The most lethal complication of asymptomatic severe AS isn't in the valve — it's the missed appointment and the symptom nobody asked about. Govern the watching as tightly as you'd govern a treatment."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the surveillance clinic

  • 🫀 Class-I intervention triggers by lesion (2021 ESC/EACTS · 2020 ACC/AHA):
    • Severe AS: symptoms; LVEF < 50%; undergoing other cardiac surgery. (IIa) exercise-unmasked symptoms / abnormal BP; very severe (Vmax > 5–5.5 m/s); rapid progression; markedly raised BNP; low procedural risk.
    • Severe AR: symptoms; LVEF ≤ 50%; LV end-systolic diameter > 50 mm (or > 25 mm/m² indexed); undergoing other cardiac surgery.
    • Severe primary MR: symptoms; LVEF ≤ 60%; LVESD ≥ 40 mm; new AF or pulmonary hypertension (in the asymptomatic) — favour durable repair over replacement.
  • 💉 Low-flow, low-gradient AS: valve area < 1 cm² but mean gradient < 40 mmHg. Classical (reduced EF): dobutamine stress echo separates true-severe (gradient rises, area stays small) from pseudo-severe, and assesses contractile/flow reserve. Paradoxical low-flow (preserved EF, small hypertrophied LV): confirm with CT aortic-valve calcium score (sex-specific thresholds).
  • 🤝 TAVI vs SAVR selection: a Heart-Team decision on age, anatomy (annulus, access, bicuspid, coronary height, calcification), surgical risk (STS/EuroSCORE), frailty, life expectancy and need for concomitant surgery. Older/higher-risk/suitable anatomy → TAVI; younger/low-risk/complex anatomy or concomitant surgery → SAVR. Trial base: PARTNER and Evolut across high-, intermediate- and low-risk cohorts.
  • 🌀 Primary vs secondary MR: primary (degenerative/prolapse) → fix the valve (surgical repair preferred; transcatheter edge-to-repair if inoperable). Secondary/functional MR → optimise guideline-directed HF therapy and CRT first; transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (COAPT-selected patients) for persistent severe MR.
  • 🌊 AR & aortic-root disease surveillance: bicuspid valve and connective-tissue disease (Marfan, Loeys-Dietz) need aortic dimension surveillance and earlier surgical thresholds; root replacement thresholds are lower in Marfan (≈ 45–50 mm) and bicuspid than in tricuspid aortas.
  • 🦠 Endocarditis prophylaxis: antibiotic prophylaxis is reserved for the highest-risk patients (prosthetic valves/material, previous endocarditis, certain congenital lesions) undergoing high-risk dental procedures — not for native calcific AS/AR/MR. Universal emphasis on oral/skin hygiene.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Man Who Was Watched Off the Cliff"

The case: A 72-year-old man with echo-proven severe aortic stenosis and a "normal" examination was labelled asymptomatic and given a routine 12-month recall — no exercise test, no written safety-net. Over the following two years he quietly stopped gardening and climbing the stairs "because of his age." No one at his brief reviews re-took the functional history or exercise-tested him; his covert exertional breathlessness was never elicited. Three months before his next scheduled echo he collapsed and died on the stairs — a sudden death from what had, unrecognised, become symptomatic severe AS: the exact lesion that, the day those symptoms began, was a class-I indication for a valve replacement that would have saved him.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🕵️ HISTORY NOT TAKEN"Feels well" accepted at face value; slowing down attributed to age, never quantified or corroborated.
🏃 NO EXERCISE TESTThe one test that unmasks covert symptoms in "asymptomatic" severe AS was never done.
🗓️ INTERVAL TOO LONG12-month recall for SEVERE AS (should be ~6-monthly) with no rapid-access route for symptoms.
📞 NO SAFETY-NETPatient/family never taught the three alarm symptoms or told to seek review the same week.
🚩 TRIGGER MISSEDSymptomatic severe AS — a clear indication to intervene — was never recognised as such.

🛡️ What would have saved him: treat every severe AS as one symptom away from a valve replacement; take a meticulous, spouse-corroborated functional history at every visit; exercise-test the apparently asymptomatic to reveal the truth; keep severe AS on a ~6-monthly interval with a written same-week safety-net; and refer to the Heart Team the moment a symptom (overt or exercise-unmasked), a falling EF, or a dilating LV appears. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 5.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'He feels fine' has watched more patients off the cliff than any echo number — in severe aortic stenosis, prove the symptom status; don't assume it."
