🧬 Why THIS patient? — a heart that arrested, and a brain that paid

A witnessed, shockable, bystander-CPR'd out-of-hospital arrest in a middle-aged man is, until proven otherwise, an acute coronary event that triggered VF. The resuscitation restored a circulation — but the whole body has just survived a global ischemia-then-reperfusion insult. The clinical state that follows has a name: post-cardiac-arrest syndrome. Four processes now run in parallel — brain injury, myocardial stunning, a systemic inflammatory (SIRS-like) reperfusion state, and the persistent precipitating pathology (the coronary lesion that started it). Tonight's job is to support the failing organs while protecting the brain from a second injury that we can actually prevent.

🔥 Favourable vs unfavourable arrest features (for outcome) ✅ Witnessed · ✅ Bystander CPR · ✅ Shockable rhythm (VF/pVT) · ✅ Short no-flow / low-flow time · ✅ Early ROSC · ❌ Unwitnessed · ❌ No bystander CPR · ❌ Asystole/PEA · ❌ Prolonged downtime — but no single feature decides the outcome on day one.

🧭 The single most important question: is there a STEMI on the post-ROSC ECG?

After airway, breathing and circulation are stabilised, the fork is the ECG. ST elevation (or new LBBB / unequivocal ischemia, or ongoing instability) → activate the cath lab now — a coronary occlusion is the persistent precipitating cause and revascularising it treats both the heart and the risk of re-arrest. No ST elevation → immediate routine angiography is NOT helpful (COACT, TOMAHAWK) and the patient goes to the ICU for the brain-protection bundle while the cause is worked up. 👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The critical-care resident arrives at 08:55. Her reasoning, out loud:

🧠 Frame the patient: this is post-cardiac-arrest syndrome

"He has ROSC, but he has just survived a whole-body ischemia–reperfusion injury. I'm going to think in four buckets at once: (1) brain injury — the thing that will most likely determine his outcome; (2) myocardial stunning — transient, supportable with inotropes; (3) systemic ischemia–reperfusion / SIRS — vasoplegia and capillary leak that I'll treat with fluids and pressors; and (4) the precipitating cause — his inferior STEMI, which goes to the cath lab. Every order I write maps to one of those four."

🎯 Immediate priorities (her actual order of operations)

  1. 🫁 Fix the ventilator FIRST: titrate FiO₂ down to SpO₂ 94–98% (stop the hyperoxia) and slow the rate to normocapnia (the EtCO₂ of 28 means I'm vasoconstricting his brain). Lung-protective tidal volumes.
  2. 🩸 Haemodynamics: keep MAP ≥ 65 (I'll often aim a little higher) with fluids and norepinephrine; add inotrope if stunning dominates the echo.
  3. 🌡️ Temperature control NOW: he's 38.1°C — start active cooling/normothermia and above all prevent fever. Antipyretics, surface or intravascular device, target ≤ 37.5°C.
  4. 📈 STEMI → cath lab: activate interventional cardiology in parallel; revascularising the culprit treats the cause and reduces re-arrest.
  5. 🧪 Find and fix the rest: ABG, electrolytes (K⁺/Mg²⁺), glucose, treat reversible causes; sedation/analgesia and consider neuromuscular blockade for shivering during temperature control.
  6. ⏳ Hands off the prognosis: the neuro exam now is uninterpretable. No prognostication today. We re-examine ≥ 72 h after rewarming, off sedation, with multiple modalities.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Protect the brain first, revascularise the heart second, and refuse to prognosticate today — the early exam is a confounded exam."

🤔 "Someone wants to call the family in and say it's hopeless. He's GCS 3."

"Absolutely not. He is sedated, possibly still hypothermic, hours out from a global insult — his exam tells us nothing about his ceiling. Prognosticating now risks a self-fulfilling prophecy: we predict a poor outcome, withdraw care, and guarantee the death we predicted. The rule is hard and protective: wait at least 72 hours off sedation and use multimodal testing."

🧪 Resident's self-test (answer before reading on) A colleague says: "Sats are 100% — great, leave the FiO₂ where it is." Is that good post-arrest care?

No. SpO₂ 100% on FiO₂ 1.0 almost always means a high PaO₂ — i.e. hyperoxia, which drives oxidative free-radical injury in reperfused brain. Titrate FiO₂ down to a target SpO₂ 94–98% (avoiding the opposite error of hypoxia). The window matters: both ends are harmful.

🌡️ What "targeted temperature management" actually means in 2026

"For years we argued about 33°C versus 36°C. TTM showed 33°C and 36°C were equivalent; TTM2 showed targeted hypothermia (33°C) did not beat targeted normothermia with aggressive fever prevention. So the modern message is simple and humbling: the essential intervention is preventing pyrexia. Control the temperature actively, keep it ≤ 37.5°C, and above all do not let him get hot — fever after arrest is itself a second neurological insult."

🧭 Consultant's three commandments for the next 72 hours

  1. 🫁 "Mind the gases: SpO₂ 94–98%, normocapnia. Hyperoxia oxidises the brain; hypocapnia starves it of flow. There is a target window, not a 'more is better'."
  2. 🌡️ "Targeted temperature management is, above all, the avoidance of fever. Pick a target ≤ 37.5°C, control it with a feedback device, and treat shivering."
  3. ⏳ "Never prognosticate too early. Wait at least 72 hours off sedation, and be multimodal — exam, EEG, SSEPs, NSE, imaging. One bad sign on day one is not a verdict; it's a confounder."
☠️ The consultant's war memory (more in S13 M&M)"The worst post-arrest outcome I ever saw wasn't a death from the arrest — it was a withdrawal of care at 24 hours on a single poor neurological exam, in a man who was still sedated and might well have recovered. 📖 Teaching Points

🔬 The mechanism — the four faces of post-cardiac-arrest syndrome

  1. 🧠 Brain injury (the main determinant of outcome): global ischemia during the arrest, then a paradoxical reperfusion injury on ROSC — oxidative stress, excitotoxicity, microcirculatory failure, cerebral oedema and impaired autoregulation (so cerebral flow becomes pressure-passive, which is why MAP and PaCO₂ matter so much). Most post-arrest deaths in those who reach ICU are from this.
  2. 💔 Myocardial stunning / dysfunction: transient global hypokinesis after the arrest, even without new infarction. It is reversible over hours-to-days and is supported with inotropes ± mechanical support — it is not a reason to give up.
  3. 🔥 Systemic ischemia–reperfusion (SIRS-like) state: a "sepsis-like" syndrome — vasoplegia, capillary leak, coagulopathy — driving the need for fluids and vasopressors and the relative adrenal/vascular failure seen post-arrest.
  4. 🫀 The persistent precipitating pathology: whatever caused the arrest is still there — most often coronary occlusion, but also PE, electrolyte derangement, primary arrhythmia, hypoxia, toxins. Find it and fix it, or he re-arrests.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"ROSC restores a circulation, not a patient. Four fires now burn at once — brain, heart, the whole vasculature, and the original cause — and the brain is the one you can lose by inattention to oxygen, CO₂, pressure and temperature."

⚖️ Contrast pair #1 — Hypoxia vs Hyperoxia (both harmful — aim for the window)

🔻 Hypoxia

  • Insufficient O₂ delivery to an already-injured brain
  • Deepens ischemic/anoxic injury
  • Avoid low SpO₂ and de-saturation events

🔺 Hyperoxia

  • Supra-physiological PaO₂ from FiO₂ 1.0 / SpO₂ 100%
  • Generates oxidative free radicals → worsens reperfusion injury
  • Titrate FiO₂ down once an SpO₂ probe reads reliably

🎯 The target window: SpO₂ 94–98% / normal PaO₂. The instinct "100% sats = good" is the trap — after arrest, both ends of the oxygen scale extend brain injury.

⚖️ Contrast pair #2 — STEMI OHCA vs non-STEMI OHCA (the angiography decision)

📈 STEMI OHCA

  • ST elevation / new LBBB / ongoing instability on the post-ROSC ECG
  • Immediate cath lab — revascularise the culprit
  • Treats the cause and reduces re-arrest

🚫 Non-STEMI OHCA

  • No ST elevation, stabilising haemodynamics
  • No benefit from immediate routine angiography (COACT, TOMAHAWK)
  • ICU first, brain bundle, delayed/selective angiography

💡 Why it matters: rushing every comatose OHCA to the lab exposes patients to risk without benefit when there's no ST elevation — but missing a true STEMI leaves the engine of the arrest running. Read the post-ROSC ECG carefully.

⚖️ Contrast pair #3 — Early vs Delayed neuroprognostication; Normocapnia vs Hypocapnia

🧠 Early prognostication on a single confounded sign risks a self-fulfilling prophecy; delayed (≥ 72 h, off sedation), multimodal prognostication is the standard. 🌬️ Normocapnia keeps cerebral blood flow appropriate; hypocapnia (from over-ventilation) vasoconstricts cerebral vessels and worsens ischemia. Don't bag a post-arrest brain to a low CO₂.

🔬 Fellowship Corner — Advanced Topics

  • 🧠 Multimodal neuroprognostication: no single test decides outcome. Combine clinical exam (bilateral absent pupillary/corneal reflexes ≥ 72 h, myoclonus pattern), EEG (suppression, burst-suppression, status), somatosensory evoked potentials (bilateral absent N20), NSE biomarker trends, and neuroimaging (CT loss of grey–white differentiation, MRI diffusion changes).
  • 🌡️ TTM logistics: surface vs intravascular feedback-controlled devices, controlled rewarming ≤ 0.25–0.5°C/h, and the central role of shivering control (counter-warming, magnesium, sedation, neuromuscular blockade) so cooling actually works.
  • 🫀 Refractory arrest & ECPR: selected refractory VF arrests may go to extracorporeal CPR (VA-ECMO) as a bridge to revascularisation; mechanical support (Impella/IABP) for severe post-arrest myocardial stunning and cardiogenic shock.
  • 🩸 Haemodynamic nuance: impaired cerebral autoregulation makes flow pressure-passive — some advocate individualised, often higher MAP targets; balance against myocardial work. Treat the vasoplegic SIRS state like distributive shock.
  • 🧪 Self-fulfilling prophecy bias: early prognostication that triggers withdrawal of life-sustaining therapy contaminates the very outcome data — a key reason guidelines mandate delay and multimodality.
  • 🔌 Secondary prevention: for a non-reversible primary arrhythmic arrest, plan an ICD later; for an ischemic cause, revascularisation ± ICD per recovered EF.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The post-ROSC order set, written as you'd actually write it (09:00)

  • 🫁 Ventilation: titrate FiO₂ to SpO₂ 94–98% (stop hyperoxia); ventilate to normocapnia (PaCO₂ 35–45 mmHg / 4.5–6.0 kPa); lung-protective tidal volume 6–8 mL/kg; confirm tube position with capnography + CXR.
  • 🩸 Haemodynamics: target MAP ≥ 65 (consider higher); fluids + norepinephrine; add inotrope (e.g. dobutamine) if echo shows stunning/low output; arterial line.
  • 🌡️ Targeted temperature management: active control to a constant target ≤ 37.5°C; aggressively prevent fever; feedback-controlled device; antipyretics; treat shivering (sedation ± neuromuscular blockade).
  • 📈 STEMI on post-ROSC ECG → activate cath lab now for angiography ± PCI; dual antiplatelet/anticoagulation per protocol.
  • 🧪 Bloods/gases: ABG, U&E, K⁺/Mg²⁺, glucose (avoid hypo- and marked hyper-glycaemia), troponin, lactate, CBC, coagulation, toxicology if indicated.
  • 💤 Sedation/analgesia short-acting where possible (to allow a later clean neuro exam); continuous EEG if available.
  • 🧠 Neuroprotection: head-up 30°, normoglycaemia, treat seizures; document explicitly: NO prognostication before ≥ 72 h off sedation, multimodal.
  • 📟 Continuous telemetry, SpO₂, capnography, core-temperature probe, hourly urine output, NG tube, VTE and stress-ulcer prophylaxis as appropriate.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Every post-ROSC order serves one of two masters: revascularise the cause, or protect the brain — oxygen window, normocapnia, MAP ≥ 65 and no fever."

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ CCU/ICU nursing priorities — hour by hour

  • 🫁 Guard the oxygen window: as soon as the SpO₂ probe reads reliably, titrate FiO₂ down to keep SpO₂ 94–98% — flag any 100% on high FiO₂ as hyperoxia, and any desaturation as hypoxia.
  • 🌬️ Watch the CO₂: capnography continuously; report EtCO₂ trending low (over-ventilation/hypocapnia) so the rate can be adjusted to normocapnia.
  • 🩸 MAP ≥ 65: hourly (or beat-to-beat arterial) BP; titrate vasopressors to target, report drops promptly — the post-arrest vasculature is leaky and labile.
  • 🌡️ Temperature is a nursing-led therapy: run the feedback-controlled device to the set target, monitor core temperature continuously, and treat fever and shivering aggressively — fever is a second brain injury.
  • 💤 Sedation/shivering: manage sedation and neuromuscular blockade per protocol; remember NMB can mask seizures — escalate if continuous EEG shows activity.
  • 🧠 Neuro obs without over-reading them: document pupils, movements and any myoclonus, but understand these are not prognostic yet — never imply a verdict to the family.
  • 📋 Document targets met and missed: oxygen, CO₂, MAP, temperature, glucose — the post-arrest bundle is a checklist the bedside owns.
  • 👨‍👩‍👦 Support the family with honest uncertainty: "he is critically ill and it is too early to know" — never "hopeless" on day one (see S16).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who keeps the SpO₂ in the window, the CO₂ normal, the MAP up and the temperature from rising is delivering most of the brain protection in the room."
📢 Escalate IMMEDIATELY forLoss of pulse / re-arrest · SpO₂ < 94% or persistent 100% on high FiO₂ · EtCO₂ trending low · MAP < 65 despite pressors · temperature rising / fever · seizures or sustained myoclonus · new ST changes or recurrent instability · any move to prognosticate or withdraw before 72 h.

📈 ECG / Imaging

🫀 The post-ROSC 12-lead — read it like it decides the cath lab (it does)

Post-ROSC sinus rhythm with inferior ST elevation and a persistently elevated ST segment. In a resuscitated OHCA this is a STEMI → immediate cath lab. Had there been no ST elevation, immediate routine angiography would not be indicated (COACT/TOMAHAWK).

🫁 CXR / CTConfirm ETT and lines; CT chest/CTPA if PE suspected as the arrest cause; CT head for trauma or to support later prognostication (grey–white loss).

🧠 SSEPs / NSE / MRIComponents of delayed (≥ 72 h), multimodal neuroprognostication — never used in isolation, never used early.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"One ECG sends him to the cath lab or to the ICU; one rule keeps you honest with the family — no prognostication before 72 hours, and never on a single test."

🫀 Post-procedure imaging in this patient

  • 📈 Cath lab: occluded dominant RCA (the inferior STEMI culprit) — primary PCI with stent, TIMI 3 flow restored. The engine of the arrest is switched off.
  • 🩻 Echo (post-PCI): EF 35% with inferior hypokinesis and global stunning out of proportion to the infarct — expected to improve over days as stunning resolves.
  • 🧠 CT head (for completeness, not prognosis): no haemorrhage, preserved grey–white differentiation early — reassuring but not predictive on day zero.
⚠️ The post-arrest ECG's favourite lie"The post-ROSC ECG is always abnormal, so ignore it." Wrong — a clear STEMI pattern is the trigger for immediate revascularisation. But also beware over-reading transient post-arrest repolarisation changes as STEMI when there is none. Read it carefully, repeat it, and correlate clinically.

💊 Medications

💉 Post-ROSC drugs — what, why, and the traps

Drug / measureRole nowMechanism / caution
Oxygen (titrated FiO₂)Maintain SpO₂ 94–98% / normal PaO₂Treat O₂ as a drug with a therapeutic window. Both hyperoxia (oxidative injury) and hypoxia (anoxic injury) extend brain damage.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Oxygen is a drug with a ceiling, vasopressors defend the brain's perfusion, and fever-control is a therapy — the post-arrest 'medications' are mostly numbers you keep inside a window."
🚫 The 'don't' list in post-cardiac-arrest care❌ Don't leave FiO₂ at 1.0 once SpO₂ reads reliably (hyperoxia) · ❌ Don't hyperventilate to a low CO₂ (cerebral vasoconstriction) · ❌ Don't let the patient become febrile · ❌ Don't sedate heavily and then "read" the neuro exam as if it were prognostic · ❌ Don't rush a non-STEMI OHCA to immediate routine angiography · ❌ Don't prognosticate or withdraw before ≥ 72 h, off sedation, multimodal.

🏠 Cause- and prevention-directed therapy (this admission)

  • 🫀 Revascularisation of the culprit coronary lesion (done at primary PCI) + full secondary-prevention ACS therapy (antiplatelets, statin, ACEi/ARB, beta-blocker as tolerated).
  • 💙 Optimise heart-failure therapy if EF remains reduced after stunning resolves; reassess EF before any device decision.
  • 🔌 ICD planning later for a non-reversible primary arrhythmic substrate, or per recovered EF after an ischemic cause — decided weeks out, not on day one.
  • 🧠 Neuro-rehabilitation pathway for survivors with hypoxic-ischemic brain injury.
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🫁 SpO₂ + serial ABGs — keep SpO₂ 94–98% and PaO₂ normal; the number you titrate against to avoid both hyperoxia and hypoxia.
  • 🌬️ Continuous capnography + PaCO₂ — maintain normocapnia; a falling EtCO₂ flags over-ventilation/hypocapnia (or falling output — check the pulse).
  • 🩸 Invasive arterial pressure — beat-to-beat MAP to defend MAP ≥ 65 (often higher) through a labile, vasoplegic circulation.
  • 🌡️ Continuous core temperature with a feedback-controlled device — the central monitored therapy; any upward drift toward fever is an alarm.
  • 🫀 Continuous ECG / telemetry — watch for re-arrhythmia and recurrent ischemia; serial troponin to track the infarct.
  • 🧠 Continuous EEG — detect treatable seizures/status (especially under neuromuscular blockade); contributes later to prognostication.
  • 🧪 Serial K⁺/Mg²⁺, glucose, lactate — keep electrolytes in band, glucose moderate, and watch lactate clearance as a perfusion marker.
  • 💧 Urine output / renal function — the kidney is a downstream marker of the same hypoperfusion that threatens the brain.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After ROSC the monitor's job is to keep four numbers in their windows — oxygen, CO₂, MAP and temperature — because every one outside its window is more brain lost."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — and the mechanism

  • 🔁 Re-arrest — the precipitating cause persists until treated; revascularise and correct electrolytes/oxygenation to prevent it.
  • 🧠 Hypoxic-ischemic brain injury — the main determinant of outcome; worsened by hyperoxia, hypoxia, hypocapnia, hypotension and fever. Cerebral oedema and seizures may follow.
  • 💔 Post-arrest myocardial dysfunction / cardiogenic shock — stunning ± the underlying infarct; supportable and usually reversible.
  • 🔥 Systemic reperfusion/SIRS state — vasoplegia, capillary leak, coagulopathy, AKI, and a sepsis-like picture (and genuine secondary infections, e.g. aspiration pneumonia).
  • 🌡️ Temperature complications — fever (extends injury), and the hazards of cooling itself (shivering, bradycardia, electrolyte shifts, infection).
  • ⚖️ Premature prognostication / self-fulfilling prophecy — withdrawing care too early on confounded signs, causing the very death it predicts.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference — "The Patient We Gave Up On Too Soon"

The case: A 52-year-old woman, witnessed VF arrest, bystander CPR, ROSC after 10 minutes, comatose on arrival. Overnight she was deeply sedated, mildly hypothermic, and her oxygen and CO₂ targets were never set — she sat at FiO₂ 1.0 and ran febrile to 38.6°C unrecognised for hours. At the 24-hour ward round, a single neurological exam — absent motor response, sluggish pupils, all while still sedated and cool — was read as "devastating hypoxic brain injury." The family were told it was hopeless and care was withdrawn. At the M&M, three things were obvious in hindsight: she had been hyperoxic and febrile (extending the very injury that was then used to condemn her), the exam was confounded by sedation and temperature, and the angiogram had been deferred so a treatable proximal LAD lesion went un-revascularised. The 72-hour, off-sedation, multimodal assessment that might have told the truth was never done.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

⏳ TOO EARLYPrognosis declared at 24 h, not after ≥ 72 h off sedation — a self-fulfilling prophecy.
🩻 SINGLE TESTOne confounded clinical exam, no EEG/SSEP/NSE/imaging — never multimodal.
🌡️ FEVER MISSEDUnrecognised pyrexia extended the brain injury overnight.
🫁 HYPEROXIAFiO₂ 1.0 left running — oxidative reperfusion injury.
🫀 CAUSE MISSEDTreatable coronary occlusion never revascularised.

🛡️ What would have saved her: set the bundle on arrival (SpO₂ 94–98%, normocapnia, MAP ≥ 65, no fever); revascularise a treatable cause; and — above all — refuse to prognosticate before 72 hours, off sedation, multimodally. "Devastating brain injury" on day one, through sedation and fever, is a hypothesis, not a verdict. This is exactly why we defer the prognosis tonight in Bed 9.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'It's hopeless' said at 24 hours through sedation and fever is how recoverable patients are lost. Prognosis is the last decision after arrest, never the first."

🔀 Differentials

🗺️ Plan

🧭 The first hour → the first 72 hours → beyond

  1. 🫁 First hour (done): titrate FiO₂ to SpO₂ 94–98%; ventilate to normocapnia; arterial line; MAP ≥ 65 with fluids/norepinephrine; start targeted temperature management (≤ 37.5°C, prevent fever); primary PCI of the inferior-STEMI culprit.
  2. 🌡️ First 24–48 h: maintain the bundle; control shivering; continuous EEG; serial gases/electrolytes/troponin/lactate; echo to track stunning; treat seizures and reversible insults; controlled rewarming if hypothermia was used.
  3. ⏳ At ≥ 72 h, off sedation: perform multimodal neuroprognostication — clinical exam (pupillary/corneal reflexes, motor, myoclonus), EEG, SSEPs (N20), NSE trend, neuroimaging — never on a single finding.
  4. 🫀 Cause & prevention: complete the ACS pathway; reassess EF after stunning resolves; plan ICD later if a non-reversible arrhythmic substrate or persistently low EF.
  5. 🧠 If recovering: wean sedation and ventilation, neuro-rehabilitation referral, cognitive and functional assessment.
  6. 👨‍👩‍👦 Family throughout: honest, repeated updates that it is "too early to know," with the explicit plan to reassess at 72 h — protecting them from a premature verdict.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Treat the cause now, protect the brain for 72 hours, then — and only then — let multiple tests, off sedation, tell you the prognosis."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Discharge plan & safety net

  • 💊 Medications: full secondary-prevention ACS therapy (dual antiplatelet, high-intensity statin, ACEi/ARB, beta-blocker as tolerated) with a clear written schedule; heart-failure therapy if EF remains reduced.
  • 🫀 Cardiology follow-up: repeat echo to reassess EF after stunning fully resolves; ICD decision deferred and reviewed per recovered EF / arrhythmic risk — not decided on the acute admission.
  • 🧠 Neuro-rehabilitation: cognitive and functional assessment, rehab referral, and support for the common post-arrest cognitive/emotional after-effects (memory, fatigue, mood).
  • 👨‍👩‍👦 Family & community: CPR/AED training offered to the family; acknowledge and support the psychological impact on the patient and relatives (and the bystander who saved him).
  • 🚩 Red flags → seek urgent care: chest pain, breathlessness, syncope/presyncope, palpitations, or any collapse — the substrate that arrested once needs vigilance.
  • 📚 Education: what happened in plain language, the importance of medication adherence and risk-factor control, smoking cessation, and follow-up appointments booked before discharge.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Surviving the arrest is the start: discharge after ROSC means a revascularised cause, optimised secondary prevention, a deferred device decision, neuro-rehab, and a family trained to act if it happens again."

💎 Clinical Pearls

🧠 The brain is the patientPost-arrest brain injury is the main determinant of outcome. Avoid hypoxia, hyperoxia, hypotension, hypocapnia and fever — each extends the injury.
🫀 Four fires at oncePost-arrest syndrome = brain injury + myocardial stunning + ischemia-reperfusion SIRS + the persistent precipitating cause. Treat all four.
🫁 Oxygen has a windowBoth ends harm. Titrate FiO₂ to SpO₂ 94–98% / normal PaO₂; "100% sats on FiO₂ 1.0" is hyperoxia, not success.
🌬️ Normocapnia, not low CO₂Hypocapnia from over-ventilation vasoconstricts cerebral vessels and worsens ischemia. Don't bag the brain to a low CO₂.
🩸 MAP ≥ 65Autoregulation is impaired and flow is pressure-passive — defend perfusion pressure (often a little higher) with fluids/pressors.
🌡️ TTM = avoid feverTTM2: targeted normothermia with aggressive fever prevention is acceptable. The essential intervention is preventing pyrexia (≤ 37.5°C).
📈 STEMI → cath lab nowST elevation on the post-ROSC ECG goes straight to angiography ± PCI; it treats the cause and prevents re-arrest.
🚫 Non-STEMI OHCAImmediate routine angiography did NOT improve outcomes (COACT, TOMAHAWK). ICU first, brain bundle, delayed/selective cath.
⏳ Never prognosticate earlyWait ≥ 72 h after rewarming and off sedation, and be multimodal — or risk a self-fulfilling prophecy.
🩻 Multimodal, alwaysExam + EEG + SSEPs + NSE + imaging together. No single early sign is a verdict.
💔 Stunning recoversPost-arrest myocardial dysfunction is transient and supportable — not a reason to give up; support it and reassess EF later.
🔌 Device later, not nowPlan an ICD for a non-reversible arrhythmic cause once EF and risk are reassessed — never decided on day one.
