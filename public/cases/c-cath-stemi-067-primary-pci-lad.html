🚦 Risk stratification & the indication

A STEMI is a clinical + ECG diagnosis — persistent ST elevation ≥ 1 mm in ≥ 2 contiguous leads (≥ 2 mm in V2–V3 for men). It mandates immediate reperfusion. Primary PCI is the preferred strategy if achievable within 120 minutes of first medical contact (FMC); otherwise fibrinolysis then transfer. Anterior STEMI carries the highest mortality (largest myocardium at risk) and the highest early VT/VF risk.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Time is muscle: every 30-minute delay to reperfusion adds roughly 8% to one-year mortality. The clock started at the door, not at the table."

📋 Pre-Procedure Workup & Planning

🧾 The 60-second pre-PCI checklist (do NOT delay reperfusion for it)

  • ✍️ Consent — emergency/verbal if peri-arrest; document risks (bleeding, stroke, dissection, contrast nephropathy, emergency CABG, death).
  • 🧪 Allergies & contrast — prior contrast reaction? Premedicate only if time allows; a true STEMI is never delayed for a mild dye allergy.
  • 🫘 Renal status — eGFR for contrast planning; minimise contrast volume, hydrate after; do NOT cancel a STEMI PCI for creatinine.
  • 🩸 Antithrombotic review — DAPT loaded (aspirin + potent P2Y₁₂: ticagrelor/prasugrel); plan procedural anticoagulation (see S3).
  • 🖐️ Access assessment — Allen/Barbeau test of the radial (see S3); check femoral pulses as a bail-out.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Rule out aortic dissection before you anticoagulate the coronary — a tearing-to-the-back story or unequal arm pressures earns a second look BEFORE heparin."

S3

🩸 Vascular Access & Setup

🖐️ Radial vs femoral — and why radial wins in STEMI

🖐️ Radial (default)

  • Fewer access-site bleeds & vascular complications
  • Lower mortality in ACS (MATRIX, RIVAL ACS subgroup)
  • Early ambulation, patient comfort
  • Confirm patency first (Barbeau/Allen)

🦵 Femoral (bail-out)

  • When radial fails / spasm / small vessel / need for large-bore support (Impella, IABP)
  • Higher bleeding — use micropuncture + ultrasound guidance, avoid a "high" or "low" stick
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Radial-first in ACS saves lives by preventing bleeds — and a bleed after PCI doubles mortality. Reach for the wrist, not the groin, unless you need big-bore support."

💉 Sheath, anticoagulation & the ACT concept

  • 🔧 Sheath: 6 Fr radial (hydrophilic); give an intra-arterial "radial cocktail" (verapamil ± nitrate ± heparin) to prevent spasm.
  • 🩸 Procedural anticoagulation: unfractionated heparin (70–100 U/kg, titrate to an ACT ~250–300 s), or bivalirudin (a reversible direct thrombin inhibitor — less bleeding, HIT-safe). The ACT (activated clotting time) is your bedside gauge that the blood is anticoagulated enough to wire a coronary without thrombus forming on the equipment.
  • 🧭 Guide catheter: for the LAD (left system) → an EBU/XB left guide; for the RCA → JR4/AL. The guide gives back-up support to push equipment across the lesion.

S5

🔬 Lesion Assessment — Physiology & Imaging

🫀 In a STEMI, physiology waitsThe culprit is an acutely occluded vessel — you do NOT need FFR to prove it needs treating. Reperfuse the culprit NOW. Physiology (FFR/iFR) belongs to the non-culprit lesions and to stable disease. Imaging (IVUS/OCT) may still guide sizing and detect mechanism (e.g., a plaque rupture vs a spontaneous dissection).

📏 FFR / iFR — the physiology of a stenosis (for stable / non-culprit disease)

  • FFR (fractional flow reserve) — the ratio of maximal flow across a stenosis to normal, measured with a pressure wire during hyperaemia (adenosine). FFR ≤ 0.80 = ischaemic → treat. Above 0.80 → defer safely.
  • iFR (instantaneous wave-free ratio) — a resting index, no adenosine. iFR ≤ 0.89 = significant.

🎯 FFR ≤ 0.80 → the stenosis limits flow enough to cause ischaemia → treat. > 0.80 → defer and treat medically (DEFER, FAME).

🖥️ Intravascular imaging — IVUS vs OCT

🔵 IVUS

  • Ultrasound — deep penetration, sizes the vessel, quantifies plaque burden, sees behind calcium
  • Great for left main & vessel sizing; no contrast needed

🟠 OCT

  • Light — ultra-high resolution; superb for edge dissection, thrombus, stent apposition, and plaque erosion vs rupture vs SCAD
  • Needs a contrast flush; less penetration
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Don't FFR the culprit — it's occluded; you already know. Save physiology for the bystander lesions, and let imaging tell you WHY the artery closed."

🛠️ Intervention — Step-by-Step Execution

🎈 Balloon angioplasty & stent deployment. Source: Wikimedia Commons (Blausen)

🪜 The sequence — each step is a decision

  1. 🧵 Wire the vessel — a workhorse guidewire across the occlusion into the distal true lumen (confirm distal position, not a side branch or subintimal).
  2. 🫧 Restore flow — cross with a small balloon or aspiration if heavy thrombus; a whiff of flow relieves ischaemia and lets you size.
  3. 📏 Lesion prep & sizing — predilate with a semi-compliant balloon; size the stent 1:1 to the reference vessel diameter (IVUS/OCT or online QCA).
  4. 🩹 Deploy a drug-eluting stent — cover the lesion with adequate landing zones; nominal-to-modest deployment pressure.
  5. 🎈 Post-dilate — a non-compliant balloon at high pressure to ensure full expansion and apposition (under-expansion is the #1 cause of stent thrombosis/restenosis).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Under-sized and under-expanded stents fail. Size 1:1 to the reference vessel and post-dilate — a beautiful angiogram over a poorly expanded stent is a future thrombosis."

S10

🏠 Post-Procedure Care, Debrief & Assessment

🩹 Access-site care

  • 🖐️ Radial: patent-haemostasis compression band, monitor for radial artery occlusion & bleeding.
  • 🦵 Femoral: manual pressure/closure device, watch for haematoma, pseudoaneurysm, retroperitoneal bleed (unexplained hypotension + back/flank pain).

💊 Secondary prevention (start day 1)

  • DAPT — aspirin + a P2Y₁₂ inhibitor (ticagrelor/prasugrel preferred) for 12 months (individualise by bleeding risk).
  • High-intensity statin (atorvastatin 80 mg) regardless of LDL; add ezetimibe/PCSK9i to target.
  • Beta-blocker once stable, ACE inhibitor (especially anterior MI / LV dysfunction), MRA if EF ≤ 40% with HF/diabetes.
  • 🏃 Cardiac rehab, smoking cessation, risk-factor control; echo at 24–48 h for EF; ICD reassessment at ≥ 40 days if EF ≤ 35%.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The stent fixed the plumbing; the pills and the rehab fix the patient. DAPT + high-intensity statin from day 1, and never let 'the artery's open' end the conversation."

🔭 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

  • 🌀 No-reflow mechanisms: distal embolisation, micro-vascular obstruction, reperfusion injury; treat with intracoronary vasodilators, consider avoiding over-aggressive thrombectomy.
  • 🪨 Calcium modification: rotational atherectomy, orbital atherectomy, and intravascular lithotripsy (Shockwave) for undilatable calcified lesions — under-prepared calcium = stent under-expansion.
  • 🔱 Bifurcation strategy: provisional single-stent (default) vs 2-stent techniques (DK-crush, culotte, T/TAP); proximal optimisation technique (POT) and kissing balloons.
  • 🚧 CTO PCI: antegrade wire escalation, antegrade dissection re-entry, retrograde approach via collaterals — hybrid algorithm.
  • 🫀 Mechanical support: IABP (no mortality benefit — IABP-SHOCK II), Impella/percutaneous LVAD (DanGer Shock in STEMI shock), VA-ECMO for refractory shock — and RV support/north-south syndrome nuances.
  • 🖥️ Imaging-guided PCI: IVUS/OCT-guided stenting improves outcomes vs angiography alone (ULTIMATE, RENOVATE-COMPLEX-PCI); OCT distinguishes plaque rupture vs erosion vs SCAD.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Perforation Nobody Ballooned"

The case: During a heavily calcified LAD PCI, an oversized non-compliant balloon was inflated to high pressure. The next cine showed a plume of contrast tracking OUTSIDE the vessel — an Ellis type III perforation. The operator, flustered, pulled the balloon out to "get a better look." The perforation bled freely into the pericardium; the patient tamponaded and arrested. Reversal, a covered stent, and pericardiocentesis came minutes too late. He did not survive.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

📏 OVERSIZINGBalloon oversized to a calcified segment without adequate prep (rota/IVL).
🎈 REMOVED THE BALLOONThe first move in a perforation is to INFLATE across it — not remove it.
🩸 HEPARIN NOT REVERSEDAnticoagulation left the leak bleeding freely.
🫗 LATE DRAINAGEPericardiocentesis set was not ready; tamponade won the race.
🧠 PANICNo rehearsed perforation drill; the team improvised under pressure.

🛡️ What would have saved him: prepare calcium before you dilate; the instant you see extravasation, re-inflate a balloon across the perforation to tamponade it, reverse the heparin (protamine), deploy a covered (PTFE) stent, and have the pericardiocentesis kit open on the table — the covered stent and the drain, together, in minutes. This is the drill in S9.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"When you see contrast leave the vessel, your hands should INFLATE, not withdraw. Balloon first, reverse heparin, covered stent, drain — in that order, every time."

🎯 The five things to carry out of this case

  1. ⏱️ Reperfuse fast — primary PCI within 120 min FMC; time is muscle.
  2. 🖐️ Radial-first, culprit-only — fewer bleeds, and don't chase bystanders during the infarct (except in shock).
  3. 📏 Size 1:1 and post-dilate — a poorly expanded stent is a future thrombosis.
  4. 🧠 Confirm tissue reperfusion — ST resolution & blush, not just epicardial flow.
  5. 🩸 Rehearse the perforation drill — balloon, reverse, covered stent, drain.
