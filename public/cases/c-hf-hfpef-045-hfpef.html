🧭 The three questions that reframe "breathlessness + normal EF"

Before accepting "deconditioning," answer three things: (1) Are there objective congestion signs and a raised natriuretic peptide? (2) Does the echo show diastolic dysfunction / structural change (LVH, LA enlargement, E/e′↑, TR velocity↑)? (3) Does a validated score (H2FPEF, HFA-PEFF) — or exercise/invasive haemodynamics — confirm raised filling pressures? (See S14.)

⚠️ The trap of "the resting numbers are fine"A resting PCWP and a resting echo can look near-normal in true HFpEF — the abnormality only declares on exertion or tachycardia, when the stiff ventricle's filling pressure spikes. If suspicion is high but resting testing is equivocal, the answer is exercise (diastolic stress echo) or invasive exercise haemodynamics, not reassurance.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The cardiology resident thinks out loud:

🧠 Frame it as "prove the filling pressure," not "the EF is normal"

"High-probability story, high-probability patient. My job isn't to 'reassure about a normal EF' — it's to demonstrate raised LV filling pressures. I'll compute an H2FPEF score, apply the HFA-PEFF algorithm to the echo, confirm the raised NT-proBNP, and if it's still borderline I'll ask for a diastolic stress echo or invasive exercise haemodynamics. And because she's got a thick-walled heart, I will actively look for amyloid red flags before I call this 'garden-variety hypertensive HFpEF'."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🧪 NT-proBNP confirmed raised (note: obesity lowers it, AF and CKD raise it — interpret with adjusted thresholds).
  2. 📈 Echo with diastolic indices — E/e′, septal/lateral e′, LA volume index, TR velocity, LV wall thickness/mass (S10).
  3. 🧮 H2FPEF and HFA-PEFF scores to place probability (S5, S14).
  4. 🏃 Exercise (diastolic stress) echo or invasive exercise haemodynamics if the diagnosis remains uncertain.
  5. 🔎 Amyloid / mimic screen — ECG voltages, wall thickness discordance, carpal tunnel history, free light chains + bone scintigraphy if flagged (S20).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Before you write 'HFpEF, hypertensive type,' rule out the phenotype with a drug: amyloid. A thick heart with low ECG voltage earns a bone scan and light chains, not a shrug."
🧪 Resident's self-testHer NT-proBNP is 1180 but she is obese (BMI 38). Does obesity make you doubt HF?

No — the opposite. Obesity lowers natriuretic peptides (up to ~50%), so a "borderline" NT-proBNP in an obese patient is actually more impressive, and 1180 ng/L is clearly raised. Use lower thresholds in the obese, and remember AF and CKD push the number up. A normal BNP in an obese patient does NOT exclude HFpEF.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY breathlessness with a "normal" EF (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧱 Stiffening & hypertrophy: hypertension, obesity, ageing, diabetes and inflammation drive concentric LV hypertrophy, interstitial fibrosis and titin-based stiffening (or infiltration in amyloid). The chamber can still eject but resists filling.
  2. 📈 The steep pressure–volume relationship: a stiff LV sits on the steep part of its end-diastolic pressure–volume curve, so a small rise in volume produces a large rise in filling pressure.
  3. 💓 Exertion / tachycardia / loss of atrial kick: exercise and AF shorten diastole and demand more filling — filling pressure spikes, back-pressure transmits to the left atrium and pulmonary capillaries → pulmonary congestion and exertional dyspnoea despite a normal resting EF.
  4. 🫁 Downstream: chronic left-atrial pressure → LA remodelling and AF → pulmonary hypertension → RV strain and systemic congestion (oedema, hepatic congestion). The "diastolic" problem becomes a whole-heart problem.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"HFpEF fails to FILL, not to eject. The EF is a snapshot of ejection; the disease lives in diastole and on exertion — which is exactly why the resting echo reassures and the patient still can't climb the stairs."

⚖️ Contrast pair — HFpEF vs HFrEF

🧱 HFpEF (this patient)

  • Stiff, non-compliant LV — a filling problem; EF ≥ 50%
  • Elderly, obese, hypertensive, AF, female-predominant phenotype
  • Cornerstone: SGLT2i + diuretics + comorbidity control; the four pillars do NOT all transfer

🔋 HFrEF

  • Weak, dilated LV — an ejection problem; EF ≤ 40%
  • Post-MI, dilated cardiomyopathy
  • Mainstay: the four pillars — ARNI/ACEi, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i — with strong mortality benefit

💡 A normal EF is not a normal heart. The trap is treating HFpEF exactly like HFrEF: beta-blockers can be harmful if they over-slow a rate-dependent stiff ventricle, and RAAS/ARNI benefit is far weaker than in HFrEF.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set

  • 🧪 NT-proBNP (confirmed 1180); FBC, U&E + eGFR, glucose/HbA1c, LFTs, TFTs, ferritin/transferrin sat, lipids.
  • 📈 12-lead ECG — rhythm (AF), voltages (low voltage = amyloid flag), LVH, conduction; echo with full diastolic indices (E/e′, e′, LA volume index, TR velocity, wall thickness, GLS).
  • 💊 Loop diuretic (furosemide) titrated to relieve congestion — the immediate symptomatic priority.
  • 💙 SGLT2 inhibitor (empagliflozin 10 mg or dapagliflozin 10 mg) — start early; the evidence-based cornerstone (EMPEROR-Preserved / DELIVER).
  • 🩸 Comorbidity control — optimise blood-pressure treatment, AF rate/rhythm strategy, diabetes, CKD, obesity (consider GLP-1 / structured weight loss), sleep apnoea.
  • 🔎 Amyloid screen if red flags — serum free light chains + immunofixation, and ⁹⁹ᵐTc-DPD/PYP bone scintigraphy (see S20).
  • 🩻 CXR (congestion, cardiomegaly); daily weights; fluid/salt advice.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Every HFpEF patient now earns an SGLT2 inhibitor and a loop diuretic for congestion — then you win or lose the case on comorbidity control and on not missing amyloid."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Ward priorities — the nurse runs the decongestion & safety net

  • ⚖️ Daily weights, strict fluid balance — the most sensitive marker of diuretic response and congestion; a rising weight predicts decompensation.
  • 🫁 Monitor for over-diuresis: HFpEF patients are preload-dependent on a stiff ventricle — too much diuresis drops output, BP and renal function fast. Watch for postural dizziness and rising creatinine.
  • 💓 Rate matters: report tachycardia and fast AF — short diastole spikes filling pressure. Support rate-control administration and monitor for over-slowing.
  • 🩸 U&E surveillance on loop diuretic + MRA + SGLT2i — flag rising K⁺ (MRA) and any AKI.
  • 🍽️ Salt/fluid education, weight-management and activity advice — reframing "deconditioning" as a real cardiac condition the patient can act on.
  • 🔎 Ask and document red-flag symptoms — carpal tunnel surgery, numbness, postural faintness — that route toward amyloid work-up.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In HFpEF you diurese to comfort, not to dryness — the stiff ventricle needs its filling volume, and over-diuresis tips the patient into low output and AKI."

Atrial fibrillation with low QRS voltages despite thick walls on echo — the classic voltage/mass discordance of cardiac amyloidosis. A thick heart that makes small voltages is amyloid until proven otherwise.

🩻 Adjunct imaging

  • Cardiac MRI: tissue characterisation — LGE pattern, native T1 and extracellular volume (ECV) for amyloid/fibrosis and infiltration.
  • Bone scintigraphy (DPD/PYP): Grade 2–3 cardiac uptake with negative light chains = ATTR cardiac amyloid — a non-biopsy diagnosis that unlocks tafamidis.
  • Diastolic stress echo: E/e′ and TR velocity that climb on exercise confirm exertional raised filling pressures when resting studies are equivocal.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • ⚖️ Daily weight & congestion signs — the front-line marker of decompensation and diuretic titration.
  • 💓 Heart rate / rhythm — fast AF spikes filling pressure; but avoid over-slowing (rate-dependent filling). Track the AF strategy's effect on symptoms.
  • 🩸 U&E / K⁺ after starting SGLT2i, loop and MRA — expect a small early eGFR dip on SGLT2i; distinguish it from true AKI.
  • 🩺 Blood pressure — the main modifiable driver; target guideline BP control, but avoid symptomatic hypotension in a preload-dependent patient.
  • 🏃 Functional status (NYHA, KCCQ, 6-minute walk) — the real endpoints; HFpEF is a quality-of-life and hospitalisation disease.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Once you've called it HFpEF, the monitor's question is 'is she congested or over-diuresed, and is her rate and pressure controlled?' — the tightrope is euvolaemia in a stiff, preload-dependent heart."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong in HFpEF

  • 🌊 Acute decompensation / flash pulmonary oedema — a hypertensive surge or fast AF spikes filling pressure abruptly on the steep P–V curve.
  • 💓 Atrial fibrillation — both cause and consequence; loss of atrial kick and rapid rates precipitate congestion; raises stroke risk (anticoagulate per CHA₂DS₂-VASc).
  • 🫁 Pulmonary hypertension & right heart failure — chronic back-pressure → RV strain, tricuspid regurgitation, systemic congestion.
  • 🩸 Cardiorenal syndrome / over-diuresis AKI — the preload-dependent ventricle punishes aggressive diuresis with low output and rising creatinine.
  • 🧬 Progressive infiltrative disease (missed amyloid) — untreated ATTR marches to advanced restrictive failure, conduction disease and arrhythmia.
  • 🏥 The system complication: years of "deconditioning" mislabelling — the biggest "complication" is the diagnosis never made.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The deadliest HFpEF complication is the one on the chart, not the patient: a diagnosis deferred as 'deconditioning' — every year lost is a year of untreated congestion and a possibly missed amyloid window."

🏠 Discharge

📋 The HFpEF discharge vs the "reassure and send home" trap — two different worlds

🫀 This patient (confirmed HFpEF)

  • SGLT2 inhibitor, loop diuretic to euvolaemia, ± MRA; BP and AF (rate + anticoagulation) controlled

  • No peptide, no diastolic assessment, no score, no amyloid thought
  • The commonest reason HFpEF goes untreated for years

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the label

  • 🧬 HFpEF is a syndrome of phenotypes, not one disease: cardiometabolic / obese-HFpEF (volume expansion, inflammation, epicardial fat), hypertensive-hypertrophic, AF-driven, CKD/cardiorenal, pulmonary-hypertension-predominant, and the infiltrative/restrictive mimics — cardiac amyloid (ATTR & AL), hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, and constrictive pericarditis. Phenotyping guides therapy.
  • 🔬 Cardiac amyloid work-up: screen when red flags cluster — thick walls with low ECG voltage, apical-sparing (“cherry-on-top”) strain, bilateral carpal tunnel, autonomic/peripheral neuropathy, discordant wall thickening, aortic stenosis in an older man, intolerance of standard HF drugs. Serum/urine free light chains + immunofixation first; if negative and bone scintigraphy (DPD/PYP) is Grade 2–3 → non-biopsy diagnosis of ATTR-CM → tafamidis. Positive light chains → haematology for AL.
  • 📈 Exercise & invasive haemodynamics: the reference standard when scores are intermediate — resting PCWP > 15 mmHg, or exercise PCWP > 25 mmHg (or PCWP/CO slope) confirms HFpEF; diastolic stress echo (E/e′ and TR velocity on exercise) is the non-invasive surrogate.
  • 💙 Pharmacology in evolution: SGLT2i (EMPEROR-Preserved, DELIVER) are the established cornerstone; MRA has a signal (TOPCAT, Americas); ARNI narrowly missed (PARAGON-HF, benefit at lower-EF end / women); finerenone is under study in HFpEF.
  • ⚖️ Obesity as a treatment target: STEP-HFpEF — semaglutide improved symptoms, weight and function in obese HFpEF; obesity-phenotype HFpEF may be a distinct, modifiable entity. Structured weight loss and GLP-1 agents are now part of the plan.
  • 💓 AF in HFpEF: rate vs rhythm control matters — restoring/maintaining sinus rhythm (and the atrial kick) can dramatically improve symptoms; avoid over-slowing; anticoagulate per CHA₂DS₂-VASc; catheter ablation is increasingly considered in appropriate patients.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "Two Years of 'Deconditioned'"

The case: A 74-year-old obese, hypertensive woman presented repeatedly over two years with exertional breathlessness and ankle swelling. Each time the echo was reported as "EF 60% — normal" and she was told she was "deconditioned and overweight — lose weight and exercise." No natriuretic peptide was ever sent. Her ECG showed small QRS voltages; her echo showed thick LV walls; and she had had bilateral carpal tunnel operations and progressive numbness — never connected. By the time a new clinician recognised the pattern, bone scintigraphy confirmed ATTR cardiac amyloidosis with advanced infiltrative heart failure. She had missed the window in which tafamidis most helps, and progressed to recurrent HF hospitalisations.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Normal EF = normal heart"; congestion re-labelled as "deconditioning/obesity."
🧪 NO PEPTIDENT-proBNP never sent — the one test that would have reframed everything.
📈 ECG NOT READLow voltage beside thick echo walls — the amyloid signature — unrecognised.
🔎 RED FLAGS MISSEDBilateral carpal tunnel + neuropathy never linked to the heart.
⏳ WINDOW LOSTATTR diagnosed too late for tafamidis to alter the trajectory.

🛡️ What would have saved her: send a natriuretic peptide in any breathless, congested patient regardless of EF; read the echo's diastole and the ECG's voltages, not just the ejection fraction; treat "deconditioning" as a diagnosis of exclusion that requires objective testing; and connect carpal tunnel, neuropathy and thick-walled/low-voltage hearts to cardiac amyloid — because ATTR has a drug you don't want to miss. This is exactly the discipline that rescued the patient in Bed 3.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Deconditioned' has hidden more HFpEF — and more amyloid — than any other word in the notes. A normal EF is not a normal heart, and low voltage with thick walls is amyloid until proven otherwise."
