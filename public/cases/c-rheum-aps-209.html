🧭 The two questions APS forces you to ask at the bedside

First: is this venous, arterial, or both? APS is one of the few thrombophilias that causes arterial AND venous thrombosis plus pregnancy morbidity. Second: is it primary or secondary? Look hard for SLE — malar rash, arthritis, oral ulcers, cytopenias, nephritis — because ~30–40% of APS is secondary to lupus and that changes the whole management.

⚠️ The trap of the isolated eventAny single feature — a DVT, a miscarriage, a migraine, a low platelet count — is easy to explain away alone. APS only reveals itself when you step back and see the pattern of recurrent clotting across arteries, veins and the placenta. The diagnosis is made by the clinician who zooms out.

The medical registrar thinks out loud at 14:40:

🧠 Frame it as a syndrome, not a single clot

"She has an unprovoked VTE — that alone earns extended anticoagulation thinking. But add three fetal losses and a non-correcting APTT, and I am now working up antiphospholipid syndrome. I will send the full antibody panel today, before warfarin. I will keep her on LMWH now. And I will NOT reach for a DOAC in this patient, because if she turns out triple-positive, DOACs are inferior and dangerous."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🧪 Antiphospholipid panel — lupus anticoagulant (functional clotting assay), anticardiolipin IgG/IgM, anti-β2-glycoprotein-I IgG/IgM — drawn NOW, before warfarin muddies the LA assay.
  2. 🩸 Continue therapeutic LMWH for the acute PE/DVT; plan the transition to warfarin, not a DOAC.
  3. 🔬 Screen for SLE (ANA, dsDNA, complement, urinalysis, FBC) — primary vs secondary APS.
  4. 📅 Book the confirmatory repeat — antibodies must be persistently positive on a second sample ≥12 weeks apart to satisfy the criteria.
  5. 🤰 Take a full obstetric history and flag her for pre-conception/pregnancy planning (aspirin + LMWH next time).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Send the antibodies BEFORE the warfarin. Warfarin distorts the lupus anticoagulant assay and the acute thrombosis lowers anticardiolipin — get your samples while the getting is good."
🧪 Resident's self-testThe intern asks: "The APTT is prolonged — should we worry she will bleed, and hold anticoagulation?"

No — this is the great paradox of APS. The lupus anticoagulant prolongs phospholipid-dependent clotting tests in vitro (hence the misleading name "anticoagulant"), but in vivo it is powerfully PRO-thrombotic. She is not at risk of bleeding from it — she is at risk of clotting. Anticoagulate her.

S6

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY antibodies cause clots (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🎯 The target is a protein, not phospholipid alone: the key antigen is β2-glycoprotein-I, a plasma protein that binds anionic phospholipid on cell surfaces. Autoantibodies bind the β2GPI–phospholipid complex.
  2. ⚡ Cell activation: antibody binding activates endothelial cells, monocytes and platelets → up-regulation of tissue factor and adhesion molecules; platelets aggregate. The vessel wall is switched to a pro-coagulant surface.
  3. 🛑 Natural anticoagulants disabled: antibodies interfere with the protein C/S system, antithrombin, and annexin-A5 (the placental anticoagulant shield) → the brakes come off coagulation.
  4. 🔥 Complement + "second hit": complement activation amplifies inflammation and thrombosis. A trigger (infection, surgery, pregnancy, stopping anticoagulation) can tip a stable state into widespread clotting.
  5. 🤰 Placental mechanism: thrombosis of the placental vessels PLUS direct antibody-mediated impairment of trophoblast/annexin-A5 → placental insufficiency → recurrent loss.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"APS is a state where the blood is told to clot and the natural brakes are cut. That is why it hits arteries, veins AND the placenta — no other thrombophilia does all three."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Inherited thrombophilia vs APS

🧬 Inherited (e.g. factor V Leiden)

  • Congenital, lifelong; family history common
  • Predominantly VENOUS thrombosis
  • DOACs are appropriate
  • Does not, by itself, cause recurrent late miscarriage or arterial stroke

🩸 APS (acquired autoimmune)

  • Acquired; autoantibody-driven; may be secondary to SLE
  • ARTERIAL + VENOUS + pregnancy morbidity
  • WARFARIN, not DOACs (esp. triple-positive/arterial)
  • Can escalate to catastrophic multi-organ APS

💡 The single most useful discriminator at the bedside: inherited thrombophilia clots veins; APS clots everything and empties pregnancies.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (14:45)

  • 🩸 Therapeutic LMWH (e.g. enoxaparin 1 mg/kg BD) for the acute PE/DVT — continue while the diagnosis is secured; do NOT default to a DOAC.
  • 🧪 Antiphospholipid panel NOW — lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin IgG/IgM, anti-β2-glycoprotein-I IgG/IgM — before warfarin is started.
  • 🔬 SLE screen — ANA, anti-dsDNA, ENA, C3/C4, FBC (thrombocytopenia?), U&E + urinalysis (nephritis?).
  • 🩹 Baseline coagulation — APTT, PT/INR, mixing study, fibrinogen; note the non-correcting APTT for haematology.
  • 📈 Repeat SpO₂/RR obs, consider RV assessment (echo/CTPA severity) given hypoxia; risk-stratify the PE (sPESI).
  • 📅 Book confirmatory antibody repeat at ≥12 weeks; refer to rheumatology and haematology (anticoagulation clinic).
  • 🤰 Document full obstetric history; flag for pre-conception counselling (aspirin + LMWH in any future pregnancy).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Draw the antibodies before the warfarin, anticoagulate with LMWH now, and book the 12-week repeat the same day — the diagnosis you fail to confirm is the one that recurs."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Ward priorities — the nurse runs the safety net

  • 📟 Monitor for PE deterioration — rising HR/RR, falling SpO₂ or BP signal RV strain and possible clot progression; escalate early.
  • 💉 Give LMWH on time and hold nothing for a "high APTT" — teach the team that the lupus anticoagulant does not cause bleeding; the risk here is clotting.
  • 🧪 Time and label the antibody bloods precisely, and ensure they were drawn before warfarin — a mis-timed sample can void the whole diagnosis.
  • 🧠 Neuro-obs / stroke awareness — APS is an arterial disease too; report any new focal weakness, dysphasia, or visual change immediately.
  • 🤰 Handle the pregnancy-loss history with care — this is a grief as well as a diagnosis; a compassionate bedside builds the trust needed for lifelong anticoagulation.
  • 🩹 Watch for bruising/bleeding once anticoagulated; educate on INR clinic attendance and the danger of missing doses.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who refuses to withhold anticoagulation for a 'scary' APTT — and who watches for the arterial event as closely as the venous one — is the safest person on this ward."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🫁 PE physiology — HR, RR, SpO₂, BP; a normotensive PE can become high-risk if the RV fails. Escalate on deterioration.
  • 🩸 INR once on warfarin — target 2–3; a subtherapeutic INR in triple-positive APS is a recurrence waiting to happen. This is why DOAC "convenience" is so seductive and so dangerous.
  • 🧠 New arterial events — focal neurology, visual loss, chest pain; APS clots arteries as readily as veins.
  • 🩹 Platelets — APS thrombocytopenia can coexist; do not misread it as a reason to stop anticoagulation (it does not protect from clotting).
  • 🚑 Warning signs of CATASTROPHIC APS — rapid multi-organ dysfunction (renal, pulmonary, cerebral, cardiac) over days; recognise it as an emergency (S13).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Once she is anticoagulated, the monitor's question becomes: is the INR truly in range, and is any NEW territory — brain, kidney, lung — beginning to clot? Sub-therapeutic anticoagulation in triple-positive APS is a stroke in waiting."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — with CATASTROPHIC APS at the top

🚨 Catastrophic APS (CAPS) — a medical emergencyRapid (days) multi-organ microthrombosis — kidneys, lungs, brain, heart, skin — with a mortality historically ~50%. Often triggered by infection, surgery, or stopping anticoagulation. Treatment is aggressive and combined: full anticoagulation + high-dose corticosteroids + plasma exchange and/or IVIG, treat the trigger, and consider rituximab/eculizumab in refractory cases.
  • 🧠 Arterial thrombosis — ischaemic stroke, TIA, MI (young); recurrent despite treatment if under-anticoagulated.
  • 🫁 Recurrent VTE / chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension after repeated PE.
  • 🤰 Pregnancy morbidity — recurrent loss, severe pre-eclampsia, HELLP, placental insufficiency, prematurity.
  • 🩸 Thrombocytopenia and haemolytic anaemia — cytopenias that complicate the picture.
  • 🫀 Libman–Sacks endocarditis — sterile valve vegetations, a cardio-embolic stroke source.
  • 💊 Bleeding from anticoagulation, and recurrence from under-treatment — the tightrope of APS.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"If an APS patient deteriorates across several organs in days, think CATASTROPHIC APS and act fast — anticoagulate, steroids, and plasma exchange/IVIG. Waiting for a tidy diagnosis loses the patient."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🩸 Acute: therapeutic LMWH for the PE/DVT; risk-stratify the PE; supportive O₂ for hypoxia.
  2. 💊 Long-term anticoagulation: transition to warfarin, target INR 2–3, indefinitely (unprovoked VTE + confirmed APS). No DOAC.
  3. 🧪 Confirm the diagnosis: repeat the antibody panel at ≥12 weeks; document triple-positivity and primary-vs-secondary status.
  4. 🤰 Reproductive planning: counsel on aspirin + LMWH for any future pregnancy; avoid oestrogen contraception; hydroxychloroquine consideration.
  5. 🏥 Multidisciplinary follow-up: rheumatology + haematology (anticoagulation clinic) + obstetric medicine; SLE surveillance.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Confirmed thrombotic APS after an unprovoked VTE means warfarin INR 2–3, usually for life — and the discipline to keep it there is the treatment. Do not let a DOAC or a stopped tablet undo the diagnosis you worked so hard to make."

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🧮 Risk stratification with the aGAPSS (adjusted Global APS Score): integrates the antibody profile with cardiovascular risk factors to quantify thrombotic risk and guide primary prophylaxis in asymptomatic carriers.
  • 🩸 Recurrent thrombosis on therapeutic warfarin: options include raising the INR target (2.5–3.5), adding low-dose aspirin or hydroxychloroquine, or switching to LMWH — an area of genuine uncertainty; DOACs are NOT the answer.
  • 🧬 "Seronegative APS" and non-criteria antibodies: anti-phosphatidylserine/prothrombin (aPS/PT), anti-domain-I β2GPI, and IgA isotypes may identify clinically classic patients who are negative on standard criteria assays.
  • 🔥 Complement in APS: mechanistic overlap with complement-mediated thrombotic microangiopathy underpins the use of eculizumab in refractory catastrophic APS.
  • 🤰 Refractory obstetric APS: beyond aspirin + LMWH — hydroxychloroquine, low-dose prednisolone in the first trimester, and IVIG in selected cases; pravastatin has emerging evidence in placental insufficiency.
  • 🫀 APS beyond thrombosis: Libman–Sacks endocarditis, APS nephropathy (thrombotic microangiopathy), livedoid vasculopathy, cognitive dysfunction and diffuse alveolar haemorrhage — the "non-criteria" manifestations that broaden the disease.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Convenient Tablet"

The case: A 37-year-old woman had confirmed triple-positive thrombotic APS after an unprovoked DVT/PE, stable for two years on warfarin with an INR in range. Tired of monthly blood tests, she asked her GP for "one of the new tablets that does not need monitoring." Without checking her antibody profile or the guidelines, she was switched to rivaroxaban "for convenience." Ten weeks later she developed sudden right-sided weakness and expressive dysphasia — a large middle-cerebral-artery territory ischaemic stroke. She was left with a disabling hemiparesis. The TRAPS trial had shown, years earlier, exactly this excess of arterial events on a DOAC in triple-positive APS.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Stable clot, low fuss" framing hid a persistently high-risk disease.
📄 GUIDELINE IGNOREDTRAPS/EULAR: avoid DOACs in triple-positive/arterial APS — not applied.
🔬 PROFILE NOT CHECKEDTriple-positivity — the exact high-risk group — was never reviewed before switching.
⚖️ CONVENIENCE OVER EVIDENCEAvoiding INR checks was allowed to outweigh a proven mortality/morbidity signal.
🗣️ NO SHARED DECISIONRisks of the switch were never explained; no rheumatology input sought.

🛡️ What would have saved her: know that thrombotic APS — especially triple-positive or arterial — stays on WARFARIN; treat "for convenience" as a red flag, not a reason; check the antibody profile and the guideline before ANY anticoagulant change; and involve rheumatology/haematology in the decision. This is exactly the discipline that protected the patient in Bed 9.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'For convenience' has caused more strokes in APS than it has ever prevented blood tests. In triple-positive thrombotic APS the answer is warfarin — TRAPS proved the alternative harms."
