🚦 Why SCAD is a different animal

Spontaneous coronary artery dissection (SCAD) is a non-atherosclerotic, non-traumatic separation of the coronary artery wall. It causes 1–4% of all acute MI overall, but up to 25–35% of MI in women under 50, and is the leading cause of pregnancy-associated MI. The diagnosis is easy to miss precisely because these patients lack the classic risk-factor story — and the treatment reflexes learned for atherosclerotic STEMI can be actively harmful here.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A young woman, few risk factors, peripartum or after intense stress, with a smooth tapering artery and no plaque — think SCAD, and think TWICE before you wire it."

📋 Pre-Procedure Workup & Planning

🧾 The pre-angiography mindset in a suspected SCAD

  • 🧠 Have the diagnosis in mind BEFORE the catheter engages. If the story fits SCAD, plan for the gentlest possible diagnostic study and a low threshold to STOP at diagnosis — do not drift into reflex intervention.
  • 💊 Antithrombotic caution: aspirin is reasonable; potent dual antiplatelet loading and full anticoagulation are debated in SCAD (an intramural haematoma is a bruise in the wall, not a luminal clot — aggressive antithrombotics can theoretically worsen bleeding). Load thoughtfully, not reflexively.
  • 🚫 Do NOT give thrombolysis. If the mechanism is a dissection with intramural haematoma, lytics can propagate the dissection and cause catastrophic extension. This is a core SCAD safety rule.
  • 🫘 Renal & contrast planning: minimise contrast and forceful injections — a hard hand injection can itself extend a dissection.
  • 🧬 Set up downstream screening: flag the patient for head-to-pelvis imaging for fibromuscular dysplasia (FMD) and note connective-tissue features.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In SCAD the artery is bruised, not clogged. Thrombolysis and rough injections do not open it — they tear it further."

🩸 Vascular Access & Setup

🖐️ Radial vs femoral — and a SCAD-specific caveat

🖐️ Radial (default)

  • Fewer access-site bleeds & vascular complications
  • Lower mortality in ACS generally
  • Early ambulation, comfort — useful in a young patient

🧬 SCAD caveat: fragile arteries

  • SCAD associates with FMD and arteriopathy — radial/iliac vessels can be tortuous, spastic, or themselves prone to iatrogenic dissection
  • Engage gently; catheter-induced dissection of an already-fragile aorta/coronary is a real hazard
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The SCAD patient has fragile arteries everywhere. Every catheter movement should be gentle — you can dissect the very vessel you are trying to diagnose."

💉 Guide catheter & the gentle-engagement principle

  • 🔧 Diagnostic-first mindset: use a diagnostic catheter for the study; do NOT reflexively swap to an aggressive guide catheter unless you have committed to intervention for high-risk anatomy.
  • 🕊️ Non-aggressive engagement: avoid deep-seating or forceful guide manipulation — catheter-induced dissection can propagate an existing SCAD retrograde into the left main.
  • 🧭 Guide choice if you must intervene: for the LAD (left system), a supportive left guide (EBU/XB) — but recognise that support that helps in atherosclerotic PCI can also transmit force that extends a dissection.
  • 🩸 Anticoagulation: heparin to a working ACT is standard for instrumentation, but keep the overall antithrombotic footprint conservative given the bruised-wall mechanism.

🔬 Lesion Assessment — Physiology & Imaging

🫀 The mechanism — the WHY behind the WHYIn SCAD, a tear in the intima and/or bleeding from the vasa vasorum creates an intramural haematoma in the medial layer — a false lumen. Because the false lumen is a pressurised pocket in the wall, it compresses the true lumen. Any force that adds to that pocket — a wire in the wrong plane, a balloon, a rough injection, thrombolysis — can propagate the haematoma up and down the vessel: the vicious cycle of SCAD propagation. This single mechanism is why the treatment philosophy inverts: leave it alone and it usually heals; poke it and it can extend.

⚠️ Physiology & imaging in SCAD — help vs harm

  • Pressure wire (FFR/iFR): the numbers below teach the general ischaemia threshold, but in an acute SCAD passing a pressure wire risks entering the false lumen and extending the dissection. Physiology is reserved for ambiguous non-SCAD/bystander lesions or healed segments — not the acute dissection.
  • OCT / IVUS: can confirm the diagnosis (intramural haematoma, double lumen, intimal flap) when angiography is ambiguous (especially Type 3) — but use with great caution: the imaging catheter itself, and the contrast flush for OCT, can extend the dissection. Image only if it will change management.

🖥️ Intravascular imaging — IVUS vs OCT in SCAD

🔵 IVUS

  • Ultrasound — deeper penetration, sees the full vessel wall and the intramural haematoma
  • No contrast flush needed (a safety advantage in a fragile vessel)

🟠 OCT

  • Light — ultra-high resolution; superb for the intimal flap, double lumen, and haematoma that confirm SCAD
  • Needs a contrast flush → an additional propagation risk; reserve for when it changes management
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In acute SCAD, the safest wire is the one you never pass. Image only to answer a question that changes management — and even then, gently."

🛠️ Intervention — When It IS Required, Step-by-Step

⚠️ Read this firstFor most SCAD the "intervention" is conservative management (S10). This section is what to do only when high-risk criteria force your hand: ongoing ischaemia, haemodynamic instability, VT/VF, left-main or proximal-vessel dissection with threatened flow. PCI in SCAD has lower success and higher complication rates than atherosclerotic PCI — go in knowing it is hard.
🎈 Stenting — a standard tool in atherosclerotic PCI, but a high-risk, last-resort manoeuvre in SCAD. Source: Wikimedia Commons (Blausen)

🪜 The cautious SCAD-PCI sequence (only if truly indicated)

  1. 🧵 Wire the TRUE lumen — and prove it. The cardinal error is wiring the false lumen. Confirm distal true-lumen position with imaging (OCT/IVUS) and/or contralateral injection before you do anything else.
  2. 🩹 Seal the entry / cover long. Consider cutting-balloon fenestration of the haematoma or stenting the entry point; dissections extend beyond the visible narrowing, so plan longer stents / cover both ends into healthy segments.
  3. 🫧 Gentle everything. Low-pressure, minimal manipulation — every extra push risks extending the haematoma proximally toward the left main.
  4. 🖥️ Image to confirm true-lumen stenting and adequate coverage — under-recognised haematoma at the stent edges re-obstructs.
  5. 🫀 Escalate early if it fails. If PCI cannot restore flow to a large jeopardised territory (especially left main / proximal LAD), consider CABG as a bail-out and mechanical support (Impella/IABP/ECMO) for instability.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"If you must stent a SCAD: prove you are in the true lumen, cover long into healthy vessel at both ends, and be gentle — because the dissection is longer than the picture shows."

🏠 Post-Procedure Care, Debrief & Assessment

🛌 Conservative management — the active treatment

  • 📟 Monitored admission 3–5 days. Early SCAD can propagate or re-infarct in the first days — telemetry and observation are the real intervention. Do not discharge too early.
  • 💊 Beta-blocker — associated with reduced recurrence; continue long-term. Treat hypertension.
  • 💊 Antiplatelets — aspirin is commonly continued; the duration/role of DAPT is individualised and debated (no routine prolonged potent DAPT as in atherosclerotic ACS).
  • 🚫 Statin NOT routine unless another indication — SCAD is not an atherosclerotic disease; do not reflexively start high-intensity statin.
  • 🏃 Cardiac rehab — dedicated SCAD-aware rehab improves outcomes and addresses the substantial anxiety/PTSD burden in these young patients.

🧬 The screen that must not be forgotten — FMD, head to pelvis

Up to ~50–80% of SCAD patients have fibromuscular dysplasia (FMD) somewhere in the arterial tree. Screen head-to-pelvis (CT or MR angiography of brain-to-pelvis) for FMD, aneurysms, and dissections. Consider connective-tissue and screening for associated arteriopathies.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"SCAD is treated with time, a beta-blocker, and a search for FMD from head to pelvis — not with a statin and a stent."

🔭 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

  • 🧬 Predisposing arteriopathies: FMD (most common association), connective-tissue disorders (Marfan, Loeys–Dietz, vascular Ehlers–Danlos), systemic inflammatory disease, and a growing list of genetic loci — SCAD is increasingly seen as an arteriopathy, not a coincidence.
  • ⚡ Precipitants: hormonal (pregnancy, postpartum, exogenous hormones), intense emotional or physical stress, Valsalva, sympathomimetics — the "hormonal + shear stress" hit on a fragile wall.
  • 🩻 Diagnostic pitfalls: Type 3 SCAD mimics atherosclerosis and Type 4 mimics thrombus; low-threshold OCT/IVUS (used gently) unmasks the intramural haematoma and intimal flap when the angiogram is ambiguous.
  • 🔧 PCI techniques when forced: cutting-balloon fenestration to decompress the haematoma, long single or overlapping stents covering both ends, bioresorbable-scaffold debate, and the deliberate distal-first true-lumen wiring strategy.
  • 🫀 Mechanical support & surgery: Impella/IABP/VA-ECMO for instability; CABG as a bail-out for failed PCI or left-main SCAD (though grafts to a healing native vessel may later compete/occlude).
  • 🔁 Recurrence & long-term: significant recurrence risk (roughly 10–30% over years), often in a different vessel; pregnancy counseling, avoidance of triggers, beta-blockade, BP control, and SCAD-specific psychological support.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The SCAD That Was Reflexively Stented"

The case: A 38-year-old woman, 6 weeks postpartum, presented with an anterior NSTEMI. Angiography showed a long, smooth, tapering mid-LAD with preserved TIMI-3 distal flow and no atheroma — a textbook Type 2 SCAD. She was pain-free and stable. Running the atherosclerotic STEMI reflex, the operator wired the lesion and inflated a balloon. The wire had tracked into the false lumen; the balloon propagated the intramural haematoma proximally into the left main. The LAD and LM abruptly closed. Emergency stenting failed to restore flow, she went into cardiogenic shock, and despite a bail-out CABG she suffered a large anterior infarct with severe LV dysfunction. A stable, self-healing dissection became a devastated ventricle.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 WRONG MENTAL MODELTreated a dissection as if it were an atherosclerotic occlusion.
🛑 IGNORED PRESERVED FLOWStable patient with TIMI-3 distal flow — the exact profile to leave alone.
🧵 FALSE-LUMEN WIRINGWire position was never confirmed with imaging before ballooning.
🎈 BALLOON PROPAGATIONInflation extended the haematoma retrograde into the left main.
⚡ REFLEX OVER RESTRAINTNo pause to ask "does this SCAD actually need intervention?"

🛡️ What would have saved her: recognise SCAD from the smooth taper and the low-risk young postpartum profile; because she had preserved flow and was stable, choose conservative management — monitored admission, beta-blocker, FMD screening, and time. Most SCAD heals. The intervention that harmed her was the one that never should have started. This is the drill in S9.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A stable SCAD with preserved flow is a trap for the reflex hand. Before you wire, ask: does this dissection NEED me — or does it just need time?"
