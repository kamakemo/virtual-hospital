🌀 The shock spiral — the vicious cycle you must break

Cardiogenic shock is primary pump failure: a large infarct drops stroke volume → low cardiac output → systemic and coronary hypoperfusion → more ischaemia and a systemic inflammatory response → the myocardium works worse → output falls further. Left alone, this spiral accelerates. Pressors buy blood pressure but raise afterload and myocardial oxygen demand — they feed the spiral as much as they fight it. The only durable exits are reperfusing the culprit and, when the native pump cannot cope, mechanically unloading and supporting it.

📋 Pre-Procedure Workup & Planning

🧾 The shock pre-PCI checklist (do NOT delay reperfusion — plan support in parallel)

  • ✍️ Consent — emergency/verbal; document the added risks of mechanical support (limb ischaemia, bleeding, haemolysis, stroke, death). The family needs the shock context, not just the stent.
  • 📟 Haemodynamic monitoring — arterial line + PA (Swan-Ganz) catheter to measure cardiac output, cardiac power output (CPO = MAP × CO / 451) and PAPi (RV function). These numbers pick the device and track the spiral.
  • 🫁 Airway & oxygenation — many shock patients need intubation; secure the airway early rather than mid-crash on the table.
  • 🩸 Access mapping — shock usually means femoral for the large-bore support device; ultrasound-guide every large stick and map both groins (one for the pump, one bail-out).
  • 🧪 Baselines — lactate, ABG, renal function, haemoglobin/platelets, fibrinogen, group-and-save; a rising lactate is your fastest shock trend.
  • 📞 Activate the shock team — interventionalist, intensivist, and (early) cardiac surgery / ECMO team; regionalised "shock team" pathways improve survival.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In shock you are running two clocks at once: time-to-reperfusion AND time-to-support. Decide the device BEFORE the pressure hits zero — escalation planned late is escalation that fails."

🩸 Vascular Access & Setup

🖐️ Radial for the coronary, femoral for the pump — the shock exception

🖐️ Radial (for the PCI)

  • Fewer access-site bleeds — still preferred for the diagnostic/PCI catheter when feasible
  • In deep shock the radial pulse is often impalpable and spasm is common → a low threshold to go femoral

🦵 Femoral (for large-bore support)

  • Mandatory for Impella / IABP / VA-ECMO — these need 13–19 Fr arterial access
  • Ultrasound-guided, front-wall, mid-common-femoral puncture; pre-close with suture devices
  • Higher bleeding & limb-ischaemia risk — the price of mechanical support
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Map the groin like your patient's leg depends on it — because it does. A high stick bleeds into the retroperitoneum; a low stick and a big sheath starve the foot."

💉 Large-bore access, anticoagulation & the support-device footprint

  • 🔧 Sheath/pump sizes: IABP ~7–8 Fr; Impella CP ~14 Fr; Impella 5.5 / VA-ECMO arterial cannulae 19–21 Fr. The bigger the device, the bigger the bleeding and limb-ischaemia footprint.
  • 🩸 Procedural anticoagulation: unfractionated heparin (titrate to ACT ~250 s for PCI, then a device-specific purge/heparin strategy) or bivalirudin. Impella runs a heparinised/bicarbonate purge; VA-ECMO needs a systemic anticoagulation target — balance clotting against bleeding.
  • 🦶 Distal-perfusion planning: for large arterial cannulae, plan an antegrade distal perfusion catheter to protect the limb; check the foot before, during and after.
  • 🧭 Guide catheter: for the LAD (left system) → an EBU/XB left guide; keep support and coronary equipment on separate access sites.

🔬 Lesion Assessment — Physiology, Haemodynamics & Imaging

🫀 In shock, the numbers that matter are haemodynamicThe culprit is an acutely occluded vessel — you do NOT FFR it. Reperfuse it now. But in shock you add a second layer of assessment: cardiac power output (CPO) and PAPi from the PA catheter tell you how deep the shock is and whether the RIGHT ventricle is failing too. Physiology (FFR/iFR) belongs only to the non-culprit lesions — and CULPRIT-SHOCK tells you to defer those acutely anyway.

📟 The shock haemodynamics you must be able to quote

  • CPO (cardiac power output) = MAP × CO / 451 (watts). CPO < 0.6 W is the strongest haemodynamic predictor of mortality in cardiogenic shock. Our patient sits at ~0.5 W.
  • PAPi (pulmonary artery pulsatility index) = (PA systolic − PA diastolic) / RA pressure. A low PAPi (< 0.9–1.0) flags right-ventricular failure — critical, because isolated LV support (Impella) can unmask a failing RV.
  • Cardiac index, PCWP, RA pressure, SvO₂ — together they classify the shock (wet/cold) and track the response to reperfusion and support.

📏 FFR / iFR — physiology of the NON-culprit (defer it in acute shock)

  • FFR (fractional flow reserve) — pressure-wire ratio during hyperaemia. FFR ≤ 0.80 = ischaemic. In shock, use it to plan STAGED treatment of bystander lesions later — not to justify treating them tonight.
  • iFR (instantaneous wave-free ratio) — resting index, no adenosine. iFR ≤ 0.89 = significant. Hyperaemic agents are poorly tolerated in shock, so resting indices are gentler if you must assess.

🎯 FFR ≤ 0.80 → flow-limiting → plan STAGED revascularisation once out of shock. > 0.80 → defer. Remember: in the acute shock setting the answer is culprit-only regardless (CULPRIT-SHOCK).

🖥️ Intravascular imaging — IVUS vs OCT

🔵 IVUS

  • Ultrasound — deep penetration, sizes the vessel, sees behind calcium, no contrast needed (kinder to the shocked kidney)
  • Useful for left-main assessment and rapid stent sizing when time and dye are precious

🟠 OCT

  • Light — ultra-high resolution; superb for edge dissection, thrombus, apposition, and plaque rupture vs erosion vs SCAD
  • Needs a contrast flush — used more selectively in the unstable shock patient
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Do not FFR the culprit — it is occluded and it is killing him. In shock, the wire you care about is the PA catheter: CPO and PAPi decide the device and predict survival."

🛠️ Intervention — Step-by-Step Execution

🎈 Balloon angioplasty & stent deployment — but in shock the stent is only half the job; the pump must be supported too. Source: Wikimedia Commons (Blausen)

🪜 The sequence — reperfuse the culprit AND support the pump

  1. 🧵 Wire the LAD — a workhorse guidewire across the occlusion into the distal true lumen (confirm distal position in two views).
  2. 🫧 Restore flow — cross with a small balloon (aspiration only for heavy thrombus — routine aspiration is not beneficial); even a whiff of flow starts to lift the ischaemia driving the shock.
  3. 🔧 Place mechanical support if the pump is failing — position the Impella across the aortic valve (pigtail in the LV, inlet in the LV, outlet in the aorta; confirm on fluoro/echo), or an IABP in the descending aorta, before or during the PCI as haemodynamics demand.
  4. 📏 Lesion prep & sizing — predilate; size the DES 1:1 to the reference vessel (IVUS if dye must be minimised).
  5. 🩹 Deploy a drug-eluting stent to the culprit only; cover the lesion with healthy landing zones.
  6. 🎈 Post-dilate with a non-compliant balloon to full expansion/apposition — under-expansion is the top cause of stent failure.
  7. 📟 Re-measure the shock — recheck CPO/PAPi, lactate trend, and pressor requirement; decide whether to escalate or wean support.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Reperfuse the culprit AND unload the ventricle — a perfect stent in a heart that cannot pump is a beautiful angiogram over a dying patient. Fix the pipe and support the pump."

🏠 Post-Procedure Care, Debrief & Assessment

🏥 The shock patient goes to CICU, not the ward

  • 📟 Continuous haemodynamic monitoring — PA catheter for CPO/PAPi trends; serial lactate; arterial line; hourly urine output. Wean pressors as the native heart recovers.
  • 🦶 Support-device rounds — check the access limb (pulses, colour, temperature), plasma-free Hb/LDH (haemolysis), device position on echo, purge pressures, and anticoagulation targets, at least every shift.
  • 🪜 Escalation / de-escalation plan — a written ladder: when to add RV support or VA-ECMO, and the daily wean-and-remove criteria (rising CPO, falling lactate, recovering echo).

💊 Secondary prevention (once out of the shock phase)

  • DAPT — aspirin + a potent P2Y₁₂ inhibitor; balance against the high bleeding risk of large-bore support.
  • High-intensity statin, and — once haemodynamically stable — ACE inhibitor/ARNI, beta-blocker, MRA, and SGLT2 inhibitor for the post-MI reduced-EF heart.
  • 🏃 Advanced-HF review — a large anterior infarct with shock often leaves a low EF; consider wearable defibrillator, ICD reassessment at ≥ 40 days, cardiac rehab, and (if it does not recover) transplant/durable LVAD referral.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Getting them off the table alive is act one. Act two is weaning the support without letting the spiral restart — de-escalate on evidence (CPO up, lactate down, echo recovering), not on the clock."

🔭 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

  • 🫀 Device physiology: IABP augments diastolic coronary flow & reduces afterload (modest, ~0.5 L/min); Impella actively pumps LV→aorta (1–5 L/min) and directly UNLOADS the LV; VA-ECMO provides full cardiopulmonary support but RAISES LV afterload.
  • 🔀 LV venting on VA-ECMO ("ECpella"): peripheral VA-ECMO increases LV afterload → LV distension, pulmonary oedema, stasis/thrombus. Add an LV vent — Impella (ECpella) or IABP — to unload the distended ventricle.
  • 🩻 Differential hypoxia / north–south (Harlequin) syndrome: with peripheral VA-ECMO, recovering native LV ejects deoxygenated blood to the upper body while oxygenated ECMO blood perfuses the lower — monitor RIGHT-arm (pre-ductal) saturations and the oxygenator.
  • 🫀 RV failure & PAPi: a low PAPi predicts RV failure; isolated LV support can unmask it. Options: Impella RP, ProtekDuo, or veno-arterial ECMO for biventricular collapse.
  • 🦶 Vascular protection: ultrasound-guided access, distal perfusion catheters, pre-close techniques, and daily limb checks reduce the limb-ischaemia and bleeding toll of large-bore support.
  • 🧭 Escalation & futility: a stepwise ladder (optimise medical therapy → temporary MCS → escalate/ECMO), hemometabolic markers (lactate clearance, CPO), and honest ceilings-of-care/transplant-eligibility conversations for the heart that will not recover.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Shock That Waited for the Whole Tree"

The case: A 71-year-old arrived in anterior STEMI with SBP 82 and a lactate of 4 — classic cardiogenic shock. The angiogram showed a proximal LAD occlusion plus significant RCA and LCx disease. Rather than open the culprit and support the pump, the operator elected an immediate complete multivessel PCI — LAD, then RCA, then LCx — a long procedure with heavy contrast. Mechanical support was "considered but deferred" while the pressure was chased with escalating noradrenaline and then adrenaline. Ninety minutes in, the LAD stent was patent but the patient was mottled to the waist, the lactate was 9, and the CPO was 0.35 W. The Impella was finally called for as the rhythm degenerated. He arrested on the table and could not be brought back.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🎯 MULTIVESSEL IN SHOCKImmediate complete PCI ignored CULPRIT-SHOCK — more contrast, more time, higher death/renal-replacement.
⏱️ DELAYED SUPPORTMechanical support was deferred until arrest — escalation planned late is escalation that fails.
💉 PRESSOR SPIRALChasing pressure with escalating pressors raised afterload and O₂ demand, feeding the spiral.
📟 NO CPO TRENDNo invasive haemodynamics; the collapsing CPO/rising lactate were not tracked until too late.
📞 NO SHOCK TEAMNo pre-agreed escalation ladder or early ECMO activation; the team improvised under pressure.

🛡️ What would have saved him: open the CULPRIT LAD ONLY, place mechanical support EARLY (before the CPO fell off a cliff), track CPO and lactate rather than chasing blood pressure with ever-more pressor, and activate the shock team with an ECMO ceiling agreed in advance. Reperfuse the pipe, unload the pump, and stage the rest — restraint and early support beat heroics and late escalation. This is the drill in S6 and S9.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In shock, the two lethal instincts are to fix EVERY vessel and to wait on support. Do the opposite: culprit-only, support early, escalate on the CPO and the lactate — not on the systolic."

🎯 The five things to carry out of this case

  1. 🌀 Recognise the spiral early — SCAI stage the patient; shock is failing perfusion (lactate, CPO), not a low systolic alone.
  2. 🎯 Culprit-only in shock — CULPRIT-SHOCK: open the culprit, stage the rest; resist immediate multivessel PCI.
  3. 🔧 Match the device to the evidence — no routine IABP (IABP-SHOCK II); Impella for selected STEMI shock (DanGer Shock); VA-ECMO only for refractory (ECLS-SHOCK).
  4. ⚡ Support early, track CPO & lactate — escalate before the CPO collapses; wean pressors as support takes over.
  5. 🦶 Respect the tradeoffs — guard the limb, watch for haemolysis/bleeding, and beware RV failure unmasked by LV support.
