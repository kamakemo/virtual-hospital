🚦 Why this lesion is different — the bifurcation problem

A coronary bifurcation is where a main vessel divides and gives off a side branch of meaningful size. Roughly 15–20% of all PCIs involve one. The trouble is mechanical: when you stent the main vessel across the origin of the side branch, you can shift plaque and shift the carina (the flow divider) into the side-branch ostium — pinching or occluding a branch that was open a minute ago. The bigger the branch and the more diseased its ostium, the higher the stakes.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Every bifurcation is a trap for the side branch: stent the main vessel and the carina shifts. Keep provisional simple, protect the branch you cannot afford to lose, and only add metal when the branch earns it."

📋 Pre-Procedure Workup & Planning

🧾 Planning a bifurcation before you touch the wire

  • 🗺️ Define the anatomy first — main-vessel reference diameters (proximal and distal segments differ — remember they are NOT equal), side-branch diameter, ostial SB disease, and the bifurcation angle. This is what drives provisional vs 2-stent.
  • 🔱 Classify it — apply the Medina classification (proximal main, distal main, side branch → each 1 if diseased). A true bifurcation = the side branch is diseased (1,1,1 / 0,1,1 / 1,0,1).
  • 🛡️ Decide the default — provisional single-stent (stent the main vessel, keep the option to treat the SB) is the default for the vast majority. Plan an upfront 2-stent technique only for specific complex true bifurcations (large SB, long/severe ostial SB disease, unfavourable re-access).
  • 🧰 Kit check — two guidewires (main + side-branch protection), appropriately sized stent, short non-compliant balloons for POT and kissing, and a mental algorithm for bail-out to a 2-stent technique.
  • 💊 Antithrombotics — DAPT loaded (aspirin + potent P2Y₁₂); procedural anticoagulation as in S3; complex/longer bifurcation work means more contrast and more time — plan for both.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Name the bifurcation before you cross it: main-vessel proximal and distal diameters are different, and the Medina number tells you whether the side branch is in play. Anatomy chosen the strategy long before the stent did."

🩸 Vascular Access & Setup

🖐️ Radial vs femoral — and the extra support a bifurcation needs

🖐️ Radial (default)

  • Fewer access-site bleeds & vascular complications; lower mortality in ACS (MATRIX)
  • Comfortable for a longer, two-wire bifurcation case
  • Confirm patency first (Barbeau/Allen); consider distal radial

🦵 Femoral (when you need more)

  • Bigger-bore support for demanding 2-stent work, complex re-access, or if radial fails/spasms
  • Higher bleeding — micropuncture + ultrasound guidance, avoid a high/low stick
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A bifurcation is a two-wire, sometimes two-stent job — set up for support and space. Radial-first, but pick a guide that will still deliver a second stent and a kissing balloon when the plan changes mid-case."

💉 Guide, sheath & the room to work

  • 🔧 Sheath: 6 Fr radial handles provisional and most 2-stent techniques; a 7 Fr (radial or femoral) gives room for simultaneous two-stent delivery (e.g., DK crush, culotte) and comfortable kissing balloons.
  • 🧭 Guide catheter: for the LAD/diagonal (left system) → a supportive EBU/XB left guide. Good back-up support lets you deliver a second stent and re-cross without losing position.
  • 🩸 Anticoagulation: unfractionated heparin (70–100 U/kg, ACT ~250–300 s) or bivalirudin. A longer bifurcation case means re-checking the ACT.
  • 🧵 Wires: a workhorse for the main vessel and a second wire to protect/jail the side branch (polymer-jacketed wires re-cross struts more easily but watch for subintimal entry).

🔬 Lesion Assessment — Physiology & Imaging

🔱 The Medina classification — the shorthand every operator speaksScore three segments as (proximal main, distal main, side branch), each 1 if diseased, 0 if not. Examples: 1,1,1 = disease in all three (a true bifurcation, our case); 1,1,0 = main-vessel disease sparing the SB ostium (a pseudo/non-true bifurcation — provisional is easy); 0,0,1 = isolated ostial SB disease (notoriously hard to treat well). "True bifurcation" = the SB ostium is diseased (the last digit is 1).

📏 Physiology of the JAILED side branch — do not over-treat what looks bad

  • An angiographically "pinched" side-branch ostium after main-vessel stenting looks worse than it behaves. Most jailed side branches with preserved TIMI-3 flow are not functionally significant.
  • FFR of the jailed side branch is the tie-breaker: the majority of angiographically threatened side branches have FFR > 0.80 and can be safely left alone (do not stent for cosmetics).
  • Treat the side branch (rewire + kissing balloon, or bail-out 2-stent) when it is flow-limited (TIMI < 3), symptomatic/ischaemic, FFR ≤ 0.80, or a large territory is genuinely threatened.

🎮 Simulation — FFR of the Jailed Side Branch

Set the dial to explore treat-vs-leave.

🎯 FFR ≤ 0.80 → the jailed branch is truly ischaemic → treat it. > 0.80 → leave it. Most angiographically threatened side branches are > 0.80 and need nothing.

🖥️ Intravascular imaging — indispensable in bifurcations

🔵 IVUS

  • Sizes the proximal & distal main vessel (they differ!), quantifies plaque, guides POT balloon size and stent expansion
  • Confirms the wire re-crossed the DISTAL cell and the SB is well apposed

🟠 OCT

  • Ultra-high resolution — shows the exact stent-strut re-cross point, malapposition, and carina; 3D-OCT confirms distal-cell rewiring
  • Superb for optimising the final kissing/POT result
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A jailed side branch that looks awful on angiography usually has an FFR above 0.80 — do not stent a picture. Measure flow, not fear, before you add metal to a bifurcation."

🛠️ Intervention — Step-by-Step Execution

🎈 Balloon angioplasty & stent deployment — the building blocks of every bifurcation technique. Source: Wikimedia Commons (Blausen)

🪜 The provisional sequence — the default, step by step

  1. 🧵 Wire both branches — main-vessel wire into the distal LAD, a second protection ("jailed") wire into the diagonal. The jailed wire keeps the ostium tented open and marks the branch if it closes.
  2. 🎈 Prep the main vessel — predilate the main-vessel lesion; predilate the SB only if it will not otherwise cross (avoid dissecting the SB ostium).
  3. 🩹 Stent the main vessel across the SB origin, sized 1:1 to the distal main reference.
  4. 🎯 POT — Proximal Optimisation Technique — a short non-compliant balloon sized to the proximal main reference, inflated up to the carina. POT apposes the proximal stent, corrects the step-up in diameter, and opens the cell facing the side branch so you can rewire it distally.
  5. 🧵 Rewire the SB through the DISTAL cell — re-cross into the branch through the most distal stent strut (distal-cell crossing keeps the struts against the wall; proximal-cell crossing lifts and malapposes the stent). Remove the jailed wire.
  6. 🎈 Kissing-balloon inflation (only if the SB needs it) — simultaneous main + SB balloons to open the SB ostium and restore geometry; then a final re-POT to fix any proximal distortion from kissing.
🔧 If you must place two stents — the named techniques
  • DK crush (double-kissing crush): SB stent first, crush, first kiss, rewire, then second kiss — the most reproducible 2-stent technique, best evidence for complex true / left-main bifurcations (DKCRUSH-II/V).
  • Culotte: both branches fully covered with overlapping stents — good for similar-sized branches; more metal at the proximal segment.
  • T / TAP (T-and-protrusion): SB stent placed at the ostium (TAP leaves a small protrusion into the main vessel) — simplest bail-out when the SB is compromised after provisional, best for near-90° angles.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"POT, rewire the distal cell, kiss, re-POT — the four moves that make bifurcation stenting reproducible. Skip the POT and you cannot rewire cleanly; rewire the proximal cell and you malappose the stent you just placed."

🏠 Post-Procedure Care, Debrief & Assessment

🩹 Access-site & early monitoring

  • 🖐️ Radial: patent-haemostasis band, watch for radial artery occlusion & bleeding after a longer case.
  • 🫀 Watch for periprocedural MI — a compromised side branch can raise troponin and cause a regional wall-motion abnormality; repeat ECG and troponin, echo if symptomatic.

💊 Secondary prevention (start day 1)

  • DAPT — aspirin + a P2Y₁₂ inhibitor; complex bifurcation / 2-stent work leans toward a full 12-month DAPT course (individualise by bleeding risk) given higher stent thrombosis risk.
  • High-intensity statin (atorvastatin 80 mg) regardless of LDL; add ezetimibe/PCSK9i to target; tight diabetes and blood-pressure control.
  • Beta-blocker / ACE inhibitor as indicated; cardiac rehab, smoking-cessation reinforcement, risk-factor control.
  • 🖥️ Document the technique (provisional vs 2-stent, POT/kissing performed, imaging findings) — it matters for any future re-intervention.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Two stents and a diabetic vessel means you protect the result with DAPT and the patient with statins and risk-factor control. The elegant kissing shot is undone by an untreated LDL and an uncontrolled sugar."

🔭 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

  • 🎯 POT geometry: the proximal main vessel obeys Murray/Finet law (proximal diameter ≈ 0.678 × [main + side]) — POT with a proximal-sized balloon corrects the step-up and prevents proximal malapposition; POT-side-POT is a rewiring aid.
  • 🧵 Distal- vs proximal-cell rewiring: distal-cell re-cross keeps struts against the SB ostial wall; proximal-cell re-cross lifts the stent and leaves metal across the ostium — 3D-OCT confirms the cell.
  • 🔧 DK crush nuances: the two kissing inflations and adequate crush length drive its superiority; balloon crush vs stent crush; step-crush variations.
  • 🔩 Culotte vs T/TAP vs DK crush: culotte for similar-caliber branches, TAP for near-90° angles as a bail-out, DK crush for large-SB complex true / left-main bifurcations.
  • 🫀 Left main bifurcation: IVUS-mandatory; MADS classification of strategies; DKCRUSH-V vs EBC MAIN debate — provisional-first vs upfront DK crush by complexity.
  • 🖥️ Imaging-guided bifurcation PCI: IVUS/OCT improves outcomes; OCT bifurcation software identifies the rewire cell and carina; final MSA and apposition are the quality metrics.
  • 🪨 Calcium at the bifurcation: unprepared calcium ruins expansion and rewiring — modify with NC/scoring balloons, atherectomy or intravascular lithotripsy before stenting.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Diagonal Nobody Protected"

The case: An LAD/diagonal true bifurcation with a large 2.75 mm first diagonal. Running behind, the operator chose provisional but skipped the protection wire — "it is only a diagonal." He stented the LAD across the ostium and did a quick POT. The next cine showed the diagonal was TIMI-0 — carina and plaque shift had slammed the ostium shut. With no jailed wire to mark the branch, repeated attempts to rewire it went subintimal; the ostium could not be found across the fresh stent struts. Twenty minutes of ischaemia later the diagonal was still occluded. The patient developed a large periprocedural MI, a new anterolateral wall-motion abnormality and a troponin in the thousands, and left the lab in heart failure.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧵 NO PROTECTION WIREThe jailed wire that would have marked the branch and eased re-access was skipped to save time.
📏 UNDERRATED THE BRANCHA 2.75 mm diagonal supplies real myocardium — it was treated as disposable.
🎯 NO EARLY POT/PLANNo stepwise provisional plan to rewire and kiss if the branch pinched.
⚠️ SUBINTIMAL REWIREWithout a marker wire, re-access failed and went subintimal — then time ran out.
⏱️ TIME PRESSURERushing turned a routine provisional into an infarct.

🛡️ What would have saved him: jail a protection wire in any side branch you cannot afford to lose. If the branch then closes, the wire marks the ostium and tents it open so you can rewire the distal cell, kiss, and re-POT within minutes. Size the branch and respect it: a 2.75 mm diagonal is not disposable. This is exactly the drill rehearsed in S9.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The wire you were too rushed to jail is the wire you will beg for when the branch closes. Protect first, stent second — a marked ostium is rescuable, a lost one is an infarct."
