🧭 The classic triad on focused exam

  • 🧠 Encephalopathy — disorientation, inattention, apathy, poor new memory. The commonest single feature (~80%).
  • 👁️ Ophthalmoplegia / nystagmus — horizontal (and vertical) nystagmus, bilateral lateral rectus (VI) palsy, conjugate gaze palsies. The most specific sign and the fastest to respond to thiamine.
  • 🚶 Ataxia — wide-based stance, truncal > limb ataxia, cannot walk heel-to-toe. Reflects vermis + vestibular involvement.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Wernicke's is a CLINICAL diagnosis. If an at-risk patient has ANY one of the triad, give parenteral thiamine NOW — do not wait for a level, an MRI, or the full picture."
⚠️ The trap of "the CT is normal"A normal CT head does NOT exclude Wernicke's — CT is usually normal. MRI is more sensitive but still misses cases, and treatment must never wait for it. Imaging confirms; it never rules out. The bedside triad and the risk factors are the diagnosis.

The medical resident thinks out loud at 21:44:

🧠 Frame it as "treat first, prove later"

"High-risk patient, and he already has two of the three features — confused with obvious nystagmus and ataxia. That is more than enough. My job is not to 'confirm Wernicke's' — it is to get parenteral thiamine in immediately, before or with any glucose, then treat his hypoglycaemia, cover withdrawal, replace magnesium, and admit. I do NOT wait for an MRI and I do NOT give dextrose on its own."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 💉 IV Pabrinex (high-dose thiamine) STAT — before/with any glucose; two pairs of ampoules TDS is a standard treatment regimen.
  2. 🍬 Then correct the glucose — treat the 3.6 once thiamine is running; never leave true hypoglycaemia untreated.
  3. 🧪 Bloods — glucose, U&E, LFTs, magnesium, phosphate, FBC, blood alcohol, VBG (lactate).
  4. 🧲 Magnesium replacement — Mg is a cofactor for thiamine-dependent enzymes; thiamine will not work in a magnesium-depleted patient.
  5. 🥃 Alcohol-withdrawal cover (CIWA-driven benzodiazepine) and monitor for refeeding; senior/neurology review, admit.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Give thiamine before the glucose, and give magnesium with the thiamine — an unreplaced magnesium is why the thiamine 'did not work.'"
🧪 Resident's self-testThe nurse says "his sugar is 3.6, I will just push the dextrose while you write up the thiamine." Allow it?

No. In a thiamine-deficient brain, a glucose load consumes the last thiamine as a cofactor for glucose metabolism and can precipitate or deepen Wernicke's — even push him into coma. Give parenteral thiamine first or at the same time, then correct the glucose. If he were severely, dangerously hypoglycaemic and thiamine were seconds away, you give them together — but never glucose alone in an at-risk patient.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY thiamine, WHY these brain regions (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🔑 Thiamine (vitamin B1) is a cofactor for three key enzymes of glucose/energy metabolism: pyruvate dehydrogenase and α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase (the Krebs cycle) and transketolase (the pentose phosphate pathway).
  2. ⚡ No thiamine → the brain cannot burn glucose aerobically. ATP production collapses, lactate accumulates, and cells lose the pumps that maintain their membranes → cytotoxic oedema and neuronal death.
  3. 🎯 High-demand regions fail first: the mammillary bodies, periaqueductal grey, medial thalami, and cerebellar vermis have the highest thiamine turnover and oxidative metabolism — so they are hit earliest and hardest. That anatomy IS the triad (memory circuits, oculomotor nuclei, cerebellum).
  4. 🍬 The glucose trap: giving glucose to a thiamine-empty brain increases demand on the very enzymes that need thiamine, consuming the last stores and precipitating or worsening Wernicke's. This is why thiamine goes in first.
  5. 💀 Untreated → irreversible: the acute, treatable oedema hardens into permanent damage (mammillary body atrophy) — the biochemical emergency becomes the fixed amnesia of Korsakoff.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Thiamine is the spark plug of glucose metabolism. Pour in sugar with no spark plug and the engine floods — that is Wernicke's precipitated by dextrose."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Wernicke's vs Korsakoff

🧠 Wernicke's encephalopathy

  • Acute, a medical emergency
  • Confusion + ophthalmoplegia/nystagmus + ataxia
  • Reversible with prompt parenteral thiamine (eyes recover fastest)
  • This is the window to act

🕳️ Korsakoff syndrome

  • Chronic, often permanent
  • Profound anterograde amnesia + confabulation, relatively preserved alertness
  • The scar left when Wernicke's is missed or under-treated
  • ~85% of untreated survivors end here

💡 Wernicke's and Korsakoff are the acute and chronic ends of one disease (the Wernicke–Korsakoff spectrum). Treating Wernicke's fast is how you prevent Korsakoff.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (21:45)

  • 💉 IV Pabrinex STAT (high-dose parenteral thiamine + B/C vitamins) — before or with any glucose; treatment dose e.g. 2 pairs of ampoules IV TDS over 30 min.
  • 🍬 Correct hypoglycaemia AFTER thiamine is in — treat the CBG 3.6 once thiamine running; do not withhold glucose from a truly hypoglycaemic patient, just sequence it.
  • 🧲 IV magnesium if low/normal-low — essential cofactor; thiamine underperforms without it.
  • 🧪 Bloods: glucose, U&E, LFTs, magnesium, phosphate, FBC (macrocytosis), coag/INR, blood alcohol, VBG/lactate, ± thiamine level (do NOT wait for it).
  • 🥃 Alcohol-withdrawal regimen — CIWA-Ar scoring with symptom-triggered benzodiazepine (chlordiazepoxide/diazepam).
  • 🍚 Refeeding precautions — monitor phosphate/potassium/magnesium; reintroduce nutrition cautiously.
  • 🩻 CT head if focal signs / head-injury concern (to exclude co-pathology — not to confirm Wernicke's); neuro obs; falls precautions; admit to medicine.
  • 📞 Continue parenteral thiamine for the full course, then switch to oral thiamine on discharge.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Parenteral thiamine costs pennies and prevents a lifetime of amnesia; the expensive mistake is the bag of dextrose given first because 'he is just drunk.'"

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Bedside priorities — the nurse runs the safety net

  • 💉 Give parenteral thiamine before/with any glucose — challenge any order to push dextrose alone in this patient; sequence matters.
  • ⚠️ Watch for anaphylaxis with IV Pabrinex — rare but real; give over ~30 min, have resuscitation facilities available, monitor during infusion.
  • 🚶 Falls precautions — ataxic + confused = high falls risk; low bed, sensor, assisted mobilising, clear the bay.
  • 🧠 Neuro obs + GCS regularly; report any drop in consciousness (evolving encephalopathy / coma) immediately.
  • 🥃 CIWA scoring for withdrawal; give symptom-triggered benzodiazepine and reassure a frightened, disorientated patient.
  • 🍚 Refeeding watch — daily phosphate/K/Mg, do not overfeed on day 1; document intake.
  • 🧾 Orientation & dignity — repeated calm reorientation; involve family/collateral; safeguard a vulnerable adult.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who queries 'thiamine first?' before hanging the dextrose is the single most important safeguard against manufacturing a Korsakoff patient."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 👁️ Eye signs — nystagmus/ophthalmoplegia should improve within hours to days of thiamine; the fastest, most reassuring marker of response.
  • 🧠 GCS & cognition — encephalopathy lifts over days; persistent amnesia + confabulation signals emerging Korsakoff.
  • 🚶 Gait/ataxia — slower to recover; assess falls risk daily.
  • 🧪 Refeeding bloods — phosphate, potassium, magnesium daily as feeding restarts; correct proactively.
  • 🥃 CIWA-Ar — track withdrawal; watch for seizures and delirium tremens.
  • 🍬 Glucose — recheck after correction; avoid repeated unsupplemented dextrose.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The eyes answer first: if the nystagmus and gaze palsy melt away after thiamine, you caught it in time — if the memory never returns, you were too late."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong

  • 🕳️ Korsakoff syndrome — irreversible anterograde amnesia + confabulation; the defining complication of missed/under-treated Wernicke's.
  • 💤 Coma & death — untreated Wernicke's mortality up to ~20%; glucose-precipitated deterioration can be abrupt.
  • 🍚 Refeeding syndrome — hypophosphataemia/hypokalaemia/hypomagnesaemia → arrhythmia, seizures; the malnourished are primed for it.
  • 🥃 Alcohol withdrawal / delirium tremens / seizures — running in parallel; can be fatal if unmanaged.
  • 🚶 Falls & head injury — ataxia + confusion; a subdural can hide beneath the encephalopathy.
  • 🫁 Aspiration — reduced consciousness + vomiting.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The deadliest complication of Wernicke's is the one you build yourself — Korsakoff, made permanent by a delayed needle and an early bag of sugar."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the classic case

  • 🤰 Non-alcoholic Wernicke's: hyperemesis gravidarum, bariatric surgery, prolonged vomiting, cancer/chemotherapy, dialysis, refeeding after starvation, and total parenteral nutrition without thiamine. Suspicion must not require an alcohol history — a vomiting pregnant woman or a post-bariatric patient can develop it just as fast.
  • 🧠 Korsakoff neuropsychology: anterograde > retrograde amnesia with a temporal gradient, preserved procedural memory, and confabulation from damage to the Papez circuit (mammillary bodies → mammillothalamic tract → anterior thalamus). Insight is typically lost.
  • 💊 Dose controversy: optimal thiamine dose/route/duration is not settled by high-quality RCTs; expert guidance favours generous parenteral dosing (e.g. 500 mg IV TDS in severe cases in some protocols) given the minimal harm and catastrophic downside of under-treatment.
  • 🧲 MRI nuance: ~53% sensitive, ~93% specific; atypical (non-alcoholic) cases more often show involvement of cranial nerve nuclei, cerebellum, and cortex; DWI restriction can occur. A normal scan never rules it out.
  • 🧬 Why some drinkers and not others: proposed genetic variation in transketolase affinity for thiamine, plus variable intake, absorption, and hepatic storage — explaining why identical alcohol histories yield different risk.
  • 🍚 Refeeding & thiamine: reintroducing carbohydrate to a starved patient sharply increases thiamine demand — always give thiamine before/at the start of refeeding, mirroring the glucose rule.

🎭 M&M — "The Bag of Sugar That Erased a Man"

The case: A 49-year-old man, well known to the department as a "frequent flyer" for intoxication, was brought in confused and unsteady one Friday night. Triaged as "drunk — for the corridor." His capillary glucose was 3.4, so a bag of dextrose-saline was started and glucose gel given; no thiamine was prescribed. No one recorded the horizontal nystagmus documented by the paramedics, and the "wobbly gait" was written off as alcohol. Over the night he became more drowsy, then combative, then quiet. By morning he was awake but could not retain a single new fact, inventing detailed stories about a holiday he had never taken. MRI weeks later showed mammillary body atrophy. He was left with a permanent Korsakoff amnesia and moved into supported care — a life dismantled by an untreated vitamin deficiency and a bag of sugar.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Just drunk" label stuck; confusion attributed entirely to alcohol.
👁️ SIGNS IGNOREDDocumented nystagmus + ataxia never joined up into a triad.
🍬 GLUCOSE FIRSTDextrose given with NO thiamine — precipitating/worsening Wernicke's.
💉 NO THIAMINENever prescribed on arrival or at previous discharges.
🏥 SYSTEMCorridor care, no monitoring, no senior review of a high-risk patient.

🛡️ What would have saved him: treat every confused, malnourished or alcohol-dependent patient as at-risk; give parenteral thiamine before any glucose; act on ANY one triad feature (2/4 Caine); never let "he is just drunk" or "the CT is normal" close the book; and prescribe thiamine at every discharge. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 7.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Just drunk' and a bag of dextrose have erased more memories than any injury. Thiamine before glucose — every at-risk patient, every time."
