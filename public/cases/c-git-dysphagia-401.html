Speed to endoscopy is the single biggest thing you control.

🧭 True dysphagia vs its mimics

Separate genuine hold-up of the bolus from odynophagia (painful swallow), globus (a lump sensation between meals that eases when eating — reassuring), and xerostomia. Only true dysphagia earns the full obstructive/motility work-up. This man describes classic bolus hold-up.

⚠️ The trap of "it is just his old reflux"Barrett's and chronic GORD are precisely the population that develops adenocarcinoma. New or changed swallowing symptoms in a long-standing refluxer is a signal to scope, not to reassure. Anchoring on the pre-existing diagnosis is how early cancers are missed.

The gastroenterology registrar thinks out loud at 10:52:

🧠 Frame it as "obstructing until proven otherwise"

"Progressive solids-then-liquids dysphagia, weight loss and iron-deficiency anaemia in a 66-year-old ex-smoker with reflux — this is a mechanical/malignant picture until the endoscope tells me otherwise. My job is not to relabel his reflux; it is to get eyes and a biopsy on that oesophagus fast, and to correct his anaemia and nutrition in parallel."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🔎 Urgent OGD (upper GI endoscopy) within 2 weeks — the single most important test; direct visualisation + biopsy.
  2. 🩸 Bloods: FBC (confirm iron-deficiency anaemia), iron studies, U&E, LFTs, albumin, bone profile, clotting.
  3. 🥤 Nutrition & hydration review — dietitian, soft/liquid diet advice; consider what he can safely swallow now.
  4. 🧪 If OGD shows an obstructing mass → biopsy, then staging (CT chest/abdo/pelvis, then EUS + PET-CT, ± laparoscopy) and upper GI cancer MDT.
  5. 🐦 If OGD is macroscopically normal despite convincing dysphagia → do not stop; arrange barium swallow and high-resolution manometry to hunt for achalasia/motility disorder.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A normal endoscopy does not end a dysphagia work-up — it redirects it. If the mucosa looks fine but the patient truly cannot swallow, the disease is in the muscle: think achalasia and get manometry."
🧪 Resident's self-testThe clinic nurse suggests a barium swallow first "to avoid an invasive test." Is barium the right first investigation here?

No — endoscopy first. With ALARM features (weight loss, anaemia, age >55, progressive dysphagia) the priority is tissue diagnosis and mucosal inspection, which barium cannot provide. Barium/manometry come after a normal or non-diagnostic OGD, or to define achalasia. Leading with barium only delays a possible cancer diagnosis.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY solids stick before liquids (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🍖 A shrinking lumen: a growing mural tumour, or a fibrous stricture, narrows the oesophageal channel. A bulky solid bolus cannot pass a narrowed lumen — so solids stick first. Only when the lumen is critically tight do liquids also hold up. Hence "progressive solids→liquids" is the fingerprint of mechanical obstruction.
  2. 🐦 A muscle that cannot relax: in achalasia there is degeneration of inhibitory (nitrergic) ganglion cells in the myenteric plexus. The lower oesophageal sphincter (LOS) fails to relax and the body of the oesophagus loses peristalsis (aperistalsis). Because the problem is a non-relaxing sphincter, both solids AND liquids are held up from the start — the discriminator from cancer.
  3. 🩸 Why the weight falls and the blood thins: reduced intake → cachexia; a friable tumour oozes → chronic occult blood loss → iron-deficiency anaemia. Two ALARM features driven by one lesion.
  4. 🧬 Two cancers, two biologies: chronic acid → Barrett's metaplasia → dysplasia → adenocarcinoma (lower third); smoking/alcohol nitrosamine injury → squamous cell carcinoma (upper/mid third).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Solids then liquids = a narrowing lumen (obstruction). Solids AND liquids together from day one = a sphincter that will not open (achalasia). The pattern of dysphagia is the diagnosis until proven otherwise."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Mechanical obstruction vs Motility disorder

🍖 Mechanical (cancer / stricture / web)

  • Progressive solids → liquids
  • Weight loss, anaemia, older, risk factors
  • Endoscopy + biopsy is diagnostic; "apple-core"/mass on barium
  • This patient's likely track

🐦 Motility (achalasia / spasm)

  • Dysphagia to solids AND liquids from the start
  • Regurgitation of undigested food, chest pain, slow weight loss
  • 'Bird-beak' on barium; manometry diagnostic
  • Treat the sphincter, not a tumour

💡 Always exclude pseudoachalasia — a tumour at the gastro-oesophageal junction can mimic achalasia exactly. Short history, older age and rapid weight loss should make you scope hard and biopsy the GOJ.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (11:10)

  • 🔎 Urgent OGD within 2 weeks with biopsies of any lesion (multiple, targeted); note distance from incisors and length of any tumour.
  • 🩸 FBC, iron studies, ferritin, U&E, LFTs, albumin, bone profile, coag, group & save.
  • 🥤 Dietitian referral + nutritional assessment (MUST score); safe-swallow / texture-modified diet advice.
  • 💊 Continue PPI for symptom relief but do not rely on it as treatment for dysphagia.
  • 🩻 Staging CT chest/abdomen/pelvis if malignancy confirmed; then EUS (T/N stage) and PET-CT (occult metastases) per MDT.
  • 🐦 If OGD normal → barium swallow + high-resolution manometry (and 24-h pH if reflux uncertain).
  • 📞 Book directly into the upper GI cancer MDT if a tumour is found — parallel-process, do not wait.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Endoscopy and a biopsy answer the only question that changes his life — cancer or not. Order it first, order it fast, and let everything else run in parallel."

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Clinic & day-case priorities — the nurse guards nutrition and airway

  • 🥤 Assess what he can safely swallow now; document any coughing/choking on liquids (aspiration risk) and escalate for a swallow/SALT review if present.
  • ⚖️ Weigh and record; track the trajectory — weight loss is both a red flag and a nutritional emergency.
  • 😰 Regurgitation of undigested food, especially nocturnal, risks aspiration pneumonia — nurse upright, advise small frequent soft meals, sit up after eating.
  • 💉 Fasting and consent for OGD; check anticoagulants/antiplatelets and diabetes meds; confirm IV access for sedation.
  • 🩸 Flag anaemia symptoms (breathlessness, dizziness) and arrange iron replacement / transfusion thresholds per protocol.
  • 🫂 Break bad news gently and keep the patient informed — the fear of cancer is itself a clinical problem; involve the CNS/specialist nurse early.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who notices he chokes on water has found an aspiration risk before it becomes a pneumonia — swallow safety is a bedside vital sign in dysphagia."

Always take a careful look and biopsy at the GOJ — treating pseudoachalasia as achalasia lets a cancer grow.

💊 Medications & Definitive Therapies

💉 The treatment map — what, why, and the traps

TherapyRoleMechanism / caution
Pneumatic (balloon) dilatationFirst-line definitive for achalasiaForcefully disrupts LOS muscle fibres. Risk: perforation (~2%); often needs repeat sessions.
Laparoscopic Heller myotomy (+ partial fundoplication)Durable surgical achalasia treatmentDivides LOS muscle; fundoplication limits reflux. Excellent long-term relief.
POEM (per-oral endoscopic myotomy)Endoscopic myotomy — esp. type III / spastic achalasiaDurable; downside is significant post-procedure GORD — needs PPI surveillance.
Botulinum toxin injection to LOSTemporising for frail/unfit patientsRelaxes LOS but wears off in months; can scar tissue planes for later surgery.
Nitrates / CCBs (e.g. nifedipine)Weak, temporary symptom relief in achalasiaReduce LOS tone; limited efficacy and side-effects — not definitive.
PPIReflux symptom control; Barrett'sDoes NOT treat obstruction/dysphagia — never a substitute for endoscopy.
Oesophageal stentPalliation of malignant dysphagiaRestores swallowing in incurable disease; risks migration, pain, reflux.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"For achalasia you either stretch it (pneumatic dilatation), cut it (Heller/POEM), or freeze it temporarily (botulinum) — pills only nibble at the edges. Choose by fitness, subtype and reflux tolerance."
🚫 The 'do not' list in dysphagia❌ Do not keep escalating a PPI as "treatment" for progressive dysphagia · ❌ Do not label pseudoachalasia as achalasia without biopsy · ❌ Do not blindly dilate an undiagnosed stricture (perforate an unstaged cancer) — diagnose, then treat.
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • ⚖️ Weight and nutritional status — serial weights, albumin, MUST score; deterioration may need a feeding jejunostomy/NG or dietetic escalation.
  • 🥤 Swallowing capacity — can he manage saliva/liquids? Complete dysphagia (cannot swallow saliva) with an impacted bolus is an endoscopic emergency.
  • 🫁 Signs of aspiration — new cough, fevers, chest signs; nocturnal regurgitation is a warning.
  • 🩸 Anaemia trend — repeat Hb; falling Hb or melaena signals active tumour bleeding.
  • 📉 Post-procedure watch — after dilatation/POEM/myotomy monitor for chest pain, surgical emphysema, fever, tachycardia → suspect perforation and image urgently.
  • 🔁 Barrett's / post-treatment surveillance — endoscopic surveillance intervals per dysplasia grade.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After any oesophageal procedure, chest pain + fever + tachycardia is a perforation until a contrast study says otherwise — do not reach for reassurance, reach for the CT."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong

  • 🫁 Aspiration pneumonia — from retained food/regurgitation (both cancer obstruction and achalasia); a common and preventable killer.
  • 📉 Malnutrition & cachexia — worsens surgical outcomes, immunity and quality of life.
  • 🩸 Tumour haemorrhage / iron-deficiency anaemia.
  • 🕳️ Oesophageal perforation — spontaneous (rare) or iatrogenic after dilatation/stent/POEM; mediastinitis is life-threatening.
  • 🎗️ Local invasion & fistula — tracheo-oesophageal fistula (cough on swallowing, recurrent pneumonia); recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy (hoarse voice).
  • 🐦 Achalasia long-term: ~few-fold increased risk of oesophageal (squamous) cancer from chronic stasis → needs long-term follow-up; megaoesophagus.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Long-standing achalasia is not benign forever — chronic stasis raises squamous cancer risk, so a treated achalasic still needs long-term surveillance and a low threshold to re-scope new symptoms."

🏠 Discharge / Follow-up

📋 Two very different destinations

🎗️ This patient (cancer pathway)

  • MDT-directed treatment; CNS key-worker; nutrition & prehab
  • Red flags: complete dysphagia/food impaction, vomiting blood, breathlessness, aspiration → seek urgent care
  • Post-oesophagectomy: small frequent meals, dumping advice, surveillance
  • Smoking/alcohol cessation; psychological support

🐦 The achalasia patient

  • Definitive therapy (dilatation/Heller/POEM); reflux advice after myotomy/POEM
  • Long-term follow-up (symptom recurrence, small squamous cancer risk)
  • Dietary advice: eat upright, chew well, avoid late heavy meals
  • Safety-net: new/worsening dysphagia or weight loss → re-scope
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Whether the answer is a tumour or a tight sphincter, the discharge is never 'all clear and forget' — both diagnoses carry a long-term surveillance obligation and a written safety-net."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 📊 Chicago Classification v4.0: high-resolution manometry defines achalasia by integrated relaxation pressure (IRP) and peristalsis — type I (aperistalsis), type II (pan-oesophageal pressurisation, best prognosis), type III (spastic, responds best to POEM). Distinguish from oesophagogastric junction outflow obstruction (EGJOO), distal oesophageal spasm and jackhammer oesophagus.
  • 🧬 Barrett's surveillance & ablation: risk-stratify by dysplasia; endoscopic eradication therapy (radiofrequency ablation + EMR/ESD) for dysplastic Barrett's and early T1a mucosal cancer avoids oesophagectomy in selected cases.
  • 🎯 Molecular oncology of adenocarcinoma: HER2 testing (trastuzumab), PD-L1/CPS-directed immunotherapy (nivolumab/pembrolizumab in the peri-operative and advanced setting), and Claudin-18.2 emerging targets.
  • 🔪 Surgical strategy: minimally invasive/robotic oesophagectomy, transthoracic (Ivor–Lewis) vs transhiatal approaches, and the impact of centralisation of upper-GI cancer surgery on outcomes.
  • 🥩 Eosinophilic oesophagitis: the "3 D" phenotype (dysphagia, food impaction, rings/furrows); diagnosis needs ≥15 eos/hpf; PPI-responsive vs steroid (swallowed budesonide/fluticasone) vs elimination diet vs dupilumab.
  • 🩹 Palliation of malignant dysphagia: self-expanding metal stents vs brachytherapy vs external-beam radiotherapy; managing tracheo-oesophageal fistula; the balance of quality-of-life against procedural risk.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The PPI That Kept Being Doubled"

The case: A 64-year-old man with 15 years of "reflux" told his GP that food had started to stick. He was on a standard PPI; the dose was doubled and he was told to "avoid big meals and review in a few months." He lost weight steadily — a stone, then two — and became breathless (his Hb was 96 but the microcytic result was filed unactioned). Eight months after the first mention of dysphagia he was finally scoped: an obstructing lower-third adenocarcinoma, invading through the wall with coeliac nodes and liver metastases. Inoperable. He died within the year, needing a palliative stent to swallow.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Known refluxer" — the pre-existing label captured every new symptom.
💊 TREAT-DO-NOT-INVESTIGATEPPI up-titrated instead of endoscopy; dysphagia treated as "worse reflux".
🩸 IGNORED ALARMWeight loss + microcytic anaemia never actioned as red flags.
⏱️ DELAYEight months from first dysphagia to first endoscope — stage advanced meanwhile.
📄 RESULT NOT READAbnormal FBC filed without follow-up.

🛡️ What would have saved him: treat new progressive dysphagia as cancer until endoscopy proves otherwise; refer on the 2-week-wait pathway the day it is mentioned; act on every ALARM feature (weight loss, iron-deficiency anaemia) rather than filing it; and never let "known reflux" or "the PPI just needs increasing" close the door on an endoscopy. This is exactly the discipline that gave the patient in Bed 1 a curative-intent pathway.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Just increase the PPI' has staged more cancers than it has ever cured. Dysphagia plus weight loss is an endoscopy this week — not a prescription and a review in the spring."
