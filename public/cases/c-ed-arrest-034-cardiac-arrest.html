🧭 The one branch that changes everything: shockable vs non-shockable

The very first fork in ACLS is whether the rhythm is shockable (VF / pulseless VT — defibrillate) or non-shockable (PEA / asystole — do NOT shock). Misfiling one for the other is the classic fatal error: shocking asystole wastes a hands-off pause and achieves nothing, while failing to shock VF forfeits the single most effective intervention.

⚠️ EtCO₂ is your CPR quality monitor and your ROSC alarmA capnograph on the airway tells you two things: a persistently low EtCO₂ (< 10 mmHg) means poor compressions or a truly non-perfusing state (consider quality and prognosis after > 20 min); a sudden surge in EtCO₂ often heralds ROSC — check a pulse at the next rhythm analysis, not by stopping compressions randomly.

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. ❤️ Uninterrupted, high-quality CPR — rate 100–120, depth 5–6 cm, full recoil, rotate compressor every 2 min, compression fraction > 60%.
  2. ⚡ Defibrillate the VF at minimal interruption — charge during compressions, clear, shock, resume immediately.
  3. 💉 Adrenaline 1 mg IV after the 2nd shock, then every 3–5 min; amiodarone 300 mg after the 3rd shock (150 mg after the 5th).
  4. 🫁 Airway without over-ventilating — bag-mask 30:2 initially; once an advanced airway is in, ~10 breaths/min with continuous compressions.
  5. 🔎 Work the 4 H's & 4 T's in parallel — send a blood gas/potassium, give empirical calcium for likely hyperkalaemia, POCUS in any organised-but-pulseless rhythm.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Don't shock asystole; find the reversible cause. And don't stop shocking VF — persistent VF is not a reason to change tack, it's a reason to keep shocking, keep compressing, and hunt the 4 H's & 4 T's."
🧪 Resident's self-testThe airway is proving difficult and someone suggests everyone pause CPR "so we can intubate cleanly." Allow it?

No. The airway is a supporting act, not the priority. Compressions and defibrillation save lives; a perfect tube does not. Intubate around ongoing compressions or use a supraglottic device — do NOT interrupt CPR for airway placement. If you cannot secure it without a pause, keep bagging 30:2 and defer. Compression fraction is the metric that predicts survival.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY CPR quality is the currency of ROSC (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🫀 The arrested heart makes no pressure of its own. The only forward flow is the pressure you generate with your hands — chest compression squeezes the heart and thorax, ejecting blood.
  2. 🩸 Coronary perfusion pressure (CPP) = aortic diastolic − right-atrial diastolic pressure. CPP builds up over consecutive uninterrupted compressions and collapses to near zero the instant you stop. A CPP above roughly 15 mmHg is associated with ROSC.
  3. ⏸️ Every interruption resets the pressure. After a pause you must build CPP again from scratch — which is why peri-shock pauses, long rhythm checks, and compressor fumbling are so costly.
  4. 🌬️ Hyperventilation is a silent CPP killer. Fast bagging raises intrathoracic pressure, which impedes venous return to the chest, lowers the pressure gradient, and drops CPP — worsening ROSC. Ventilate slow and shallow.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Good CPR is not a formality — coronary perfusion pressure is the currency of ROSC, and it is spent every time your hands leave the chest."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Shockable vs Non-shockable arrest

⚡ Shockable (VF / pulseless VT)

  • Defibrillate — the single most effective intervention
  • CPR + early shock + adrenaline (after 2nd shock) + amiodarone (after 3rd)
  • Best prognosis; our patient's rhythm

🚫 Non-shockable (PEA / asystole)

  • Do NOT shock — there is nothing to defibrillate
  • CPR + adrenaline ASAP + relentless hunt for reversible causes
  • Worse prognosis unless a cause is found & fixed

💡 The first decision in every arrest is this fork. VF vs asystole: one earns a shock, the other earns a search. Shocking asystole is not benign — it is a wasted, harmful pause.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The code order set (21:05)

  • ❤️ Continuous high-quality CPR — rate 100–120, depth 5–6 cm, full recoil; rotate compressor every 2 min; target compression fraction > 60%.
  • ⚡ Defibrillate VF now and every 2-minute cycle while shockable — biphasic 150–200 J (or per device); charge during compressions, minimise peri-shock pause.
  • 💉 Adrenaline 1 mg IV/IO after the 2nd shock, then every 3–5 min; amiodarone 300 mg IV/IO after the 3rd shock, 150 mg after the 5th.
  • 🫁 Airway: bag-mask 30:2 with 100% O₂; supraglottic or ETT without interrupting compressions; capnography on. Do NOT hyperventilate (~10 breaths/min once advanced airway placed).
  • 🩸 IV/IO access ×2; VBG with potassium; empirical calcium gluconate/chloride if hyperkalaemia suspected (dialysis patient) plus insulin-dextrose ± bicarbonate.
  • 🔎 Reversible-cause sweep — 4 H's & 4 T's (S15); POCUS for tamponade/PE/hypovolaemia in any organised rhythm.
  • 📈 If ROSC → 12-lead ECG immediately; if STEMI → activate primary PCI; start post-arrest bundle (S17).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Compressions and a charged defibrillator are the orders that save lives; the drugs and the tube are the ones you must never let interrupt them."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ The resus team runs the choreography of a good code

  • ❤️ Compressor rotation every 2 minutes — fatigue silently degrades depth and rate; swap at each rhythm check to keep quality high without hands-off time.
  • ⏱️ Call the clock: announce the 2-minute cycles, the adrenaline every-3–5-min timer, and total down-time. A nurse with the code sheet is the team's memory.
  • ⚡ Defibrillator readiness: pads on early, charge during compressions, clear safely, and get hands straight back — the nurse who pre-charges saves seconds of CPP.
  • 💉 Draw and label drugs ahead of time — adrenaline and amiodarone ready before they're called; flush after each IV/IO push.
  • 🔎 Own a reversible cause: get the VBG/potassium to the machine, ready the calcium and insulin-dextrose for a dialysis patient, set up POCUS.
  • 🌬️ Guard the ventilation rate — challenge anyone bagging too fast; count breaths aloud (~10/min) to protect coronary perfusion.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who pre-charges the defibrillator and counts the breaths aloud protects the two things that actually decide the code: peri-shock pause and coronary perfusion pressure."

📈 ECG / Imaging + Visual Enhancements

🫀 The rhythm strip — VF, then organised rhythm after a shock

The coarse fibrillatory waveform has no organised QRS — it is shockable. After defibrillation, an organised rhythm appears: resume CPR for 2 minutes, then check for a pulse and ROSC at the next analysis.

🔎 Rhythm recognition you must know cold

RhythmLook forAction
⚡ Ventricular fibrillation (VF)Chaotic, irregular, no discernible QRS; coarse or fineShockable — defibrillate, CPR, drugs per algorithm.
⚡ Pulseless VTBroad, regular, rapid complexes with no pulseShockable — treat as VF.
🚫 AsystoleFlat line (confirm leads/gain, check another lead)Non-shockable — CPR + adrenaline + causes. Do NOT shock.
🚫 PEAOrganised electrical activity, no pulseNon-shockable — CPR + adrenaline + urgent reversible-cause hunt (POCUS).

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Shockable or not is the only question that matters at the rhythm check — get that fork right, deliver the shock in seconds, and get back on the chest."

🩻 Point-of-care ultrasound (during a < 10-second pause only)

  • Cardiac POCUS in PEA: pericardial effusion/tamponade, a dilated RV (PE), an empty hyperdynamic LV (hypovolaemia), or true cardiac standstill (prognostic).
  • Lung POCUS: absent lung sliding → tension pneumothorax. Integrate imaging into the rhythm-check pause — never add a hands-off pause for it.

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why during the code

  • 📟 Rhythm at every 2-minute cycle — the only question is shockable vs not; deliver the decision in seconds and resume CPR.
  • 🌬️ Waveform capnography (EtCO₂) — your live CPR-quality gauge (aim > 10–20 mmHg) and your ROSC alarm (a sudden surge). Also confirms tube placement.
  • ⏱️ The clocks: total down-time, 2-minute CPR cycles, adrenaline every-3–5-min timer, and peri-shock pause duration — the metrics of a good code.
  • ❤️ Compression feedback (device or coach) — rate, depth, recoil, and compression fraction > 60%.
  • 🔎 Reversible-cause tracking — has each of the 4 H's & 4 T's been actively considered and, where possible, excluded or treated?
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Capnography is the code's honest witness — it tells you if your compressions are working and shouts the moment ROSC arrives, without anyone lifting their hands off the chest."

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — during the code and after ROSC

  • ⏸️ Prolonged hands-off time / high peri-shock pause — the commonest self-inflicted killer; CPP never builds.
  • 🌬️ Hyperventilation — ↑ intrathoracic pressure → ↓ venous return → ↓ CPP → failed ROSC.
  • 🔁 Re-arrest after ROSC — unstable early rhythm; keep pads on, treat the cause, avoid hypotension.
  • 🧠 Hypoxic-ischaemic brain injury — the dominant determinant of long-term outcome; every minute of low-flow counts.
  • 🫁 Post-arrest complications: rib/sternal fractures from CPR, aspiration, hyperoxia injury, and post-cardiac-arrest syndrome (myocardial stunning, SIRS-like state).
  • 🔎 The missed reversible cause — the code that never converts because a hyperkalaemia, tamponade, tension pneumothorax or PE was never sought.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The deadliest complications of an arrest are the ones the team creates — hands-off time, hyperventilation, and the reversible cause nobody looked for."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Post-ROSC care & the two divergent trajectories

🫀 This patient (ROSC after VF)

  • Post-arrest bundle: SpO₂ 94–98% (avoid hypoxia AND hyperoxia), normocapnia, MAP ≥ 65 mmHg
  • 12-lead → STEMI → immediate PCI; treat hyperkalaemia, urgent dialysis
  • Avoid fever, target normothermia (TTM2); neuroprognostication delayed & multimodal
  • ICU admission; ICD assessment for secondary prevention once recovered

🕊️ When the code does not succeed

  • Stop after a reversible-cause sweep is complete and resuscitation is futile (persistent asystole, EtCO₂ < 10 despite good CPR > 20 min)
  • Prolong for hypothermia, PE thrombolysis, or ECPR candidacy
  • Compassionate team debrief; family communication; organ-donation pathway where appropriate
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"ROSC is half-time, not full-time: the post-arrest bundle — oxygenation targets, blood pressure, avoiding fever, and a 12-lead that finds the STEMI — decides whether the brain survives the save."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the standard algorithm

  • 🫀 ECPR / E-CPR (extracorporeal CPR): veno-arterial ECMO instituted during ongoing CPR for selected refractory arrests (witnessed, shockable, short low-flow, reversible cause such as coronary occlusion or PE). Trials (ARREST, PRAGUE-OHCA, INCEPTION) show benefit is highly system- and selection-dependent — it demands a rapid-deployment team and strict criteria.
  • ⚡ Double-sequential (dual) defibrillation and vector change for refractory VF: the DOSE-VF trial suggested improved survival with a vector change or double-sequential external defibrillation over standard repeated shocks in shock-refractory VF.
  • 🩻 Point-of-care ultrasound in PEA: differentiates the "pseudo-PEA" (organised cardiac motion, often hypovolaemia/obstruction — better prognosis) from true standstill, and rapidly identifies tamponade, massive PE (dilated RV), tension pneumothorax and hypovolaemia — integrated into the < 10-second rhythm-check pause.
  • 🧊 The post-cardiac-arrest bundle: targeted temperature management (normothermia/fever avoidance per TTM2), controlled oxygenation (avoid hyperoxia) and ventilation (normocapnia), haemodynamic optimisation (MAP ≥ 65), early coronary angiography for STEMI, and delayed multimodal neuroprognostication.
  • 🔌 Refractory-cause-directed therapy: thrombolysis for presumed PE arrest (then CPR 60–90 min), resuscitative thoracotomy for tamponade/penetrating trauma, and hyperkalaemia protocols (calcium, shift, emergent dialysis) — the arrest that won't convert almost always has a cause to fix.
  • 💉 Airway & drug-route nuance: intraosseous vs IV (fast, reliable in arrest), supraglottic-first strategies (AIRWAYS-2/PART), and continuous-compression CPR with asynchronous ventilation once an advanced airway is placed.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Code We Lost to the Clock"

The case: A 62-year-old man on haemodialysis arrested in a busy resus bay — a witnessed VF, the most salvageable rhythm there is. Yet he never achieved ROSC. The debrief, replayed from the defibrillator's own log, was painful: the compressor stopped repeatedly to "reassess," pushing the compression fraction down to 40%; the peri-shock pauses ran to 20 seconds each; and someone bag-masked at 30 breaths a minute throughout. No one sent a blood gas. His potassium, found later, was 7.4 — a hyperkalaemia that calcium would have fixed. The team shocked a heart that could not sustain a rhythm because its metabolism was never corrected, while its coronary perfusion pressure was repeatedly reset to zero.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

⏸️ HANDS-OFF TIMECompression fraction 40%; long peri-shock pauses reset CPP every cycle.
🌬️ HYPERVENTILATIONBagging at 30/min ↑ intrathoracic pressure → ↓ venous return → ↓ CPP.
🧪 MISSED CAUSENo gas sent; a K⁺ of 7.4 in a dialysis patient — calcium never given.
👥 NO ROLE CLARITYNo one owned the timer, the reversible causes, or CPR quality.
📟 TREATED THE MONITORShocks delivered; the metabolism behind the VF ignored.

🛡️ What would have saved him: protect compression fraction (charge during CPR, peri-shock pause < 5 s, rotate the compressor); ventilate slow and shallow (~10/min); send a gas on every arrest and give empirical calcium for an obvious hyperkalaemia; and assign explicit roles — a timekeeper, a CPR-quality coach, and someone whose only job is the 4 H's & 4 T's. This is exactly the discipline that achieved ROSC in Bed 4.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"You can shock perfect VF forever and still lose the patient if the compressions stop, the bag runs too fast, and the potassium of 7 goes unlooked-for. Fix the CPR, fix the ventilation, find the cause."
