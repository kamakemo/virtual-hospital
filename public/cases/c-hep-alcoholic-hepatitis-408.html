🧭 The three questions you answer at the bedside first

In severe alcoholic hepatitis your reflex is: (1) Is there infection? (blood/urine cultures, CXR, ascitic tap if any fluid — infection is present in ~25% at admission and precludes immediate steroids). (2) Is there a GI bleed? (varices/gastritis — another steroid contraindication and a withdrawal-masked emergency). (3) Is the brain safe? (Wernicke's, hypoglycaemia, withdrawal, encephalopathy — thiamine and glucose sequencing).

⚠️ The trap of "it is obviously alcoholic hepatitis, start steroids"Corticosteroids are only justified in severe disease (Maddrey ≥ 32) after active infection and GI bleeding are treated/excluded. Starting them blind, into occult sepsis, worsens infection and mortality — the classic, examinable error captured in S21.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The medical resident thinks out loud at 14:35:

🧠 Frame it as "earn the steroid, then protect the brain and gut"

"This looks like severe alcoholic hepatitis, but I do not start prednisolone on a hunch. My plan: calculate the Maddrey discriminant function to confirm severity, screen and treat for infection and GI bleed, give Pabrinex (thiamine) before any glucose, manage his withdrawal on a symptom-triggered CIWA regimen, and get aggressive nutrition going — the intervention with the strongest evidence. Only when infection is excluded/controlled do steroids go in, and I will reassess the response with the Lille score at day 7."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🧮 Maddrey DF = 4.6 × (PT − control seconds) + bilirubin(mg/dL). ≥ 32 = severe (also calculate GAHS and MELD).
  2. 🩸 Septic screen — blood + urine cultures, CXR, ascitic tap if fluid present; treat any infection before steroids.
  3. 🩸 Assess for GI bleed (Hb, PR exam, look for melaena/haematemesis).
  4. 💉 Pabrinex (IV thiamine) BEFORE glucose; correct hypoglycaemia only after thiamine; check for Wernicke's.
  5. 🍽️ Aggressive enteral nutrition — dietitian, ~35 kcal/kg and 1.2–1.5 g/kg protein; NG feeding if not eating.
  6. 🥃 Symptom-triggered CIWA with benzodiazepines (shorter-acting if severe liver impairment); VTE and refeeding precautions.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Nutrition and abstinence are the strongest, safest treatments in alcoholic hepatitis — steroids only nudge short-term mortality and only in severe disease. Feed the patient, protect the brain, and earn the steroid."
🧪 Resident's self-testThe nurse asks to give IV dextrose now for his glucose of 4.0 and his confusion. What is the correct sequence?

Thiamine first. Give IV thiamine (Pabrinex) before any glucose. In a thiamine-depleted chronic drinker, a glucose load accelerates the last thiamine into carbohydrate metabolism and can precipitate acute Wernicke's encephalopathy (confusion, ophthalmoplegia, ataxia). A glucose of 4.0 is only borderline low — treat the brain first, then, if truly hypoglycaemic, give glucose after thiamine is running.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the liver fails, WHY the AST:ALT ratio (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🥃 Alcohol metabolism: ethanol → acetaldehyde (via ADH/CYP2E1) generates reactive oxygen species and shifts the NADH:NAD⁺ ratio, driving fat accumulation (steatosis) and mitochondrial injury.
  2. 🧪 Acetaldehyde adducts + gut endotoxin: damaged hepatocytes and translocated bacterial LPS activate Kupffer cells → a burst of TNF-α and IL-8 → a neutrophilic inflammatory infiltrate (the histological hallmark, with Mallory–Denk bodies and ballooning).
  3. 🟡 Cholestasis + hepatocyte death: bilirubin transport fails and hepatocytes die faster than they regenerate → the deep, rapid jaundice and coagulopathy of liver failure.
  4. ⚖️ The AST:ALT ratio > 2: alcohol depletes pyridoxal-5-phosphate (needed more by ALT) and injures mitochondria (releasing AST), so AST > ALT — but both are usually modest (< 300–500); a very high transaminase points elsewhere (ischaemia, paracetamol, viral).
  5. 🦠 Immune paralysis: paradoxically, these patients are also functionally immunosuppressed — which is exactly why steroids (further immunosuppression) are dangerous if infection is lurking.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Alcoholic hepatitis is inflammation you might calm with steroids in a patient already too immunosuppressed to fight infection — that paradox is why 'exclude sepsis first' is not bureaucracy, it is survival."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Alcoholic hepatitis vs Decompensated cirrhosis

🍺 Alcoholic hepatitis

  • Acute syndrome: rapid jaundice, tender liver, neutrophilia
  • AST:ALT > 2, both usually < 300–500
  • Steroids (if severe + no infection) + nutrition; reassess with Lille

🫄 Decompensated cirrhosis

  • Chronic scarring with ascites/varices/encephalopathy
  • Managed by treating the complication (see the sister cirrhosis case)
  • No role for steroids; nutrition and abstinence still central

💡 The two coexist often — a cirrhotic can have a superimposed acute alcoholic hepatitis. The acute syndrome is what makes steroids and the Maddrey/Lille pathway relevant.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (14:40)

  • 💉 Pabrinex (IV thiamine) STAT and BEFORE any glucose; continue high-dose B-vitamins for the admission.
  • 🩸 Septic screen — blood cultures ×2, urine culture, CXR, ascitic tap if fluid; low threshold to start empirical antibiotics.
  • 🧮 Calculate Maddrey DF, GAHS, MELD; book day-0 and day-7 bilirubin for the Lille score.
  • 🥃 Symptom-triggered CIWA alcohol-withdrawal protocol (benzodiazepines — lorazepam/oxazepam if significant liver impairment); seizure precautions.
  • 🍽️ Dietitian + aggressive nutrition (~35 kcal/kg, 1.2–1.5 g/kg protein); NG feeding if intake poor; refeeding-syndrome precautions with phosphate/magnesium/potassium.
  • 🩸 Bloods: FBC, U&E, LFTs, INR/PT, glucose, magnesium/phosphate, CRP, blood film, and a full non-invasive liver screen (viral hepatitis, ferritin, autoantibodies, paracetamol level) to exclude other causes.
  • 💊 Do NOT start prednisolone yet — only after infection excluded/treated and no active GI bleed, and only if Maddrey ≥ 32.
  • 📞 Hepatology referral; discuss transplant pathway early for potential non-responders.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Thiamine before glucose, cultures before steroids, nutrition before everything — three orderings that decide the outcome more than the prednisolone ever will."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Ward priorities — the nurse runs withdrawal, nutrition and the sepsis watch

  • 🥃 Symptom-triggered CIWA scoring each shift; give benzodiazepines per protocol and watch for over- or under-treatment (both dangerous in liver failure).
  • 💉 Give Pabrinex before any dextrose — challenge any order for IV glucose that precedes thiamine.
  • 🌡️ Full observations; a new fever, tachycardia or hypotension means re-culture and reassess — infection can appear or worsen at any point, especially once steroids start.
  • 🍽️ Support and record oral/NG nutrition; monitor for refeeding syndrome (phosphate, magnesium, potassium); daily weights.
  • 🧠 Screen for encephalopathy and Wernicke's (confusion, eye signs, ataxia); protect the airway if drowsy.
  • 🩸 Watch for GI bleeding (melaena/haematemesis) and bruising from coagulopathy; report a rising creatinine or falling urine output (HRS risk).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who insists on thiamine before the dextrose bag, and who re-scores the fever before the steroid dose, has already prevented the two commonest fatal errors in this disease."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📉 Bilirubin at day 0 and day 7 — the Lille score input; a falling bilirubin = responder (continue to 28 days), flat/rising = non-responder (stop steroids).
  • 🌡️ Infection surveillance — temperature, WCC/CRP trend, cultures; steroids blunt the signs, so watch closely and have a low threshold to treat.
  • 🧠 CIWA + Wernicke screen — withdrawal severity and neuro signs each shift.
  • 💧 Creatinine + urine output — HRS is a leading cause of death; a rising creatinine is a red alert.
  • 🩸 Hb and signs of GI bleeding; glucose (both hypo- and steroid-induced hyper-).
  • 🧪 Electrolytes for refeeding syndrome (phosphate, magnesium, potassium).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Two dates run this admission: day 0 and day 7. The bilirubin between them, via the Lille score, tells you whether the steroids you fought to justify are worth continuing."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong

  • 🦠 Infection / sepsis — the leading cause of death; risk amplified by steroids. SBP, pneumonia, UTI, bacteraemia.
  • 💧 Hepatorenal syndrome / AKI — a rising creatinine is the single most ominous sign.
  • 🧠 Wernicke's encephalopathy — precipitated by glucose without thiamine; and hepatic encephalopathy from liver failure.
  • 🥃 Severe alcohol withdrawal — delirium tremens, withdrawal seizures.
  • 🩸 Variceal / GI bleeding and coagulopathic bleeding.
  • 🍽️ Refeeding syndrome in the malnourished when nutrition is (correctly) started.
  • 💀 Progression to acute-on-chronic liver failure with multi-organ failure in non-responders.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Most patients with severe alcoholic hepatitis who die, die of infection or the kidney — not directly of the liver. Guard those two fronts as fiercely as you score the liver."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🍺 Diagnosis: severe alcoholic hepatitis (Maddrey ≈ 58, GAHS 10, MELD 25), AST:ALT > 2, no dilated ducts, mimics excluded.
  2. 💉 Thiamine before glucose; aggressive nutrition; symptom-triggered CIWA withdrawal management; septic screen with treatment of any infection.
  3. 💊 Once infection excluded/treated and no GI bleed → prednisolone 40 mg/day; recompute the Lille score at day 7.
  4. 🏥 If Lille > 0.45 (non-responder) → stop steroids, intensify supportive care, and consider early liver transplant in highly selected abstinent-committed patients. Abstinence support throughout.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The plan is a gate and a checkpoint: the Maddrey gate (≥ 32) lets steroids in, the Lille checkpoint (day 7) decides if they stay — and nutrition and abstinence run the whole length of the road."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Discharge & the abstinence pivot — two worlds

🍺 This patient (steroid responder)

  • Complete the 28-day prednisolone course with taper per protocol
  • Total alcohol abstinence — the single biggest determinant of long-term survival — with structured support/addiction services
  • Ongoing nutrition, thiamine/vitamins, treat any cirrhosis complications
  • Hepatology follow-up; transplant discussion if disease recurs and abstinence sustained
  • Red flags: fever, confusion, black stools, less urine, deepening jaundice → return

🟢 The non-responder (Lille > 0.45)

  • Stop steroids; intensify supportive care and infection vigilance
  • Consider early liver transplant in carefully selected, abstinence-committed patients
  • Honest prognostic conversation; palliative input where appropriate
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Steroids buy days; abstinence buys years. Every discharge from alcoholic hepatitis lives or dies on whether the next drink is taken — invest in the addiction support as seriously as the prednisolone."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🫀 Early liver transplantation (Mathurin, NEJM 2011; ACCELERATE-AH): highly selected steroid non-responders at their first liver-decompensating event can achieve excellent survival with early transplant, challenging the traditional 6-month abstinence rule — but requires rigorous psychosocial selection.
  • 🧪 Lille & combination models: combining Lille with MELD, or dynamic day-4 assessment, refines who to continue steroids in; static baseline scores alone under-perform the trajectory.
  • 🦠 Infection & the gut–liver axis: bacterial translocation and endotoxaemia drive the inflammation; the microbiome, faecal microbiota transplant and targeted anti-inflammatory strategies (IL-1 antagonists, anti-TNF — the latter historically increased infection deaths) are active research areas.
  • 🧬 Molecular therapies: IL-22 agonists (hepatoprotective/regenerative), G-CSF (mobilises regeneration — promising in some cohorts, unproven in Western trials), FXR agonists and DUR-928 are under study.
  • 🧫 ACLF framing (CANONIC / CLIF-C): severe alcoholic hepatitis is a common trigger of acute-on-chronic liver failure; grading organ failures guides intensive-care escalation and futility discussions.
  • 🧠 N-acetylcysteine + steroids: some trials show improved short-term (but not long-term) survival when NAC is added to prednisolone — protocol-dependent, mechanistically an antioxidant strategy.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "Steroids Into the Sepsis, Sugar Before the Thiamine"

The case: A 45-year-old heavy drinker was admitted deeply jaundiced with a Maddrey of 55 and a temperature of 38.0. Keen to "treat the alcoholic hepatitis," the team started prednisolone on admission and, for his confusion and a glucose of 3.9, ran a bag of IV dextrose before any thiamine. The fever was never cultured — it was assumed to be "just the hepatitis." Two days later he had florid pneumonia raging under the steroids, and had developed ophthalmoplegia and profound amnesia — established Wernicke's encephalopathy. He progressed to acute-on-chronic liver failure and died on day 9.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Fever is just the hepatitis" — never cultured, never treated.
💊 STEROIDS TOO SOONPrednisolone started before infection was excluded, worsening the pneumonia.
🍬 SUGAR BEFORE THIAMINEIV dextrose given first, precipitating Wernicke's encephalopathy.
🩸 NO SEPTIC SCREENNeutrophilia wrongly read as disease, not infection.
⏱️ RUSH TO TREATBoth fatal errors made in the first hour by a team in a hurry to "do something."

🛡️ What would have saved him: a full septic screen and treatment of infection before any steroid; thiamine (Pabrinex) drawn up and given before any glucose; the neutrophilia interpreted with suspicion rather than reassurance; and the steroid decision made deliberately against the Maddrey/infection checklist, not reflexively. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 8.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In alcoholic hepatitis the two fastest ways to kill your patient are a steroid into an unscreened fever and a bag of sugar before the thiamine — both take one careless minute and neither is forgiven."
