🚦 Why the left main is different

The left main coronary artery perfuses roughly 75% of the left ventricular myocardium (the entire LAD and circumflex territories). A significant left main stenosis puts a huge amount of muscle at risk from a single point — an "unprotected" left main (no patent bypass graft to LAD or LCx protecting the territory) that occludes acutely is frequently fatal. That is why left main disease has historically been a Class I indication for revascularisation and, for decades, the domain of the surgeon. Modern trials have opened a genuine PCI option — but ONLY through a structured Heart Team decision, not a reflex ad-hoc stent.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Significant left main disease is a decision, not a stent. Stop, image it, score it (SYNTAX), and take it to the Heart Team — the reflex to fix it on the table is the mistake that kills."

📋 Pre-Procedure Workup & Planning

🧾 Before you touch the left main — the planning checklist

  • 🧮 Anatomical SYNTAX score — calculate it from the diagnostic angiogram (lesion complexity of the whole tree). Low ≤22 · Intermediate 23–32 · High ≥33. This is the single biggest anatomical driver of the PCI-vs-CABG call.
  • 📊 SYNTAX II / clinical risk — adds age, sex, creatinine/eGFR, EF, COPD, peripheral disease, unprotected LM to individualise the 4-year mortality prediction for PCI vs CABG. Anatomy alone is not the whole story.
  • 🔪 Surgical risk (STS / EuroSCORE II) — a prohibitively high surgical risk shifts a borderline case toward PCI; a low surgical risk with high SYNTAX shifts toward CABG.
  • 🍬 Diabetes status — diabetic multivessel disease has a durable CABG survival advantage (FREEDOM). Flag it early.
  • 🫀 Completeness & targets — are the distal vessels graftable? Is there a suitable LIMA target on the LAD? Comorbidity (frailty, prior sternotomy, porcelain aorta, hostile chest) all feed the decision.
  • 💊 Antithrombotic plan — if PCI is likely, ensure DAPT loading; if CABG is likely, hold potent P2Y₁₂ inhibitors (ticagrelor/clopidogrel typically 3–5 days, prasugrel ~7) to avoid perioperative bleeding.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"SYNTAX scores the arteries; SYNTAX II scores the patient. Low score, less diabetes, prohibitive surgical risk → PCI is reasonable. High score, diabetes, good surgical candidate → CABG wins."

🩸 Vascular Access & Setup

🖐️ Access for a left main PCI — plan for support

🖐️ Radial (default)

  • Fewer access-site bleeds, lower mortality in ACS (MATRIX)
  • 6–7 Fr radial handles most left main bifurcation work, IVUS and two-stent techniques
  • Confirm patency first (Barbeau/Allen); consider distal radial

🦵 Femoral (for big-bore / support)

  • When you anticipate mechanical circulatory support (Impella / IABP) for a high-risk unprotected LM PCI
  • Allows 7–8 Fr guides for complex two-stent left main work
  • Ultrasound + micropuncture; avoid a high or low stick
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"For an unprotected left main, decide your bail-out BEFORE you engage: what guide, what support (Impella/IABP), and which second access — because when the left main dissects, there is no time to plan."

💉 Guide, anticoagulation & support planning

  • 🧭 Guide catheter: a supportive left guide (EBU/XB 3.5–4.0) that sits coaxially without deep-seating the left main — deep intubation of a diseased LM can cause dissection. Consider a 7 Fr guide if a two-stent (DK crush) strategy is planned.
  • 🩸 Anticoagulation: unfractionated heparin (70–100 U/kg to an ACT ~250–300 s) or bivalirudin. Keep the LM territory perfused — hypotension during LM work is poorly tolerated.
  • 🫀 Support strategy: for a truly high-risk unprotected LM (poor LV, last remaining vessel, complex two-stent), consider prophylactic Impella or IABP — a "protected PCI." An arrest on the table during LM work needs support already in place, not scrambled for.
  • 🖥️ Imaging ready: IVUS catheter on the table from the start — you will use it before AND after, not as an afterthought.

🔬 Lesion Assessment — Physiology & Imaging

🫀 The left main earns imaging, not eyeballingAn angiographically intermediate left main (40–70%) is notoriously hard to grade by eye — foreshortening, ostial disease and overlap all deceive. IVUS is the reference standard for left main sizing and severity. For the intermediate NON-left-main lesions (the mid-LAD, the OM), FFR/iFR physiology decides whether they need treating or grafting at all.

📏 FFR / iFR — physiology of the non-LM lesions

  • FFR (fractional flow reserve) — maximal flow across a stenosis vs normal, measured with a pressure wire during hyperaemia (adenosine). FFR ≤ 0.80 = ischaemic → treat. Above 0.80 → defer.
  • iFR (instantaneous wave-free ratio) — a resting index, no adenosine. iFR ≤ 0.89 = significant.
  • ⚠️ Caveat in left main disease: FFR of a downstream LAD/LCx lesion is confounded by a proximal left main stenosis (and vice-versa). Grade the left main by IVUS, then physiology the branches.

🖥️ IVUS minimal luminal area (MSA) targets — memorise these

🖥️ Intravascular imaging — IVUS vs OCT in the left main

🔵 IVUS (left-main workhorse)

  • Ultrasound — deep penetration, sizes the vessel and ostium, quantifies plaque and calcium
  • Reference standard for left main severity, sizing and post-stent optimisation (MSA targets)
  • No contrast needed — safe for the ostial LM

🟠 OCT

  • Light — ultra-high resolution; superb for edge dissection, malapposition, calcium arc
  • Ostial LM is hard to flush and clear → IVUS usually preferred for the LM ostium
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Do not stent a left main you have not IVUS-ed. Angiography under-reads the ostium; the ultrasound tells you the true size, the plaque, the calcium — and later, whether your stent is actually expanded (LM ≥ 8.7 mm²)."

🛠️ Intervention — Step-by-Step Execution

🎈 Stent deployment — in the left main, precision and expansion decide survival. Source: Wikimedia Commons (Blausen)

🪜 Left main bifurcation PCI — provisional first

The default left-main bifurcation strategy is provisional single-stent (stent LM into the LAD, keep the LCx accessible), escalating to a two-stent technique only if the side branch is compromised. When a planned two-stent is needed for a true, large, diseased LCx, DK crush (double-kissing crush) has the best evidence (DKCRUSH-II/-V).

  1. 🧵 Wire both branches — protect the LCx; keep a jailed wire if going provisional.
  2. 📏 Lesion prep & size by IVUS — size the stent to the distal reference (LAD); the left main is bigger, so you will up-size proximally with POT.
  3. 🩹 Deploy DES LM→LAD — cover the LM and ostial LAD with a single stent (provisional).
  4. 🎈 POT — Proximal Optimisation Technique — a short non-compliant balloon sized to the LEFT MAIN, inflated in the proximal stent up to the carina. POT expands the stent to the true LM diameter, corrects the "step-up," and re-opens the side-branch cell.
  5. 🔄 Re-wire the side branch through a distal cell, kissing-balloon inflation, and re-POT — the POT–side–POT sequence. Only convert to DK crush if the LCx ostium is truly compromised.
  6. 🖥️ IVUS the result — confirm expansion against MSA targets (LM ≥ 8.7, ostial LAD ≥ 6.3 mm²), no malapposition, no edge dissection.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"POT is not optional in a left main. One stent sized to the LAD will never fill the left main — the proximal optimisation balloon is what makes a bifurcation stent fit the vessel it actually lives in."

S10

🏠 Post-Procedure Care, Debrief & Assessment

🩹 After a left main PCI

  • 🖐️ Access-site care; monitor for radial occlusion / femoral haematoma; watch closely as any LM ischaemia is high-stakes.
  • 📟 Telemetry and haemodynamic monitoring — a threatened LM result can declare itself as ischaemia hours later; low threshold to re-look.
  • 🖥️ Document the IVUS MSA numbers in the report — they are the audit trail that the stent was optimised.

💊 Secondary prevention (especially the diabetic)

  • DAPT — aspirin + a P2Y₁₂ inhibitor for 12 months after left main PCI (individualise by bleeding risk; left main is a high-thrombotic-consequence site).
  • High-intensity statin (atorvastatin 80 mg) to LDL target; add ezetimibe/PCSK9i as needed.
  • Diabetes optimisation — SGLT2 inhibitor and/or GLP-1 receptor agonist with proven cardiovascular benefit; tighten glycaemic control (this patient is at HbA1c 8.4%).
  • ACE inhibitor / ARB, beta-blocker if indicated, blood-pressure and lifestyle control, cardiac rehab.
  • 🔪 If CABG was chosen: sternal wound care, early rehab, graft-protective aspirin, and the same aggressive risk-factor control.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Revascularisation — stent or graft — buys time; it does not cure the atherosclerosis. In a diabetic, the statin, the SGLT2 inhibitor, and glycaemic control do as much for survival as the procedure."

⚖️ The EXCEL vs NOBLE controversy — told honestly

EXCEL concluded PCI was non-inferior to CABG for left main disease of low-intermediate complexity. NOBLE, in a similar population, concluded CABG was superior. Both are quality randomised trials that reached opposite headline conclusions. Why?

  • 📏 Different endpoints: EXCEL used a composite of death/stroke/MI; NOBLE used MACCE that included repeat revascularisation (which favours surgery) and excluded periprocedural MI.
  • 🔬 The MI definition fight: EXCEL used a protocol (largely enzymatic) MI definition; critics argued it counted CABG periprocedural enzyme rises as events, flattering PCI. A very public dispute (including a resignation from the EACTS guideline group) followed.
  • ⏳ Time matters: longer follow-up tends to reveal CABG's durability advantage (fewer repeat procedures, fewer late MIs), especially in complex/diabetic anatomy.

Bottom line: for LOW/intermediate-SYNTAX left main disease PCI is a legitimate, guideline-supported option (2021 ESC/EACTS: Class IIa for low, IIb intermediate; 2021 ACC/AHA similar), but the evidence is genuinely contested — which is exactly why it is a shared Heart Team decision, not an operator reflex.

🔭 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

  • 🧮 SYNTAX II 2020 & individualised risk: the updated model integrates anatomy with clinical variables to predict PCI vs CABG mortality — a nomogram, not a single cut-off; use it to inform, not replace, the Heart Team.
  • 🔱 Bifurcation algorithms: provisional (default) vs planned 2-stent; DK crush (best 2-stent evidence, DKCRUSH-II/-V), culotte, T/TAP; the mandatory triad — POT → side-branch → re-POT, plus final kissing-balloon inflation.
  • 🪨 Calcium modification in the LM: heavy calcium underlies under-expansion — rotational/orbital atherectomy or intravascular lithotripsy (Shockwave) BEFORE stenting a calcified left main; under-prepared calcium = fatal under-expansion.
  • 🫀 Protected vs unprotected LM & MCS: "protected" = a patent graft (LIMA-LAD/SVG) already supplies the territory; "unprotected" LM PCI in poor LV or last-remaining-vessel warrants prophylactic Impella/IABP (protected PCI); VA-ECMO for refractory collapse.
  • 🖥️ Imaging-guided LM PCI: IVUS-guided left main PCI improves survival vs angiography alone; MSA targets (LM ≥8.7, ostial LAD ≥6.3, ostial LCx ≥5.0, POC ≥7.2 mm²) drive optimisation.
  • 🔪 Hybrid & surgical nuance: LIMA-to-LAD is the survival graft; hybrid coronary revascularisation (LIMA-LAD + PCI of non-LAD) for selected anatomy; arterial vs venous grafting and completeness of revascularisation.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Unprotected Left Main We Ad-Hoc Stented"

The case: A 71-year-old diabetic came for a diagnostic angiogram that showed a distal left main bifurcation lesion, a diseased LAD and a diseased circumflex — a high SYNTAX score. Rather than stop, image, score and convene the Heart Team, the operator ad-hoc stented the unprotected left main in the same sitting, with no IVUS and no surgical back-up on stand-by. A single stent, sized to the LAD, was pushed into the LM without POT. On the next cine a dissection flap ran from the LM into the LAD; both branches lost flow. The unprotected left main closed, the whole left ventricle stunned, the BP collapsed. There was no Impella in the room and no surgeon in the building. The patient arrested and could not be recovered.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

⚡ AD-HOC REFLEXA complex, diabetic, high-SYNTAX left main stented on impulse instead of taken to the Heart Team.
👥 NO HEART TEAMFREEDOM/high SYNTAX both favoured CABG — never discussed.
🖥️ NO IVUSThe LM was neither sized nor optimised; the stent was under-sized to the LAD.
🎈 NO POTAn LAD-sized stent left the proximal LM under-expanded and the flap primed.
🫀 NO SUPPORT / NO SURGEONUnprotected LM, no MCS on stand-by, no surgical back-up when it closed.

🛡️ What would have saved him: STOP at the diagnostic run. Calculate SYNTAX, flag the diabetes, and convene the Heart Team — high SYNTAX diabetic multivessel disease favours CABG (FREEDOM/SYNTAX). If PCI were genuinely chosen, it would be an elective, protected procedure: IVUS-sized, provisional-first with POT (or planned DK crush), MCS available, surgery informed. The ad-hoc unprotected left main stent is the error this whole case exists to prevent. This is the drill in S9.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"There is no such thing as a routine ad-hoc unprotected left main. Stop, image, score, and convene the team — the reflex to stent it in the same sitting is the decision that kills."

🎯 The five things to carry out of this case

  1. 👥 Heart Team the left main — significant LM / multivessel disease is a shared decision, never an ad-hoc stent.
  2. 🧮 Score it (SYNTAX / SYNTAX II) — low/intermediate LM → PCI reasonable; high SYNTAX or diabetic multivessel → CABG (FREEDOM).
  3. ⚖️ Hold the EXCEL/NOBLE controversy honestly — the LM evidence is genuinely contested; present both paths.
  4. 🎈 Do the LM technique right — IVUS-size, provisional-first with POT, MSA targets (LM ≥8.7, ostial LAD ≥6.3 mm²).
  5. 🫀 Respect the unprotected left main — plan support and surgical back-up; a dissection arrests the whole LV.
