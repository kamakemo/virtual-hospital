📜 Past history — every line points to systemic sclerosis

Skin looks like the whole story, so clinicians focus on cosmetics while the vessels and lungs quietly fail. Scleroderma renal crisis was once the leading cause of death; the ACE inhibitor transformed a >90% mortality into a survivable event — but only if you recognise it and reach for the right drug.

🧭 The three things you confirm at the bedside first

Before anything else you nail the triad: (1) is this new/accelerated hypertension? (2) is the kidney acutely injured (rising creatinine, active urine)? (3) is there microangiopathic haemolysis (anaemia, low platelets, schistocytes, high LDH, low haptoglobin)? All three present here — this is renal crisis, and the clock on the kidney is already running.

⚠️ The trap of "just control the pressure with whatever is to hand"Any antihypertensive will lower the number, but only the ACE inhibitor addresses the mechanism (a renin-angiotensin storm from renal vascular injury) and preserves long-term renal function. Reaching for a calcium blocker or beta-blocker alone treats the manometer, not the disease.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The medical resident thinks out loud at 14:30:

🧠 Frame it as a renin-driven emergency, not "essential hypertension"

"She has diffuse scleroderma, she is anti-RNA-polymerase III positive, she was recently put on steroids, and now she has malignant hypertension, AKI and a microangiopathic blood film. This is textbook scleroderma renal crisis. My reflex is NOT to withhold treatment because the creatinine is rising — it is to START an ACE inhibitor (captopril) and titrate it up aggressively over hours to days. I expect creatinine to rise a bit further before it turns; that is not a reason to stop."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 💊 Start captopril 6.25–12.5 mg and up-titrate every few hours, aiming to drop systolic BP by ~20 mmHg per day toward normal (not crash it).
  2. 🩸 Confirm MAHA — film, LDH, haptoglobin, platelets, reticulocytes; send a direct antiglobulin test to show it is NOT immune haemolysis.
  3. 🧪 Renal work-up — U&E, urinalysis, protein:creatinine, renin (high), and importantly send disease serology if not already known.
  4. 🛑 Stop the prednisolone driver where possible and avoid escalating steroids — they can worsen crisis.
  5. 🖥️ Echo to screen for pulmonary hypertension and pericardial effusion; fundoscopy documented.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In scleroderma renal crisis, a rising creatinine after starting the ACE inhibitor is EXPECTED, not a signal to stop. You push the ACE inhibitor THROUGH the rise — withholding it is how kidneys are lost."
🧪 Resident's self-testThe creatinine climbs from 248 to 300 twelve hours after starting captopril. A colleague says "the ACE inhibitor is harming the kidney — stop it." Do you?

No. A transient rise in creatinine is the expected haemodynamic effect and does not mean the drug is failing. The evidence (Steen & Medsger) shows outcomes are best when the ACE inhibitor is continued and titrated to BP control, even if dialysis is temporarily needed — many patients recover renal function over weeks to months and come OFF dialysis. Stopping the ACE inhibitor is the classic error.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — the vasculopathy + fibrosis + autoimmunity triad (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 💥 Endothelial injury (vasculopathy): a triggered small-vessel endothelium is damaged and dysfunctional. Loss of vasodilators (nitric oxide, prostacyclin), excess endothelin-1 → vasoconstriction. This is Raynaud's, digital ulcers, PAH and — in the kidney — the renal crisis.
  2. 🛡️ Autoimmunity: T cells, B cells and autoantibodies (anti-Scl-70, anti-centromere, anti-RNA-pol III) drive and mark the disease; cytokines (TGF-β, IL-6, PDGF) recruit fibroblasts.
  3. 🧱 Fibrosis: activated fibroblasts/myofibroblasts pour out collagen. Skin thickens; the oesophagus stops moving; the lung interstitium scars; the heart fibroses.
  4. 🩸 The renal-crisis loop: intimal proliferation and fibrinoid necrosis narrow renal arterioles → renal ischaemia → massive RENIN release → angiotensin II surge → catastrophic vasoconstriction → more ischaemia → more renin. Malignant hypertension + AKI + shredding of red cells through narrowed vessels (MAHA). The ACE inhibitor breaks this loop at angiotensin II.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Scleroderma is not a skin disease — it is a small-vessel disease that happens to scar the skin. Vasculopathy + fibrosis + autoimmunity: master the triad and every organ complication falls into place."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Limited vs Diffuse cutaneous SSc

🌿 Limited (lcSSc / CREST)

  • Calcinosis, Raynaud's, oEsophageal, Sclerodactyly, Telangiectasia
  • Skin distal to elbows/knees + face
  • Anti-centromere; years of Raynaud's first
  • Late threat: pulmonary arterial hypertension

🔥 Diffuse (dcSSc)

  • Proximal + truncal skin, rapid onset
  • Anti-Scl-70 / anti-RNA-pol III
  • Raynaud's close to skin onset
  • Early threats: ILD and renal crisis (this patient)

💡 The subtype is not academic — it tells you which organ to screen and which emergency to expect.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (14:35)

  • 💊 Captopril 12.5 mg now, then up-titrate every 4–8 h to control BP (short-acting ACE inhibitor lets you titrate fast); target ~20 mmHg systolic fall per day.
  • 🩸 Bloods: FBC + film, LDH, haptoglobin, reticulocytes, U&E, LFTs, coagulation, direct antiglobulin test (expected negative), renin.
  • 🧪 Urine: dipstick, microscopy, protein:creatinine ratio.
  • 🧬 Autoantibodies if not already known: ANA, anti-Scl-70, anti-centromere, anti-RNA-polymerase III.
  • 🛑 Wean/stop the prednisolone and do NOT escalate steroids.
  • 🖥️ Echocardiogram (PAH screen, effusion, LV function) + fundoscopy documented; CXR ± HRCT for ILD baseline.
  • 📟 Continuous BP monitoring (consider arterial line), strict fluid balance, daily weights, hourly urine output.
  • 📞 Early nephrology + rheumatology involvement; plan for possible dialysis support WITHOUT stopping the ACE inhibitor.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Use a SHORT-acting ACE inhibitor (captopril) in renal crisis so you can titrate quickly — and never substitute it for a calcium blocker or beta-blocker as your primary agent."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Ward priorities — the nurse runs the safety net

  • 🩸 Frequent BP (every 15–30 min while titrating) on the same arm; flag both over- and under-shoot. The titration decision lives on your chart.
  • 💧 Strict fluid balance + hourly urine output — falling output warns of worsening AKI; document daily weights.
  • 🖐️ Protect the hands: keep the patient warm, avoid cold exposure and cannulating ischaemic digits; report new digital ulcers or blue fingertips (Raynaud's/critical ischaemia).
  • 🧠 Neuro obs for hypertensive encephalopathy; report worsening headache, visual change or confusion.
  • 💊 Never omit the ACE inhibitor dose without medical review — challenge any plan to hold it because "creatinine is up."
  • 🍽️ Reflux care: sit upright, small meals, proton-pump inhibitor; the fibrotic oesophagus aspirates easily.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who questions a held ACE-inhibitor dose in renal crisis is protecting the kidney — in this disease, an omitted dose is a lost nephron."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🩸 Blood pressure trend — the primary titration signal; aim for controlled decline (~20 mmHg systolic/day), not a crash that under-perfuses the injured kidney.
  • 🧫 Creatinine & urine output — expect a transient creatinine rise after starting the ACE inhibitor; watch the trajectory over days, not hours.
  • 🔴 Haemolysis markers (platelets, LDH, haptoglobin, film) — resolving MAHA signals the crisis is turning.
  • 🫁 Longitudinal SSc surveillance: annual echo + PFTs (DLCO) for PAH and ILD, even after this crisis settles.
  • 🧠 Neuro and visual status for hypertensive encephalopathy/retinopathy resolution.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you start the ACE inhibitor, the monitor's question is not 'is the creatinine up today?' but 'is the BP controlled and the haemolysis settling over the week?' — judge the disease on the trend."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 The organ threats of systemic sclerosis

  • 🩸 Scleroderma renal crisis — malignant hypertension + AKI + MAHA; may need temporary dialysis. Treat with ACE inhibitor.
  • 🫁 Interstitial lung disease — the leading cause of death; progressive breathlessness, falling FVC/DLCO.
  • 🫀 Pulmonary arterial hypertension — especially limited disease; right heart failure if missed.
  • ❄️ Digital ischaemia / ulcers / gangrene — from the peripheral vasculopathy.
  • 🍽️ GI: severe reflux/strictures, gastric antral vascular ectasia (watermelon stomach), pseudo-obstruction, malabsorption from bacterial overgrowth.
  • ❤️ Cardiac: myocardial fibrosis, arrhythmia, pericardial effusion.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Scleroderma kills through the LUNG (ILD/PAH) and the KIDNEY (renal crisis) far more than the skin — screen the silent organs before they declare."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🩸 Diagnosis: scleroderma renal crisis (diffuse dcSSc, anti-RNA-pol III, steroid-precipitated) — malignant HTN + AKI + MAHA.
  2. 💊 Start and up-titrate captopril to controlled BP; continue through any transient creatinine rise, even onto dialysis.
  3. 🛑 Stop/avoid steroids; supportive renal replacement therapy if needed (many recover and come off dialysis over months).
  4. 🖥️ Screen the other organs: echo (PAH), HRCT + PFTs (ILD), and treat what you find (mycophenolate/nintedanib for ILD; PAH therapy for confirmed PAH).
  5. 🖐️ Long-term SSc care: Raynaud's/digital-ulcer therapy, reflux management, annual PAH/ILD surveillance.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Renal crisis is not the end of the kidney — with a titrated ACE inhibitor, many patients who need dialysis recover renal function over 6–18 months. Persistence with the right drug wins."

🏠 Discharge

📋 After the crisis — two parallel plans

🩸 Renal-crisis survivor

  • Continue ACE inhibitor long-term; monitor renal function & BP closely
  • Home BP diary; teach red flags (headache, visual change, falling urine)
  • Avoid steroids; document the trigger in the record
  • Nephrology follow-up; dialysis review if still dependent

🖐️ Whole-disease scleroderma care

  • Rheumatology follow-up; annual PAH (echo) + ILD (PFTs) screening
  • Raynaud's/digital-ulcer plan: keep warm, calcium blocker, escalate if ulcers
  • Reflux/nutrition management; consider ILD immunosuppression if progressive
  • Patient education: it is a systemic vascular disease, not just skin
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Discharge from a renal crisis is a beginning, not an end: the ACE inhibitor stays, the steroids stay away, and the lungs and pulmonary artery get screened for life."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🧬 Normotensive renal crisis: ~10% present without severe hypertension — usually steroid-associated with prominent MAHA. A high index of suspicion is needed; still treat with ACE inhibitors and it carries a worse prognosis.
  • 🩸 Renal recovery kinetics: up to half of dialysis-dependent renal-crisis patients recover enough function to discontinue dialysis, sometimes 6–18 months later — so give the ACE inhibitor and kidney time before declaring end-stage.
  • 🫁 SSc-ILD treatment stack (2023 evidence): mycophenolate first-line (SLS II), tocilizumab (focuSSced) to preserve FVC in early inflammatory ILD, and nintedanib (SENSCIS) added for progressive fibrosis — increasingly combined.
  • 🫀 PAH nuance: the DETECT algorithm improves early detection; group 1 PAH in SSc is treated with combination upfront therapy (endothelin antagonist + PDE5 inhibitor); riociguat for select cases; screen because SSc-PAH has worse outcomes than idiopathic PAH.
  • 🔄 Autologous haematopoietic stem-cell transplantation: ASSIST, ASTIS and SCOT trials show event-free survival benefit in selected early, severe, progressive diffuse SSc — at the cost of upfront treatment-related mortality; patient selection is everything.
  • 💉 Emerging biology: anti-IL-6 (tocilizumab), rituximab (B-cell depletion), and antifibrotics reflect the shift toward targeting the fibro-inflammatory drivers rather than just supportive care.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Steroids That Broke the Kidney"

The case: A 45-year-old woman with 8 months of rapidly thickening skin, Raynaud's and puffy hands was diagnosed with early diffuse scleroderma. Her arthralgia and swelling were troublesome, so she was started on prednisolone 30 mg daily to "settle the inflammation." Three weeks later she presented with headache and BP 236/134; creatinine had risen from 82 to 470 with schistocytes and thrombocytopenia. A junior started captopril — but overnight the creatinine climbed to 560, and a covering doctor, alarmed, stopped the ACE inhibitor and switched to amlodipine "to protect the kidney." The BP fell but the kidney did not recover. She became permanently dialysis-dependent.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

💊 STEROID TRIGGERHigh-dose prednisolone in early diffuse SSc precipitated renal crisis.
🧬 RISK NOT FLAGGEDAnti-RNA-pol III / diffuse subtype meant renal crisis was foreseeable; no BP monitoring set up.
🔁 ACE INHIBITOR STOPPEDThe rising creatinine was misread as harm; the one kidney-saving drug was withdrawn.
🔄 WRONG SUBSTITUTEA calcium blocker controlled the number but not the renin loop.
🗣️ HANDOVER GAPThe plan to persist with the ACE inhibitor through the creatinine rise was never communicated.

🛡️ What would have saved her kidney: avoid moderate/high-dose steroids in diffuse SSc; recognise a foreseeable renal crisis and monitor BP; start an ACE inhibitor AND continue it through the expected creatinine rise, supporting with dialysis if needed rather than withdrawing the drug; and hand over explicitly that a rising creatinine is not a reason to stop. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 7.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In scleroderma renal crisis, two mistakes lose kidneys: giving the steroids that trigger it, and stopping the ACE inhibitor that treats it. Know both cold."
