🧬 Why THIS patient? — plaque rupture meets a closed pipe

An atherosclerotic plaque in his proximal LAD — built over decades by smoking, hypertension and high LDL — developed a thin, inflamed fibrous cap. This morning that cap ruptured, exposing the lipid core and tissue factor to flowing blood. Platelets swarmed, aggregated, and a platelet-rich thrombus grew until it acutely and totally occluded the vessel. Downstream, the entire anterior wall is now without blood. Necrosis begins at the endocardium (furthest from epicardial flow) and marches outward toward the epicardium as a wavefront over the next several hours. The ST elevation on the monitor is the electrical signature of that transmural injury. The clock that started 40 minutes ago is counting down living muscle.

The cardiology resident arrives at 08:44. Her reasoning, out loud:

🧠 Pattern recognition first, then physiology

"Crushing pain, diaphoresis, ST elevation V1–V4 with reciprocal inferior depression in a man with every coronary risk factor going. That is an acute proximal LAD occlusion — I don't need the troponin to act, and waiting for it would be malpractice. The reciprocal changes aren't noise; they raise my confidence that this is a true coronary occlusion and not pericarditis or early repolarization. The dangerous instinct here is delay — for a CT, for a second ECG, for bloods. The artery is shut now."

🎯 Immediate priorities (her actual order of operations)

  1. 🎈 Confirm cath lab is activated and rolling. Reperfusion is the whole game; everything else is adjunctive. Say the door-to-balloon clock out loud.
  2. 💊 Dual antiplatelet: aspirin (already 300 mg) plus a potent P2Y12 inhibitor — ticagrelor or prasugrel, preferred over clopidogrel.
  3. 🩸 Anticoagulation: unfractionated heparin (or bivalirudin) per the cath lab's protocol.
  4. 🩻 Exclude aortic dissection in 20 seconds: both arm pulses, inter-arm BP, tearing/back pain, mediastinum on any prior imaging — before heparin.
  5. 😖 Treat pain and hypoxia: IV opioid for ongoing pain (which lowers oxygen demand), oxygen only if SpO₂ < 90%. High-intensity statin started from day 1.
  6. 🚫 Hold the reflexes that harm: no routine oxygen if sats are fine, and no IV beta-blocker while there are any signs of early failure or shock (the COMMIT lesson).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Open the artery first; load the antiplatelets and anticoagulant on the way; rule out dissection before the heparin — and keep the beta-blocker in your pocket until you are sure he isn't failing."

🤔 "But shouldn't we wait for the troponin to be sure?"

"No. STEMI is an electrocardiographic and clinical diagnosis. The troponin will be normal or barely up this early — necrosis takes hours to leak detectable enzyme. If I wait for a positive troponin, I am waiting for muscle to die before I act. The ECG plus the story is the diagnosis. Troponin is for risk stratification and for the NSTEMI/unstable-angina branch — not for licensing reperfusion in an obvious STEMI."

🧪 Resident's self-test (answer before reading on) A colleague wants to push IV metoprolol now "to slow the heart and rest the myocardium." Good idea in this patient?

Not yet — and be careful. He has a tachycardia of 110 and crackles at the bases — early pump dysfunction. COMMIT showed that early routine IV beta-blockade in unstable STEMI increased cardiogenic shock. Beta-blockers are valuable in STEMI, but give them oral, once hemodynamically stable, and avoid them in acute heart failure or shock. In a patient with signs of failing, an IV beta-blocker bolus can tip him into shock.

📖 Teaching Points

🔬 The mechanism — WHY a ruptured plaque kills muscle (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧱 The substrate: decades of smoking, hypertension and high LDL build a lipid-laden atheroma in the LAD with a thin, inflamed fibrous cap.
  2. 💥 The trigger — plaque RUPTURE: the cap tears (often a modest stenosis, not the tightest one), exposing the thrombogenic lipid core and tissue factor to flowing blood.
  3. 🩸 Thrombosis: platelets adhere, activate and aggregate into a platelet-rich thrombus; the coagulation cascade locks it in with fibrin until the LAD is acutely and totally occluded.
  4. 🌊 The wavefront of necrosis: downstream myocardium loses all flow. Necrosis starts at the endocardium (last to receive epicardial perfusion, highest wall stress) and advances outward to become transmural over hours — hence "time is muscle." ST elevation reflects this transmural injury current.
  5. 💀 Why it kills: a large anterior infarct destroys pump function → cardiogenic shock; the ischemic, electrically unstable border zone spawns VT/VF; days later the necrotic wall can rupture. Open the artery early and you abort the wavefront, salvaging muscle, pump and rhythm.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"STEMI is a clot stopping a wavefront of death from the inside out. Reperfusion reverses the wavefront — and every 30 minutes you save is muscle, pump and life you keep."

⚖️ Contrast pair #1 — STEMI vs PERICARDITIS (the benign mimic that must not be lysed)

🫀 STEMI IS… (act on this)

  • Crushing, pressure pain ± radiation to arm/jaw, diaphoresis
  • Regional ST elevation matching a coronary territory (e.g. V1–V4)
  • Reciprocal ST depression in opposite leads
  • Convex ("tombstone") ST morphology, evolving Q waves
  • Rising troponin over hours; reperfuse urgently

🩹 Pericarditis is NOT that…

  • Sharp, pleuritic/positional pain, better leaning forward
  • Diffuse, concave ST elevation across many territories
  • PR depression; NO reciprocal changes (except aVR/V1)
  • Often a friction rub; viral prodrome
  • Do NOT thrombolyse — anticoagulation risks hemorrhagic effusion

😈 The trap: diffuse concave ST elevation gets mistaken for STEMI (over-treatment), or a true STEMI with subtle elevation gets dismissed as "pericarditis" (catastrophic under-treatment). Reciprocal change and a regional, convex pattern point to occlusion; PR depression with diffuse concave elevation and no reciprocal change point to pericarditis.

⚖️ Contrast pair #2 — TYPE 1 MI vs TYPE 2 MI

🩸 Type 1 MI (tonight)

  • Primary plaque rupture + thrombus → coronary occlusion
  • Treat the clot: antiplatelets, anticoagulant, reperfusion
  • The classic STEMI pathway

⚖️ Type 2 MI

  • Supply–demand mismatch — tachyarrhythmia, anemia, hypotension, sepsis, severe hypertension
  • No acute plaque event; ischemia from imbalance
  • Treat the trigger (rate, blood, BP, sepsis); reperfusion is usually not the answer

💡 Why it matters: reflexively cath-and-stent a Type 2 troponin rise and you expose a patient to procedural and bleeding risk for a problem that needed a transfusion or rate control. Read the whole picture — but a convincing STEMI ECG with the right story is Type 1 until proven otherwise.

🔬 Fellowship Corner — Advanced Topics

  • 🩻 Subtle/occlusion-MI ECG patterns: de Winter T waves (upsloping ST depression + tall T in precordials = LAD occlusion equivalent), Wellens' syndrome (biphasic/deep T in V2–V3 = critical proximal LAD stenosis), hyperacute T waves, and posterior MI (tall R + ST depression V1–V3). Sgarbossa/modified-Sgarbossa criteria for STEMI in LBBB or paced rhythm.
  • 🎈 PCI technicalities: radial-first access, aspiration thrombectomy (no longer routine — TOTAL/TASTE), drug-eluting stents, intracoronary imaging (IVUS/OCT) for stent optimization, and physiology (FFR/iFR) for non-culprit lesions.
  • 💔 Cardiogenic shock pathway: CULPRIT-SHOCK culprit-only strategy, vasopressors/inotropes, mechanical support (IABP — neutral in IABP-SHOCK II; Impella; VA-ECMO), and early shock-team activation.
  • 🧨 Mechanical complications (days 3–7): free-wall rupture (tamponade), ventricular septal rupture (new harsh murmur + shock), papillary muscle rupture (acute severe MR, flash pulmonary edema) — surgical emergencies.
  • 🩸 MINOCA & mimics: myocardial infarction with non-obstructive coronaries — plaque erosion, coronary spasm, embolism, SCAD (especially younger women), Takotsubo. The angiogram doesn't always show a classic culprit.
  • 💉 Antithrombotic nuances: bivalirudin vs heparin, cangrelor for the P2Y12-naïve/vomiting patient, GP IIb/IIIa bailout for large thrombus burden, and DAPT-duration tailoring by bleeding/ischemic risk.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set, written as you'd actually write it (08:45)

  • 🎈 ACTIVATE / CONFIRM CATH LAB — PRIMARY PCI. Door-to-balloon target < 90 min. This order outranks all others.
  • 💊 Aspirin 300 mg chewed (given pre-hospital — confirm) then 75–100 mg daily.
  • 💊 P2Y12 inhibitor: ticagrelor 180 mg load (or prasugrel 60 mg if PCI planned and no prior stroke/TIA, age/weight appropriate). Clopidogrel 600 mg only if the potent agents are contraindicated.
  • 🩸 Anticoagulation: unfractionated heparin IV bolus per cath-lab protocol (or bivalirudin) — only after dissection is excluded.
  • 🩻 Dissection check FIRST: both arm BPs/pulses, tearing/back pain, review CXR mediastinum — document before heparin.
  • 😖 Analgesia: IV opioid (e.g. morphine 2–4 mg) titrated for ongoing pain; antiemetic as needed.
  • 🫁 Oxygen ONLY if SpO₂ < 90% — routine oxygen in normoxic MI is not beneficial (and may harm). Target ≥ 90–94%.
  • 💉 High-intensity statin from day 1 (e.g. atorvastatin 80 mg) regardless of baseline LDL.
  • 🚫 NO routine IV beta-blocker while there are signs of failure/shock (COMMIT); oral beta-blocker once stable. Avoid nitrates if hypotensive or RV involvement suspected.
  • 🧪 Bloods: troponin (for trend, not to license reperfusion), U&E, glucose/HbA1c, lipids, CBC, coagulation; continuous ECG monitoring; serial 12-leads. NPO for the lab.
  • 🔌 Defibrillator at the bedside — anterior STEMI is the highest-risk territory for VT/VF.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In STEMI there is one priority — open the artery in time — and a short list of drugs that make it safer: aspirin, a potent P2Y12, an anticoagulant, analgesia and a statin. Everything else waits."

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ CCU nursing priorities — minute by minute

  • 📟 Continuous cardiac monitoring with the defibrillator at the bedside, pads ready. Anterior STEMI is the highest-risk territory for VT/VF — be one button from defibrillation.
  • ⏱️ Own the clock: note and call out first-medical-contact time and the door-to-balloon target; every avoidable minute on the ward erodes salvage.
  • 🩸 BP every 5 min in both arms initially (a real inter-arm difference is a dissection flag) and continuous once the arterial line is in.
  • 💊 Give the dual antiplatelet and anticoagulant promptly and accurately — confirm the P2Y12 choice and that aspirin was truly given; document times.
  • 😖 Titrate analgesia and reassess pain — ongoing pain means ongoing ischemia; report a pain that won't settle.
  • 🫁 Oxygen only if SpO₂ < 90% — don't reflexively mask a normoxic patient.
  • 🩻 Prep for the cath lab: consent support, shave/clean access sites, remove metal, IV access patent, NPO, weight for drug dosing.
  • 🧠 Watch for deterioration: falling BP, rising HR, new breathlessness/crackles, a new murmur, or sudden collapse → escalate immediately.
  • 👨‍👩‍👦 Keep the patient and family informed and calm (see S16) — fear drives catecholamines and oxygen demand. A composed bedside is therapeutic.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who guards the door-to-balloon clock, keeps the defibrillator within arm's reach and checks both arm pressures is doing half the reperfusion work in the room."
📢 Escalate IMMEDIATELY forVT/VF on the monitor (→ defibrillate) · falling BP / rising HR (cardiogenic shock) · new breathlessness or pink frothy sputum (pulmonary edema) · a new harsh murmur (VSD / papillary rupture) · ongoing or recurrent pain · an inter-arm BP difference or tearing back pain (dissection) · any plan for an IV beta-blocker in a failing patient.

📈 ECG / Imaging

🫀 The presenting ECG — read it like a detective

Convex ("tombstone") ST elevation across V1–V4 from a proximal LAD occlusion, with reciprocal ST depression in the inferior leads. The regional, convex pattern plus reciprocal change is near-diagnostic of acute coronary occlusion.

🔎 The STEMI-localization toolkit (memorize the high-yield map)

LeadsTerritory / artery — and what to fear
📈 V1–V4Anterior — LAD. The biggest infarct; proximal occlusion (aVR/V1 elevation, new RBBB/LAFB) means the largest territory and highest shock/arrhythmia risk.
↘️ II, III, aVFInferior — RCA (or LCx). Always add V4R: RV involvement makes the patient preload-dependent — nitrates can crash the BP.
↔️ I, aVL, V5–V6Lateral — circumflex (LCx), often electrically "silent."
🪞 V1–V3 ST depression + tall RPosterior MI — a mirror image; confirm with posterior leads (V7–V9). Easily missed.
🔄 Reciprocal changesST depression in leads opposite the infarct — they strengthen the diagnosis of true occlusion and argue against pericarditis/early repolarization.
🧩 STEMI equivalentsde Winter T waves, Wellens', hyperacute T waves, posterior MI, and STEMI in LBBB/paced rhythm (Sgarbossa) — occlusions hiding without classic elevation.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Regional convex ST elevation WITH reciprocal change is an occluded artery. Diffuse concave elevation with PR depression and no reciprocal change is pericarditis. The reciprocal lead is your lie-detector."

🫀 Adjunctive imaging

  • 📈 Coronary angiography (the definitive test & the treatment): expected 100% proximal LAD occlusion with a fresh thrombus — proceed straight to primary PCI of the culprit.
  • 🩻 Echo (peri-/post-procedure): anterior/apical regional wall-motion abnormality; estimate EF; screen for mechanical complications (VSD, MR, free-wall rupture) and later LV thrombus in an akinetic apex.
  • 🖥️ CXR: assess for pulmonary congestion and — importantly — mediastinal width as a dissection screen before anticoagulation.
⚠️ The ECG's favorite lie in chest pain"It's diffuse, so it's just pericarditis" — said over a true STEMI. Or the opposite: a normal-looking trace hiding a de Winter or posterior occlusion. The reassuring signs are a clearly regional pattern, reciprocal change, and an evolving troponin — and a prior ECG to compare. When the story is classic and the ECG shows territorial elevation, treat as occlusion.

🏠 Secondary-prevention therapy (this admission, then for life)

  • 💊 DAPT for ≥ 12 months (aspirin + ticagrelor/prasugrel), then aspirin lifelong — tailor by bleeding/ischemic risk.
  • 💙 High-intensity statin ± ezetimibe/PCSK9 to drive LDL well below 1.8 (often < 1.4) mmol/L.
  • 🫀 ACEi/ARB, beta-blocker, and MRA if EF < 40% — the post-MI remodeling-prevention quartet; add SGLT2i where indicated.
  • 🚭 Smoking cessation, cardiac rehab, BP and glucose control — the interventions that change his next decade.
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📈 Continuous telemetry with the defibrillator at the bedside for ≥ 24–48 h — anterior STEMI carries the highest VT/VF and AV-block risk; print any ectopy.
  • 📉 Serial 12-leads and ST-segment monitoring — track ST resolution (≥ 50% by 60–90 min signals reperfusion), and catch re-occlusion or new territory.
  • 🩸 Beat-to-beat BP (arterial line) through PCI and the early hours — a falling pressure heralds cardiogenic shock; rising suggests pain/anxiety.
  • 🫁 Respiratory status & SpO₂ — new crackles, breathlessness or frothy sputum = developing pulmonary edema/pump failure.
  • 🩺 Daily exam for mechanical complications (days 3–7): a new harsh murmur (VSD or papillary rupture), muffled sounds/pulsus paradoxus (rupture/tamponade).
  • 🧪 Serial troponin, renal function and glucose — infarct size, contrast nephropathy, and stress hyperglycemia/diabetes management.
  • 🩻 Post-procedure echo for EF and wall motion — drives ACEi/MRA, ICD timing (reassess EF ≥ 40 days later), and LV-thrombus surveillance in an akinetic apex.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you open the artery, the monitor's job changes from 'is it still occluded?' to 'is the pump failing or the rhythm turning?' — and the two readouts that answer it are the blood pressure and the ST segments."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — and the mechanism

  • ⚡ Ventricular arrhythmia (VT/VF) / cardiac arrest — the ischemic anterior border zone is electrically unstable; the leading early killer. Defibrillator at the bedside.
  • 💔 Cardiogenic shock / acute pump failure — a large anterior infarct destroys contractile mass; rising filling pressures → pulmonary edema → shock. Culprit-only PCI, support devices.
  • 🧨 Mechanical complications (days 3–7): free-wall rupture (tamponade), ventricular septal rupture (new murmur + shock), papillary muscle rupture (acute severe MR, flash edema) — surgical emergencies.
  • 🚧 Conduction block — anterior MI can cause infranodal/bundle-branch block and complete heart block (a bad-prognosis sign from a large infarct); have pacing available.
  • 🩸 Bleeding / stent thrombosis — the price of antithrombotics; and re-occlusion if antiplatelets are interrupted early.
  • 🫀 LV thrombus, aneurysm, pericarditis, and later remodeling/heart failure — the akinetic apex breeds clot and, weeks on, Dressler's pericarditis or chronic HFrEF.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference — "The STEMI That Was a Dissection"

The case: A 64-year-old man arrived with severe chest pain and inferior ST changes. A busy night team read "inferior STEMI," and with the cath lab 30 minutes away the senior chose thrombolysis to "save time." Nobody felt both radial pulses; nobody asked about the pain radiating to the back. It was an aortic dissection that had sheared into the right coronary ostium and mimicked an MI. The lytic converted a contained dissection into a hemopericardium with tamponade. He arrested within twenty minutes and could not be saved. The nursing observations recorded a 20 mmHg inter-arm BP difference — unread.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Chest pain + ST changes = STEMI" — the diagnosis closed before dissection was considered.
🩻 PULSES NOT FELTBoth radials and inter-arm BP were never compared at the bedside.
📋 SIGN UNREADA 20 mmHg inter-arm difference sat in the notes, never acted on.
💉 LETHAL DRUGFibrinolysis into a dissection → hemopericardium → tamponade → arrest.
🌙 SYSTEMNight fatigue, time pressure, no "rule-out-dissection-before-lysis" default.

🛡️ What would have saved him: a 20-second dissection screen before any anticoagulant or lytic — feel both radial pulses, compare arm BPs, ask about tearing/back pain, glance at the mediastinum; favor primary PCI (where the angiogram reveals the truth) over blind lysis; and never anchor on "STEMI" without the bedside exam. This is exactly why your consultant feels both wrists on every chest-pain patient tonight.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'It's obviously a STEMI, just lyse it' is the sentence on the chart of patients who die of dissection. Tearing pain or unequal pulses must be excluded before any lytic or anticoagulant."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Discharge plan & safety net

  • 💊 Medications: aspirin + ticagrelor (DAPT ≥ 12 months — do NOT stop without cardiology advice), high-intensity statin, ACEi, beta-blocker, MRA if indicated, with a clear written schedule.
  • 🩺 Stent counselling: why dual antiplatelet therapy prevents stent thrombosis, the danger of interrupting it for procedures (always ask cardiology first), and carrying a stent/medication card.
  • 🚭 Risk-factor plan: structured smoking cessation (NRT/varenicline), BP and diabetes management, diet and weight, with clinic and GP follow-up booked before he leaves.
  • 🏃 Cardiac rehabilitation enrollment; graded activity advice; driving and return-to-work guidance per local rules after MI.
  • 🚩 Red flags → call emergency services: recurrent chest pain/pressure (especially at rest or unrelieved by GTN), breathlessness, fainting, palpitations — "treat new chest pain as the artery re-closing until proven otherwise."
  • 📚 Education: what a heart attack is in plain language, why each tablet matters, the absolute importance of not stopping antiplatelets, and CPR/AED awareness offered to his wife.

💎 Clinical Pearls

⏱️ Time is muscleEvery 30-minute delay to reperfusion adds roughly 8% to one-year mortality. The clock is the diagnosis-after-the-diagnosis.
🎈 Door-to-balloon < 90Primary PCI within 90 min door-to-device; FMC-to-device < 120 min. This is the number that everything else serves.
📈 ECG diagnoses STEMIRegional ST elevation + reciprocal change = occlusion. Never wait for the troponin to act.
💊 Potent P2Y12Ticagrelor or prasugrel beat clopidogrel in most STEMI (PLATO/TRITON). Clopidogrel is the fallback.
🚫 No early IV beta-blockerAvoid in acute heart failure or shock — COMMIT showed it precipitates cardiogenic shock. Oral once stable.
🩻 Rule out dissectionTearing-to-the-back pain or unequal pulses? Exclude aortic dissection before any lytic or anticoagulant.
🩸 PCI > lysis when timelyPrimary PCI is the standard if achievable < 120 min of FMC (DANAMI-2). Lyse only when PCI isn't timely.
🪞 Reciprocal change is realST depression opposite the infarct strengthens the diagnosis and argues against pericarditis.
🗺️ V1–V4 = LADAnterior = the biggest, most dangerous infarct. Inferior STEMI? Get V4R for the RV before any nitrate.
💙 Statin from day 1High-intensity statin on day 1 regardless of LDL; ACEi within 24 h; MRA if EF < 40%.
⚖️ Type 1 vs Type 2Plaque rupture (Type 1) → reperfuse. Supply–demand mismatch (Type 2) → treat the trigger.
🩹 STEMI vs pericarditisDiffuse concave ST + PR depression, no reciprocal change, pleuritic/positional pain = pericarditis — do not lyse.
