🔥 Why the failing RV is so treacherousThe right ventricle is a thin-walled, compliant volume-pump built to eject into a low-pressure lung circuit. It is exquisitely afterload-sensitive — a small rise in pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) sharply drops its output. And unlike the LV, its failure signature is systemic venous congestion with relatively clear lungs, so it is repeatedly mis-read as "dehydration" or "sepsis" and given fluid — the one thing it cannot tolerate.

🧭 The one physiological question that governs everything

At the bedside, decide: is this RV under-filled or over-filled? Almost always in decompensated PH the RV is congested and over-distended — the JVP is sky-high, the liver is pulsatile, there is ascites and oedema. That patient needs decongestion and perfusion-pressure support, NOT volume. Only the genuinely under-filled RV (e.g. an acute RV infarct with flat neck veins, or over-diuresis) benefits from a small, cautious fluid challenge.

⚠️ The trap of "the BP is low, so give fluid"In an over-filled, dilated RV, more preload stretches an already-failing chamber further, bows the interventricular septum into the LV, under-fills the LV, and drops systemic output even more. The "fluid responsive" reflex is the single most dangerous move in RV failure.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The cardiology resident thinks out loud at 14:26:

🧠 Frame it as a pump with the wrong loading conditions

"This is decompensated RIGHT heart failure on a background of pulmonary hypertension. He's congested AND under-perfused — the worst quadrant. My job is to optimise (not overload) preload, reduce RV afterload / treat the cause, support contractility, and protect the systemic perfusion pressure so the RV coronary keeps flowing. I am NOT giving him a fluid bolus. I want an urgent bedside echo and I need to know his PH group before anyone reaches for a pulmonary vasodilator."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🫀 Bedside echo now — RV size & function, septal shift (D-shaped LV), TAPSE, estimated PASP, IVC, tamponade/effusion, and crucially the LV (to sort pre- vs post-capillary PH).
  2. 💧 Optimise preload — because he is congested, start/uptitrate IV loop diuretic (± infusion); NO routine fluid bolus.
  3. 🩸 Maintain perfusion pressure — early noradrenaline to keep MAP and thus RV coronary perfusion; this is protective, not cosmetic.
  4. 💪 Support contractility — add an inodilator (dobutamine or milrinone) if output stays low despite adequate MAP.
  5. 🫁 Treat the cause / reduce afterload — correct hypoxia, hypercapnia and acidosis (all raise PVR); look hard for a precipitant (PE, sepsis, arrhythmia, missed PH meds).
  6. 📞 Early PH / ICU discussion — refractory RV failure may need mechanical support (RVAD/VA-ECMO) and a specialist centre.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Optimise, reduce, support, perfuse: OPTIMISE preload (usually diurese), REDUCE afterload, SUPPORT contractility, and PERFUSE the RV with adequate aortic pressure."
🧪 Resident's self-testHe is hypotensive. A colleague wants to give 1 litre of saline "to fill the tank." Correct?

No. This RV is congested and over-distended (JVP to the ear, ascites, oedema). Fluid loading a failing, dilated RV worsens the septal shift, under-fills the LV, and drops systemic output further — it can precipitate arrest. The right moves are decongestion + noradrenaline to protect coronary perfusion + inotrope. Reserve a small (≈250 mL) fluid challenge only for the genuinely under-filled RV with flat neck veins.

🎓 Consultant Round + Evidence-Based Medicine

The PH/HF consultant at the bedside, 14:34:

🫁 The WHO classification of pulmonary hypertension — and why treatment lives or dies by the group

Haemodynamic definition (2022 ESC/ERS): PH is a resting mean pulmonary artery pressure > 20 mmHg. The critical split is pre-capillary (PVR > 2 Wood units, pulmonary artery wedge pressure ≤ 15) versus post-capillary (wedge > 15 — a LEFT-heart problem).

WHO GroupCausePre/Post-capillaryTreatment principle
1 — PAHPulmonary arterial hypertension (idiopathic, heritable, connective-tissue disease, drugs, congenital)Pre-capillaryTargeted pulmonary vasodilators (endothelin antagonists, PDE5i/riociguat, prostacyclins)
2 — LEFT heartHFrEF, HFpEF, valve disease — the commonest causePost-capillaryTreat the LEFT heart; do NOT give PAH vasodilators (risk of flash pulmonary oedema)
3 — Lung / hypoxiaCOPD, ILD, sleep-disordered breathing, chronic hypoxiaPre-capillaryTreat lung disease + oxygen; correct hypoxia (a potent pulmonary vasoconstrictor)
4 — CTEPHChronic thromboembolic PHPre-capillaryLifelong anticoagulation; pulmonary endarterectomy / balloon angioplasty ± riociguat
5 — Misc / multifactorialSarcoid, haematological, metabolic, mechanicalMixedTreat the underlying disorder
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Never give a pulmonary vasodilator to pulmonary hypertension caused by the LEFT heart (group 2) — you'll flood the lungs. Classify BEFORE you vasodilate."

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — the RV FAILURE SPIRAL you will never forget

  1. 🫀 The thin, afterload-sensitive RV: the right ventricle is a compliant volume-pump made for a low-pressure circuit. A small rise in pulmonary vascular resistance (PH, PE, hypoxia, high PEEP) — or over-filling it with volume — sharply reduces its output.
  2. 🎈 RV dilatation & rising pressure: the overloaded RV enlarges and its wall tension climbs; tricuspid annulus stretches → functional TR → more volume churning back into the right atrium and the systemic veins (JVP↑, liver, ascites, oedema).
  3. ↔️ Septal shift: the swollen RV pushes the interventricular septum into the LV — the LV becomes D-shaped and is mechanically UNDER-FILLED (ventricular interdependence within a shared pericardium).
  4. 📉 Systemic output & BP fall: an under-filled LV ejects less → cardiac output and aortic pressure drop.
  5. 🩸 RV coronary starvation: the RV coronary is perfused in BOTH systole and diastole and depends on aortic pressure. A falling aortic pressure means falling RV coronary flow → RV ischaemia → weaker RV → the spiral tightens toward collapse.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The right ventricle hates afterload and hates over-filling — fix the lungs and DECONGEST, don't drown it in fluid."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Right ventricular failure vs Left ventricular failure

🫀 RV failure

  • Raised JVP, oedema, ascites, pulsatile liver
  • Lungs relatively CLEAR; hypotension, low output
  • Preload- and afterload-SENSITIVE
  • Fix: decongest, reduce PVR, support perfusion pressure — usually fluid OFF

🫁 LV failure

  • Pulmonary congestion / crackles / orthopnoea dominate
  • Pink frothy sputum, bibasal crepitations
  • Fix: preload & afterload reduction, decongestion of the LUNGS
  • PH from LV disease = Group 2 (post-capillary)

💡 And the second essential contrast: pre-capillary PH (groups 1/3/4 — treat the pulmonary vasculature/lung/clots) vs post-capillary PH (group 2 — treat the LEFT heart, do NOT vasodilate the lungs).

🎮 Simulation — The RV Loading Dial & the Spiral

The thin RV lives or dies by its loading conditions. Drag the dial to change total RV load (preload + pulmonary afterload) and watch RV output — there is a middle sweet spot. Too little and it can't fill; too much and it over-distends, the septum shifts, and the spiral begins.

Set the dial to explore under-filling, the sweet spot, and the failure spiral.

🎯 The goal of RV-failure management is to move the patient back INTO the sweet spot — decongest an over-loaded RV, and give only a small cautious fluid challenge to a truly under-filled one. Overshooting either way costs output.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (14:24)

  • 🫀 Urgent bedside echo — RV size/function (TAPSE, S′), septal shift/D-sign, estimated PASP, IVC, effusion; assess the LV to classify PH group.
  • 💧 IV loop diuretic (bolus then infusion) for decongestion — target a net negative balance; NO routine fluid bolus. A small (≈250 mL) challenge ONLY if echo shows a genuinely under-filled RV.
  • 🩸 Noradrenaline infusion via central access to maintain MAP ≥ 65 mmHg and protect RV coronary perfusion.
  • 💪 Inodilator (dobutamine or milrinone) if low-output state persists despite adequate MAP.
  • 🫁 Oxygen to correct hypoxia (hypoxia raises PVR); check ABG for hypercapnia/acidosis and correct; avoid over-sedation/hypoventilation.
  • 🧪 Bloods: NT-proBNP, troponin, lactate, U&E/LFTs (congestive hepatopathy), FBC, coag, ABG; consider CTPA if PE suspected.
  • 💊 Continue his established PAH therapy (endothelin antagonist, PDE5 inhibitor) — abrupt withdrawal can precipitate rebound PH crisis.
  • 📞 Early referral to the pulmonary hypertension centre / ICU; escalate toward mechanical support if refractory.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In decompensated RV failure the default order is DIURESE and PERFUSE — not a bag of saline; and never stop the PAH drugs abruptly."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Ward priorities — the nurse guards the loading conditions

  • ⚖️ Strict fluid balance and daily weights — the failing RV is a fluid problem; every mL in and out matters. Challenge any unnecessary IV fluids or "maintenance" saline.
  • 🩸 Continuous BP / MAP monitoring (arterial line if on pressors) — a falling MAP is directly dangerous to the RV coronary; titrate noradrenaline to target.
  • 🌊 Track the JVP, oedema, girth and liver tenderness shift-to-shift — rising congestion means the decongestion is not keeping up.
  • 🫁 Maintain oxygenation and avoid hypoventilation/over-sedation — hypoxia, hypercapnia and acidosis all raise pulmonary afterload.
  • 💉 Never interrupt continuous prostacyclin / PAH infusions — a line disconnection or pump failure can trigger a fatal rebound PH crisis; know the emergency backup.
  • 🧾 Keep the patient calm and upright; anxiety and pain raise catecholamines and PVR. Report any drop in urine output (cardiorenal congestion) promptly.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who questions the 'routine' bag of fluid and guards the prostacyclin line is the RV's best friend on the ward."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🩸 Arterial line / continuous MAP — the RV coronary depends on aortic pressure; a falling MAP is an emergency in RV failure. Titrate noradrenaline to MAP ≥ 65.
  • ⚖️ Hourly fluid balance, urine output and daily weight — the objective read-out of decongestion; rising urine output as MAP is restored is a good sign.
  • 🌊 Serial JVP / IVC / lactate — falling lactate + falling congestion = the spiral is reversing.
  • 📈 ECG / telemetry — atrial arrhythmias (AF/flutter) are poorly tolerated in RV failure (loss of atrial kick, rate-dependent RV filling) and often need prompt rate/rhythm control.
  • 🫁 SpO₂ and ABG — keep well-oxygenated; watch CO₂ (hypercapnia raises PVR). If ventilated, monitor plateau pressure and PEEP for RV tolerance.
  • 🧮 Consider invasive haemodynamics (PA catheter) in refractory shock to guide preload, PVR, cardiac output and pressor/inotrope titration in a specialist setting.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In RV failure the two numbers that keep the patient alive are the MAP (feeds the RV coronary) and the volume trend (unloads the RV) — watch them like a hawk."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong

  • 🌀 The RV failure spiral to cardiovascular collapse — over-load → septal shift → LV under-filling → hypotension → RV ischaemia → arrest. The core catastrophe.
  • 💧 Iatrogenic fluid overload — the "hypotension = bolus" reflex tipping a congested RV over the edge.
  • 🫁 Flash pulmonary oedema — from giving a PAH pulmonary vasodilator to group-2 (LV) PH.
  • 🫀 Peri-intubation / induction arrest — sedation, positive-pressure ventilation and loss of sympathetic tone can collapse a pressure-overloaded RV. Severe PH patients are among the highest-risk intubations in medicine.
  • 💔 PH / RV crisis — an acute rise in PVR (hypoxia, pain, acidosis, PE, sepsis) causing abrupt RV decompensation and syncope/arrest.
  • 🩸 Cardiorenal & congestive hepatopathy — AKI and liver dysfunction from venous congestion; severe cases progress to ischaemic hepatitis.
  • ⚡ Atrial arrhythmias — frequently precipitate decompensation; often need urgent control.
  • 🫀 Sudden cardiac death — a leading mode of death in advanced pulmonary hypertension.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The two most lethal complications of RV failure are both iatrogenic and avoidable — a fluid bolus into a congested RV, and a pulmonary vasodilator into a left-heart PH."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🫀 Diagnosis: decompensated RIGHT ventricular failure on group-1 pulmonary arterial hypertension, congested and low-output. Echo: dilated hypokinetic RV, D-shaped LV, dilated non-collapsing IVC, functional TR, normal LV.
  2. 💧 Optimise preload: IV loop diuretic (bolus → infusion) for decongestion; NO routine fluid; small challenge only for a proven under-filled RV.
  3. 🩸 Perfuse the RV: noradrenaline to MAP ≥ 65 to protect RV coronary flow.
  4. 💪 Support contractility: add dobutamine/milrinone for persistent low output.
  5. 🫁 Reduce afterload / treat cause: correct hypoxia/hypercapnia/acidosis; exclude/treat PE; continue PAH therapy; consider inhaled selective vasodilator; escalate PAH-specific treatment with the specialist centre.
  6. 🏥 Escalate: refractory RV failure → ICU, RVAD or VA-ECMO as a bridge; PH multidisciplinary team; transplant assessment where appropriate.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Decongest, perfuse, support, and treat the cause — in that mental order — and reach for mechanical support early rather than late in the RV that won't turn the corner."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Getting the failing right heart safely home — and keeping it there

🫀 This patient (group-1 PAH, RV failure)

  • Optimised diuretic regimen + strict fluid/salt restriction; daily weights with a clear action plan
  • PAH-targeted therapy reviewed/escalated by the PH centre; prostacyclin training if applicable
  • Oxygen if hypoxic; vaccination; avoid pregnancy and high altitude; caution with drugs that raise PVR
  • Red flags: rising weight/oedema, syncope, worsening breathlessness, chest pain → seek help early
  • PH multidisciplinary follow-up; transplant discussion where appropriate; advance-care planning

🧭 The reversible-cause patient

  • Acute PE → anticoagulation, CTEPH surveillance; RV infarct → revascularisation & recovery
  • Group-2 (LV) PH → optimise the LEFT heart and valve disease, NOT pulmonary vasodilators
  • Group-3 → treat lung disease + oxygen; correct hypoxia
  • Address the precipitant (arrhythmia, sepsis, non-adherence) so it doesn't recur

💎 Clinical Pearls

🫀 Clear lungs + huge JVP + low BP= the RIGHT ventricle is failing. Think fluids OFF, not on.
⚖️ Afterload-sensitive pumpA small rise in PVR sharply cuts RV output — the RV hates afterload.
🌀 The RV spiralOver-load → septal shift → LV under-fills → BP falls → RV coronary starves → collapse.
🩸 Perfuse the RVSystemic hypotension starves the RV coronary — keep aortic pressure up with noradrenaline.
💧 Decongest, don't drownMost congested failing RVs need diuresis; only the truly under-filled RV gets a small bolus.
🫁 Classify before you vasodilateNever give a PAH pulmonary vasodilator to group-2 (LV) PH — flash pulmonary oedema.
🌬️ RV-friendly ventilationAvoid high PEEP, hypercapnia, hypoxia and acidosis — all raise PVR.
💉 Don't stop prostacyclinAbrupt withdrawal of continuous prostacyclin can trigger a fatal rebound PH crisis.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the ward

  • 🫁 Pre- vs post- vs combined PH: pre-capillary (wedge ≤15, PVR >2 WU — groups 1/3/4) is a pulmonary-vascular disease; isolated post-capillary (wedge >15, PVR ≤2 — group 2) is a LEFT-heart backpressure problem; combined post- and pre-capillary (CpcPH) has wedge >15 AND PVR >2 — a left-heart substrate that has acquired fixed pulmonary vascular remodelling and a worse prognosis.
  • 🔗 RV–PA coupling: the RV works well only while its contractility (Ees) keeps pace with the afterload it faces (Ea). The Ees/Ea ratio quantifies coupling; as afterload outstrips contractility the ventricle "uncouples," dilates, and output falls — the physiological basis of afterload-sensitivity.
  • 🌀 Acute-on-chronic RV failure: a hypertrophied chronic RV has more reserve than a naïve RV (which fails acutely at an mPAP it cannot generate above ~40 mmHg), but once a chronically adapted RV decompensates, mortality is high. A trigger (PE, sepsis, arrhythmia, non-adherence) usually tips it over.
  • 💥 PH / RV crisis physiology: an acute PVR spike (hypoxia, acidosis, pain, PE) abruptly raises RV afterload → acute RV dilatation → septal shift → LV under-filling → systemic hypotension → RV coronary hypoperfusion → RV ischaemia → the spiral to arrest. Management targets every step: oxygenate, correct pH, sedate/analgese carefully, and support MAP.
  • 🫀 The failing RV in specific settings: massive/submassive PE (acute pressure overload), RV infarction (an under-filled RV that needs preload and reperfusion), ARDS (hypoxic vasoconstriction + high ventilator pressures = acute cor pulmonale), and LV failure (group-2 backpressure) — same organ, different levers.
  • ⚙️ Mechanical support: RV assist devices (RVAD, percutaneous options) and VA-ECMO can unload a failing RV as a bridge to recovery, decision, or transplant; success depends on early rather than late deployment and a clear exit strategy.
  • 🩸 Why systemic hypotension is uniquely lethal to the RV: unlike the thick LV (perfused mainly in diastole), the normal RV coronary is perfused in BOTH systole and diastole and depends on the aortic-to-RV pressure gradient. As RV pressure rises and aortic pressure falls, that gradient collapses — so maintaining aortic pressure (noradrenaline) is a direct RV-protective, anti-ischaemic intervention.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "Three Boluses for a Blood Pressure"

The case: A 62-year-old man with pulmonary hypertension and a known dilated, failing right ventricle became hypotensive on the ward (BP 88/64). His JVP was to the ear and his legs were oedematous, but the covering team focused on the number: over ninety minutes they ran three 500 mL saline boluses "to fill him up." With each bolus his RV distended further, the interventricular septum bowed into the LV, the LV under-filled, the systemic output collapsed and the aortic pressure — and with it the RV's own coronary perfusion — fell away. He became bradycardic and arrested with a dilated, akinetic RV on the crash echo. Resuscitation failed. (In a parallel case discussed the same afternoon, a patient with group-2 PH from severe LV disease was given IV prostacyclin "to lower the pulmonary pressures" and developed fatal flash pulmonary oedema within the hour.)

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 REFLEX"Hypotension = give fluid" applied to a congested RV that was already over-filled.
🌊 SIGNS IGNOREDA JVP to the ear and gross oedema were the RV shouting "I'm full" — and were overlooked.
🫀 NO ECHONo bedside echo to see the dilated RV and D-shaped LV before treating.
🩸 PERFUSION NEGLECTEDThe RV coronary was starved as aortic pressure fell; no pressor to protect it.
🫁 GROUP NOT CHECKEDIn the parallel case, a pulmonary vasodilator was given to LEFT-heart (group-2) PH.

🛡️ What would have saved them: read "clear lungs + huge JVP + hypotension" as a failing RIGHT heart; get a bedside echo before treating; decongest the over-filled RV rather than fluid-load it; protect the perfusion pressure with noradrenaline to feed the RV coronary; support contractility with an inodilator; correct hypoxia/acidosis; and classify the PH group before ever vasodilating the lungs. The failing right heart needed decongestion and perfusion-pressure support — not litres of saline. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 6.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Treating the blood-pressure number instead of the loading conditions has killed more failing right ventricles than the disease itself — decongest and perfuse, don't drown."
