🧭 The four adult lesions to hold in mind at the bedside

When a congenital lesion walks into clinic, pattern-match against the common adult four: coarctation (upper-limb HTN, radio-femoral delay — this patient), atrial septal defect (fixed split S2, RV heave, pulmonary flow murmur), bicuspid aortic valve (ejection click, AS/AR, and an aortopathy), and Eisenmenger physiology (cyanosis, clubbing, loud P2 — a shunt that has reversed). Coarctation and bicuspid disease frequently travel together, as here.

⚠️ The trap of "he was fine as a child""Repaired in childhood" is not "cured," and "innocent murmur" written once at age 6 is not a lifetime clearance. Re-coarctation, aneurysm at the repair site, progressive aortopathy and late arrhythmia all appear in adulthood. Re-examine, re-image, and re-refer.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The cardiology resident thinks out loud at 09:52:

🧠 Frame it as "secondary hypertension in a young adult," not "add a fourth drug"

"A 24-year-old with drug-resistant hypertension, an arm-leg gradient, radio-femoral delay, an ejection click and an interscapular murmur is a coarctation with a bicuspid aortic valve until imaging says otherwise. My job isn't to titrate antihypertensives — it's to confirm the lesion, image the whole aorta, and refer to a specialist ACHD centre. I will not simply vasodilate the pressure down and send him back to the GP."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. ✋ Four-limb blood pressures and simultaneous radio-femoral palpation — quantify the gradient.
  2. 🫀 Transthoracic echo — coarctation gradient & diastolic tail, LV mass/function, aortic valve morphology (bicuspid?), aortic root/ascending dimensions, screen for associated ASD/VSD.
  3. 🩻 CT or MR aortogram — define coarctation site/severity, collaterals, post-stenotic dilatation/aneurysm, and the ascending aorta (bicuspid aortopathy).
  4. 📈 ECG & CXR — LVH; rib-notching and the "figure-3" sign.
  5. 📞 Refer to the regional ACHD centre for confirmation and repair planning; continue current antihypertensives meanwhile (the fix is mechanical relief of the obstruction, not more drugs).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Don't chase the number, find the lesion. Coarctation hypertension is relieved by opening the aorta — antihypertensives alone treat the symptom and leave the aneurysm to grow."
🧪 Resident's self-testA colleague suggests just starting a potent systemic vasodilator (e.g., hydralazine) to force the arm pressure down quickly. Wise?

No — and potentially dangerous. In coarctation, aggressive systemic vasodilatation does not relieve the fixed obstruction and can worsen lower-body perfusion. More importantly, blindly vasodilating any ACHD patient before you know the physiology is unsafe: in Eisenmenger syndrome, dropping systemic vascular resistance increases right-to-left shunting → worsening cyanosis and cardiovascular collapse. Define the lesion first, then treat it mechanically.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the pressure splits, WHY the shunt is dangerous (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧱 Coarctation = a fixed narrowing (typically just distal to the left subclavian, at the ductal site). Blood must generate high pressure to cross it → upper-body hypertension, while the lower body is under-perfused → weak, delayed femorals and leg fatigue.
  2. 🌉 Collaterals grow (internal mammary → intercostals) to bypass the narrowing — these dilated, pulsatile intercostals erode the ribs → rib-notching, and produce the interscapular/continuous murmur.
  3. 💪 Chronic afterload → LVH, and the persistent hypertension drives premature coronary disease, heart failure, and aortic aneurysm/dissection — the lethal endpoints of an unrepaired or unsurveilled coarctation.
  4. 🔀 Shunt lesions overload chambers: a left-to-right shunt (ASD/VSD/PDA) recirculates blood through the lungs (Qp:Qs > 1). Over years, high pulmonary flow raises pulmonary vascular resistance; once PVR exceeds systemic, the shunt reverses to right-to-left = Eisenmenger → cyanosis. Now the physiology is exquisitely sensitive to systemic vascular resistance.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A shunt you can still close is a chamber you can still save; an Eisenmenger shunt has reversed and must NOT be treated with systemic vasodilatation — you can reverse the shunt and kill the patient."

⚖️ Contrast pair — shunt (volume/pressure) vs obstruction (relieve it)

🔀 Shunt lesions

  • ASD / VSD / PDA — left-to-right flow, Qp:Qs > 1
  • Problem = chamber volume/pressure overload & rising PVR
  • Fix = CLOSE in time (for RV overload / paradoxical embolism) before Eisenmenger
  • Danger = a reversed (Eisenmenger) shunt must never be systemically vasodilated

🚧 Obstructive lesions

  • Coarctation, bicuspid AS/aortopathy, pulmonary stenosis
  • Problem = fixed obstruction → pressure load proximally, under-perfusion distally
  • Fix = RELIEVE the obstruction (repair/stent/valve)
  • Watch = the associated aortopathy (bicuspid) and re-coarctation/aneurysm

💡 Ask of any congenital lesion: is this a hole to close, a narrowing to open, or a shunt that has already reversed? The answer dictates the entire management — and whether vasodilators are safe.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The clinic order set (10:20)

  • ✋ Four-limb BP + simultaneous radio-femoral palpation documented; pre/post-ductal SpO₂ (screen for differential cyanosis).
  • 🫀 Transthoracic echocardiogram — coarctation gradient & diastolic run-off, LV mass/function, aortic valve morphology (bicuspid?), aortic root/ascending diameters, ASD/VSD screen, estimated pulmonary pressures.
  • 🩻 CT or MR aortogram — coarctation site/severity, collaterals, post-stenotic dilatation/aneurysm, full thoracic aorta (bicuspid aortopathy).
  • 📈 12-lead ECG (LVH) and CXR (rib-notching, "figure-3" sign, cardiac size).
  • 🧪 Bloods: renal function, lipids, HbA1c; complete the secondary-hypertension screen (renin/aldosterone) already started.
  • 💊 Continue current antihypertensives; do NOT add a systemic vasodilator empirically before the lesion is defined.
  • 📞 Refer to the regional specialist ACHD centre for confirmation, repair/stent planning and lifelong follow-up; flag bicuspid aortopathy for aortic surveillance.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Four-limb pressures and a picture of the whole aorta cost almost nothing; the expensive mistake is a fourth antihypertensive that leaves the coarctation — and the aneurysm — untreated."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Clinic nursing priorities — the nurse catches the physical sign the doctor forgot

  • 🩺 Measure BP in BOTH arms and a leg, and document radio-femoral timing — the nurse who does this routinely diagnoses coarctation before the consultant walks in.
  • 🫁 Pre- and post-ductal SpO₂ (right hand vs foot) to screen for differential cyanosis / right-to-left shunting.
  • 📋 Take a focused congenital history: prior surgery/catheter, "murmurs," exercise tolerance, cyanotic spells, and — crucially — whether specialist follow-up ever lapsed.
  • 🤰 Ask every woman of childbearing age about pregnancy plans and contraception — ACHD pregnancy risk (mWHO) must be counselled before conception.
  • 🦷 Reinforce dental hygiene and endocarditis awareness; clarify who does/doesn't need antibiotic prophylaxis.
  • 🚫 Flag any plan to start a systemic vasodilator or to send the patient for non-cardiac surgery without ACHD input — especially if cyanosis/Eisenmenger is possible.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who measures a leg pressure on every young hypertensive is the most reliable coarctation detector in the building."

🩻 Adjunct imaging

  • Transthoracic echo: first-line — gradient, valve morphology, chamber sizes, shunt screen, pulmonary pressures.
  • Cardiac MRI: gold-standard for aortic anatomy, coarctation gradient, collateral flow and RV volumes (shunt quantification) without radiation — ideal for lifelong young-adult surveillance.
  • CT angiography: fast, high-resolution aortic anatomy for stent planning and aneurysm detection.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 Lifelong surveillance — what to watch and why

  • 🩸 Blood pressure (and residual gradient): hypertension often persists or recurs after coarctation repair — monitor arm BP and rest/exercise gradients for life.
  • 🩻 Aortic imaging (MR/CT): periodic surveillance for re-coarctation and aneurysm at the repair/stent site, and for the bicuspid aortopathy (ascending-aorta diameter and growth rate).
  • 🔀 Shunt lesions: track RV size/function and pulmonary pressures; a rising PVR is the window closing on operability.
  • 💙 Cyanotic/Eisenmenger patients: resting/exertional SpO₂, haematocrit, iron status, arrhythmia — and a standing plan to avoid SVR-lowering insults.
  • 🤰 Reproductive review: re-counsel pregnancy risk (mWHO) at each life stage; pre-conception planning at an ACHD/maternal-cardiac centre.
  • 📅 Structured ACHD follow-up: defined intervals at a specialist centre — the single biggest predictor of good outcomes is not being lost to follow-up.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After ACHD 'repair,' the monitor's question is never 'is it fixed?' — it's 'what is the aorta, the shunt and the pulmonary pressure doing this decade?' That is why follow-up is lifelong and specialist."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong across the ACHD lifespan

  • 🩸 Aortic aneurysm & dissection — the feared endpoint of unrepaired/unsurveilled coarctation and bicuspid aortopathy; sustained hypertension accelerates it.
  • 💔 Premature coronary disease, LVH & heart failure — from years of untreated upper-body hypertension.
  • 🔁 Re-coarctation & residual hypertension after repair — why lifelong BP and gradient monitoring matters.
  • 🔀 Progressive pulmonary hypertension → Eisenmenger — a shunt closed too late; then cyanosis, erythrocytosis, haemoptysis, arrhythmia, sudden death.
  • 🧠 Paradoxical embolism / stroke — across an ASD/PFO, especially with venous clot or during Valsalva.
  • 🦠 Infective endocarditis — bicuspid valve and residual-defect lesions; the reason for targeted prophylaxis and meticulous dental care.
  • 🤰 Pregnancy catastrophe — in high-risk lesions (Eisenmenger, severe obstruction), maternal mortality is very high; counsel before conception.
  • 🩹 The system complication: being lost to specialist follow-up — arguably the commonest and most preventable of all.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The deadliest ACHD complication is often administrative — the patient who dropped out of specialist follow-up and came back with a dissection or a reversed shunt. Keep them in the system."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🚧 Diagnosis: coarctation of the aorta (gradient ~50 mmHg, upper-limb hypertension, radio-femoral delay, rib-notching) with a bicuspid aortic valve and early aortopathy.
  2. 📞 Refer to the specialist ACHD centre for confirmation and to decide surgical repair vs catheter stenting; continue antihypertensives meanwhile.
  3. 🩻 Lifelong surveillance post-repair: BP/gradient, and cross-sectional imaging for re-coarctation and aneurysm; track the ascending aorta (bicuspid).
  4. 🧾 Whole-patient ACHD care: pregnancy/contraception counselling (mWHO), endocarditis-prophylaxis clarification, cardiovascular risk-factor control, and a plan so he is never lost to follow-up.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Coarctation is not 'a bit of hypertension' — recognise it, image the whole aorta, refer to a specialist ACHD centre, relieve the obstruction, and then follow the aorta for the rest of his life."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the clinic — the ACHD landscape

  • 🔀 The shunt lesions & Qp:Qs: ASD subtypes (secundum — commonest, amenable to device closure; primum — AV-septal, left-axis, cleft mitral valve; sinus venosus — anomalous pulmonary veins, needs surgery), VSD and PDA. A shunt fraction Qp:Qs ≥ 1.5 with RV volume overload (or a prior paradoxical embolism) drives closure — while PVR still permits. ASD/PFO also enables paradoxical embolism and cryptogenic stroke.
  • 🚧 Obstructive lesions: coarctation (intervene for gradient ≥ 20 mmHg or upper-body hypertension with significant narrowing; repair vs stent; lifelong aneurysm/re-coarctation watch), bicuspid aortic valve (AS/AR PLUS aortopathy — ascending-aorta surveillance and surgical size thresholds), and pulmonary stenosis (balloon valvuloplasty for significant gradients).
  • 💙 Cyanotic / Eisenmenger physiology: a reversed shunt with severe PAH. Because flow follows the SVR–PVR balance, an abrupt fall in SVR (systemic vasodilators, vasodilating anaesthesia, dehydration, Valsalva) increases right-to-left shunt → profound hypoxaemia and collapse. Manage secondary erythrocytosis, iron, arrhythmia and haemoptysis; advanced pulmonary-vasodilator therapy under expert care only.
  • 🌀 Fontan circulation: single-ventricle palliation — systemic venous blood flows passively to the lungs with no sub-pulmonary pump. Depends on low PVR and adequate preload; vulnerable to arrhythmia, thrombosis, protein-losing enteropathy and Fontan-associated liver disease. Preload and rhythm are everything.
  • 🔁 Transposition of the great arteries: atrial switch (Mustard/Senning) leaves a systemic right ventricle at risk of failure and baffle problems; arterial switch is the modern repair — each with its own late complications and surveillance.
  • 🤰 Modified WHO pregnancy-risk classification: mWHO I (minimal added risk) → IV (extremely high / pregnancy contraindicated — Eisenmenger, severe PAH, severe unrepaired obstruction, systemic-RV/Fontan with complications). Counsel and plan pre-conception at an ACHD/maternal-cardiac centre.
  • 🦷 Endocarditis prophylaxis: reserved for the highest-risk lesions — prosthetic valve/material, prior IE, unrepaired cyanotic CHD, and repaired defects with residual leak adjacent to prosthetic material — for high-risk (chiefly dental) procedures. All ACHD patients need meticulous dental hygiene and lifelong specialist follow-up — the through-line of the whole field.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Femoral Pulse Nobody Felt"

The case: A 26-year-old man was managed for "resistant essential hypertension" for six years, cycling through four antihypertensives with a GP and two general clinics. He was athletic, had a soft murmur once dismissed as "innocent," and complained of tired legs on running. His arm pressures were repeatedly high; no one ever palpated his femoral pulses or measured a leg pressure, and the whole aorta was never imaged. He presented to the ED with sudden severe chest and interscapular pain — an acute aortic dissection on a background of an unrecognised coarctation with bicuspid aortopathy. He died before surgery. (A parallel M&M in the same unit: an Eisenmenger patient given a systemic vasodilator and taken for elective non-cardiac surgery without ACHD input — SVR fell, the shunt reversed, and he arrested.)

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Essential hypertension" label stuck for six years in a young man — the secondary cause was never sought.
✋ EXAM SKIPPEDFemoral pulses never palpated; no arm-leg pressure; radio-femoral delay never elicited.
🩻 NEVER IMAGEDThe whole aorta was never pictured; rib-notching / bicuspid aortopathy missed.
💙 PHYSIOLOGY IGNOREDIn the parallel case, an Eisenmenger patient was systemically vasodilated — SVR dropped, shunt reversed.
🏥 LOST TO FOLLOW-UPNo specialist ACHD referral; "innocent murmur" written once was treated as a lifetime clearance.

🛡️ What would have saved them: feel BOTH femoral pulses and measure a leg pressure in every young hypertensive; treat a soft murmur + resistant hypertension in a 24-year-old as a congenital lesion until imaged; image the whole aorta with CT/MR; refer to a specialist ACHD centre for repair and lifelong surveillance; and never systemically vasodilate an Eisenmenger patient or send them to non-cardiac surgery without ACHD input. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 9.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Resistant essential hypertension' in a young adult has killed patients whose coarctation was one femoral pulse away from diagnosis. Feel the pulse, image the aorta, refer to the specialist."
