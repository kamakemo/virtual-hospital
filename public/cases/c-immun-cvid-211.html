🧭 The four questions that frame every immunodeficiency

Before any label, structure the problem around WHICH arm of immunity has failed, because the arm predicts the organisms and the treatment: antibody (B-cell), T-cell/combined, phagocyte, or complement. The type of infection is the map to the defect (see S14).

⚠️ The trap of "she just gets a lot of infections"Recurrent infection is casually attributed to smoking, stress, "run down," or bad luck for years. The discipline is to ask: are these infections too many, too severe, too persistent, or too unusual (SPUR)? If yes even once, the immune system is a suspect — send immunoglobulins and lymphocyte subsets rather than another antibiotic script.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The immunology registrar thinks out loud:

🧠 Frame it as "which defect, and what have I missed?"

"Recurrent encapsulated-organism sinopulmonary infection + low IgG/IgA/IgM screams a primary antibody deficiency, and the age of onset plus the autoimmunity and lymphoproliferation makes CVID my lead. But CVID is a diagnosis of exclusion — before I commit her to lifelong immunoglobulin, I must rule out the secondary causes of low IgG, and I must document a poor vaccine response to prove the antibodies do not work, not just that there are few of them."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🧪 Repeat/confirm immunoglobulins (IgG, IgA, IgM) + IgG subclasses.
  2. 💉 Vaccine-response testing — baseline pneumococcal/tetanus/Hib titres, then re-check 4 weeks after immunisation (the functional test).
  3. 🩸 Lymphocyte subsets (CD3/4/8, CD19 B cells, CD16/56 NK) and, where available, B-cell memory phenotyping.
  4. 🚫 Exclude secondary causes: serum protein electrophoresis + free light chains (myeloma), HIV test, urine/stool for protein loss (nephrotic, protein-losing enteropathy), drug history (rituximab, steroids, anti-epileptics, immunosuppressants).
  5. 🩻 HRCT chest to map bronchiectasis; baseline spirometry.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Low IgG is not automatically CVID. Always exclude the secondary thieves of antibody — drugs, myeloma/lymphoma, protein loss and HIV — before you label a primary immunodeficiency."
🧪 Resident's self-testShe asks whether it is safe to give this patient the live nasal influenza or MMR vaccine while awaiting results. Answer?

Be cautious — and in severe combined/T-cell defects, NO. Live vaccines (MMR, live-attenuated influenza, BCG, oral polio, yellow fever, varicella) can cause disseminated infection in significant T-cell or combined immunodeficiency. In pure antibody deficiency the risk is lower, but the safe default while the immune phenotype is unknown is to avoid live vaccines and use inactivated ones. Never give BCG to a possible immunodeficient infant.

S6

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY she cannot fight encapsulated bacteria (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧬 Failure of B-cell terminal differentiation: in CVID, B cells reach the periphery but fail to mature into antibody-secreting plasma cells and class-switched memory B cells.
  2. 📉 Loss of specific antibody: without adequate IgG (and IgA at mucosal surfaces), there is no opsonisation — no antibody to coat bacteria for phagocytosis and complement fixation.
  3. 💊 Encapsulated organisms exploit this exactly: Strep pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, Neisseria hide behind polysaccharide capsules that only antibody + complement opsonisation can defeat. No antibody → they run riot in the sinuses and lungs.
  4. 🫁 Repeated infection → structural damage: each pneumonia inflames and dilates airways; the vicious cycle of infection → inflammation → bronchial wall destruction → impaired clearance → more infection produces irreversible bronchiectasis. Time-and-again infection is lung lost.
  5. ⚖️ Immune dysregulation, not just deficiency: the same broken tolerance that fails to make protective antibody also produces autoantibodies (cytopenias), granulomas and lymphoproliferation — why CVID is deficiency AND autoimmunity in one disease.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The capsule is the clue. Recurrent infection with ENCAPSULATED bacteria points straight at the antibody/complement axis — the opsonisation system — not at neutrophils or T cells."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Antibody deficiency vs T-cell / combined defect

🅱️ Antibody (B-cell) deficiency — e.g. CVID, XLA

  • Onset often after 6 months (maternal IgG wanes) or in adulthood
  • Encapsulated bacteria: sinopulmonary infection, giardia, enterovirus
  • Treat with immunoglobulin replacement; live vaccines usually tolerated but avoided if uncertain
  • Our patient

🅃 T-cell / combined defect — e.g. SCID

  • Presents in early infancy: failure to thrive, thrush, diarrhoea
  • Viruses (CMV, EBV), fungi (PJP, candida), opportunists
  • LIVE vaccines are dangerous (disseminated BCG); needs haematopoietic stem-cell transplant
  • A neonatal emergency — different world

💡 The age of onset and the type of organism tell you which arm has failed before a single immunology result returns.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set

  • 🧪 Confirm immunoglobulins (IgG, IgA, IgM) + IgG subclasses; serum protein electrophoresis + free light chains.
  • 💉 Vaccine-response panel: pre-immunisation pneumococcal/tetanus/Hib titres → immunise (inactivated) → recheck at 4 weeks.
  • 🩸 Lymphocyte subsets (CD3/CD4/CD8, CD19 B cells, CD16/56 NK); B-cell memory phenotyping where available.
  • 🚫 Exclude secondary causes: HIV test, drug review (rituximab/steroids/anti-epileptics), urinalysis for protein, faecal alpha-1-antitrypsin for protein-losing enteropathy.
  • 🧫 Complement: CH50/AH50 (screen for terminal/complement defects if recurrent Neisseria).
  • 🩻 HRCT chest + baseline spirometry; sputum MC&S including atypicals.
  • 💊 Treat the current infection aggressively: culture-directed / broad antibiotics with a low threshold and full course.
  • 🚑 Refer for immunoglobulin replacement (IVIG or SCIG) once CVID confirmed; screen baseline hepatitis serology & renal function before starting.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Send the vaccine-response test BEFORE you start immunoglobulin — once she is on replacement you can no longer measure her own antibody response, and the diagnostic window closes."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Immunology day-unit priorities — the nurse runs the safety net

  • 💉 Immunoglobulin infusion safety: baseline observations, slow initial rate, watch the first 30 min for rate-related reactions (headache, flushing, back pain, chills); slow or pause and pre-medicate as per protocol.
  • 🌡️ Recognise infusion reactions vs anaphylaxis: true IgA-deficiency anaphylaxis is rare but possible — know where the adrenaline is.
  • 🎓 Teach self-infusion: SCIG empowers home therapy — train aseptic technique, site rotation, and a symptom diary of breakthrough infections.
  • 🫁 Airway clearance: reinforce chest physiotherapy and hydration for bronchiectasis; teach sputum sampling at the first colour change.
  • 📒 Infection diary & trough tracking: the nurse-led record of every infection and trough is what lets the team titrate the dose to infection-freedom.
  • 💉 Vaccination governance: flag "NO LIVE VACCINES" prominently; ensure household contacts are vaccinated (cocooning).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who teaches confident home self-infusion turns a hospital-dependent patient into an independent one — steadier troughs, fewer infections, a life back."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why — lifelong surveillance

  • 🧪 IgG troughs before each IVIG dose (or periodically on SCIG) — titrate to the infection-free level, not a textbook number.
  • 📒 Infection diary: frequency/severity of breakthrough infections is the real endpoint that drives dose changes.
  • 🫁 Annual spirometry ± HRCT surveillance: catch progressive bronchiectasis and interstitial lung disease (GLILD) early.
  • 🩸 FBC & screen for autoimmunity: cytopenias can recur; watch for new autoimmune disease.
  • 🎗️ Lymphoma vigilance: CVID carries a markedly raised non-Hodgkin lymphoma risk — investigate persistent lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, B-symptoms, weight loss.
  • 🧫 Liver & gut: monitor for nodular regenerative hyperplasia/portal hypertension and enteropathy; LFTs periodically.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Once immunoglobulin is running, the question shifts from 'is she infected?' to 'is she developing the non-infectious complications?' — autoimmunity, granulomas, enteropathy and, above all, lymphoma."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What CVID does beyond infection

  • 🫁 Bronchiectasis & chronic suppurative lung disease — the commonest structural cost; the driver of long-term morbidity.
  • 🩸 Autoimmunity — immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), autoimmune haemolytic anaemia (Evans syndrome), autoimmune enteropathy, thyroid disease. Often the presenting feature.
  • 🫘 Granulomatous-lymphocytic interstitial lung disease (GLILD) and non-caseating granulomas mimicking sarcoidosis.
  • 🍽️ Enteropathy — chronic diarrhoea, malabsorption, nodular lymphoid hyperplasia; giardia is common.
  • 🎗️ Malignancy — markedly increased risk of non-Hodgkin lymphoma and gastric cancer.
  • 🫀 Splenomegaly & lymphoproliferation — benign, but can mimic or mask lymphoma.
  • 💉 Treatment complications: IVIG-related headache/aseptic meningitis, thrombosis, renal impairment; the rare IgA-anaphylaxis.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"CVID is a two-headed disease: too little immunity (infection) AND too much dysregulated immunity (autoimmunity, granulomas, lymphoma). Immunoglobulin fixes the first head; only vigilance manages the second."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🛡️ Diagnosis: CVID (low IgG/IgA/IgM, failed vaccine response, secondary causes excluded, CD19 present). Established bronchiectasis documented.
  2. 💉 Start immunoglobulin replacement (IVIG or SCIG), titrated to the infection-free trough; teach home self-infusion where appropriate.
  3. 🫁 Bronchiectasis care: airway clearance physiotherapy, sputum-directed antibiotics, consider prophylactic azithromycin; annual spirometry.
  4. 🩸 Manage complications: treat autoimmune cytopenias; screen for enteropathy, GLILD, and lymphoma; monitor spleen/nodes.
  5. 🏥 MDT & lifelong follow-up: immunology, respiratory, specialist nurse; genetic counselling; psychological support; NO live vaccines and cocoon household contacts.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"CVID is a lifelong MDT diagnosis, not a prescription. Immunoglobulin keeps her out of hospital; surveillance for lymphoma, lung and autoimmune disease keeps her alive."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🧬 Monogenic "CVID-like" disorders: what looks like sporadic CVID is increasingly a defined genetic disease — CTLA4 haploinsufficiency, LRBA deficiency, PIK3CD (activated PI3K-delta, APDS), NFKB1/NFKB2, TACI (TNFRSF13B). Genetic diagnosis can unlock targeted therapy (abatacept for CTLA4/LRBA, sirolimus/leniolisib for APDS).
  • 🫁 GLILD (granulomatous-lymphocytic interstitial lung disease): a lymphoproliferative/granulomatous lung complication distinct from infection; often needs immunosuppression (steroids, rituximab ± azathioprine/mycophenolate) rather than more antibiotics.
  • 🩸 Immune dysregulation as the presenting phenotype: some CVID patients present with cytopenias, enteropathy or lymphoproliferation and near-normal infection history — the "infection-light, dysregulation-heavy" phenotype carries the worst prognosis.
  • 💉 Ig product & route nuance: IVIG vs SCIG vs facilitated SCIG (with recombinant hyaluronidase); managing IgA-deficient patients and anti-IgA antibodies; thrombotic/renal risk mitigation; individualised biological trough targeting rather than fixed cut-offs.
  • 🧫 Newborn screening & the combined-defect overlap: TREC-based newborn screening detects SCID early; some antibody-deficiency phenotypes overlap with combined immunodeficiency, changing transplant vs replacement decisions.
  • 🎗️ Malignancy & transplant: the raised lymphoma/gastric-cancer risk and, in selected severe monogenic cases, the role of haematopoietic stem-cell transplantation.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Pneumonias Nobody Counted"

The case: A 28-year-old man was treated for "recurrent chest infections" across nine years — at least six radiologically-confirmed pneumonias, endless sinusitis, and one episode of immune thrombocytopenia dismissed as "post-viral." Each infection was seen by a different GP or acute team; each got a course of antibiotics and a discharge. No single clinician ever laid the episodes side by side, and no one ever sent a serum immunoglobulin level. He finally collapsed with severe pneumonia and respiratory failure. This time someone sent an IgG: it was 1.8 g/L. HRCT showed extensive bilateral bronchiectasis. He had CVID all along. Immunoglobulin replacement was started, but he was left with permanently damaged lungs and, three years later, developed a non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ONE-OFF THINKINGEach pneumonia treated in isolation; the pattern was never assembled.
🧪 IgG NEVER SENTThe single cheap test that diagnoses CVID was never ordered in nine years.
🩸 CLUE IGNOREDThe ITP episode — an autoimmune warning sign of CVID — was written off as "post-viral."
🏥 FRAGMENTED CAREDifferent clinicians each saw one episode; no continuity to notice the recurrence.
🫁 IRREVERSIBLE DAMAGEBy diagnosis, bronchiectasis was established — preventable years earlier.

🛡️ What would have saved his lungs: treat recurrent infection as a pattern, not a series of episodes; apply the SPUR warning signs; send a serum immunoglobulin level after the SECOND pneumonia; read an autoimmune cytopenia in a young adult as a possible immune-dysregulation clue; and refer to immunology early. Immunoglobulin replacement prevents bronchiectasis — it cannot reverse it. This is exactly the discipline that protected the patient in Bed 11.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Just another chest infection' has cost more lungs than any single organism. The recurrence IS the diagnosis — count the pneumonias and send the immunoglobulins."
