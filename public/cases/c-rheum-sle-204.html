🧭 The bedside question that must be asked every visit

In known or suspected SLE, three checks catch the organ threats early: the urine (nephritis), the blood pressure (renal/vascular), and a neuro/mental-state screen (cerebral lupus). Add a quick blood count for cytopenias and a question about pleuritic chest pain for serositis (see S14).

⚠️ The trap of treating lupus organ-by-organThe commonest way lupus nephritis is missed is by treating the rash in dermatology, the joints in rheumatology, and never joining the dots with a urine dip. Active urine sediment can exist with a "well-looking" patient and a normal creatinine. Screen the kidney at every contact — do not wait for the creatinine to rise.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The rheumatology registrar thinks out loud:

🧠 Frame it as "confirm lupus, then stage the kidney"

"Multisystem: skin, mucosa, joints, constitutional — plus new proteinuria, haematuria and hypertension. This is SLE with likely lupus nephritis. My job is to confirm the diagnosis serologically (ANA, then anti-dsDNA and complement), quantify the kidney (uPCR, sediment, renal function), and get an urgent renal biopsy to classify the nephritis, because the class (I–VI) dictates whether she needs aggressive immunosuppression. And I start hydroxychloroquine now — everyone with lupus gets it."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🧪 ANA (screen) → if positive, anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm, complement C3/C4, plus anti-Ro/La and antiphospholipid antibodies (S9).
  2. 🚽 Quantify the kidney: uPCR/24-h protein, urine microscopy for dysmorphic red cells and red-cell casts, creatinine/eGFR, albumin.
  3. 🩻 Urgent renal biopsy to assign the ISN/RPS class (I–VI) and score activity vs chronicity (S10).
  4. 💊 Start hydroxychloroquine immediately; plan induction immunosuppression scaled to the biopsy class (S11).
  5. 🩸 Control BP (ACE inhibitor/ARB for proteinuria once class known), screen APS/anti-Ro given her pregnancy plans.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"You cannot treat lupus nephritis you have not classified — proteinuria plus active sediment earns an URGENT renal biopsy, because Class II is watched while Class IV is treated hard, and the treatment for one would harm the other."
🧪 Registrar's self-testThe ANA is positive at 1:640. Does that confirm lupus?

No — ANA is sensitive, not specific. ANA is positive in >95% of SLE (so a negative ANA makes lupus very unlikely — a great rule-OUT), but it is also positive in healthy people, other autoimmune diseases, thyroid disease and infections. Confirmation comes from the specific antibodies (anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm) and the clinical picture. anti-dsDNA and low C3/C4 also track disease activity and nephritis — that is their real power here.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY immune complexes wreck organs (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧬 Loss of tolerance: in a genetically primed host, defective clearance of apoptotic cell debris exposes nuclear antigens (DNA, histones, Sm). Autoreactive B and T cells escape tolerance.
  2. 🎯 Autoantibodies: the immune system makes antibodies to nuclear material — ANA broadly, and specifically anti-dsDNA and anti-Sm.
  3. 🔗 Immune-complex formation: antibody binds antigen forming circulating and in-situ immune complexes (a type III hypersensitivity).
  4. 🌊 Deposition + complement: complexes deposit in the glomerular basement membrane, skin, serosa and vessels; they activate complement, consuming C3 and C4 (which is WHY complement falls when disease is active) and recruiting neutrophils.
  5. 💀 Inflammation + organ injury: the resulting inflammation damages the glomerulus → proteinuria, haematuria, red-cell casts; in the brain, serosa, marrow and vessels it produces the protean features. Rising anti-dsDNA + falling complement is the biochemical signature of an active flare.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Anti-dsDNA UP and complement (C3/C4) DOWN = the flare is real and the kidney is at risk. These two numbers, moving in opposite directions, are your disease-activity dashboard."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Lupus arthritis vs Rheumatoid arthritis

🦋 Lupus (SLE)

  • Non-erosive, reducible (Jaccoud) deformity; joints look worse than the X-ray
  • Multisystem: rash, ulcers, renal, cytopenias, serositis
  • ANA+, anti-dsDNA/anti-Sm, LOW complement in flare
  • Hydroxychloroquine backbone ± immunosuppression by organ

🔥 Rheumatoid arthritis

  • Erosive pannus, fixed deformity; X-ray shows marginal erosions
  • Joint-dominant (though extra-articular disease exists)
  • anti-CCP/RF; complement usually normal
  • Methotrexate anchor, treat-to-target DMARDs

💡 Both are autoimmune and both can hurt the same knuckles — but lupus reduces, rheumatoid erodes, and only lupus routinely threatens the kidney and brain.

🎮 Simulation — The Disease-Activity Dial (anti-dsDNA vs complement)

Lupus activity is read from two opposing trends. Drag the anti-dsDNA up and watch complement fall and the flare/nephritis risk climb.

Set the dial to explore quiescent vs active/nephritic lupus.

🎯 Low, stable anti-dsDNA with normal C3/C4 → quiescent. A rising anti-dsDNA with falling complement AND active urine → a flare threatening the kidney: escalate and re-check the urine.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (urgent clinic visit)

  • 🧪 ANA, then anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm, anti-Ro/La, complement C3/C4; antiphospholipid antibodies (lupus anticoagulant, anti-cardiolipin, anti-β2GPI).
  • 🚽 Urinalysis + microscopy (dysmorphic RBCs, red-cell casts), uPCR / 24-h protein, creatinine/eGFR, serum albumin.
  • 🩸 FBC (cytopenias — haemolytic anaemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia), reticulocytes, DAT/Coombs, LDH, haptoglobin if haemolysis suspected.
  • 🩻 Urgent renal biopsy referral to classify nephritis (ISN/RPS I–VI) and score activity/chronicity.
  • 💊 Start hydroxychloroquine now (weight-based dosing; baseline ophthalmology screening arranged).
  • 🩸 Blood pressure control; ACE inhibitor/ARB for proteinuria (anti-proteinuric + renoprotective) once class known and pregnancy status considered.
  • ☀️ Photoprotection advice; screen infection and vaccination status before immunosuppression.
  • 🤰 Pregnancy test and pre-conception counselling flag — teratogenic drugs to be avoided; document anti-Ro status.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Every lupus order set starts with a urine dip and ends with hydroxychloroquine. The dip finds the kidney; the hydroxychloroquine protects every organ — neither is optional."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ The rheumatology/renal nurse runs the safety net and the education

  • 🚽 Dip the urine at every visit and quantify new proteinuria — the nurse is often the one who catches the silent nephritis first.
  • 💊 Hydroxychloroquine adherence: explain it is protective, not just symptomatic; it takes weeks to work; do NOT stop it in a flare or pregnancy (it is continued in pregnancy). Arrange annual retinal screening.
  • ☀️ Photoprotection & triggers: high-SPF sunscreen, sun avoidance, smoking cessation; counsel that UV, infection and some drugs trigger flares.
  • 🦠 Immunosuppression safety: infection sick-day rules, vaccination (no live vaccines while heavily immunosuppressed), PJP prophylaxis where indicated; monitor bloods on cyclophosphamide/MMF.
  • 🩸 BP & fluid: daily weights and BP, watch oedema and for signs of nephrotic complications (infection, VTE).
  • 🤰 Family planning: reinforce reliable contraception on teratogens (mycophenolate, cyclophosphamide), and flag pre-conception counselling and anti-Ro status for neonatal-lupus risk.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who dips every lupus urine and says 'never stop your hydroxychloroquine' prevents both the missed nephritis and the avoidable flare — the two catastrophes of this disease."

🩻 The rest of the workup

  • Serology monitoring: serial anti-dsDNA + C3/C4 to track activity and response.
  • Cardiorespiratory: echo/CXR if serositis or pleuritic pain (pericardial/pleural effusions); consider Libman-Sacks endocarditis.
  • Brain: MRI + lumbar puncture if neuropsychiatric features (seizure, psychosis) — exclude infection first.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🚽 Urine at every visit — proteinuria (uPCR) and sediment are the earliest sign of renal flare; do not wait for creatinine.
  • 🧪 Serial anti-dsDNA + C3/C4 — the activity dashboard; rising dsDNA with falling complement heralds a flare, often before symptoms.
  • 🩸 Renal function & BP — eGFR, albumin, tight BP control (ACE inhibitor/ARB) to protect the kidney.
  • 🩸 FBC — autoimmune cytopenias AND drug marrow toxicity (MMF/cyclophosphamide); trend regularly.
  • 👁️ Hydroxychloroquine retinopathy — baseline and annual ophthalmology screening (esp. after ~5 years / high dose).
  • ❤️ Cardiovascular & thrombosis — accelerated atherosclerosis and APS: manage CV risk, consider anticoagulation if APS with thrombosis.
  • 🦠 Infection — the leading cause of death alongside renal/CV disease in immunosuppressed lupus; vaccinate, prophylax, have sick-day rules.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In lupus you monitor two clocks: the immune clock (anti-dsDNA/complement + urine) telling you if it is flaring, and the drug clock (bloods, eyes, infection) telling you if the treatment is safe."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — organ threats and iatrogenic harm

  • 🩸 Lupus nephritis → end-stage renal failure — the prognosis-defining complication; Class IV missed goes to dialysis/transplant.
  • 🧠 Neuropsychiatric lupus (cerebral lupus) — seizures, psychosis, stroke, aseptic meningitis, cognitive dysfunction; always exclude infection first.
  • 🫁 Serositis — pleuritis/pleural effusions, pericarditis/effusion (rarely tamponade); pneumonitis; the rare catastrophic diffuse alveolar haemorrhage.
  • 🩸 Haematological — autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, immune thrombocytopenia, leukopenia/lymphopenia; TTP-like microangiopathy.
  • 🧬 Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) overlap — arterial/venous thrombosis, recurrent miscarriage; the nephrotic state itself is prothrombotic.
  • ❤️ Accelerated atherosclerosis — chronic inflammation + steroids → premature cardiovascular disease (a leading late cause of death); Libman-Sacks endocarditis.
  • 🦠 Infection — from the disease and its immunosuppression; a leading cause of death — always in the differential for a "flare".
  • 💊 Iatrogenic: steroid toxicity (osteoporosis, avascular necrosis, diabetes), cyclophosphamide infertility/cystitis, MMF teratogenicity, HCQ retinopathy.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"When a lupus patient deteriorates, ask THREE questions before you reach for steroids: is it a FLARE, is it INFECTION, or is it THROMBOSIS (APS)? Treating the wrong one is how lupus patients die."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🦋 Diagnosis: SLE (ANA+, anti-dsDNA/anti-Sm+, low complement, mucocutaneous + arthritis + renal) with active lupus nephritis and APS overlap.
  2. 🩻 Classify the kidney: urgent renal biopsy → ISN/RPS class + activity/chronicity to select induction.
  3. 💊 Treat: hydroxychloroquine for all; steroids to bridge; induction immunosuppression scaled to class (MMF or low-dose cyclophosphamide ± belimumab/voclosporin for proliferative nephritis); ACE inhibitor/ARB + BP control.
  4. 🫀 Whole-patient care: cardiovascular-risk reduction, VTE/APS management, infection prophylaxis & vaccination, bone protection, pre-conception planning and anti-Ro documentation.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Lupus treatment is a dimmer, not a switch — hydroxychloroquine underneath everyone, and immunosuppression turned up to match the organ at risk; the kidney turns it up the most."

🏠 Discharge / Shared Care

📋 Two different worlds — remission maintenance vs the flaring kidney

🎯 This patient (nephritis in remission)

  • Continue hydroxychloroquine + maintenance immunosuppression; ACE inhibitor/ARB; BP control
  • Monitoring: urine + anti-dsDNA/complement + renal function on protocol; annual eye screening
  • Red flags: frothy urine/oedema (renal flare), seizure/confusion (cerebral lupus), pleuritic pain, fever (infection), leg swelling/breathlessness (VTE/APS) → urgent contact
  • Rheumatology + nephrology shared care; pre-conception clinic before pregnancy

🟢 Safe self-management

  • Knows to NEVER stop hydroxychloroquine and to continue it in pregnancy
  • Photoprotection, smoking cessation, vaccination, infection sick-day rules
  • Reliable contraception on teratogens; flare hotline; understands anti-Ro/neonatal-lupus relevance
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A safe lupus discharge is a shared-care plan with a urine-and-serology monitoring schedule, a flare hotline, continued hydroxychloroquine and a patient who knows the three faces of deterioration — flare, infection, thrombosis."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🤰 Pregnancy, anti-Ro & neonatal lupus: anti-Ro/SSA (and anti-La) antibodies cross the placenta and can cause neonatal lupus — a transient rash and, critically, irreversible congenital complete heart block. Plan pregnancy in quiescent disease, continue hydroxychloroquine (which reduces heart-block recurrence), monitor fetal heart, and time conception ≥6 months after renal remission on pregnancy-safe drugs.
  • 🩸 Antiphospholipid syndrome overlap: lupus anticoagulant / anti-cardiolipin / anti-β2GPI → thrombosis and pregnancy morbidity; management with aspirin/anticoagulation, and the feared catastrophic APS (multi-organ thrombosis).
  • 🧬 Type I interferon signature & new biologics: the interferon pathway drives lupus; anifrolumab (anti-IFN-α receptor) and B-cell/BAFF-targeted therapy (belimumab, and emerging CAR-T-cell approaches inducing drug-free remission) reflect a mechanistic revolution.
  • 🩻 Nephritis nuance: repeat/protocol biopsy to reassess activity vs chronicity, the significance of the chronicity index for prognosis, mixed Class III/IV + V lesions, and multi-target induction (MMF + calcineurin inhibitor / voclosporin).
  • 🧠 Neuropsychiatric SLE: distinguishing primary NPSLE (immune-complex / anti-neuronal / antiphospholipid mechanisms) from infection, metabolic and steroid-induced causes — and why you exclude infection before immunosuppressing the brain.
  • 💉 Drug-induced lupus mechanism: anti-histone antibodies, typical culprits (hydralazine, procainamide, isoniazid, minocycline, anti-TNF), sparing of kidney/CNS, and resolution on withdrawal — the mimic you must not treat as idiopathic SLE.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Urine Nobody Dipped"

The case: A 24-year-old woman was seen five times over a year — twice in dermatology for a "photosensitive facial rash", twice in general practice for "viral" joint pains and fatigue, and once for mouth ulcers. Her ANA had been positive but was dismissed as "non-specific". Nobody ever dipped her urine or checked her blood pressure. She then presented to the emergency department with breathlessness, gross oedema and a blood pressure of 180/110; her creatinine was 480 and eGFR 12. Biopsy showed Class IV diffuse proliferative lupus nephritis with a high chronicity index — much of the kidney was already scarred. Despite aggressive immunosuppression she progressed to dialysis and the transplant waiting list, aged 25. A second patient on the unit, previously stable, had a life-threatening flare after quietly stopping her hydroxychloroquine because she felt well.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🚽 URINE NEVER DIPPEDThe single cheapest test that finds silent nephritis was never done.
🧩 SILOED CARERash, joints and ulcers treated separately; nobody joined the multisystem dots.
🧪 ANA DISMISSEDA positive ANA in a compatible clinical picture was written off as noise.
🩸 BP IGNOREDNew hypertension — a renal red flag — went unmeasured/unaddressed.
💊 HCQ STOPPEDThe second patient flared after stopping hydroxychloroquine while feeling well.

🛡️ What would have saved them: treat lupus as ONE multisystem disease and dip the urine and check the BP at every single contact; act on a positive ANA in a compatible patient by sending specific antibodies and complement; biopsy proteinuria with active sediment early, while the chronicity index is still low and the kidney is still salvageable; and drill every patient that hydroxychloroquine is lifelong and must not be stopped, even when they feel well. This is exactly the discipline that saved the kidneys of the patient in Bed 4.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The urine you did not dip has cost more lupus kidneys than any drug ever saved. In lupus, the dipstick is the prognosis — do it every time."
