🚦 STEMI vs NSTEMI — the fork that sets the clock

NSTEMI is myocardial injury (rising troponin) with ischaemia but without persistent ST elevation — the culprit is usually a sub-totally occluded or dynamically obstructing vessel rather than a fixed TIMI-0 occlusion. Reperfusion is not a 90-minute emergency; instead, the patient is risk-stratified and the timing of invasive angiography is chosen by risk. High-risk features (GRACE >140, rise/fall of troponin, dynamic ST/T change) earn an early invasive approach within 24 h. Very-high-risk features (haemodynamic instability, refractory angina, life-threatening arrhythmia, mechanical complication) earn an immediate (<2 h) strategy.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"STEMI is a stopwatch; NSTEMI is a triage. In NSTEMI you do not race the clock to a balloon — you grade the risk and let it choose how fast the angiogram happens."

The randomized signal for very-early timing is strongest in the highest-risk patients (TIMACS subgroup, VERDICT high-GRACE subgroup).

🧾 The pre-catheter checklist

  • ✍️ Consent — elective/urgent (not peri-arrest); document bleeding, stroke, dissection, contrast nephropathy, need for revascularisation, and the possibility of deferral if physiology is negative.
  • 🧪 Antiplatelet strategy — aspirin loaded; hold the P2Y₁₂ pre-treatment in NSTEMI when early angiography is planned and anatomy is unknown (2023 ESC: routine pretreatment not recommended if going to the lab promptly — it may be surgical disease).
  • 🫘 Renal function & contrast plan — diabetic + possible multivessel disease means higher contrast load; hydrate, minimise dye.
  • 💊 Adenosine readiness — you plan FFR; confirm no severe reactive airways disease / high-grade AV block, and that IV adenosine and a giving set are ready (or plan a resting index such as iFR).
  • 🖐️ Access assessment — Barbeau/Allen of the radial; femoral back-up.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In NSTEMI, do not blindly pre-load the P2Y₁₂ before you know the anatomy — the intermediate lesion you are about to measure might turn out to be three-vessel disease heading to CABG."

🩸 Vascular Access & Setup

🖐️ Radial vs femoral — radial wins in ACS

🖐️ Radial (default)

  • Fewer access-site bleeds & vascular complications
  • Lower mortality in ACS (MATRIX, RIVAL ACS subgroup)
  • Early ambulation, comfort — ideal for a stable NSTEMI who may be deferred
  • Confirm patency first (Barbeau/Allen)

🦵 Femoral (bail-out)

  • When radial fails / spasm / small vessel / need for large-bore support
  • Higher bleeding — micropuncture + ultrasound guidance
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Radial-first in ACS — a post-procedure bleed roughly doubles mortality, and the NSTEMI patient is often on triple therapy. Protect the wrist, protect the patient."

💉 Sheath, anticoagulation & the pressure-wire mindset

  • 🔧 Sheath: 6 Fr radial (hydrophilic); intra-arterial "radial cocktail" (verapamil ± nitrate ± heparin) to prevent spasm — a spastic radial ruins the smooth wire handling physiology demands.
  • 🩸 Procedural anticoagulation: unfractionated heparin (70–100 U/kg to an ACT ~250 s) for PCI; a pure diagnostic-plus-physiology run still needs adequate anticoagulation before a pressure wire crosses the coronary.
  • 🧭 Guide catheter: for the LAD → an EBU/XB left guide; note that deep-seating or a damped guide will corrupt the aortic (Pa) pressure and invalidate your FFR — keep the guide coaxial and disengage side-holes if used.
  • 💉 Nitrate before physiology: give intracoronary GTN to abolish epicardial spasm before any FFR/iFR — spasm mimics a fixed stenosis.

🔬 Lesion Assessment — Physiology & Imaging

📏 This is the teaching coreThe LAD lesion is intermediate and the culprit is ambiguous. Angiographic diameter stenosis correlates poorly with ischaemia in the 40–70% range. The answer is coronary physiology: a pressure wire tells you whether this narrowing actually limits flow enough to cause ischaemia — and therefore whether a stent will help or merely add risk.

📏 The physiology indices — know the thresholds cold

IndexHow measuredIschaemic cut-off
📉 FFR (fractional flow reserve)Pd/Pa at maximal hyperaemia (IV/IC adenosine)≤ 0.80 = treat · >0.80 = defer
🌊 iFR (instantaneous wave-free ratio)Resting Pd/Pa in the wave-free diastolic window — no adenosine≤ 0.89 = treat · >0.89 = defer
💤 Resting Pd/Pa · RFR · DFRWhole-cycle or diastolic resting ratios, no hyperaemiaBroadly track iFR (~0.89–0.92 zones)

FFR is the gold standard with the largest outcome evidence (DEFER, FAME, FAME-2). iFR is a validated, adenosine-free alternative (DEFINE-FLAIR, iFR-SWEDEHEART) — equivalent outcomes, faster, and it spares patients the flush, dyspnoea, and AV block of adenosine.

🎮 Simulation — The FFR Pressure-Wire Pullback (the intermediate LAD)

You have crossed the mid-LAD lesion with a pressure wire and given adenosine to reach maximal hyperaemia. Drag the FFR value the wire reads distal to the lesion and decide: treat or defer? The 0.80 line is everything.

Set the dial to explore treat-vs-defer.

🎯 FFR ≤ 0.80 → the lesion limits flow enough to cause ischaemia → PCI (FAME/FAME-2). > 0.80 → defer and treat medically — deferring a >0.80 lesion is safe (DEFER). During pullback, a step-up in FFR across one segment localises the flow-limiting spot; a gradual gradient over a diffuse vessel warns you a stent may not fix it.

⚠️ The FFR pitfalls that fool trainees
  • 📉 Pressure drift — always re-equalise Pd=Pa with the sensor at the guide tip at the end; a drifted wire reading 0.78 may truly be 0.83. Verify before you stent.
  • 🔗 Serial (tandem) lesions — one lesion shields the other; a single crossing-lesion FFR is unreliable. Do a pullback to see which lesion carries the gradient.
  • 🫀 Microvascular disease / prior MI — blunted hyperaemia raises FFR falsely (less flow, smaller gradient) and can under-call a lesion; the diabetic microcirculation here is relevant.
  • 🫁 Aortic stenosis / LVH — altered haemodynamics and adenosine response make FFR less certain; interpret with imaging.
  • 💨 Sub-maximal hyperaemia — inadequate adenosine dose/route gives a falsely high FFR. Ensure true maximal hyperaemia (central line preferred for IV).

🖥️ Intravascular imaging — the complement to physiology

🔵 IVUS

  • Ultrasound — sizes the vessel, quantifies plaque burden, sees behind calcium
  • MLA thresholds (e.g., left main) help when physiology is borderline; guides stent sizing

🟠 OCT

  • Light — ultra-high resolution; superb for thrombus, plaque rupture vs erosion, edge dissection, apposition
  • Clarifies an ambiguous culprit — a ruptured plaque or thrombus outs the true offender
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Physiology tells you WHETHER to treat; imaging tells you WHAT and HOW. FFR ≤ 0.80 or iFR ≤ 0.89 makes the intermediate lesion a target — above the line, the kindest stent is no stent."

🛠️ Intervention — Step-by-Step Execution

🎈 Balloon angioplasty & stent deployment. Source: Wikimedia Commons (Blausen)

🪜 The sequence — when FFR says treat (FFR 0.75)

  1. 💊 Load the P2Y₁₂ now — anatomy is known and PCI is committed (aspirin already on board; ticagrelor/prasugrel preferred in ACS).
  2. 🧵 Wire the LAD — a workhorse guidewire across the intermediate lesion into the distal true lumen (confirm in two views).
  3. 📏 Lesion prep & sizing — predilate a fibrocalcific lesion; size the stent 1:1 to the reference vessel (IVUS/OCT or online QCA — the diabetic vessel may be diffusely diseased, so pick healthy landing zones).
  4. 🩹 Deploy a drug-eluting stent covering the flow-limiting segment your pullback identified — not necessarily the whole hazy vessel.
  5. 🎈 Post-dilate — non-compliant balloon to full expansion and apposition (under-expansion is the #1 cause of stent thrombosis/restenosis).
  6. 📉 Re-measure physiology if needed — a post-PCI FFR < 0.88 predicts worse outcomes and may reveal a residual gradient (in-stent under-expansion or a second lesion).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Stent the gradient the pullback showed you, size 1:1, post-dilate — then measure again. A post-PCI FFR under 0.88 means the job is not done, not that the wire is lying."

⚠️ Complication Recognition & Management

💀 The physiology-specific complications you must recognise instantly

ClueComplicationAction
Transient AV block / bradycardia + flushing + chest tightness + dyspnoea during hyperaemia💉 Adenosine effectStop the adenosine infusion — half-life is seconds; it self-resolves. Reassure; avoid over-treating

🏠 Post-Procedure Care, Debrief & Assessment

🩹 Access-site care

  • 🖐️ Radial: patent-haemostasis compression band; watch for radial artery occlusion & bleeding.
  • 🦵 Femoral: manual pressure/closure device; watch for haematoma, pseudoaneurysm, retroperitoneal bleed (unexplained hypotension + back/flank pain).

💊 Secondary prevention & antithrombotics (start day 1)

  • DAPT — aspirin + a potent P2Y₁₂ inhibitor (ticagrelor or prasugrel preferred over clopidogrel in ACS; PLATO/TRITON) for 12 months by default, then individualise. High bleeding risk → consider shortened DAPT (1–3 months) then P2Y₁₂ monotherapy; high ischaemic/low bleeding risk → consider extension.
  • The deferred lesion still gets medical therapy — a >0.80 FFR lesion is treated with the same aggressive risk-factor control, not forgotten.
  • High-intensity statin (atorvastatin 80 mg) regardless of LDL; add ezetimibe/PCSK9i to target (LDL < 1.4 mmol/L, ≥50% reduction).
  • ACE inhibitor / ARB (diabetic, hypertensive), beta-blocker if reduced EF/ongoing ischaemia, SGLT2 inhibitor for the diabetes with CV benefit, and tight glycaemic control.
  • 🏃 Cardiac rehab, smoking-cessation reinforcement, echo for EF.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The stent treated one lesion; the pills treat the whole atherosclerotic patient. A deferred FFR-negative lesion is a promise to treat it medically — not permission to ignore it."

🔭 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

  • 🌊 Coronary flow reserve (CFR) & index of microvascular resistance (IMR): when FFR is normal but angina persists, look for microvascular dysfunction (low CFR, high IMR) — the CorMicA-style diagnosis of ANOCA/INOCA; the diabetic microcirculation is a classic setting.
  • 📉 FFR pullback & the pullback pressure gradient (PPG): distinguishes focal (stent-treatable) from diffuse disease; a high PPG focal lesion responds beautifully to a stent, a low PPG diffuse vessel may not.
  • 🔗 Serial-lesion physiology: the true FFR of each lesion is coupled; hyperaemic pullback (or the classic prediction equations) separates them — never trust a single crossing-lesion number in tandem disease.
  • 🧮 Angiography-derived physiology: QFR, FFRangio, vFFR — computational FFR from the angiogram without a wire (FAVOR III); useful but validate against wire in ambiguous cases.
  • 🫀 Post-PCI physiology: DEFINE-PCI showed a large minority have a residual post-PCI gradient; a low post-PCI FFR predicts events and flags under-expansion or a missed lesion to fix on the table.
  • 🖥️ Imaging-guided PCI: IVUS/OCT-guided stenting improves outcomes vs angiography alone (ULTIMATE, RENOVATE-COMPLEX-PCI); pairs naturally with physiology in ambiguous intermediate disease.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The 0.78 That Was Really 0.84"

The case: A mid-LAD intermediate lesion was crossed with a pressure wire. Adenosine was given; the FFR read 0.78 and the operator, satisfied it was ischaemic, stented it. He skipped the end-of-run re-equalisation. What he never saw was that the wire had drifted during the case — the true FFR was 0.84, a lesion that should have been deferred. The stent went into a diffusely diseased vessel with poor landing zones; weeks later it presented with an edge restenosis and a further procedure. A second lesion that had actually been ischaemic on pullback was never appreciated because no pullback was done. The patient was harmed by an unnecessary stent placed on a drifted number, and by a missed real lesion.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

📉 NO DRIFT CHECKThe wire was never re-equalised at the guide tip — a drifted 0.78 was really 0.84.
👁️ TRUSTED ONE NUMBERA single crossing-lesion FFR, no pullback, in a diffusely diseased vessel.
💨 SUB-MAXIMAL DOUBTNo confirmation of true maximal hyperaemia before committing.
🩹 STENTED A NON-LESIONAn unnecessary DES in poor landing zones invited restenosis.
🔗 MISSED THE REAL ONENo pullback meant the truly ischaemic segment was never found.

🛡️ What would have saved this: confirm maximal hyperaemia, then always re-equalise Pd = Pa at the guide tip at the end of every case to exclude drift; do a pullback in any diffusely diseased or borderline vessel to localise the gradient; and respect the 0.80 line in both directions — a drifted 0.78 that is truly 0.84 deserves deferral, not a stent. Measure well, or do not measure at all.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A pressure wire is only as honest as your drift check. Re-equalise at the end, pull back through the vessel, and never let a number you did not verify talk you into a stent."

  2. 📏 Measure the intermediate lesion — FFR ≤ 0.80 or iFR ≤ 0.89 → treat; above the line → defer safely. Do not stent a silhouette.
  3. 📉 Respect the pitfalls — drift, serial lesions, microvascular disease, sub-maximal hyperaemia, aortic stenosis. Re-equalise and pull back.
  4. 🎯 Functionally complete revascularisation — treat the ischaemic lesions, defer the FFR-negative ones (FAME/DEFER/COMPLETE logic).
  5. 💉 Know the physiology complications — adenosine AV block resolves on stopping the drug; wire perforation → balloon, reverse, echo.
