🧭 The Forrester / Stevenson quadrants — where does she sit?

Classify every decompensated HF patient on two axes: congestion (wet vs dry) and perfusion (warm vs cold). Our patient is cold & wet — congested AND hypoperfused — the quadrant with the worst prognosis and the one that needs perfusion support, not diuresis alone.

⚠️ The trap of "the blood pressure is only a bit low"Do not be reassured by a "soft" BP in isolation. A patient can maintain a near-normal BP by intense vasoconstriction while the organs starve (compensated / pre-shock, SCAI B). The lactate, the urine output and the mental state reveal the truth before the BP collapses. Stage the shock — don't wait for it to declare.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — the cardiogenic-shock DEATH SPIRAL (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 📉 Falling stroke volume: the primary pump fails (large MI, end-stage HFrEF, arrhythmia, mechanical lesion). Cardiac output drops despite adequate — often elevated — filling pressures.
  2. 🩸 Hypotension: low output → low mean arterial pressure and a narrow pulse pressure. Compensatory vasoconstriction makes the periphery cold and clammy.
  3. 🫀 Coronary hypoperfusion: the heart is perfused in diastole by (aortic diastolic − LV) pressure; a falling MAP and a high LV filling pressure squeeze coronary perfusion pressure — the pump's own supply falls.
  4. 💀 Worse pump → worse output: ischaemic, under-perfused myocardium contracts even less. Output falls again. Round and round: low output → hypotension → coronary hypoperfusion → worse pump → lower output.
  5. 🌊 Congestion coexists (backward failure): the failing ventricle can't move the blood forward, so pressures back up into the lungs (and, with RV failure, the systemic veins) — hence "wet." Systemic hypoperfusion drives lactic acidosis and multi-organ failure.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Cardiogenic shock is a self-reinforcing spiral: every minute of hypoperfusion starves the very pump you need to recover — break the spiral early or it closes."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Cold & Wet vs Warm & Wet

🧊 Cold & Wet (this patient)

  • Congested AND hypoperfused (cold, oliguric, lactate up)
  • Low cardiac output — the pump has failed
  • Needs PERFUSION support (vasopressor + inotrope) THEN cautious decongestion
  • Diuretics alone will worsen organ ischaemia

🔥 Warm & Wet

  • Congested but WELL perfused (warm, making urine, normal lactate)
  • Cardiac output adequate; problem is fluid/afterload
  • Diuretics ± vasodilators usually suffice
  • No routine need for inotropes/pressors

💡 The two look similar on the chart ("breathless, oedematous HF") but demand opposite reflexes. Feel the periphery and read the lactate — that single distinction changes the whole plan.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Bedside priorities — the nurse runs the perfusion safety net

  • 📟 Continuous telemetry with defib pads on; shocked myocardium is arrhythmogenic and can arrest.
  • 💧 Hourly urine output via catheter — the cheapest continuous perfusion monitor at the bedside; flag oliguria (< 0.5 mL/kg/h) immediately.
  • 🧪 Draw serial lactates on schedule — a falling lactate means the resuscitation is working; a rising one means escalate.
  • 💉 Vasoactive infusions: confirm central access, double-check pump rates, never bolus/interrupt a noradrenaline line, and watch the arterial trace for real-time MAP.
  • 🧊 Reassess peripheries and mentation each round — warming hands and clearing confusion are the first signs perfusion is returning.
  • 🚫 Challenge any further diuretic order while she is hypoperfused — and escalate to the doctor if the plan is "more furosemide" in a cold patient.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The hourly urine and the serial lactate are the nurse's two most honest perfusion monitors — trust them over a single reassuring blood pressure."

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🩸 Invasive arterial MAP beat-to-beat — target ≥ 65 mmHg; the cuff is unreliable in a vasoconstricted, low-output patient.
  • 🧪 Serial lactate + clearance — the single best "is my resuscitation working?" trend.
  • 💧 Hourly urine output — real-time renal perfusion; a rising output means the pump is being restored.
  • 🧠 Mentation & peripheral temperature — clearing confusion and warming hands = perfusion returning.
  • 📈 Continuous telemetry + ST monitoring, defib pads on — arrhythmia and ischaemia both peak here.
  • 🧵 Consider a PA catheter (selective) to measure cardiac output, filling pressures and SVR when the picture is unclear or MCS/transplant decisions loom (S20).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In shock the monitor's question is not 'what's the BP?' but 'is the lactate falling and the urine coming?' — those two say whether the pump is winning."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong in cardiogenic shock

  • 🩺 Multi-organ failure — cardiorenal AKI, ischaemic/congestive hepatopathy, gut ischaemia, DIC. The lactate is the herald.
  • ⚡ Malignant arrhythmia / cardiac arrest — VT/VF on an ischaemic, catecholamine-loaded myocardium; why pads stay on.
  • 🌀 Refractory shock (SCAI D→E) — failure to respond to escalating pressors/inotropes; the point to have already called the shock team.
  • 🫁 Worsening pulmonary oedema / respiratory failure — may need NIV or intubation (mind the haemodynamic hit of induction).
  • 🔧 Mechanical support complications — limb ischaemia, haemolysis, bleeding, stroke, and ECMO "north–south" (differential hypoxia) syndrome.
  • 🕳️ The system complication — delay: late recognition and late escalation. The commonest lethal "complication" is the hours lost chasing congestion while perfusion collapsed.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The deadliest complication of cardiogenic shock is the clock — every hour of unrecognised hypoperfusion is an organ you won't get back."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Two very different journeys out of the shock bed

💔 This patient (recovered from CS)

  • Full GDMT reinstated & uptitrated (ARNI/β-blocker/MRA/SGLT2i), diuretic to euvolaemia
  • Adherence & access to medication addressed (the precipitant here); pharmacist + HF nurse
  • Advanced-HF referral: durable LVAD/transplant candidacy given recurrent shock, EF 20%
  • Red flags: weight gain, breathlessness, cold/clammy, presyncope → contact HF team / 999
  • Early HF-clinic follow-up, home monitoring, cardiac rehab

🕊️ The patient not for escalation

  • Frail/multi-morbid, not an MCS/transplant candidate, recurrent refractory shock
  • Honest prognosis; shared decision-making; ceiling-of-care agreed
  • Palliative HF pathway: symptom control (breathlessness, congestion), deprescribing
  • Community palliative + HF support; avoid futile escalation

💎 Clinical Pearls

🧊 Cold AND wetRestore perfusion first, then decongest — diuretics alone in a hypoperfused patient cause organ failure.
🩸 Noradrenaline, not dopamineFirst-line vasopressor in cardiogenic shock; dopamine means more arrhythmia and harm.
💪 Inotrope choiceDobutamine vs milrinone are equivalent (DOREMI); pick by physiology — milrinone is renally cleared and more vasodilatory.
📶 Stage & escalate earlySCAI A→E: the 'D' at midnight is 'E' by morning if you wait — call the shock team early.
🎈 No IABP reflexIABP-SHOCK II showed no mortality benefit — call the shock team, not the balloon pump.
🧪 Trust the lactateLactate and end-organs tell the truth the blood pressure hides; clearance = winning.
🔎 Find the causeRevascularise AMI-CS (SHOCK), fix arrhythmia, exclude a mechanical complication on echo.
🪜 Escalation ladderOptimise → inotropes/pressors → temporary MCS → durable LVAD/transplant or palliation.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the bundle

  • 🧵 Haemodynamic tailoring & the PA (Swan-Ganz) catheter: ESCAPE showed no routine benefit of PAC-guided therapy in decompensated HF — but the modern nuance is selective use in cardiogenic shock: to clarify LV vs RV vs biventricular failure, calculate cardiac power output and PAPi, and guide MCS/transplant decisions. Register data associate PAC use in shock with better outcomes when it changes management.
  • 🔧 Temporary MCS options & their physiology:
    • IABP — modest afterload reduction & coronary augmentation; no mortality benefit (IABP-SHOCK II); limited, selective role.
    • Impella / percutaneous LVAD — directly unloads the LV and augments forward flow; DanGer Shock showed a survival benefit in selected STEMI-CS at the cost of more complications (limb ischaemia, haemolysis, bleeding).
    • VA-ECMO — full cardiopulmonary support for the sickest (biventricular/refractory), but increases LV afterload → may need venting/LV unloading; watch for "north–south" (differential hypoxia) syndrome with returning native cardiac function.
  • 🧑‍⚕️ The shock team: a rapid, protocolised multidisciplinary team (HF cardiology, intensivist, interventional/surgery, perfusion) — associated with earlier escalation and improved survival. The single highest-yield "intervention" may be picking up the phone early.
  • 🧪 Lactate clearance & cardiac power output (CPO): lactate trend is a core resuscitation target; CPO (< 0.6 W predicts high mortality) and PAPi (RV failure) refine escalation timing.
  • 🌉 Bridge strategies: bridge-to-recovery (myocarditis, stunning), bridge-to-decision, bridge-to-transplant, bridge-to-durable-LVAD — define the destination early so temporary support has a purpose.
  • 🕊️ Palliative pathways: for the non-candidate or refractory patient, honest prognostication, ceilings of care and symptom-focused HF palliation are part of good shock care, not a failure of it.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "Diuresed Into the Ground"

The case: A 72-year-old woman with known HFrEF (EF 30%) was admitted with breathlessness and treated for "fluid overload." Overnight her BP drifted to 80/55 and her hands went cold, but the picture was read as "still congested." The on-call team gave escalating IV furosemide boluses through the night to "get the fluid off." Her urine dried up, her lactate climbed from 3 to 8, and her creatinine and transaminases soared. No one named the cold-and-wet cardiogenic shock, no perfusion support was started, and the shock team was never called. At 05:50 she became unresponsive and arrested in PEA from refractory shock and multi-organ failure. Resuscitation failed — with a ventricle that, hours earlier, could still have been supported.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Congested HF" label stuck; the low BP + cold skin were read as "still wet," not shock.
🩸 NO PERFUSION SUPPORTEscalating diuresis into a hypoperfused pump; no vasopressor/inotrope started.
📶 SHOCK NOT STAGEDNever placed on SCAI; the fall from B → C → D → E went unnamed.
📞 NO SHOCK-TEAM CALLAdvanced-HF / MCS options never considered while there was still time.
🧪 LACTATE IGNOREDA rising lactate on "treatment" was not recognised as losing ground.

🛡️ What would have saved her: recognise cold-and-wet cardiogenic shock at the bedside (cold, oliguric, rising lactate); stop reflex diuresis and restore perfusion first (noradrenaline ± inotrope to a MAP ≥ 65); stage it on SCAI and re-stage as she responds; trust the lactate and urine over the cuff; and call the shock team EARLY — before medical therapy fails, not after she arrests. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 4.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"IABP does not save lives in cardiogenic shock — call the shock team, not the balloon-pump reflex; and never diurese a cold, hypoperfused patient into organ failure."
