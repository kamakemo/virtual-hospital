The two classic failures are equal and opposite: over-testing/over-delaying a fit patient, and rushing a patient with a fresh stent to theatre by stopping antiplatelets.

🩺 Initial Assessment

🧭 The two axes of perioperative cardiac risk

Every preoperative estimate is the product of two axes: the PATIENT (clinical risk factors + functional capacity) and the SURGERY (low vs intermediate vs high risk). Neither alone decides — a fit patient tolerates high-risk surgery; a frail patient with poor capacity may struggle even with intermediate surgery.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The cardiology registrar thinks out loud:

🧠 Frame it as an estimate-and-optimise problem, not "yes/no clearance"

"He has no active cardiac condition today, so I don't cancel outright. I'll compute an RCRI, anchor everything on his functional capacity, and match it to the surgical risk. I will not reflexively order a stress test — I only test if the result would change management. His chronic beta-blocker and statin continue. The dominant issue is the 4-month-old drug-eluting stent on DAPT — I will not stop his antiplatelets to hit a surgical date; I'll talk timing with the surgeons."

🎯 The registrar's order of operations

  1. 🚦 Screen for active/unstable conditions — none today → proceed to stratify (S3).
  2. 🧮 RCRI (± NSQIP-MICA/Gupta) to estimate the major cardiac event rate (S5).
  3. 🚶 Functional capacity (METs / DASI) — the pivot for whether any further testing is even considered (S6).
  4. 🧪 Reserve stress imaging for poor/unknown capacity + high-risk surgery and only if it would change management; consider a pre-op BNP and plan post-op troponin surveillance (S9).
  5. 💊 Continue beta-blocker & statin; individualise ACEi/ARB; hold SGLT2 inhibitor; do NOT start acute high-dose beta-blockade (S11).
  6. 🩹 Do not stop DAPT early on a fresh DES → discuss delaying the elective operation until the mandatory antiplatelet window has passed (S15).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Only order the pre-op test if the result would change what you do — otherwise you're just delaying the operation and generating false trails."
🧪 Resident's self-testThe surgeon asks the registrar to "start a beta-blocker today to protect the heart during surgery." Should she?

No. Continue his existing chronic bisoprolol, but do NOT start high-dose beta-blockade acutely around surgery. The POISE trial showed that starting metoprolol succinate perioperatively cut non-fatal MI but caused more strokes and more total deaths (through hypotension and bradycardia). Chronic beta-blockade is protective and should not be stopped; acutely initiated high-dose beta-blockade is harmful.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY surgery stresses the heart (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. ⚡ Surgical stress response: tissue injury, pain, catecholamine and cortisol surges → tachycardia, hypertension, and increased myocardial oxygen demand.
  2. 🩸 Supply threats: anaemia from blood loss, hypotension, hypoxia and fluid shifts reduce myocardial oxygen supply. The result is a demand–supply mismatch — the substrate for a Type 2 peri-operative MI.
  3. 🧱 Prothrombotic, pro-inflammatory state: surgery activates platelets and coagulation and inflames vulnerable plaques → plaque rupture and thrombosis (Type 1 MI). On a fresh stent with antiplatelets withdrawn, this is exactly how stent thrombosis occurs.
  4. 🤫 Silent by design: anaesthesia and analgesia mask ischaemic pain — hence peri-operative injury is often MINS, detected only by troponin surveillance, not by symptoms.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Surgery is a stress test you can't cancel mid-way. Good functional capacity says the heart already passed a tougher one — poor capacity plus a big operation is where the mismatch bites."

⚖️ Contrast pair — good capacity vs poor capacity + high-risk surgery

🟢 Good functional capacity (≥ 4 METs)

  • Climbs two flights / walks uphill briskly without stopping
  • Tolerates surgery well; further cardiac testing NOT indicated
  • Proceed with optimisation — don't delay for tests

🟠 Poor / unknown capacity + high-risk surgery + clinical risk

  • Stops after one flight; multiple RCRI factors; major surgery
  • Consider biomarkers ± stress imaging — only if it would change management
  • This is the one box where a test can earn its place

💡 A pre-op stress test is not a ritual — it is only useful if a positive result would lead you to do something different (optimise medically, alter the anaesthetic plan, or — rarely — revascularise on its own indication). If the answer wouldn't change, the test just delays the operation.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The pre-op clinic order set

  • 🚦 Document absence of active/unstable cardiac conditions (no unstable angina, decompensated HF, significant arrhythmia, severe symptomatic valve disease).
  • 🧮 Calculate & record RCRI (± NSQIP-MICA / Gupta) and functional capacity (METs / DASI).
  • 📈 Resting 12-lead ECG (baseline for comparison — not a screening ritual in the well, low-risk patient).
  • 🩸 Baseline bloods: FBC (anaemia optimisation), U&E (CKD), HbA1c/glucose; consider pre-op BNP/NT-proBNP in this elevated-risk patient.
  • 🩺 Plan post-op high-sensitivity troponin surveillance (e.g., days 1–3) given elevated risk — to detect MINS.
  • 🚫 No routine stress imaging unless poor/unknown capacity + high-risk surgery AND the result would change management.
  • 💊 Continue beta-blocker & statin; individualise ACEi/ARB on the day; hold SGLT2 inhibitor ~3–4 days pre-op (euglycaemic DKA risk).
  • 🩹 Antithrombotic plan: do NOT stop DAPT to meet the surgical date on a 4-month DES → coordinate delaying the elective operation; if surgery were ever truly non-deferrable, continue aspirin and manage in a heart-team/anaesthetic discussion.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The cheapest, highest-value pre-op 'tests' are two questions and a calendar: 'How many stairs?' and 'When was the stent?' They decide more than any scan."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Pre-op clinic & peri-operative nursing priorities

  • 💊 Medication reconciliation is the safety net — confirm exactly which drugs continue, which hold, and when. Flag the DAPT and the SGLT2 inhibitor explicitly.
  • 🩹 Guard the antiplatelets — question any instruction to stop DAPT before the stent's window has passed; a fresh-stent patient who "held his blood thinners for surgery" is a red alert.
  • 🚶 Elicit functional capacity plainly — "Can you climb two flights without stopping? Walk up a hill? Do the garden?" Nurses often get the truest activity history.
  • 🩸 Draw baseline bloods; ensure anaemia and glucose are optimised before a major operation.
  • 🧾 Clear written instructions for the patient: which tablets to take on the morning of surgery, which to omit, and the agreed surgical date/timing.
  • 🩺 Post-op: ensure the planned troponin surveillance and pain control happen; report new hypotension, tachycardia, or breathlessness promptly (may be silent ischaemia/MINS).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who spots 'stop all blood thinners for surgery' on a fresh-stent patient and questions it prevents a stent thrombosis before it happens."

The correct move for an elective operation is to DELAY the surgery until the mandatory DAPT period has elapsed — longer for drug-eluting than bare-metal stents. Stopping DAPT early to keep a surgical date is how stent thrombosis and peri-op MI happen.
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch peri-operatively and why

  • 🩸 Blood pressure & heart rate: avoid intra-op hypotension and tachycardia — both drive the demand–supply mismatch behind Type 2 MI.
  • 🩺 Post-op high-sensitivity troponin surveillance (e.g., days 1–3) in elevated-risk patients — to detect silent MINS, which anaesthesia and analgesia mask.
  • 🫁 Oxygenation & haemoglobin: treat hypoxia and significant anaemia; both reduce myocardial oxygen supply.
  • 💧 Fluid balance: avoid extremes — hypovolaemia and overload both stress the heart, especially with renal impairment.
  • 🩹 Antithrombotic continuity: ensure aspirin/agreed antiplatelet plan is honoured; watch for any inadvertent early cessation on a stented patient.
  • 📈 ECG for new symptoms or a troponin rise — compare against the pre-op baseline.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After surgery the ischaemia is usually silent — the monitor is the troponin, not the patient's complaint. A rise without chest pain (MINS) is still prognostically loud."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong peri-operatively

  • 🩹 Stent thrombosis — the catastrophic complication of stopping DAPT too early on a fresh stent; presents as acute MI, often with the operation barely finished.
  • 🫀 Peri-operative MI — Type 1 (plaque rupture) or Type 2 (demand–supply mismatch); frequently silent (MINS).
  • 🧠 Stroke — a specific harm of acutely-initiated high-dose beta-blockade (POISE) and of poorly managed antithrombotics.
  • 💔 Heart failure / arrhythmia from fluid shifts, ischaemia, or electrolyte derangement.
  • 🩸 Bleeding — the flip-side risk that tempts premature antiplatelet cessation; managed by timing, not by stripping a fresh stent of protection.
  • 🕳️ The system complications: over-testing/over-delaying a fit patient (needless stress tests that delay urgent surgery) and rushing a fresh-stent patient (stopping DAPT) — equal and opposite failures.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The two peri-op disasters are mirror images: delaying the fit patient who never needed a test, and rushing the fresh-stent patient who needed to wait. Good assessment avoids both."

💎 Clinical Pearls

🚶 Stairs beat scans≥4 METs (two flights / uphill briskly) predicts good tolerance — good functional capacity beats most stress tests.
🧮 RCRI counts sixHigh-risk surgery, IHD, HF, stroke/TIA, insulin diabetes, renal impairment; ≥3 = high risk.
🧪 Test to change managementOnly order the pre-op test if the result would change what you do — otherwise it just delays care.
🩹 Never strip a fresh stentDon't stop DAPT to operate on a recent stent — delay the elective surgery instead.
💊 Continue, don't start (β-blocker)Continue chronic beta-blockers; NEVER start high-dose beta-blockade acutely (POISE — strokes/deaths).
🧴 Statin staysContinue statins throughout the peri-operative period.
🍬 Hold the SGLT2iStop SGLT2 inhibitors a few days before surgery — euglycaemic DKA risk.
🩺 Watch for silent MINSPost-op troponin rises are often painless but strongly prognostic — plan surveillance.
🚫 CARP lessonRoutine pre-op coronary revascularisation doesn't improve outcomes — revascularise only on its own indication.
🚦 Active conditions firstUnstable angina, decompensated HF, big arrhythmia, severe symptomatic AS → postpone & treat.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🧮 Risk calculators beyond RCRI: the NSQIP-MICA (Gupta) calculator and the ACS-NSQIP Surgical Risk Calculator use procedure-specific data and often outperform RCRI for absolute risk, but RCRI remains the memorable bedside index. Use a calculator to give patients a real number for shared decision-making.
  • 🚶 Functional capacity, objectively: subjective METs are imperfect — the METS study showed clinician gestalt poorly predicted objective capacity and outcomes. DASI (Duke Activity Status Index) and CPET (cardiopulmonary exercise testing, anaerobic threshold / VO₂ peak) add objectivity when capacity is genuinely unclear and would change the plan.
  • 🧪 Biomarker-based strategy: pre-op BNP/NT-proBNP refines risk; structured post-op high-sensitivity troponin surveillance detects MINS (VISION) — silent injury that predicts 30-day mortality. Management of MINS (intensifying secondary prevention; the MANAGE trial of dabigatran) remains an evolving area.
  • 💊 Medication nuance: continue statins & chronic beta-blockers; the ACEi/ARB hold-vs-continue debate (vasoplegia & intra-op hypotension vs rebound) is individualised; hold SGLT2 inhibitors for euglycaemic DKA; antiplatelet/anticoagulation bridging decisions weigh thrombotic vs bleeding risk (bridging rarely helps and often harms in low-risk AF).
  • 🩹 Timing after coronary stents: defer elective surgery — historically ≥6 weeks after bare-metal and ≥6 months (minimum 3 months if surgery cannot wait) after drug-eluting stents — keeping DAPT intact; newer-generation DES and shortened-DAPT data are shifting these thresholds, but the principle (don't operate mid-DAPT electively) holds.
  • 🫀 Revascularisation before surgery: CARP and DECREASE-V showed routine pre-op coronary revascularisation does NOT improve non-cardiac surgical outcomes — revascularise only for standard indications, not "to get through surgery."
  • 🧓 Frailty & the meta-principle: frailty independently predicts poor surgical outcomes and complements cardiac risk assessment. Overarching all of it: do not delay or over-test when it won't change management — a needless test cascade harms by delaying needed (sometimes cancer) surgery.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Stent That Couldn't Wait"

The case: A 64-year-old man had a drug-eluting stent implanted six weeks earlier and was booked for an elective inguinal hernia repair. To "reduce bleeding," his dual antiplatelet therapy was stopped five days before the operation and the surgery went ahead on schedule. On the second post-operative day he developed crushing chest pain, hypotension and anterior ST elevation — acute stent thrombosis with a large peri-operative myocardial infarction. The stent's mandatory antiplatelet window had not passed. The operation was entirely elective — it should simply have been postponed, and the antiplatelet therapy never interrupted.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

📅 TIMING IGNOREDElective surgery scheduled well inside a fresh DES's DAPT window.
🩹 DAPT STOPPEDAntiplatelets ceased to "reduce bleeding" — stripping the stent of protection.
🗣️ POOR COMMUNICATIONNo heart-team conversation between surgeon, anaesthetist & cardiologist.
⏱️ SCHEDULE OVER SAFETYThe surgical date drove the decision instead of the stent's biology.
🤫 SILENT RISK UNDERWEIGHTEDPeri-op stent thrombosis wasn't anticipated or surveilled for.

🛡️ What would have saved him: recognise a fresh drug-eluting stent as an absolute reason to delay elective surgery until the DAPT window passes; never stop dual antiplatelet therapy to keep a surgical date; convene a heart-team discussion; and continue aspirin if any interruption is ever unavoidable. Contrast this with the opposite failure — a fit patient sent through a needless cascade of stress tests that delayed urgent cancer surgery. Both errors share one root: a decision that did not match the biology in front of it. The patient in Bed 12 avoided both.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Never stop dual antiplatelet therapy to operate on a fresh coronary stent — and never delay urgent surgery for a test that won't change the plan. Match the decision to the biology, not to the calendar or the checklist."
