🧭 The three cardio-oncology emergencies you must not miss

Alongside slow-burn CTRCD, keep three time-critical diagnoses in mind: immune checkpoint inhibitor myocarditis (fulminant, high mortality — troponin rise, arrhythmia, conduction block), acute decompensated heart failure / cardiogenic shock from severe CTRCD, and VEGF/TKI-driven hypertensive emergency. One echo, one ECG, troponin and natriuretic peptides triage all three (see S14).

⚠️ The trap of "the EF is still normal"A normal LVEF does not exclude early cardiotoxicity. By the time EF falls, meaningful myocyte injury has already occurred. Global longitudinal strain and biomarkers detect the injury earlier — a relative GLS fall >15% is abnormal even with a preserved EF.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The cardiology resident thinks out loud:

🧠 Frame it as risk + surveillance, not stop/continue

"High cardiotoxicity risk: anthracycline near the threshold dose, trastuzumab on board, planned chest radiotherapy, and baseline CV risk factors. My job isn't a reflex 'stop the chemo' — it's to define the injury (echo/GLS, troponin, NT-proBNP), grade the CTRCD, start heart-failure GDMT early, discuss cardioprotection, and sit down with oncology to decide whether HER2 therapy can safely continue under monitoring. And every review I ask myself the ICI question: is she on a checkpoint inhibitor, and could this be myocarditis?"

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🖼️ Echo with global longitudinal strain — compare to baseline; a relative GLS drop >15% or an EF fall defines/grades CTRCD (S5, S9).
  2. 🧪 Troponin + natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) — trend against baseline; both rising supports evolving injury.
  3. 🩺 Grade the CTRCD (asymptomatic mild/moderate/severe vs symptomatic) — this drives whether therapy continues, pauses, or stops.
  4. 💊 Start HF GDMT early (ACEi/ARB, beta-blocker ± MRA/SGLT2i) for detected dysfunction — cardioprotective and often EF-recovering.
  5. 📞 Joint decision with oncology on continuing/modifying cancer therapy ± cardioprotection (e.g. dexrazoxane for high-dose anthracycline). Do not stop curative therapy unilaterally.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Cardio-oncology's job is to enable the cancer cure, not to cancel it — monitor and protect the heart so oncology can keep treating."
🧪 Resident's self-testHer EF is 45% and asymptomatic-mild; a colleague says "stop the trastuzumab immediately." Correct?

Not reflexively. Trastuzumab dysfunction is usually reversible and often manageable: for mild/moderate asymptomatic CTRCD, start HF GDMT, intensify surveillance, and discuss with oncology whether to continue (sometimes with a brief hold and re-echo) — HER2 therapy can frequently be resumed. Unilaterally abandoning curative treatment without cardio-oncology input can cost the cancer cure.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY these drugs hurt the heart (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 💉 Anthracyclines (type-I): inhibit topoisomerase-2β in cardiomyocytes, generating reactive oxygen species and mitochondrial/DNA damage → myocyte death. Injury is dose-dependent, cumulative, and largely irreversible — lost myocytes don't come back.
  2. 🎯 Trastuzumab (type-II): blocks HER2/ErbB2 signalling that cardiomyocytes use for stress survival → functional dysfunction without much cell death. Usually reversible, especially on interruption; risk is amplified by prior anthracycline.
  3. 🩸 VEGF inhibitors / TKIs: impair nitric-oxide/endothelial signalling → hypertension, proteinuria (RAS activation), and sometimes LV dysfunction or QT effects.
  4. ⚡ Immune checkpoint inhibitors: release the brakes on T-cells, which can attack myocardium → myocarditis: T-cell infiltration, troponin release, conduction-system involvement (block/arrhythmia). Rare but often fulminant.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Anthracycline kills myocytes (permanent); trastuzumab stuns them (reversible). Mechanism is prognosis — and it dictates whether you can resume the drug."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Type-I vs Type-II cardiotoxicity

💉 Type-I (Anthracycline)

  • Cumulative, dose-dependent, largely IRREVERSIBLE (myocyte death)
  • Risk climbs steeply with cumulative dose
  • Prevent: dose limits, dexrazoxane, surveillance, early GDMT

🎯 Type-II (Trastuzumab)

  • Usually REVERSIBLE, functional dysfunction
  • Often recovers on interruption; manageable to allow continued therapy
  • Manage: GDMT, brief hold + re-echo, frequently resume HER2 therapy

💡 A falling EF on treatment is not automatically "stop everything" — ask which mechanism, how severe, and whether the heart can be protected while the cancer is still being cured.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set

  • 🖼️ Echo with GLS now (compare to baseline), then per surveillance schedule; cardiac MRI if images poor or ICI myocarditis suspected.
  • 🧪 Troponin + NT-proBNP now and serially against baseline; U&E, FBC, TFTs, glucose/HbA1c, lipids.
  • 💊 Start HF GDMT for detected CTRCD: ACEi/ARB (or ARNI), beta-blocker, ± MRA, ± SGLT2 inhibitor — titrate as tolerated.
  • 🛡️ Cardioprotection discussion: dexrazoxane for high cumulative anthracycline; optimise BP; treat CV risk factors.
  • 📞 Joint oncology–cardiology decision on continuing/modifying trastuzumab under monitoring — do not stop curative therapy unilaterally.
  • ⚡ ICI safety net: if on/starting a checkpoint inhibitor and troponin rises with arrhythmia/block → admit, telemetry, urgent workup, hold the drug.
  • 🩺 12-lead ECG (QTc, conduction), BP log, daily weights; patient education on HF red-flag symptoms.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Start the heart-failure meds early and protect the myocardium — the cheapest way to keep a cancer patient on curative therapy is to treat the heart, not to abandon the oncology."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Cardio-oncology nursing — the surveillance safety net

  • 📅 Never let surveillance slip — chase the scheduled echo/GLS and biomarker draws; a "deferred" surveillance echo is how subclinical injury becomes overt heart failure.
  • 🫁 Screen for HF symptoms each visit — new breathlessness, orthopnoea, PND, ankle swelling, fatigue; daily weights; a 2 kg jump flags fluid retention.
  • 🩸 Draw and time-stamp troponin / NT-proBNP precisely; the trend against baseline is what matters.
  • ⚡ On immunotherapy, watch for myocarditis — palpitations, syncope, new chest discomfort, or an abnormal rhythm strip → escalate immediately; do not wait for the next clinic.
  • 💊 Support GDMT titration (BP, HR, renal function, symptoms) and adherence; reassure that heart meds are how the cancer treatment continues.
  • 🧾 Reinforce that the goal is to continue curative therapy safely — reduce the fear that drives patients to abandon treatment.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who protects the surveillance schedule protects both the heart and the cancer cure — a missed echo is a missed chance to catch reversible injury."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 Surveillance — what to watch and when

  • 🖼️ Baseline echo + GLS + biomarkers before therapy, then serially during treatment (schedule set by drug, dose and baseline risk category), and after completion for late toxicity.
  • 💉 Anthracyclines: surveillance intensifies with cumulative dose and high-risk patients; repeat imaging at higher doses and if symptoms change.
  • 🎯 Trastuzumab: periodic echo (commonly ~3-monthly) during therapy; act on EF/GLS change with GDMT and a hold/re-echo decision.
  • 🩸 VEGF/TKI: monitor blood pressure closely (frequent early BP checks), plus proteinuria and QTc where relevant.
  • ⚡ Immune checkpoint inhibitors: baseline troponin/ECG and a low threshold to recheck — any cardiac symptom, arrhythmia or troponin rise triggers the myocarditis pathway.
  • 📅 Survivorship: long-term cardiovascular follow-up — anthracycline/radiation injury can declare years later.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Surveillance is not paperwork — it's the difference between catching reversible strain change and meeting an EF of 25%. Baseline before, serial during, follow-up after."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong

  • 💔 Symptomatic heart failure / cardiogenic shock — from severe or unrecognised CTRCD (the missed-surveillance endgame).
  • ⚡ Fulminant ICI myocarditis — arrhythmia, complete heart block, cardiogenic shock, sudden death; the highest-mortality cardio-oncology emergency.
  • 🩸 VEGF/TKI hypertensive emergency, proteinuria/nephrotic-range renal injury, and rarely arterial thrombotic events.
  • 📏 QT prolongation → torsades from QT-affecting oncology drugs plus electrolyte disturbance.
  • 🩻 Radiation heart disease — accelerated coronary disease, valve disease, pericardial constriction, conduction disease (over years).
  • 🚫 The system complication: reflexively stopping curative cancer therapy without cardio-oncology input — costing the cancer cure to "protect" a heart that could have been treated instead.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Two opposite failures kill in cardio-oncology: missing the heart injury until overt HF, and abandoning the life-saving cancer therapy that a little heart medicine would have let you continue."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 💉 Type-I vs type-II toxicity: anthracycline (topoisomerase-2β-mediated free-radical myocyte death — dose-dependent, cumulative, largely irreversible) vs trastuzumab (HER2/ErbB2-blockade functional dysfunction — usually reversible). Mechanism dictates whether the drug can be resumed.
  • 🩸 VEGF-inhibitor / TKI cardiovascular toxicity: endothelial NO disruption → hypertension; RAS activation and glomerular injury → proteinuria; some agents prolong QT or depress LV function. Aggressive BP control enables continued therapy.
  • 📏 QT-prolonging oncology drugs: arsenic trioxide, some TKIs, antiemetics and azoles — cumulative QTc risk with electrolyte disturbance; monitor QTc, correct K⁺/Mg²⁺, review interactions to prevent torsades.
  • 🩻 Radiation heart disease: a delayed, additive injury over years — accelerated coronary disease, valvular fibrosis/stenosis, constrictive pericarditis, and conduction disease; drives long-term survivorship surveillance.
  • ⚡ ICI myocarditis: rare (~1%) but often fulminant with very high mortality; troponin rise, arrhythmia/AV block, MRI oedema/LGE, ± myositis/myasthenic overlap. Treat with urgent high-dose corticosteroids and permanent drug cessation; escalate to second-line immunosuppression (MMF, ATG, abatacept, tocilizumab) if steroid-refractory.
  • 📐 GLS- and biomarker-guided cardioprotection: strain/biomarker surveillance detects subclinical injury early, allowing pre-emptive GDMT and dexrazoxane before the EF falls — the paradigm shift from "wait for overt HF" to "protect the heart in advance."
  • 🤝 The enabling principle: the modern goal is to enable life-saving cancer therapy — grade the toxicity, protect and treat the heart, and continue or safely modify oncology treatment through a shared MDT decision, rather than reflexively stopping curative therapy.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Surveillance That Was Deferred"

The case: A 54-year-old woman with HER2-positive breast cancer received doxorubicin then trastuzumab on a busy curative protocol. Her baseline echo was normal, but during a stretched, over-booked treatment course her surveillance echoes were repeatedly "deferred" and her biomarkers not trended. She mentioned mild breathlessness twice; it was attributed to "chemo fatigue." Four months later she arrived breathless at rest, orthopnoeic, with an EF of 25% and florid heart failure. The subclinical strain change that would have flagged reversible injury months earlier had never been measured. Her HER2 therapy was stopped, her cancer treatment derailed, and despite GDMT her ventricle only partially recovered.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

📅 SURVEILLANCE SKIPPEDScheduled echo/GLS + biomarkers "deferred" — the subclinical injury was never seen.
📐 STRAIN NOT MEASUREDGLS would have fallen >15% while the EF still looked acceptable.
🫁 SYMPTOMS DISMISSEDBreathlessness labelled "chemo fatigue" instead of early HF.
💊 GDMT TOO LATEHeart-failure meds started only at EF 25% — after irreversible loss.
🎯 THERAPY LOSTCurative HER2 treatment abandoned — a stop that early protection might have avoided.

🛡️ What would have saved her: a baseline study, protected serial surveillance with echo/GLS and biomarkers, treating any new breathlessness as possible HF, starting GDMT and cardioprotection at the first subclinical drop — and quite possibly continuing her HER2 therapy safely under monitoring. The mirror-image disaster is the fulminant checkpoint-inhibitor myocarditis mistaken for "a viral illness," sent home, and found in arrest — the antidote there is a troponin, an ECG, and the reflex to give steroids and stop the drug. This is exactly the discipline that protected the patient in Bed 10.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A 'deferred' surveillance echo has cost more hearts than any single drug — and a checkpoint-inhibitor myocarditis called 'a virus' has cost lives. Measure the strain; check the troponin; act early."
