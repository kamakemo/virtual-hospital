🧭 The three sight-robbers you weigh at the bedside

For "new headache + visual symptoms in an older adult," your reflex differential is GCA (arteritic AION), non-arteritic AION (small crowded disc, no systemic features), and retinal artery occlusion / embolic amaurosis (carotid, cardiac source). GCA is the one that is both treatable and bilateral-blinding if missed (see S14).

⚠️ The trap of "the ESR/CRP is not back yet"Do NOT wait for inflammatory markers to start treatment when suspicion is high. Up to ~4% of biopsy-proven GCA has a normal ESR; CRP raises sensitivity but is not perfect. Markers support and monitor — they do not gate the first steroid dose.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the headache, WHY the blindness (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🛡️ Trigger + immune activation: in a genetically primed older adult, dendritic cells in the arterial adventitia activate; the immune-privileged vessel wall loses tolerance.
  2. 🧫 Granulomatous panarteritis: CD4⁺ T-cells (Th1 → IFN-γ; Th17 → IL-17), macrophages and multinucleated giant cells infiltrate large- and medium-sized arteries with an elastic lamina — especially branches of the external carotid (temporal, ophthalmic, posterior ciliary) and the aorta.
  3. 🧱 Intimal hyperplasia + luminal occlusion: IL-6-driven inflammation and growth factors (PDGF, VEGF) thicken the intima. The lumen narrows and occludes — this is why the ESR/CRP soar and why the artery becomes hard and pulseless.
  4. 👁️ Ischaemia downstream: occlusion of the posterior ciliary arteries starves the optic nerve head → arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy → sudden, painless, often irreversible visual loss. Jaw claudication = masseter ischaemia; scalp tenderness = superficial temporal ischaemia; the headache = inflamed cranial arteries.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"GCA is a race between the granuloma closing the artery and you opening the steroids. Amaurosis fugax is the last warning before the door to the optic nerve shuts for good."

⚖️ Contrast pair — GCA vs PMR

👁️ Giant cell arteritis (GCA)

  • Large/medium-vessel granulomatous vasculitis
  • Cranial: headache, scalp tenderness, jaw claudication, visual loss
  • Sight-threatening emergency — high-dose steroids (IV if visual)
  • Prednisolone 40–60 mg/day

💪 Polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR)

  • Proximal girdle pain & morning stiffness, no true weakness
  • Raised ESR/CRP; NO cranial or visual features by itself
  • Not sight-threatening — lower-dose steroids (prednisolone 15 mg/day)
  • Dramatic response to steroids within days is characteristic

💡 They are two ends of one disease spectrum. Any PMR patient who develops a new headache, jaw claudication, or visual symptom has "converted" and needs GCA-dose steroids immediately.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (09:50)

  • 💊 IV methylprednisolone 500–1000 mg once daily × 3 days (visual symptoms) → then oral prednisolone 40–60 mg/day. Give the first dose NOW.
  • 🧪 ESR + CRP, FBC, U&E, LFTs, glucose, HbA1c, bone profile, ± CK (to exclude myositis mimicking PMR).
  • 🔬 Temporal artery ultrasound today; book temporal artery biopsy within 1–2 weeks (do not delay steroids for it).
  • 🦴 Bone protection from day 1: calcium/vitamin D + bisphosphonate; PPI for gastric protection.
  • 🩸 Glucose monitoring (steroid hyperglycaemia); baseline BP and weight.
  • 💊 Consider low-dose aspirin in selected cranial/ischaemic cases; plan tocilizumab discussion for steroid sparing.
  • 👁️ Urgent same-day ophthalmology review and documented visual acuity in both eyes.
  • 📞 If acuity drops acutely → escalate steroid pulse and ophthalmology immediately.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The first prescription in suspected GCA is the steroid, written before the biopsy is booked — and the second is the bone/gastric protection written in the same breath."

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Day-unit priorities — the nurse runs the safety net

  • 👁️ Serial visual checks — record acuity and any new visual change every few hours; any drop is an emergency requiring immediate escalation.
  • 💊 Give the IV methylprednisolone promptly and correctly — infuse over the prescribed time, monitor for arrhythmia, flushing, mood/insomnia, and glucose spikes.
  • 🩸 Capillary glucose monitoring — high-dose steroids commonly unmask or worsen diabetes; flag persistent hyperglycaemia.
  • 🧾 Counsel on the steroid card, never to stop steroids abruptly (adrenal crisis risk), and PPI/bone-protection adherence.
  • 🧠 Reassure and inform — she is frightened of going blind; clear explanation reduces distress and improves adherence to a long taper.
  • 🦷 Support soft/easy-chew meals while jaw claudication persists; document intake and weight (constitutional weight loss).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The nurse who rechecks the vision on every round is the earliest detector of the fellow eye going — and the one who guards the glucose and bones from the cure."

🔬 Imaging / Biopsy / Serology + Visual Enhancements

🩻 Temporal artery ultrasound — the "halo sign"

The GCA vessel shows a thick, hypoechoic circumferential "halo" that stays visible under probe compression; the normal vessel has a thin wall and collapses. Ultrasound is fast, same-day, and stays positive for days after steroids start.

🔎 The confirmatory tests you must know cold

TestLook forMeaning / caveat
🩻 Vascular ultrasoundHalo sign; non-compressible artery ("compression sign")First-line, same-day; operator-dependent. Positive result can replace biopsy in expert hands (fast-track clinics).
🔬 Temporal artery biopsyTransmural granulomatous inflammation, giant cells, disrupted internal elastic laminaWithin ~1–2 weeks of starting steroids; skip lesions mean a negative biopsy does NOT exclude GCA — take an adequate length (≥1 cm).
🖥️ PET-CT / MRA / CTALarge-vessel FDG uptake / wall thickening of aorta & branchesFor suspected large-vessel GCA (extracranial disease, aortitis) — atypical presentations, fever of unknown origin, aneurysm surveillance.
🧪 Serology (to exclude mimics)ANCA, ANA, blood culturesGCA has NO specific autoantibody — serology is to rule out ANCA-vasculitis, infection, and other mimics, not to confirm GCA.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A negative temporal artery biopsy in a classic GCA patient means 'you missed a skip lesion,' not 'she does not have GCA.' Keep treating and confirm with ultrasound, the fellow side, or large-vessel imaging."

🩻 Adjunct assessment

  • Fundoscopy / OCT: pallid disc swelling of arteritic AION; distinguishes from hyperaemic non-arteritic AION.
  • Aortic imaging: long-term surveillance for thoracic aortic aneurysm/dissection — a late large-vessel complication of GCA.

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 👁️ Visual acuity and symptoms — the fellow eye stays at risk early; any new visual change is an emergency and prompts steroid escalation.
  • 🧪 ESR + CRP trajectory — should fall rapidly with steroids; a re-rise with symptoms signals relapse (note: tocilizumab suppresses CRP, so track symptoms too).
  • 🩸 Glucose/HbA1c and BP — steroid hyperglycaemia and hypertension; adjust proactively.
  • 🦴 Bone health — DEXA and ongoing bone protection through the long taper.
  • 💪 PMR symptoms — girdle stiffness should melt within days of steroids; failure to respond should make you doubt the diagnosis.
  • 🫀 Aortic surveillance — long-term imaging for aneurysm in large-vessel GCA.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you start steroids, the monitor's question shifts from 'is this GCA?' to 'is the taper going too fast (relapse) or the steroids doing their own harm (bones, glucose)?' — and both answers save the patient."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — from the disease and from the cure

  • 👁️ Irreversible blindness (arteritic AION / CRAO) — the feared endpoint; often the fellow eye within 1–2 weeks if untreated.
  • 🧠 Stroke / TIA — vertebrobasilar territory from vertebral/carotid arteritis.
  • 🫀 Aortitis, thoracic aortic aneurysm and dissection — late large-vessel complications requiring surveillance.
  • 🦴 Steroid osteoporosis and fragility fractures — the commonest iatrogenic harm.
  • 🩸 Steroid diabetes, hypertension, infection, GI ulceration, adrenal suppression — the price of prolonged therapy.
  • 🔁 Relapse on taper — recurrence of headache, PMR, or acute-phase rise as the dose comes down (a driver for tocilizumab).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The deadliest complication of GCA is the sight you failed to save by hesitating; the commonest is the fracture you failed to prevent by forgetting bone protection. Guard against both from hour one."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🫀 Large-vessel GCA (LV-GCA): up to a third of GCA has extracranial large-vessel involvement (aorta, subclavian, axillary). It may present with limb claudication, bruits, fever of unknown origin or few cranial symptoms — PET-CT and MRA/CTA are key, and it drives long-term aneurysm/dissection risk.
  • 🧬 Immunology: GCA is a Th1 (IFN-γ) and Th17 (IL-17) driven granulomatous arteritis; IL-6 underlies the acute-phase response and is the therapeutic target of tocilizumab. Dendritic cells in the adventitia and vascular smooth-muscle remodelling (PDGF/VEGF, intimal hyperplasia) explain luminal occlusion.
  • 💉 Steroid-sparing agents: tocilizumab (GiACTA) is first-line adjunct; methotrexate has modest steroid-sparing benefit; emerging agents target GM-CSF (mavrilimumab) and JAK pathways (upadacitinib in trials).
  • 🩻 Fast-track GCA clinics: same-day ultrasound-led pathways reduce time-to-treatment and permanent visual loss; a positive halo in a high-probability patient can obviate biopsy.
  • 👁️ Arteritic vs non-arteritic AION: pallid ("chalky") disc swelling, profound acuity loss, raised ESR/CRP and systemic features point to arteritic — a genuine emergency — versus the hyperaemic, small-disc, marker-negative non-arteritic form.
  • 🔗 GCA / PMR overlap and Takayasu: understanding the spectrum of large-vessel vasculitis (Takayasu in the young, GCA over 50) and PMR as a shared inflammatory phenotype informs imaging choice and follow-up.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Tension Headache That Took an Eye"

The case: A 74-year-old woman saw her GP with three weeks of a new right temporal headache and aching in her jaw when eating. Labelled "tension headache — stress and screen time," she was started on amitriptyline. An ESR of 92 sent that day returned to an inbox no one reviewed. She mentioned that her sight had "flickered" once; it was not documented. Four days later she woke blind in the right eye from arteritic AION. High-dose steroids were finally started — but nine days after that, before the taper stabilised, she lost useful vision in the left eye too. The temporal artery biopsy, taken late, still showed florid giant-cell arteritis.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Tension headache" label stuck despite a NEW headache in a 74-year-old.
🦷 SYMPTOM IGNOREDJaw claudication and transient visual flicker not recognised as GCA red flags.
🧪 RESULT UNREADESR 92 sat unactioned — the alarm that should have triggered treatment.
💊 STEROIDS DELAYEDTreatment withheld until fixed blindness, then again while "waiting" — losing the second eye.
🏥 SYSTEMNo fast-track GCA pathway; no safety-net for visual symptoms.

🛡️ What would have saved her sight: treat every new headache over 50 with jaw or visual symptoms as GCA; start high-dose steroids on clinical suspicion — IV methylprednisolone for visual involvement — WITHOUT waiting for the ESR or biopsy; act on the raised ESR the day it returns; and safety-net every patient to return instantly for any visual change. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 5.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Probably a tension headache' in an older patient with a raised ESR has blinded more people than any rare disease. When in doubt in GCA, the steroid goes in first and the biopsy waits."
