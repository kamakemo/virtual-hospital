🧬 Why THIS patient? — a calcium leak that only opens under adrenaline

CPVT is a channelopathy of intracellular calcium handling. The commonest culprit is a gain-of-function mutation in RyR2, the ryanodine receptor that releases calcium from the sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) on each beat (autosomal dominant). A recessive form involves CASQ2 (calsequestrin, the SR's calcium buffer). At rest the system behaves: the leak is silent. But under catecholamine drive — sport, fright, anger, a startle — phosphorylation of the channel makes the leaky RyR2 dump calcium into the cytosol during diastole. The sodium–calcium exchanger pumps that calcium out in exchange for sodium, generating a depolarizing transient inward current → delayed afterdepolarizations (DADs) → triggered beats → bidirectional/polymorphic VT → VF. Adrenaline is the trigger; the resting heart is innocent. That is why she has collapsed only ever while running or startled, and why every resting test has been spotless.

🔥 Red-flag features of an inherited arrhythmia in the young 🏃 Syncope during exertion or emotion (not on standing) · 🧬 Family history of sudden/unexplained death < 40 · 😵 Syncope with seizure-like activity miscalled epilepsy · 🏊 Syncope while swimming · 🔔 Collapse on being startled · 📈 A normal resting ECG that lulls the team into reassurance.

🧪 Resident's self-test (answer before reading on) Someone suggests giving IV epinephrine in clinic "to bring out the arrhythmia faster than the treadmill." Wise?

Be very careful. Catecholamine (epinephrine/isoprenaline) provocation can unmask CPVT and is used in specialist labs, but it directly feeds the exact trigger that causes lethal arrhythmia in this substrate. The exercise treadmill test is the preferred, more physiological and controllable provocation. If a drug challenge is done at all, it is in expert hands with full resuscitation readiness and conservative dosing — casually pushing epinephrine in a CPVT heart can precipitate the very VF you fear.

🔀 What is "bidirectional VT" — and is she in it?

Bidirectional VT is a ventricular tachycardia in which the QRS axis alternates 180° from beat to beat — one beat points up, the next points down, in a regular alternating pattern. It is the classic exercise signature of CPVT, arising from alternating foci of DAD-triggered activity (often the two fascicles firing in turn). The two great causes are CPVT and digoxin toxicity — the consultant's first question on seeing it is "which adrenaline (or which drug) am I feeding?" On this 14-year-old, not on digoxin, with exertional syncope and a positive family history, bidirectional VT on the treadmill is essentially diagnostic of CPVT.

🧭 Consultant's three commandments for this child

  1. 💊 "Nadolol, a non-selective beta-blocker, is first-line — it is the most effective single drug, beating selective agents. Start it, push the dose, and restrict competitive sport. The beta-blocker is the foundation everything else sits on."
  2. 💉 "If exercise still provokes ectopy on full beta-blockade, add flecainide — it inhibits the RyR2 leak directly and cuts exercise-induced arrhythmia. Beta-blocker plus flecainide is the combination that controls most patients."
  3. 🔌 "Reserve the ICD for arrest survivors or breakthrough events on maximal therapy — and never as a substitute for drugs. A painful shock is a catecholamine surge that can trigger MORE VT and even a shock storm. The ICD goes in alongside maximal beta-blockade and flecainide, programmed conservatively."
☠️ The consultant's war memory (more in S13 M&M)"I once inherited a boy with an ICD and nothing else — implanted after an arrest, then sent home on a tiny dose of a selective beta-blocker. He ran for a bus, went into VT, the device shocked him, the pain poured adrenaline onto the fire, he stormed, shock after shock, and we lost him in a corridor. The ICD didn't fail. We failed it — by giving it no drug therapy to lean on. An ICD alone can pour petrol on the fire."

📖 Teaching Points

🔬 The mechanism — WHY adrenaline makes a normal heart fire (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧬 The defective gene: a gain-of-function mutation in RyR2 (the ryanodine receptor, autosomal dominant) or loss-of-function in CASQ2 (calsequestrin, the SR's calcium buffer, recessive) makes the sarcoplasmic reticulum prone to leak calcium.
  2. 🔥 The trigger — catecholamines: under sport, fright or anger, β-adrenergic signalling phosphorylates the channel and loads the SR. Now the leaky RyR2 releases calcium into the cytosol during diastole, when it should be silent.
  3. 🔁 The exchanger generates current: the sodium–calcium exchanger (NCX) extrudes that diastolic calcium, importing 3 Na⁺ for each Ca²⁺ → a net inward (depolarizing) transient current.
  4. ⚡ Delayed afterdepolarizations (DADs): that current drags the membrane back up after repolarization. If it reaches threshold → a triggered beat → couplets → bidirectional VT → polymorphic VT → VF.
  5. 💥 Why it kills, and why the resting ECG lies: at rest there is no catecholamine drive, the leak is silent, and the 12-lead and QT are normal. Only adrenaline opens the trap — so the rhythm appears exactly when the child is sprinting or terrified, and disappears the moment they stop. The danger is in exertion; the alibi is in the resting strip.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"CPVT is a calcium leak that only opens under adrenaline. Block the adrenaline (beta-blocker) and plug the leak (flecainide) — and never trust the heart's behaviour at rest."

⚖️ Contrast pair #1 — CPVT vs Congenital Long QT Syndrome

🏃 CPVT

  • Normal resting ECG and QT
  • Arrhythmia only under catecholamines (exercise/emotion)
  • Signature: bidirectional / polymorphic VT on stress
  • RyR2/CASQ2 → SR Ca²⁺ leak → DADs
  • Diagnosed by exercise stress test, not resting strip

📏 Long QT Syndrome

  • Prolonged QT visible at rest
  • Torsades de Pointes (twisting around baseline)
  • Ion-channel repolarization defect (KCNQ1, KCNH2, SCN5A)
  • LQT1 exercise/swim, LQT2 startle, LQT3 sleep
  • Diagnosed largely by the resting QTc ± stress/genetics

😈 The trap: both can cause exercise-induced syncope in a young person, but the resting ECG separates them — long QT shows itself at rest, CPVT does not. Calling a CPVT child "normal ECG, therefore fine" is the lethal error. A normal QT sends you to the treadmill, it does not send you home.

⚖️ Contrast pair #2 — Bidirectional VT: CPVT vs Digoxin toxicity

🧬 CPVT (this patient)

  • Young, structurally normal heart, not on digoxin
  • Bidirectional VT provoked by exercise/emotion
  • Mechanism: RyR2 SR Ca²⁺ leak → DADs
  • Treat the substrate: nadolol + flecainide, sport restriction

💊 Digoxin toxicity

  • Older patient on digoxin, often renal impairment / low K⁺
  • Bidirectional VT plus other digoxin rhythms (e.g. PAT with block)
  • Mechanism: Na/K-ATPase poisoning → Ca²⁺ overload → DADs (same final pathway!)
  • Treat the toxin: stop digoxin, correct K⁺/Mg²⁺, digoxin-specific antibody (Fab)

💡 Why it matters: bidirectional VT has essentially two answers. Both work through DAD-triggered activity from calcium overload — but one is fixed by treating a genetic calcium leak and one by reversing a drug. Always ask, as the consultant did, "which adrenaline — or which drug — am I feeding?"

🔬 Fellowship Corner — Advanced Topics

  • 🧬 Genetics & inheritance: RyR2 (autosomal dominant, ~60% of cases), CASQ2 (autosomal recessive); newer genes (TECRL, CALM, TRDN). Cascade screening of first-degree relatives is mandatory — silent carriers may be at risk.
  • 💊 Why flecainide here is not "pro-arrhythmic CAST flecainide": in a structurally normal heart with a calcium-leak substrate, flecainide's direct RyR2 inhibition is therapeutic — the opposite of its danger in ischemic/structural disease.
  • 🔪 Left cardiac sympathetic denervation (LCSD): removal of the lower stellate ganglion and T2–T4 thoracic ganglia reduces left-sided sympathetic input to the heart; an antiadrenergic surgical option for refractory cases.
  • 🔌 ICD pitfalls in CPVT: shocks are pro-arrhythmic via catecholamine surge → shock storm; programme long detection, ATP-before-shock, high rate cut-offs, and always pair with maximal beta-blockade/flecainide and LCSD if needed.
  • 🏊 Phenotype clues: swimming-triggered and startle-triggered events overlap with LQT1/LQT2 — genetics and the stress test disambiguate. "Seizures" during exertion that are actually convulsive syncope are a classic missed CPVT presentation.
  • 🧪 Bidirectional VT differential beyond CPVT and digoxin: rare Andersen–Tawil syndrome and severe myocarditis — but in a young exertional-syncope patient, CPVT leads the field.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Nursing priorities — protect against the adrenaline trigger

  • 📟 Continuous telemetry with a defibrillator at the bedside during and after the stress test — watch into recovery, where the arrhythmia often peaks.
  • 🧊 Keep the environment calm and the patient settled. Fear, pain and agitation are catecholamine surges — in CPVT a stressful bedside is literally arrhythmogenic. Explain procedures gently, minimise startle.
  • 💊 Give nadolol on time, every time — adherence is the difference between a quiet heart and a fatal sprint. Watch for bradycardia/fatigue/wheeze (non-selective beta-blockade) and report.
  • 🏃 Reinforce activity restriction at every interaction; do NOT let a well-looking teenager be cleared to "just kick a ball around the ward."
  • 🚫 Avoid adrenergic stimulants — flag decongestants, sympathomimetics, high-caffeine energy drinks on the chart; double-check before any PRN.
  • 📋 Document every rhythm strip from the stress test and any spontaneous ectopy with the heart rate at which it appeared — gold for the EP team and for titrating therapy.
  • 🧠 Recognise convulsive syncope: a "seizure" during exertion in this patient is cardiac until proven otherwise — check pulse and rhythm first, don't reach for anticonvulsants.
  • 👨‍👩‍👧 Support the patient and family through a frightening, life-changing diagnosis (see S16); arrange a liaison if staffing allows.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In CPVT the nurse who keeps the room calm, the beta-blocker on time and the sport off the menu is doing half the antiarrhythmic work in the building."

🧊 Recovery-phase arrhythmiaEctopy often peaks early in cool-down — never unhook the monitor the moment exercise stops.
🧬 Structurally normal heartEcho/MRI normal — distinguishes CPVT from ARVC, HCM, scar VT and anomalous coronary.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"See bidirectional VT and ask: which adrenaline am I feeding? In a young exertional-syncope patient not on digoxin, the answer is CPVT — find the threshold, suppress it, prove it on a repeat test."

🫀 Resting ECG, echo & further imaging

  • 📈 Resting 12-lead: sinus rhythm, normal axis, QTc 410 ms, no delta wave, no coved ST — entirely normal. (A slightly low resting heart rate is sometimes seen.)
  • 🩻 Echocardiogram: structurally normal heart, normal biventricular size and function, no hypertrophy or scar — excludes structural causes of exercise syncope.
  • 🧲 Cardiac MRI (if any doubt): to exclude ARVC and other infiltrative/structural disease — in CPVT it is normal, by definition.
⚠️ The ECG's favorite lie in CPVT"The resting 12-lead is normal, so her heart is fine and this is vasovagal." A normal resting ECG is expected in CPVT and reassures you of nothing. The only test that tells the truth is the one that adds adrenaline — the exercise stress test. Absent that, assume an inherited arrhythmia.
S11

💊 Medications

💉 The drugs — what, why, and the traps

DrugRoleMechanism / caution
Nadolol (non-selective beta-blocker)First-line — the cornerstone of CPVT therapyBlunts the catecholamine trigger; non-selective beats selective agents. Titrate to maximal tolerated dose. Watch bradycardia, fatigue, bronchospasm; never stop abruptly.
FlecainideAdd-on when exercise still provokes ectopy on full beta-blockadeDirectly inhibits the leaky RyR2 and Na current → fewer DADs. (Its CAST danger is in structural/ischemic hearts — here the heart is normal and the effect is therapeutic.) Recheck with a stress test.
Propranolol / esmolol (IV)Acute antiadrenergic control during an electrical stormSympatholysis is therapeutic in CPVT storm; titrate, watch for hypotension/bradycardia.
LCSD (surgical, not a drug)Left cardiac sympathetic denervation for refractory casesReduces left-sided sympathetic drive; effective add-on for breakthrough events; mild ptosis/anhidrosis possible.
ICDSecondary prevention in arrest survivors / breakthrough on maximal therapy⚠️ Never alone. A painful shock is a catecholamine surge that can trigger MORE VT/shock storm. Always combine with maximal beta-blocker + flecainide; program conservatively (long detection, ATP).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Beta-blocker plus flecainide; an ICD alone can pour petrol on the fire. The shock that should save a CPVT heart can, if it stands alone, be the spark that storms it."
🚫 The 'never' list in CPVT❌ Catecholamines / sympathomimetics as casual "diagnostic" tests · ❌ Decongestants, stimulants, high-dose caffeine · ❌ Clearing the patient for competitive sport · ❌ Stopping the beta-blocker abruptly · ❌ Implanting an ICD as a stand-alone substitute for drug therapy. When in doubt, reduce the adrenaline and lean on nadolol + flecainide.

🏠 Long-term, disease-modifying strategy

  • 💊 Maximal nadolol + flecainide, with adherence reinforced at every visit — these are the foundation.
  • 🏃 Lifelong avoidance of competitive/strenuous sport and adrenergic triggers — lifestyle is treatment.
  • 🔪 LCSD for breakthrough events despite drugs.
  • 🔌 ICD for arrest survivors or recurrent syncope/VT on maximal therapy — always alongside drugs, conservatively programmed.
  • 🧬 Cascade family screening and treatment of affected relatives.
S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 🏃 Repeat exercise stress testing on therapy — the key outcome measure: nadolol ± flecainide should suppress exercise-induced ectopy and abolish bidirectional VT. If it doesn't, escalate.
  • 📟 Telemetry during and after every provocation, with attention to the recovery phase where arrhythmia peaks; defibrillator at the bedside.
  • 📈 Holter / ambulatory monitoring to capture day-to-day ectopy and correlate with symptoms and activity.
  • 💊 Beta-blocker dose & adherence — heart rate, symptoms, and exercise-test response; a quietly skipped nadolol is a quietly re-armed disease.
  • 🔌 If an ICD is placed: monitor for appropriate vs inappropriate shocks; in CPVT every shock risks a catecholamine surge — review programming (long detection, high rate cut-off, ATP).
  • 🧬 Family screening status — ensure first-degree relatives are tested and, if affected, treated.
  • 🧠 Symptom diary — any palpitations, dizziness, near-faint or collapse, and the activity that triggered it.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In CPVT the monitor's job is to answer one question — 'can adrenaline still make her heart misbehave?' — and the test that answers it is a repeat stress test on full therapy."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — and the mechanism

  • ⚡ Sudden cardiac death — the great fear; exercise/emotion triggers bidirectional/polymorphic VT degenerating to VF. Often the first "symptom" in undiagnosed family members.
  • 😵 Recurrent exertional syncope / convulsive syncope — repeatedly misdiagnosed as vasovagal or epilepsy, delaying the real diagnosis for years.
  • 🌀 Electrical storm — runs of VT, especially under stress, pain or after ICD shocks; sympathetically driven, needs deep antiadrenergic therapy.
  • 🔌 ICD shock storm (the CPVT-specific trap) — a painful shock pours catecholamines onto the substrate → more VT → more shocks, a self-feeding cycle. The device meant to save can ignite the storm if it stands alone.
  • 💊 Under-treatment from a normal resting ECG — false reassurance leading to inadequate drugs or clearance for sport; and inadvertent adrenergic stimulation (decongestants, stimulants, fright).
  • 🧬 Unrecognised affected relatives — without cascade screening, the disease silently endangers the family.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference — "The Child Who Was Cleared to Play"

The case: A 13-year-old boy had three "faints," all while playing sport, and one episode called a "seizure" after a startle. Each time the resting ECG was normal, the QT normal, the echo normal — so he was reassured, never stress-tested, never genetically screened, and cleared to play competitive football. His mother had mentioned a brother who "died young, running," but it was never charted. Six weeks later he collapsed during a match and could not be revived. At post-mortem the heart was structurally normal; targeted genetics found a pathogenic RyR2 variant. (In a separate near-miss the M&M reviewed, a CPVT teenager arrested in recovery and received repeated epinephrine in the code, which the substrate hated and which fuelled refractory VF.)

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 NORMAL ECG = ALL CLEARA normal resting 12-lead was taken as proof of a normal heart — in CPVT it proves nothing.
🏃 NEVER PROVOKEDNo exercise stress test was ever done — the one test that would have shown bidirectional VT.
🧬 FAMILY HX UNHEARDA young exertional death in the family was mentioned but never charted or acted on.
⚽ CLEARED FOR SPORTThe single most dangerous environment for this disease was actively permitted.
💉 ADRENALINE FEDIn the near-miss, epinephrine in the code poured fuel on a catecholamine-driven arrhythmia.

🛡️ What would have saved him: treat exertional syncope with a normal resting ECG as CPVT until a stress test says otherwise; provoke the heart (treadmill) before clearing anyone for sport; chart and act on a family history of young sudden death; start nadolol and restrict sport early; screen the family; and in a code, minimise gratuitous catecholamines. This is exactly why the registrar refused to discharge our patient as 'vasovagal.'

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Normal resting ECG, must be a faint' is the sentence written in the notes of children who then die of CPVT. A resting strip is not a stress test."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Discharge plan & safety net

  • 💊 Medications: nadolol at maximal tolerated dose + flecainide, with a clear written schedule and a "never stop the beta-blocker abruptly" warning. Provide a sick-day/forgotten-dose plan.
  • 🏃 Activity: strict avoidance of competitive and strenuous sport; a school letter excusing high-intensity PE; counsel on emotional/startle triggers and graded, gentle activity only as advised by EP.
  • 🚫 Avoid adrenergic stimulants: no decongestants/sympathomimetics, caution with high-caffeine energy drinks; check medications with pharmacy.
  • 🧬 Genetics & family: cascade screening of first-degree relatives booked; affected relatives referred for treatment; genetic counselling for the family.

  • 📚 Education: what CPVT is in plain language, why a normal resting ECG never reassured us, the importance of adherence and trigger avoidance, and CPR/AED training offered to the family.

💎 Clinical Pearls

📈 The resting ECG liesExercise/emotion-induced syncope with a NORMAL resting ECG is CPVT until a stress test proves otherwise. The resting 12-lead is the great liar.
🏃 Provoke to diagnoseThe exercise treadmill test, not the resting strip, makes the diagnosis — reproducible ectopy at a heart-rate threshold escalating to bidirectional VT.
🔬 Calcium-leak mechanismRyR2 gain-of-function → diastolic SR Ca²⁺ leak → NCX inward current → DADs → triggered bidirectional/polymorphic VT. Adrenaline is the trigger.
💊 Nadolol firstNon-selective beta-blockade (nadolol) is first-line and beats selective agents. Start early, titrate to maximum, restrict sport.
💉 Flecainide add-onFlecainide directly inhibits the leaky RyR2 — strong evidence as an add-on when exercise still provokes ectopy on a beta-blocker.
🔀 Bidirectional VT = 2 answersBidirectional VT means CPVT or digoxin toxicity — find out which adrenaline (or drug) you're feeding.
🔌 Never an ICD aloneBeta-blocker plus flecainide; an ICD alone can pour petrol on the fire — a painful shock surges catecholamines and can storm the heart.
🧬 It runs in familiesRyR2 is autosomal dominant, CASQ2 recessive. A young exertional death in the family is a red flag — screen first-degree relatives.
😵 "Seizures" can be cardiacConvulsive syncope during exertion is CPVT until proven otherwise — check the rhythm before reaching for anticonvulsants.
🚫 Avoid adrenalineNo competitive sport, no sympathomimetics/decongestants, minimise startle and stress — lifestyle is treatment.
🧊 Watch recoveryArrhythmia often peaks early in cool-down — never unhook the monitor the moment exercise stops.
🔪 LCSD for refractoryLeft cardiac sympathetic denervation reduces events when drugs aren't enough — before, or alongside, an ICD.
