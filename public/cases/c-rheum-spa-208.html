🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the pain, WHY the fusion (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🧬 Genetic priming: HLA-B27 (MHC class I) is present in ~90% of AS patients. Leading theories: aberrant peptide presentation, HLA-B27 heavy-chain misfolding triggering an ER stress/unfolded-protein response, and cell-surface homodimers driving innate immune activation.
  2. 🎯 The enthesis is the target organ: unlike rheumatoid arthritis (synovium-centred), spondyloarthritis centres on the enthesis — where tendon, ligament and capsule insert into bone. Biomechanical stress at entheses plus IL-23/IL-17 signalling drives local inflammation.
  3. 🔥 Inflammation → erosion: enthesitis and osteitis produce bone marrow oedema (the MRI signal) and erosions at the sacroiliac joints and vertebral corners (shiny "Romanus" corners). This is the painful, treatable, reversible phase.
  4. 🦴 Repair overshoots into new bone: after inflammation, reparative new bone formation bridges the vertebrae as syndesmophytes. Over years these fuse the spine into a rigid "bamboo spine" — the pain may fade but the spine is now brittle and fixed.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Rheumatoid arthritis erodes the joint; spondyloarthritis inflames the enthesis and then BUILDS bone. That is why AS ends not in a floppy destroyed joint but in a fused, rigid, fracture-prone spine."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Spondyloarthritis vs Rheumatoid Arthritis

🦴 Spondyloarthritis (this patient)

  • Target: the enthesis and axial skeleton (sacroiliitis)
  • Seronegative — RF and anti-CCP negative; linked to HLA-B27
  • Asymmetric large-joint / axial pattern; new bone formation → fusion
  • Extra-articular: uveitis, IBD, psoriasis

🖐️ Rheumatoid arthritis

  • Target: the synovium; small symmetric peripheral joints (MCP/PIP)
  • Seropositive — RF and anti-CCP often positive
  • Erosive joint destruction, no new-bone bridging
  • Extra-articular: nodules, lung, vasculitis

💡 "Seronegative" does not mean "mild" — it means the classic RA antibodies are absent and the disease belongs to the spondyloarthritis family (AS, psoriatic arthritis, reactive arthritis, IBD-associated arthritis).

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (11:10)

  • 🧲 MRI sacroiliac joints (STIR/T2 + T1) for active sacroiliitis (bone marrow oedema) and structural change; add MRI whole spine if fusion or fracture ever suspected.
  • 🧬 HLA-B27, CRP & ESR, FBC, U&E, LFTs, glucose.
  • 🦠 Pre-biologic screen anticipating escalation: latent TB (IGRA / CXR), hepatitis B/C, HIV — biologics reactivate TB.
  • 🏃 Refer to specialist physiotherapy today — structured exercise and posture work are disease-modifying, not just symptomatic.
  • 💊 Regular NSAID (e.g. naproxen) at anti-inflammatory dose, continuous trial ≥ 2–4 weeks, with gastroprotection (PPI).
  • 👁️ Advise urgent same-day ophthalmology if a red, painful, photophobic eye recurs (acute anterior uveitis).
  • 📏 Record baseline BASDAI, BASMI (Schober, chest expansion, occiput-to-wall), and a smoking-cessation conversation (smoking worsens outcomes).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Do not wait for the scan to start treatment. Physiotherapy plus a full-dose NSAID begins on the day you suspect axial SpA — the MRI confirms, it does not gate first-line care."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing & Physiotherapy Care

🛏️ The multidisciplinary backbone — nurse-led and physio-led care

  • 🏃 Daily exercise is the cornerstone: the physiotherapist builds a programme of spinal extension, mobility, deep breathing and posture — as important as any drug in preserving function.
  • 📏 Serial metrology by the specialist nurse: Schober, chest expansion, occiput-to-wall and BASDAI at each visit track progression and treatment response.
  • 💉 Biologic education: if escalated, teach self-injection technique, infection red flags, and to hold the drug and seek review during febrile illness.
  • 👁️ Uveitis safety-net: teach the patient that a red, painful, light-sensitive eye needs same-day ophthalmology — sight-threatening if missed.
  • 🚭 Smoking cessation & bone health: smoking accelerates radiographic progression; counsel on vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise and fracture awareness.
  • 🧠 Psychological support: a lifelong diagnosis in a young person — address mood, work adjustments, and fatigue, which drives much of the disease burden.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In axial SpA the physiotherapist is a disease-modifying prescriber. A patient who stops moving stiffens and fuses faster than one who trains every day."

🦴 Imaging / Serology + Visual Enhancements

🧲 Sacroiliac joints — read the MRI before you trust the X-ray

The plain X-ray is silent early. The MRI STIR shows bright bone marrow oedema at both sacroiliac joints — active sacroiliitis. Years later, syndesmophytes bridge the vertebrae into a fused "bamboo spine." The diagnosis lived on the MRI, not the radiograph.

🔎 The imaging progression you must know cold

StageModality / look forMeaning
🌊 Active sacroiliitisMRI STIR/T2: subchondral bone marrow oedema at the SI jointsEarliest sign — present before ANY X-ray change. Makes early diagnosis possible.
🕳️ Structural SI changeX-ray / MRI T1: erosions, sclerosis, joint-space widening then narrowingGraded sacroiliitis; bilateral grade ≥ 2 or unilateral grade ≥ 3 = radiographic AS.
✨ Romanus lesions / shiny cornersVertebral corner inflammation → sclerotic "shiny corner"; squaring of vertebraeEnthesitis at the vertebral corners — early spinal disease.
🎋 SyndesmophytesVertical bridging ossification between adjacent vertebral bodiesNew bone formation; distinguishes from the chunky, non-marginal osteophytes of degeneration.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"On MRI, bone marrow oedema at the sacroiliac joints is active spondyloarthritis you can still treat; on X-ray, syndesmophytes and a bamboo spine are the damage you failed to prevent. Image early, treat the oedema."

🩻 Adjunct imaging

  • Whole-spine imaging (CT): mandatory after ANY trauma in a known AS spine — fractures are often transverse, unstable, and easily missed on plain films (see S13/S21).
  • DEXA: AS causes paradoxical osteoporosis of the vertebral bodies despite the dense fused appearance — contributing to fracture risk.

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📊 Disease activity (BASDAI / ASDAS-CRP) at each visit — the treat-to-target metric that drives escalation decisions.
  • 📏 Metrology (BASMI): Schober, chest expansion, occiput-to-wall, lateral flexion — objective progression of spinal mobility.
  • 🩸 CRP/ESR trend for inflammation and treatment response.
  • 💊 Drug safety: NSAIDs — renal function, BP, GI symptoms; biologics — infection surveillance, LFTs, and vigilance for TB reactivation.
  • ❤️ Extra-articular surveillance: ask about eye symptoms every visit; periodic cardiovascular review (aortic root, conduction) and bone-density (DEXA) checks.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Treat axial SpA to a target: if BASDAI/ASDAS stays high on adequate NSAIDs, escalate — do not accept a young person living at pain 6/10 because 'the bloods are only mildly up.'"
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — the "A"s of AS and the extra-articular disease

  • 🦴 Spinal fusion & fixed kyphosis (Ankylosis): progressive loss of mobility, stooped posture, reduced chest expansion.
  • 💥 Spinal fracture of the rigid spine: the ankylosed spine behaves like a long bone — even minor trauma can cause an unstable, often cervical, transverse fracture with high risk of cord injury. Frequently missed on plain films. Image the WHOLE spine (CT) and immobilise.
  • 👁️ Acute Anterior uveitis: ~25–40% over a lifetime — a painful, red, photophobic eye needing same-day ophthalmology; recurrent, can threaten sight.
  • ❤️ Aortitis & conduction disease: aortic-root dilatation → aortic regurgitation, and AV block — the cardiac "A"s.
  • 🫁 Apical pulmonary fibrosis (upper-lobe) — a classic late association; can cavitate and become colonised.
  • 🩹 Amyloidosis (AA) from chronic inflammation → renal impairment; IgA nephropathy also associated.
  • 🦵 Cauda equina syndrome (late, from dural ectasia) and osteoporotic fractures despite the dense-looking spine.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A known AS patient with new neck or back pain after ANY fall has a spinal fracture until a whole-spine CT says otherwise. Never send the rigid spine home with a diagnosis of 'strain.'"

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🦴 Diagnosis: axial spondyloarthritis — inflammatory back pain, HLA-B27 positive, raised CRP, MRI bone marrow oedema at both SI joints (non-radiographic to early radiographic).

💎 Clinical Pearls

🌅 Inflammatory vs mechanicalYoung, insidious, night pain, AM stiffness > 30 min, better with exercise = inflammatory. The highest-yield distinction in back pain.
🧲 MRI beats X-ray earlyBone marrow oedema at the sacroiliac joints appears years before any X-ray change — a normal film never excludes axial SpA.
🧬 B27 supports, not diagnoses~8% of healthy people are HLA-B27 positive; use it to raise probability, not to rule in or out.
🎯 Enthesitis is the targetSpA inflames the enthesis and then BUILDS bone (syndesmophytes → bamboo spine) — unlike RA which erodes synovium.
👁️ Mind the extra-articular A'sAnterior uveitis, Aortitis/AV block, Apical fibrosis, Amyloid, IBD, psoriasis — screen every visit.
🏃 Physio + NSAID firstExercise and full-dose NSAIDs for everyone; biologic (anti-TNF/anti-IL-17) only if activity stays high.
💊 DMARDs skip the spineMethotrexate/sulfasalazine help peripheral joints only — never rely on them for axial disease.
💥 The rigid spine snapsMinor trauma to a fused AS spine can cause an unstable cervical fracture — image the WHOLE spine and immobilise.

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🧬 HLA-B27 mechanisms: the arthritogenic-peptide hypothesis, heavy-chain misfolding with an unfolded-protein/ER-stress response, and cell-surface B27 homodimers engaging KIR/LILR receptors — plus non-MHC risk genes (ERAP1, IL23R) that converge on the IL-23/IL-17 axis.
  • 🔬 The IL-23/IL-17 axis & the gut–joint link: subclinical gut inflammation is common in axial SpA; IL-23-responsive cells at entheses drive IL-17-mediated inflammation and new bone formation — the biological rationale for anti-IL-17 therapy and its caution in IBD.
  • 📏 Classification vs diagnosis: ASAS criteria are for classification/research, not a diagnostic checklist; over-reading MRI oedema (which occurs in athletes, postpartum women, and mechanical stress) risks over-diagnosis — read imaging in clinical context.
  • 🦴 Radiographic vs non-radiographic axial SpA: a spectrum of one disease; not all non-radiographic patients progress to ankylosis, and progression is influenced by CRP, smoking, and B27 status.
  • 💉 Does treatment slow ankylosis? Evidence that early, sustained inflammation control (anti-TNF, anti-IL-17) may retard radiographic progression is growing — reinforcing early diagnosis and treat-to-target.
  • 💥 Spinal fracture biomechanics: the ankylosed spine acts as a long lever arm with osteoporotic vertebrae; fractures are typically transverse through fused segments, unstable in three columns, prone to epidural haematoma and delayed cord injury — hence whole-spine CT/MRI and early surgical involvement.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Strain That Broke a Neck"

The case: A 47-year-old man with long-standing ankylosing spondylitis — diagnosed only in his 40s after years of "mechanical" back pain — slipped on wet stairs and jarred his neck. He walked into the ED complaining of new neck pain. He was fully alert with only "mild" tenderness. Plain cervical X-rays were read as "no fracture, degenerative fusion" and he was discharged with a diagnosis of soft-tissue strain and paracetamol. No one recognised that a rigid, ankylosed spine fractures like a long bone from minor trauma, that such fractures are transverse and easily hidden within fused segments on plain film, and that a whole-spine CT was mandatory. Overnight the unstable fracture displaced; he developed a dense tetraparesis and, despite emergency surgery, never regained function.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"Minor fall, walking, mild pain" framed as trivial — the rigid AS spine was not respected.
🖥️ WRONG IMAGINGPlain X-ray only; the transverse fracture through fused bone was missed. Whole-spine CT never done.
🧷 NOT IMMOBILISEDAllowed to move freely and forced flat; the neck should have been splinted in its own fixed posture.
📉 OSTEOPOROSIS IGNOREDThe dense fused spine was assumed strong — in fact it is brittle and osteoporotic.
🩺 DELAY UPSTREAMYears of "mechanical" mislabelling let the spine fuse in the first place — the original missed diagnosis set the trap.

🛡️ What would have saved him: treat ANY new spinal pain after ANY trauma in a known AS spine as an unstable fracture; image the WHOLE spine with CT (not plain films) and add MRI for cord/epidural haematoma; immobilise in the patient's own fixed position and never force the neck straight; involve spinal surgery early; and — upstream — recognise inflammatory back pain in youth so the spine never fuses at all. Both failures in this conference are the same lesson from opposite ends of the disease: do not dismiss the AS spine, early or late.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Mechanical back pain' has delayed more spondyloarthritis diagnoses, and 'soft-tissue strain' has paralysed more fused spines, than almost any other pair of throwaway labels. Respect the AS spine at both ends of its life."
