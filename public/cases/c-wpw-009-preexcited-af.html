🧬 Why THIS patient? — an extra wire and a chaotic atrium

In Wolff-Parkinson-White there is an extra muscular connection — the bundle of Kent — that bridges atrium directly to ventricle, bypassing the AV node. The AV node is normally the heart's only gatekeeper between atria and ventricles, and it conducts with decremental (rate-limiting) physiology — the faster the atria fire, the more it blocks, protecting the ventricle. The accessory pathway has no such brake: it conducts fast and 1-to-1. For years this only caused re-entry tachycardias. Tonight he developed atrial fibrillation, where the atria fire 400–600 times a minute. Most of those impulses pour straight down the unguarded accessory pathway to the ventricle, producing rates over 250–300, a chaotic broad-complex rhythm, and a real risk of degeneration into ventricular fibrillation and sudden death.

🔥 Risk factors / amplifiers for dangerous pre-excited AF 🛣️ A high-conduction accessory pathway (short refractory period) · ⚡ Onset of atrial fibrillation in a WPW heart · 🍺 Alcohol / catecholamine surge triggering AF · 💊 Any drug that blocks the AV node (shifts conduction to the pathway) · 🏃 Young, athletic, structurally normal heart that "looks too well" · 🧬 Multiple accessory pathways

📖 Teaching Points

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the accessory pathway makes AF lethal (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🛣️ The extra wire: the bundle of Kent is a strand of working myocardium that crosses the AV groove, directly connecting atrium to ventricle and bypassing the AV node. On the resting ECG it produces a short PR (no nodal delay) and a delta wave (the ventricle starts depolarising early and slowly from muscle, not the fast His-Purkinje system).
  2. 🚦 The missing brake: the AV node conducts decrementally — the faster the atria fire, the more it blocks. This is a protective rate-limiter. The accessory pathway has no decremental property: it conducts fast and one-to-one, however fast the atrium drives it.
  3. 🌀 Add atrial fibrillation: in AF the atria fire 400–600 times a minute. The AV node would filter most of these out. The accessory pathway does not — it can conduct a large fraction straight through, giving ventricular rates of 250–300/min.
  4. 📐 Why the QRS varies beat-to-beat: each ventricular beat is a fusion in changing proportions of conduction down the pathway (wide, delta) and down the AV node (narrow). The result is the diagnostic irregular rhythm with constantly changing QRS width and morphology.
  5. 💥 Why it kills: at >250 there is almost no diastole, output collapses, and — critically — the very short coupling intervals can fall on the ventricle's vulnerable period and trigger ventricular fibrillation. Pre-excited AF is one of the few mechanisms by which a structurally normal young heart suffers sudden cardiac death.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Procainamide or shock — never adenosine, verapamil, beta-blocker or digoxin. The AV node is the seatbelt; do not unbuckle it at 250 km/h."

⚖️ Contrast pair #1 — Pre-excited AF vs monomorphic VT (the lethal mimic)

⚡ Pre-excited AF IS… (tonight)

  • Irregularly irregular
  • Varying QRS width & morphology beat-to-beat
  • Often the fastest rhythm you'll see (>250–300)
  • Young patient, delta-wave history, structurally normal heart
  • Treat: procainamide/ibutilide or cardiovert; ablate

🫀 Monomorphic VT is NOT that…

  • Regular
  • Uniform QRS — every complex identical
  • Usually slower (130–200)
  • Older patient, prior MI / scar / low EF
  • Treat: amiodarone/procainamide or cardiovert; ICD

😈 The trap: both are broad-complex tachycardias, and both unstable patients get the same first move (synchronized cardioversion). But for the stable patient, mislabelling pre-excited AF as 'SVT' invites a lethal AV-nodal blocker. The irregularity and varying QRS width are what separate them — and what keep your hands off the AV node.

⚖️ Contrast pair #2 — WPW pattern vs WPW syndrome

📏 WPW pattern (asymptomatic)

  • Delta wave + short PR on ECG, no arrhythmia
  • Often an incidental finding
  • Most never have an event; risk-stratify (especially athletes / high-risk jobs)
  • Management debated — consider EP study for SPERRI

🌀 WPW syndrome (this patient)

  • Delta wave PLUS a documented arrhythmia (AVRT or pre-excited AF)
  • Symptomatic — palpitations, syncope, or arrest
  • Mandates definitive therapy: catheter ablation
  • Pre-excited AF is the high-risk presentation

💡 Why it matters: a delta wave alone is a pattern; the same delta wave plus the arrhythmia he had tonight is the syndrome — and the syndrome with pre-excited AF is curable, dangerous, and a Class I reason to ablate.

🔬 Fellowship Corner — Advanced Topics

  • 🗺️ Accessory-pathway localisation: delta-wave polarity algorithms (Arruda, etc.) predict pathway location (left-lateral, posteroseptal, right free wall); manifest vs concealed pathways; multiple pathways increase pre-excited AF risk.
  • 🧪 Risk stratification of asymptomatic pre-excitation: EP study measuring SPERRI and anterograde pathway effective refractory period; inducible AVRT or pre-excited AF; loss of pre-excitation at higher rates / on exercise / with procainamide suggests a lower-risk pathway.
  • 💉 Why IV amiodarone is risky here: it can paradoxically accelerate accessory-pathway conduction and cause hypotension — it is not a safe default in pre-excited AF despite being a class III "antiarrhythmic for everything."
  • 🛣️ Catheter ablation technique: map the earliest ventricular/atrial activation along the AV groove during pre-excitation or pacing; radiofrequency at the pathway insertion; ~95% acute success, with retrograde/transseptal access for left-sided pathways.
  • 🫀 Special pathways: Mahaim (atriofascicular) fibres produce LBBB-pattern antidromic tachycardia with decremental conduction; PJRT (permanent junctional reciprocating tachycardia) from a slow concealed posteroseptal pathway.
  • ⚽ WPW in athletes: competitive sport amplifies catecholamine-driven AF risk; guidelines favour risk stratification ± ablation before clearance in symptomatic or high-risk pre-excitation.

🫀 Post-conversion 12-lead & echo

  • 📈 Post-shock 12-lead: sinus rhythm ~70/min with a short PR (~100 ms) and a delta wave — the manifest accessory pathway revealed. Delta-wave polarity helps localise the pathway for ablation.
  • 🩻 Echo: structurally normal heart, normal LV function, no scar — confirming the danger is purely electrical.
  • 🔬 EP study (this admission): measures the accessory pathway's anterograde refractory period and the SPERRI, induces and maps the pathway, then delivers curative radiofrequency ablation.
⚠️ The ECG's favorite lie in a fast broad rhythm"It's fast and the QRS is wide — it must be VT, give amiodarone." But an irregular broad tachycardia with varying QRS width in a young patient is pre-excited AF, where IV amiodarone is itself unsafe and AV-nodal blockers are lethal. Let the irregularity and the changing QRS — not just the width — guide you.

💉 Acute drugs — what, why, and the traps

🚫 The 'never' list in pre-excited AF❌ Adenosine · ❌ Verapamil / diltiazem · ❌ Beta-blockers · ❌ Digoxin · ❌ IV amiodarone (avoid). Each blocks/bypasses the AV node and can accelerate accessory-pathway conduction into VF. When the patient is unstable or you are unsure, the safest "drug" is synchronized electricity; when stable, procainamide or ibutilide.

🏠 Definitive & long-term therapy (this admission, not just tonight)

  • 🛣️ Catheter ablation of the accessory pathway — curative in ~95%, abolishes the sudden-death risk. This is the headline treatment, now a Class I indication after pre-excited AF.
  • 🔬 EP study & risk stratification — measure SPERRI / anterograde pathway ERP, induce and map, then ablate in the same session.
  • 💊 Bridging antiarrhythmics (e.g. flecainide/propafenone in a structurally normal heart, or sotalol) only if ablation is delayed — but ablation is the goal.
  • 🍺 Address AF triggers — alcohol, thyroid status, stimulants — while arranging definitive therapy.

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 📈 Continuous telemetry with the defibrillator at the bedside until definitively treated; print strips of any AF recurrence or runs of pre-excitation.
  • 🩸 Invasive arterial line for beat-to-beat BP through cardioversion and any procainamide infusion.
  • 📐 QRS width and QT on every 12-lead — procainamide/ibutilide widen QRS and prolong QT; stop procainamide if QRS widens > 50% or QT prolongs excessively.
  • 🌀 Watch for AF recurrence and for any acceleration of rate — a climbing or chaotic rate signals impending VF; have the defibrillator ready in UNSYNC.
  • 📈 Confirm the delta wave on the post-conversion ECG and document it for the EP team.
  • 💤 Sedation/airway readiness kept at bedside in case of recurrence requiring repeat cardioversion.
  • 🔬 Pre-procedure monitoring en route to the EP lab for ablation — the destination of this admission.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"After you convert pre-excited AF, the monitor's job changes from 'is he in AF?' to 'is the delta wave there, and is anyone about to give him an AV-nodal blocker?' — guard both."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 What can go wrong — and the mechanism

  • ⚡ Degeneration to VF / sudden cardiac death — the great fear; extreme rates down an unguarded pathway fall on the vulnerable period. Treatment pivots to UNSYNC defibrillation + CPR.
  • ☠️ Iatrogenic acceleration from an AV-nodal blocker — adenosine, verapamil, beta-blocker, digoxin or IV amiodarone shunting conduction to the pathway → faster ventricular rate → VF. The classic lethal error.
  • 🧠 Cardiogenic shock / cerebral hypoperfusion — prolonged extreme-rate low-output state; the syncope was the warning.
  • 🩸 Post-cardioversion complications — skin burns, transient bradyarrhythmia, and (because we are cardioverting AF) a thromboembolic risk — though in an unstable emergency the shock cannot wait.
  • 💊 Pro-arrhythmia from therapy — procainamide/ibutilide can prolong QT and provoke Torsades; titrate and monitor.
  • 🔁 Recurrence — until the pathway is ablated, another bout of AF can reproduce the whole emergency. Definitive treatment is not optional.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference — "The Young Man They Called SVT"

The case: A 24-year-old man arrived to a busy ED with palpitations and a fast, broad, irregular tachycardia at 270, BP 90/60, alert and talking. The tired night team called it "fast SVT — he's young and fine." They gave IV verapamil (and, when that didn't work, a slug of adenosine). Within two minutes his ventricular rate accelerated, the rhythm degenerated into VF, and a long resuscitation followed. He did not survive. His clinic ECG from a year before, projected at the M&M, showed an unmistakable short PR and delta wave — WPW that nobody had checked.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 ANCHORING"He's young, it's just SVT" — youth does not make a broad irregular tachycardia benign.
📈 ECG NOT READThe irregularity and varying QRS width (and the old delta wave) were there and unread.
🌀 PATTERN MISSEDFast + broad + IRREGULAR = pre-excited AF, not simple SVT.
💊 LETHAL DRUGVerapamil/adenosine → AV-nodal block → faster pathway conduction → VF.
🌙 SYSTEMNight fatigue, no senior/EP review, no "FBI = hands off the AV node" default.

🛡️ What would have saved him: recognise the triad — fast + broad + irregular = pre-excited AF; never give AV-nodal blockers (ABCD); if unstable, synchronized cardioversion (correct whatever the rhythm); if stable, procainamide/ibutilide; check the old ECG for a delta wave; get senior/EP eyes early; then ablate. This is exactly why the crew held the adenosine tonight.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"'Too young to be dangerous' is the sentence written on the chart of WPW patients who then die in pre-excited AF. Youth is not a diagnosis."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Discharge plan & safety net

  • 💊 Medications: after successful ablation, usually no long-term antiarrhythmic needed. If ablation were deferred, a structurally-normal-heart agent (flecainide/propafenone or sotalol) — and a written warning that AV-nodal blockers must be avoided until cured.

  • 🍺 Trigger plan: moderate alcohol, address any thyroid issue, and recognise that AF was the spark that lit the emergency.
  • ⚽ Sports clearance: return-to-play discussed with cardiology/EP after confirmed successful ablation and resolution of pre-excitation.
  • 🚩 Red flags → call emergency services: palpitations with dizziness/presyncope, syncope, fast irregular heartbeat, chest pain or breathlessness.
  • 📚 Education & family: what WPW is in plain language, why ablation is curative, the importance of family screening (a cousin already has "an electrical problem"), and CPR/AED awareness for his partner.

💎 Clinical Pearls

⚡ FBI = pre-excited AFFast + Broad + Irregular = pre-excited atrial fibrillation. Hands OFF the AV node.
🚫 ABCD don't applyAdenosine, Beta-blockers, Calcium-channel blockers, Digoxin — never in pre-excited AF; they can precipitate VF.
💉 Procainamide or shockStable → IV procainamide (or ibutilide). Unstable → synchronized cardioversion. Never an AV-nodal blocker.
🪢 The AV node is the seatbeltIt's the only rate-limiter to the ventricle. Blocking it in AF cuts the seatbelt at 250 km/h.
📐 Varying QRS = pre-excitationBeat-to-beat changing QRS width in an irregular fast rhythm is near-specific for pre-excited AF.
🔀 Irregular vs regularRegular + uniform broad → think VT. Irregular + varying width → think pre-excited AF.
📈 Find the delta waveShort PR + slurred upstroke on any old ECG confirms WPW and explains the whole night.
🧬 Pattern vs syndromeDelta wave alone = WPW pattern. Delta wave + arrhythmia = WPW syndrome → ablate.
🛣️ Ablation cures itAccessory-pathway ablation is curative (~95%) and abolishes the sudden-death risk.
🧮 SPERRI < 250 ms = dangerThe shortest pre-excited R–R interval risk-stratifies the pathway; short = high VF risk.
🩺 "Too young" is a trapA structurally normal young heart can suffer sudden death in pre-excited AF. Youth is not a diagnosis.
