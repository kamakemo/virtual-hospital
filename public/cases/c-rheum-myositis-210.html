Treat only the muscle and you may win the battle while the war (an undetected cancer) is lost.

🧭 The three bedside safety questions in myositis

(1) Swallow — is she aspirating? NBM + speech-and-language assessment if any doubt. (2) Breathing — is this ILD or failing respiratory muscles? Check sitting/lying vital capacity and oxygenation. (3) Cancer — dermatomyositis is paraneoplastic; the malignancy screen starts today, not "after the steroids work."

⚠️ The trap of "just weakness"It is easy to admit "proximal myopathy," start steroids, and feel finished. But the aspiration risk, the evolving ILD, and the hidden cancer are all live threats on day one. The bedside job is to protect the airway, assess the lungs, and open the cancer work-up simultaneously.

👨‍⚕️ Resident Review

The rheumatology registrar thinks out loud:

🧠 Frame it as "confirm the myositis AND hunt its threats"

"Clinically this is dermatomyositis — proximal weakness, heliotrope, Gottron's, CK > 6000. My job is threefold: confirm the myopathy (CK, EMG, MRI, myositis antibodies, and biopsy if needed), find the threats (swallow, ILD, and above all an occult malignancy), and treat with high-dose steroids plus a steroid-sparing agent. I will not discharge her without an age-appropriate cancer screen — that is not optional in adult dermatomyositis."

🎯 Her order of operations

  1. 🧪 Confirm: CK (and LDH, AST/ALT, aldolase), myositis-specific/associated antibody panel (anti-Jo-1 and other synthetases, anti-MDA5, anti-Mi-2, anti-TIF1-γ, anti-NXP2), EMG, muscle MRI ± biopsy.
  2. 🍽️ Protect the swallow: NBM pending speech-and-language assessment; aspiration precautions.
  3. 😮‍💨 Assess the lungs: HRCT chest + pulmonary function tests (especially with anti-Jo-1 / anti-MDA5 risk).
  4. 🎗️ Cancer screen: history/exam, bloods, CT chest-abdomen-pelvis, age-appropriate screening (mammography, cervical, colorectal), ± further imaging (TIF1-γ raises malignancy risk).
  5. 💊 Treat: high-dose corticosteroids + early steroid-sparing immunosuppression; IVIG if severe/dysphagic.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In adult dermatomyositis the cancer screen is part of the diagnosis, not an afterthought. Treat the muscle AND search for the tumour on the same admission."
🧪 Resident's self-testThe intern says: "Her CK is huge — should we assume statin myopathy and just stop a statin?"

No — she is not even on a statin. And crucially, the heliotrope rash and Gottron's papules are NOT features of statin myopathy — they define an inflammatory dermatomyositis. (Note there is a specific statin-associated immune-mediated necrotising myopathy with anti-HMGCR antibodies that persists after stopping the statin — but that is a distinct autoimmune entity, not simple statin intolerance.) The rash reframes everything.

🧠 Teaching Points + Interactive Simulation

🔬 The mechanism — WHY the muscle fails, WHY the rash, WHY the cancer link (the WHY behind the WHY)

  1. 🎯 Autoimmune attack on muscle: in dermatomyositis the primary target is the microvasculature — a complement-mediated (membrane-attack-complex) humoral attack on capillaries causes ischaemia and perifascicular atrophy. In polymyositis it is a T-cell (CD8) mediated attack directly on muscle fibres.
  2. 💪 Proximal pattern: the large proximal muscles (hip/shoulder girdle) are most metabolically demanding and are hit first → difficulty with stairs, standing, and lifting arms overhead.
  3. 🌸 The skin: the same vasculopathy/interferon-driven inflammation strikes sun-exposed skin → heliotrope rash, Gottron's papules, shawl/V signs. A strong type-I interferon signature underlies DM.
  4. 🎗️ The cancer link: tumour antigens (e.g. targets like TIF1-γ) may trigger an autoimmune cross-reaction against regenerating muscle/skin — the myositis can be a paraneoplastic immune response, which is why it can precede the cancer by months.
  5. 😮‍💨 The lung: the same autoimmunity attacks alveolar/interstitial tissue → interstitial lung disease, especially with anti-synthetase and anti-MDA5 antibodies.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Dermatomyositis is a complement attack on muscle capillaries with a skin and lung front — and sometimes a tumour is quietly directing the whole immune assault. Treat the muscle, protect the lung, find the cancer."

⚖️ Contrast pair — Dermatomyositis vs Inclusion body myositis

🌸 Dermatomyositis

  • Younger–middle age; F>M; subacute (weeks–months)
  • SYMMETRIC PROXIMAL weakness + rash
  • Very high CK; steroid-responsive
  • Paraneoplastic — screen for cancer

🧓 Inclusion body myositis (IBM)

  • Older (>50), M>F; insidious over years
  • ASYMMETRIC, DISTAL too — finger flexors & quadriceps
  • CK normal–mildly raised; POOR steroid response
  • Rimmed vacuoles on biopsy; a key mimic to not miss

💡 If "myositis" is not responding to steroids and the weakness is asymmetric with finger-flexor/quadriceps wasting, rethink — that is inclusion body myositis, and steroids will not help it.

📋 Orders

🖊️ The order set (admission)

  • 🍽️ NBM pending speech-and-language swallow assessment; aspiration precautions; sit upright for any oral intake.
  • 🧪 Bloods: CK, LDH, AST/ALT, aldolase, U&E (rhabdo/renal), FBC, CRP/ESR, TSH (hypothyroid mimic), calcium.
  • 🧬 Myositis antibody panel — anti-Jo-1/synthetases, anti-MDA5, anti-Mi-2, anti-TIF1-γ, anti-NXP2, anti-HMGCR/SRP; plus ANA/ENA.
  • 😮‍💨 HRCT chest + pulmonary function tests (FVC, TLCO) to screen for ILD; ABG if hypoxic.
  • 🎗️ Malignancy screen: CT chest-abdomen-pelvis; age-appropriate mammography, cervical screening, colorectal screening; consider further imaging if TIF1-γ/NXP2 positive.
  • ⚡ EMG + muscle MRI (identify an oedematous muscle to biopsy); muscle biopsy if diagnosis uncertain.
  • 💊 Start high-dose corticosteroid once samples are secured; plan early steroid-sparing agent; IVIG if severe/dysphagic.
  • 🦴 Bone protection, PJP prophylaxis and glucose monitoring alongside high-dose steroids.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Same admission, three parallel tracks: SECURE the diagnosis (CK/antibodies/EMG/MRI/biopsy), PROTECT the patient (swallow + lungs), and SCREEN for cancer. None waits for the others."
S8

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Care

🛏️ Ward priorities — the nurse guards the airway and the lungs

  • 🍽️ Enforce NBM until the swallow is cleared; sit fully upright to eat; report any wet voice, coughing or choking — silent aspiration is the quiet killer here.
  • 😮‍💨 Monitor respiratory function — RR, SpO₂, and (where ordered) serial vital capacity; a falling VC signals respiratory-muscle failure needing escalation.
  • 💪 Falls prevention — proximal weakness makes standing and transfers unsafe; use aids and assist.
  • 🌸 Skin care — protect fragile, photosensitive DM skin; watch for ulceration (a warning sign in anti-MDA5 disease).
  • 💊 Steroid safety — glucose monitoring, gastric/bone protection, infection vigilance (immunosuppressed + high-dose steroids).
  • 🗣️ Support through the cancer work-up — the parallel malignancy screen is frightening; clear, compassionate communication keeps the patient engaged.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"In myositis the nurse who keeps the patient NBM until the swallow is cleared, and who watches the vital capacity, prevents the two silent deaths — aspiration and respiratory-muscle failure."

S12

📟 Monitoring

📡 What to watch and why

  • 💪 Muscle power + CK trend — improving power with a falling CK signals response; a divergence (weaker but low CK) suggests steroid myopathy rather than disease flare.
  • 😮‍💨 Respiratory function — SpO₂, RR, serial vital capacity and TLCO; rapidly progressive ILD (esp. anti-MDA5) can decline over days — treat as an emergency.
  • 🍽️ Swallow — re-assess before upgrading diet; aspiration pneumonia is a common, preventable killer.
  • 🎗️ Cancer surveillance — complete the initial screen and continue surveillance for ~3 years (risk highest around diagnosis); a new symptom = re-screen.
  • 💊 Immunosuppression safety — glucose, infection, blood counts, liver function; PJP prophylaxis on high-dose steroids.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Track power AND CK together, watch the vital capacity like a hawk, and never close the cancer file too early — the tumour can announce itself months after the muscle improves."
S13

⚠️ Complications

💀 The threats — muscle, swallow, lung, and the hidden tumour

🚨 The two that kill early(1) Aspiration & respiratory-muscle failure — pharyngeal and diaphragmatic weakness cause aspiration pneumonia and hypercapnic failure. (2) Rapidly progressive ILD — especially anti-MDA5 dermatomyositis, which can be fatal within weeks and needs early aggressive combined immunosuppression.
  • 🎗️ Underlying malignancy — the association that must never be missed; adult dermatomyositis is paraneoplastic (ovarian, lung, GI, nasopharyngeal, breast).
  • 😮‍💨 Interstitial lung disease — the commonest serious organ complication; drives long-term morbidity and mortality.
  • 🍽️ Dysphagia / aspiration pneumonia — from bulbar and upper-oesophageal muscle involvement.
  • 🦴 Calcinosis cutis — subcutaneous calcification (more in juvenile DM, anti-NXP2), painful and disabling.
  • 💊 Treatment harms — steroid myopathy, infection, osteoporosis, hyperglycaemia; a fine balance against disease control.
  • 🫀 Cardiac involvement — myocarditis/conduction disease (uncommon but serious).
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"The muscle weakness rarely kills; the SWALLOW, the LUNG and the CANCER do. Guard all three from day one and you have understood inflammatory myopathy."

🗺️ Plan + Procedure Guides with Checklists

🧭 The management plan

  1. 🔒 Secure the diagnosis: CK/enzymes, myositis antibodies, EMG, muscle MRI, and biopsy if uncertain.
  2. 🛡️ Protect the patient: NBM + swallow assessment; HRCT + PFTs for ILD; escalate rapidly progressive ILD.
  3. 🎗️ Screen for malignancy: CT chest-abdomen-pelvis + age-appropriate screening; intensify given anti-TIF1-γ; continue surveillance ~3 years.
  4. 💊 Treat: high-dose corticosteroids + early steroid-sparing agent (mycophenolate/calcineurin inhibitor if ILD); IVIG if severe/dysphagic; rituximab if refractory.
  5. 🏥 Multidisciplinary: rheumatology + respiratory + SLT + physiotherapy + oncology as indicated; bone/PJP/glucose protection.
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"Treat the muscle, guard the lung and the swallow, and — non-negotiably — complete an age-appropriate cancer screen. In adult dermatomyositis, the diagnosis is not finished until the tumour is found or excluded."

🏠 Discharge

📋 Two futures — the fully worked-up patient vs the muscle-only miss

🌸 This patient (worked up fully)

  • Steroid taper + steroid-sparing agent; skin, swallow and ILD monitored
  • Cancer found early and treated; 3-year surveillance plan
  • Red flags: worsening breathlessness, choking/coughing on food, new lumps/bleeding/weight loss → urgent review
  • Rheumatology + respiratory + oncology + physiotherapy follow-up

⚠️ The avoidable disaster

  • Muscle treated, cancer screen skipped → tumour presents late and inoperable
  • Diet upgraded before swallow cleared → aspiration pneumonia
  • Methotrexate given despite ILD → pneumonitis
  • New weakness on steroid taper misread as flare (actually steroid myopathy)
⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A safe dermatomyositis discharge has a controlled muscle, a protected swallow, a monitored lung, a completed cancer screen, and a surveillance plan — never a muscle treated in isolation."

🔬 Advanced Topics — Fellowship Level

🧠 Beyond the pathway

  • 🧬 Antibody-defined disease: myositis-specific antibodies now define discrete clinical entities better than the old PM/DM histological split — anti-synthetase syndrome, MDA5-DM, TIF1-γ cancer-associated DM, immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (anti-HMGCR/SRP).
  • 🫁 Anti-MDA5 rapidly progressive ILD: a respiratory emergency with high mortality; emerging aggressive strategies include early triple therapy (steroids + calcineurin inhibitor + cyclophosphamide) and JAK inhibitors (e.g. tofacitinib), reflecting the interferon-driven biology.
  • 🎗️ The malignancy window: risk is highest in the year around diagnosis; anti-TIF1-γ and anti-NXP2 mark cancer-associated DM; PET-CT is increasingly used when standard screening is negative but suspicion remains.
  • 🔥 Type-I interferon signature: DM is strongly interferon-driven, rationalising JAK-inhibitor therapy and explaining the vasculopathic skin and muscle changes.
  • 🧪 Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM): very high CK with sparse inflammation on biopsy; anti-HMGCR (statin-triggered but statin-independent thereafter) and anti-SRP; often needs IVIG early.
  • 💉 Immune-checkpoint-inhibitor myositis: a modern iatrogenic mimic — can overlap with myocarditis and myasthenia (a dangerous triad) in patients on cancer immunotherapy.

🎭 Morbidity & Mortality Conference

🎭 M&M — "The Rash We Treated But Never Questioned"

The case: A 58-year-old woman presented with classic dermatomyositis — proximal weakness, a heliotrope rash, Gottron's papules, and a CK of 4800. She was started on high-dose steroids and methotrexate, and her strength and CK improved beautifully; everyone was pleased. But no age-appropriate cancer screen was ever performed — no CT body, no mammography, and the myositis antibody panel (which returned anti-TIF1-γ positive) was filed unread. Six months later she presented with abdominal distension and ascites: advanced ovarian carcinoma, now inoperable. The paraneoplastic rash had been her body's early warning, and it went unanswered.

🧀 The Swiss-cheese holes that lined up:

🧠 SATISFACTION OF SEARCHThe muscle improved, so the work-up felt "done" — the cancer question was never asked.
🎗️ NO CANCER SCREENAge-appropriate screening + CT body were simply never ordered in an adult with DM.
📄 ANTIBODY IGNOREDAnti-TIF1-γ positive — a strong cancer marker — returned but was not acted on.
⏳ NO SURVEILLANCEThe 3-year highest-risk window was not built into follow-up.
🗣️ NO SAFETY-NETThe patient was never told which new symptoms should prompt urgent review.

🛡️ What would have saved her: treat every adult dermatomyositis as paraneoplastic until proven otherwise; complete an age-appropriate cancer screen + CT chest-abdomen-pelvis at diagnosis; ACT on the myositis antibodies (TIF1-γ/NXP2 → hunt harder, consider PET-CT); and set a surveillance plan for ~3 years with a clear safety-net. This is exactly the discipline that saved the patient in Bed 12.

⚔️ ONE-LINER RULE"A treated muscle is not a finished job. In adult dermatomyositis the rash is a paraneoplastic flag — answer it with a cancer screen, or the tumour you did not look for will find the patient later."
