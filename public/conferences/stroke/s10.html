🎯 The case on the table

A 45-year-old describes "the worst headache of my life," maximal the instant it began, with a stiff neck and photophobia. The CT six hours later looks normal — and that is exactly the trap. Subarachnoid haemorrhage is diagnosed by taking the thunderclap seriously and not stopping at a negative early scan.

1 · The headache that must never be dismissed

A thunderclap headache — maximal at onset, "like being hit on the head" — is a subarachnoid haemorrhage until definitively excluded, no matter how well the patient looks now.

Most SAH is from a ruptured saccular (berry) aneurysm; other causes include perimesencephalic (benign) bleeds and AVMs. Presentation: sudden severe headache ± transient loss of consciousness, vomiting, neck stiffness, photophobia, and sometimes a sentinel "warning" bleed days before.

2 · Diagnosis — CT then LP

  • Non-contrast CT is highly sensitive in the first 6 hours, but sensitivity falls with time as blood disperses.
  • If CT is negative but suspicion persists, do a lumbar puncture at ≥12 hours looking for xanthochromia (bilirubin from breakdown of red cells — distinguishes true SAH from a traumatic tap).
  • Once SAH is confirmed, CT angiography (or catheter angiography) locates the aneurysm for treatment.

4 · Securing the aneurysm — stop the rebleed

💡 Rebleeding is the early killer

An unsecured aneurysm can rebleed catastrophically within hours to days. Secure it early by endovascular coiling or surgical clipping; the ISAT trial favoured coiling for suitable aneurysms (better outcomes), though clipping is chosen by anatomy and multidisciplinary decision. Keep BP controlled and avoid straining until secured.

☠️ M&M — the negative CT that wasn't the end

A patient with a thunderclap headache had a normal CT at eight hours and was discharged with "migraine"; no LP was done. Five days later the aneurysm rebled and she died. Lesson: a negative CT beyond the first few hours does not exclude SAH — a thunderclap headache needs a CT and, if negative and still suspected, an LP for xanthochromia. Miss the sentinel bleed and you miss the only chance to secure the aneurysm before the fatal one.

6 · Take-home

  • Thunderclap headache = SAH until excluded — CT, then LP at ≥12 h for xanthochromia if CT negative.
  • Secure the aneurysm early (coiling favoured by ISAT) to prevent rebleeding.
  • Nimodipine and euvolaemia for delayed cerebral ischaemia/vasospasm (day 4–14).
  • Watch for hydrocephalus and hyponatraemia.
