🎯 The case on the table

CT shows no blood, ASPECTS 9, onset 70 minutes ago, NIHSS 12. The drug is drawn up. But before it runs you must clear a checklist that, if skipped, turns a clot-buster into a brain-bleeder. Thrombolysis is the highest-benefit, highest-stakes decision in acute medicine.

1 · Mechanism and the drugs

Thrombolytics convert plasminogen to plasmin, which digests the fibrin clot and restores flow.

  • Alteplase (tPA) — 0.9 mg/kg (max 90 mg), 10% as a bolus then the rest over 60 minutes. The long-standing standard.
  • Tenecteplase — a single weight-based bolus (0.25 mg/kg), more fibrin-specific and easier to give; non-inferior/superior in several trials and increasingly preferred, especially before thrombectomy (EXTEND-IA TNK).

💡 One-liner

Benefit is time-critical — the earlier, the greater. "Door-to-needle" under 60 minutes (ideally under 30) is the quality metric that saves the most brain.

3 · Eligibility & the contraindication checklist

Broadly: a disabling deficit, onset (or last-known-well) within the window, and no bleeding risk that outweighs benefit. Absolute contraindications include:

  • Any intracranial haemorrhage on CT; prior ICH; recent ischaemic stroke or head trauma (<3 months).
  • Active bleeding, known bleeding diathesis, platelets <100, INR >1.7 / therapeutic anticoagulation / a DOAC within 48 h.
  • BP >185/110 that cannot be lowered.
  • Recent major surgery, arterial puncture at a non-compressible site, GI/GU bleed, or a large established infarct (very low ASPECTS).

4 · Blood pressure — the pre-lysis gate

Do not give thrombolysis until BP is <185/110 — and keep it <180/105 for 24 hours afterwards.

Lower gently with IV labetalol or a GTN/nicardipine infusion. If BP will not come down safely, the patient is not a candidate.

5 · Complications you must anticipate

  • Symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage (~2–6%) — sudden deterioration, headache, rising BP, falling GCS: stop the infusion, urgent CT, reverse with cryoprecipitate/fibrinogen ± tranexamic acid, neurosurgical/haematology input.
  • Orolingual angioedema — tongue/lip swelling, commoner in patients on ACE inhibitors; protect the airway, stop the drug, give antihistamine/steroid/adrenaline as needed.
☠️ M&M — the overlooked contraindication

A patient was thrombolysed for a dense hemiparesis; the team did not chase that he had been started on a DOAC two days earlier for new AF. He developed a devastating intracerebral haemorrhage. Lesson: the contraindication checklist is not bureaucracy — recent anticoagulation, an unnoticed low platelet count, or an uncontrolled BP are exactly the boxes that, unchecked, convert benefit into catastrophe. Slow down for the checklist even as the clock runs.

6 · Take-home

  • Alteplase 0.9 mg/kg or tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg bolus; TNK is easier and favoured before thrombectomy.
  • Window <4.5 h, extended by imaging selection for wake-up/late strokes.
  • BP <185/110 before, <180/105 after; run the contraindication checklist every time.
  • Know how to manage sICH and angioedema before you push the drug.
