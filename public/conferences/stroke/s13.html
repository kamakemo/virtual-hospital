🎯 The case on the table

Your patient survived the stroke. Now comes the quieter, longer battle: making sure it never happens again. The right antithrombotic — and the right timing — can halve recurrence; the wrong one causes a fatal bleed or leaves an untreated clot source. This is where the mechanism you found in Session 12 becomes a prescription.

1 · Non-cardioembolic stroke — antiplatelets

  • Long-term: a single antiplatelet — clopidogrel (or aspirin ± dipyridamole).
  • Short-term DAPT: after a high-risk TIA or minor stroke, aspirin + clopidogrel for 21 days (CHANCE/POINT), or ticagrelor + aspirin in selected patients (THALES), then a single agent.
  • Do not continue long-term dual antiplatelet therapy — MATCH/SPS3 showed it adds bleeding without benefit.

2 · Cardioembolic (AF) — anticoagulation, and the timing question

For AF-related stroke, a DOAC is preferred over warfarin — the harder question is when to start it after an acute infarct.
💡 The "1–3–6–12 day" rule

Start anticoagulation later after larger strokes (higher haemorrhagic-transformation risk): roughly day 1 for a TIA, 3 for a small stroke, 6 for a moderate stroke, and 12 for a large stroke. The ELAN trial supports earlier initiation being reasonably safe in many patients, but the size-of-infarct principle still guides the individual decision.

4 · Intracranial stenosis

For symptomatic intracranial atherosclerosis, aggressive medical therapy beats stenting (SAMMPRIS: dual antiplatelet for 90 days plus intensive risk-factor control outperformed stenting, which had high periprocedural complications).

☠️ M&M — the AF stroke that was never anticoagulated

A patient with an AF-related stroke was discharged on aspirin because "the bleeding risk seemed high," and anticoagulation was deferred and then forgotten. He returned with a second, fatal cardioembolic stroke. Lesson: in AF, the recurrent-stroke risk usually far outweighs the bleeding risk, and aspirin is not an adequate substitute for anticoagulation. Set a clear start date using the infarct-size rule and make sure it actually happens.

5 · Take-home

  • Non-cardioembolic: single antiplatelet long-term; 21-day DAPT after high-risk TIA/minor stroke, then mono.
  • AF: DOAC, timed by infarct size (1–3–6–12 day rule; ELAN supports earlier).
  • Symptomatic carotid 50–99%: endarterectomy within 2 weeks.
  • Intracranial stenosis: medical therapy > stenting (SAMMPRIS).
