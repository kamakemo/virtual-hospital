🎯 The case on the table

Your patient's acute stroke is treated and stable — but unless you find why it happened, you are just waiting for the next one. Two strokes with identical scans can have completely different causes and therefore completely different prevention. Aetiology is the hinge between the acute phase and a lifetime of protection.

1 · Why aetiology drives everything

Secondary prevention is only as good as the cause you identify — an antiplatelet does nothing for an untreated AF, and anticoagulation does not fix a critical carotid. Find the mechanism, then match the prevention.

2 · The TOAST classification

3 · The work-up toolkit

  • Vessels: carotid Doppler / CTA / MRA — find operable stenosis and dissection.
  • Heart: ECG, telemetry, and prolonged/ambulatory monitoring (24 h → 7-day → implantable loop recorder) to catch paroxysmal AF; echocardiography (TTE, TOE for cardiac source/PFO/endocarditis).
  • Blood: glucose/HbA1c, lipids; targeted thrombophilia/vasculitis screens in the young or atypical.

4 · ESUS and the hunt for occult AF

💡 Cryptogenic ≠ no cause — it means "keep looking"

Embolic stroke of undetermined source (ESUS) often hides paroxysmal atrial fibrillation that a single ECG misses. CRYSTAL-AF and EMBRACE showed that prolonged monitoring (implantable loop recorders, extended Holter) detects far more AF than routine testing — and finding it changes the patient from an antiplatelet to anticoagulation. Empirical anticoagulation for ESUS without AF did not beat aspirin (NAVIGATE-ESUS/RE-SPECT ESUS), so the answer is to find the AF, not to guess.

☠️ M&M — the AF that a single ECG missed

A patient with a cortical stroke had one normal ECG, was labelled cryptogenic and discharged on aspirin. Months later he had a second, disabling stroke; a loop recorder then revealed paroxysmal AF he had had all along. Lesson: one ECG does not exclude paroxysmal AF. In embolic-pattern strokes, pursue prolonged cardiac monitoring — because the difference between aspirin and anticoagulation is the difference between the first stroke and the second.

5 · Take-home

  • Match prevention to mechanism — TOAST is the map.
  • Image the carotids and hunt for AF — the two most actionable causes.
  • ESUS = keep looking; prolonged monitoring finds occult AF (CRYSTAL-AF/EMBRACE).
  • Do not empirically anticoagulate ESUS without documented AF.
