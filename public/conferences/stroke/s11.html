🎯 The case on the table

A 70-year-old had 40 minutes of right arm weakness and slurred speech that fully resolved in the taxi to hospital. He feels fine and wants to go home. He is, in fact, standing on the edge of a cliff — his stroke risk over the next 48 hours is at its peak, and what you do now decides whether he falls.

1 · What a TIA really is

A TIA is not a "mini-stroke that got better" — it is a warning shot, and the highest risk of a completed stroke is in the first 48 hours to 7 days. Treat it as a medical emergency, not an all-clear.

The old time-based definition (focal deficit <24 h) has given way to a tissue-based one: transient symptoms without infarction on imaging. If there is infarction, it is a stroke, however brief the symptoms.

2 · Risk stratification — and its limits

The ABCD2 score (Age, BP, Clinical features, Duration, Diabetes) was traditionally used to triage, but it under-performs alone. The real risk drivers are imaging (DWI lesion), carotid stenosis, and atrial fibrillation — so the modern approach is urgent assessment for everyone, not risk-gating by score.

3 · The urgent work-up

  • Same-day / within-24-hour specialist TIA assessment.
  • Brain + vessel imaging (MRI-DWI ideal; carotid Doppler or CTA for anterior-circulation symptoms → is there operable carotid stenosis?).
  • ECG and prolonged cardiac monitoring for atrial fibrillation.
  • Bloods (glucose, lipids), BP.

4 · Immediate antithrombotic loading

💡 DAPT for the high-risk TIA / minor stroke

Give aspirin immediately, and for a high-risk TIA (ABCD2 ≥4) or minor ischaemic stroke, add a short course of dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin + clopidogrel) for 21 days then single agent — this reduces early recurrence (CHANCE, POINT, THALES). Do not continue DAPT long-term (bleeding without benefit). If AF is found, the plan shifts to anticoagulation (Session 13).

5 · Mimics and dangerous variants

  • TIA mimics: migraine aura (spreading, positive symptoms), focal seizure, syncope, hypoglycaemia, functional, and transient global amnesia — the history (spread, positive vs negative symptoms, stereotypy) usually distinguishes them.
  • Crescendo TIA (increasing frequency) and the capsular warning syndrome (recurrent stereotyped lacunar events) herald imminent completed stroke — admit and treat aggressively.
☠️ M&M — sent home from the front door

A patient with a resolved episode of dysphasia and facial droop was reassured and discharged from the emergency department without imaging or antiplatelets. Three days later he returned with a completed dominant-hemisphere stroke — and a tight, operable carotid stenosis that a Doppler would have found. Lesson: a resolved TIA is the best warning you will ever get. Load antiplatelets, arrange urgent imaging and carotid assessment, and never let "it got better" become "we did nothing."

6 · Take-home

  • TIA = emergency; stroke risk peaks in the first 48 h–7 days.
  • Move to a tissue-based definition; assess everyone urgently rather than gating by ABCD2.
  • Aspirin now; 21-day DAPT for high-risk TIA/minor stroke (CHANCE/POINT), then mono.
  • Hunt for the treatable cause: carotid stenosis and AF.
