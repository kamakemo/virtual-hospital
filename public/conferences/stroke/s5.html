🎯 The case on the table

NIHSS 18, gaze deviated, dense hemiplegia, aphasia — and CTA shows a clot plugging the proximal middle cerebral artery. A drug alone will rarely dissolve a clot this big. This patient needs someone to reach in and physically pull it out. In 2015, stroke medicine changed forever.

1 · The concept

For a large-vessel occlusion, mechanical thrombectomy — catheter clot retrieval — is the most powerful treatment in acute stroke, with a number-needed-to-treat of roughly 3 for reduced disability.

Targets are proximal anterior occlusions: intracranial ICA, M1, and proximal M2 — and the basilar artery in the posterior circulation. A stent-retriever and/or aspiration catheter is advanced to the clot and withdraws it, restoring flow.

💡 The paradigm shift

DAWN and DEFUSE-3 proved that tissue, not time, should select late-window patients. A small core with a large penumbra at 20 hours can still be rescued — imaging decides.

3 · Eligibility

  • Anterior LVO on CTA, disabling deficit (usually NIHSS ≥6), ASPECTS ≥6, good pre-stroke function.
  • Within 6 h by time alone; 6–24 h if perfusion/clinical mismatch (DAWN/DEFUSE criteria).
  • Give bridging thrombolysis first if eligible and not contraindicated — do not withhold the drug to wait for the catheter, but do not let it delay transfer.

4 · Reperfusion grading & workflow

Success is measured by the TICI scale (Thrombolysis In Cerebral Infarction); the goal is TICI 2b–3 (near-complete/complete reperfusion). Workflow metrics: fast door-to-groin and door-to-reperfusion times, achieved through pre-notification and parallel processing.

⚔️ Contrast pair — thrombolysis vs thrombectomy

Thrombolysis is a drug for any eligible ischaemic stroke within the time window; it struggles against large proximal clots. Thrombectomy physically removes proximal LVO clots with far higher recanalisation, out to 24 h with imaging — but only for the specific large vessels a catheter can reach. They are complementary, not rivals.

☠️ M&M — the LVO nobody referred

A patient with NIHSS 20, gaze deviation and aphasia was thrombolysed at a district hospital and admitted to the ward — no CTA was done, so the M1 occlusion was never identified and no thrombectomy centre was called. He was left with a devastating dominant-hemisphere infarct that a clot retrieval could have prevented. Lesson: every severe anterior-circulation stroke deserves vessel imaging and a thrombectomy conversation. The clot you do not look for is the disability you do not prevent.

5 · Take-home

  • Thrombectomy for proximal anterior LVO (ICA/M1/proximal M2) and basilar occlusion — NNT ≈ 3.
  • Within 6 h by time; 6–24 h with mismatch (DAWN/DEFUSE-3).
  • Bridge with thrombolysis when eligible, but always image the vessels and refer.
  • Aim for TICI 2b–3; every minute of door-to-groin counts.
