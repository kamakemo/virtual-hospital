🎯 The case on the table

A 72-year-old on warfarin collapses with a dense hemiplegia and a crushing headache; the CT lights up white in the basal ganglia. This is not a clot to dissolve — it is a bleed to contain, and the first hour is about pressure and reversal. Intracerebral haemorrhage is the deadliest stroke, and its early management is a race against the expanding haematoma.

1 · The mirror-image rule

Ischaemic stroke: leave the blood pressure high. Haemorrhagic stroke: bring it down and reverse the anticoagulation now. Same white-coat emergency, opposite reflex — the CT decides.

2 · Causes by pattern

3 · The target: haematoma expansion

Up to a third of ICHs expand in the first hours, and expansion drives death and disability. The two levers that limit it:

  • Intensive BP lowering: aim systolic to ~140 mmHg promptly and smoothly (INTERACT2, ATACH-2 — benefit from control, but avoid overly aggressive dips and large variability).
  • Urgent reversal of anticoagulation (below) — the single most time-critical action in an anticoagulated bleed.

5 · The rest of the bundle

  • Neurosurgery: evacuation is not routinely beneficial for most supratentorial ICH (STICH/STICH II), but is life-saving for cerebellar haemorrhage with deterioration/brainstem compression/hydrocephalus, and considered for accessible lobar clots and select minimally-invasive approaches.
  • ICP/oedema care (head up, osmotherapy), seizure treatment (not prophylaxis), reverse coagulopathy, and manage the airway.
  • Prognosis: the ICH score stratifies mortality — but beware early self-fulfilling pessimism; avoid premature DNAR/withdrawal in the first 24–72 hours.
☠️ M&M — the warfarin bleed reversed too late

A patient on warfarin with an ICH waited hours for "FFP to run through" while the INR stayed high and the haematoma doubled. PCC would have corrected the coagulopathy in minutes. Lesson: in an anticoagulated intracerebral haemorrhage, reversal is a stat, minutes-matter intervention — PCC + vitamin K for warfarin, the specific agent for a DOAC — given in parallel with BP control, not after a slow product.

6 · Take-home

  • ICH = lower the BP (~140 systolic) and reverse anticoagulation immediately — the mirror image of ischaemic stroke.
  • Deep = hypertensive; lobar elderly = amyloid.
  • Reversal: PCC + vitamin K (warfarin), idarucizumab (dabigatran), andexanet/PCC (Xa); no routine platelets (PATCH).
  • Surgery mainly for the deteriorating cerebellar bleed; avoid premature prognostic pessimism.
