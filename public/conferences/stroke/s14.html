🎯 The case on the table

The clot has been treated and the aetiology found — but the artery that threw it is diseased along its whole length, and so are the others. Antithrombotics stop the next embolus; only the risk-factor bundle stops the disease. This is the unglamorous, high-yield work that prevents most recurrent strokes.

1 · Blood pressure — the single biggest lever

Hypertension is the most important modifiable risk factor for stroke, and lowering it is the most powerful thing you can do to prevent recurrence.

Aim for a sustained lower BP (individualised, often a target around 130/80 long-term) once the acute phase has passed, using agents the patient will actually take. Every few mmHg of sustained reduction meaningfully cuts recurrence.

2 · Lipids — high-intensity statin

SPARCL showed high-intensity statin therapy reduces recurrent stroke after an ischaemic stroke/TIA. Start a high-intensity statin, target a substantial LDL reduction (add ezetimibe / PCSK9 inhibitors to reach goal in high-risk atherosclerotic patients). The benefit is for atherosclerotic mechanisms; statins are not for haemorrhage prevention.

4 · PFO closure — the young cryptogenic stroke

💡 Close the right PFOs, not all of them

In younger patients (≈18–60) with a cryptogenic embolic stroke and a patent foramen ovale with high-risk features (large shunt or atrial septal aneurysm), percutaneous PFO closure + antiplatelet reduces recurrence versus medical therapy alone (RESPECT, CLOSE, REDUCE). Patient selection matters — the RoPE score and a specialist/neurology–cardiology decision identify who genuinely benefits. Do not close incidental PFOs in older patients with another likely cause.

5 · Making prevention actually happen

The best regimen fails if it is not taken. Consolidate onto simple, tolerable regimens, address cost and side effects, use the stroke follow-up clinic, and reinforce lifestyle change at every contact. A "global vascular risk" approach — treating BP, lipids, glucose and antithrombotics together — reflects that stroke, MI and vascular death share the same soil.

☠️ M&M — a second stroke from an untreated blood pressure

A patient discharged after a lacunar stroke stopped his antihypertensives and statin because he "felt fine," and no one reviewed him. A year later he had a larger stroke with a BP of 190/110. Lesson: secondary prevention is not a one-off prescription but an ongoing relationship — the risk factors are silent, adherence drifts, and structured follow-up is what turns a good discharge letter into a prevented recurrence.

6 · Take-home

  • Blood pressure is the biggest lever; treat it to target long-term.
  • High-intensity statin for atherosclerotic stroke (SPARCL); drive LDL down.
  • Address diabetes, smoking, diet, weight — and above all adherence.
  • PFO closure for selected young cryptogenic strokes (RESPECT/CLOSE/REDUCE, RoPE-guided).
