🎯 The case on the table

Day 2 after a big MCA stroke, a 52-year-old who was stable becomes drowsy, one pupil blows, the BP climbs and the pulse falls. The infarct did not stop when the artery reopened — it swelled. The acute complications of stroke kill in the days after the event, and several are reversible if you act.

1 · Malignant MCA infarction

A large MCA infarct can swell massively over 2–5 days, shift the midline and herniate — decompressive hemicraniectomy is a life-saving operation, not a last resort.
  • Who: large (>⅔ MCA territory) infarct, declining consciousness, usually age ≤60.
  • Evidence: DESTINY, DECIMAL, HAMLET — early hemicraniectomy (ideally within 48 h) reduces mortality and severe disability.
  • Signs of trouble: declining GCS, pupillary asymmetry, Cushing's response (hypertension + bradycardia). Do not wait for a blown pupil to refer.

2 · Haemorrhagic transformation

Reperfused or large infarcts can bleed — spontaneously or after thrombolysis/thrombectomy. Ranges from harmless petechial staining to a space-occupying parenchymal haematoma. Symptomatic transformation → stop antithrombotics, control BP, reverse thrombolysis (cryoprecipitate/TXA), neurosurgical review.

4 · The other early complications

  • Post-stroke seizures — treat clinical/electrographic seizures; no routine prophylaxis.
  • Aspiration pneumonia — the commonest medical complication and a leading cause of death; prevent with the swallow screen and NBM until assessed.
  • Neurological deterioration — always re-scan: is it swelling, bleeding, new infarct extension, hydrocephalus, seizure, or a metabolic/infective cause?
🧭 Deterioration checklist

When a stroke patient drops: reassess ABC, glucose, and re-image the head. The differential is oedema/herniation, haemorrhagic transformation, infarct extension, seizure (± non-convulsive status), hydrocephalus, and systemic causes (hypoxia, sepsis, hyponatraemia). Do not attribute a decline to "the stroke" without looking.

☠️ M&M — the hemicraniectomy that came too late

A 48-year-old with a large MCA infarct was observed on a general ward; by the time drowsiness prompted a scan, the midline shift was severe and the pupil was fixed. Surgery at that point could not save him. Lesson: in a young patient with a large hemispheric infarct, involve neurosurgery early and monitor closely — hemicraniectomy works when it is done before herniation, not after.

5 · Take-home

  • Malignant MCA oedema peaks day 2–5 — early hemicraniectomy saves lives, especially ≤60y within 48 h.
  • Haemorrhagic transformation — stop antithrombotics, control BP, reverse lytic.
  • Manage raised ICP (head up, osmotherapy, avoid hypotonic fluids) as a bridge to surgery.
  • Aspiration is the commonest killer — protect the swallow; always re-scan a deteriorating stroke.
