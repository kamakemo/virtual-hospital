🎯 The case on the table

Your ischaemic stroke patient's BP reads 196/104. Every instinct trained on hypertensive emergencies screams "bring it down." In acute ischaemic stroke, that instinct can extend the infarct. The physiology of the penumbra rewrites the rules.

1 · Permissive hypertension — the counterintuitive rule

In acute ischaemic stroke, high blood pressure is usually the brain defending its penumbra — do not lower it routinely.

The penumbra survives on collateral flow, and in the injured brain autoregulation is lost, so penumbral perfusion becomes pressure-passive: drop the systemic pressure and you drop penumbral flow, converting salvageable tissue into core. Most acute hypertension settles on its own.

2 · When you DO lower BP

SituationTarget
Before thrombolysis< 185/110
After thrombolysis (24 h)< 180/105
After thrombectomy (reperfused)Individualised, often < 160–180 to avoid reperfusion injury/haemorrhage
Not for reperfusionOnly treat if > 220/120, or hypertensive emergency (aortic dissection, ACS, pulmonary oedema, eclampsia) — then cautiously (~15% reduction)
💡 The mirror image

Ischaemic stroke = permissive hypertension. Haemorrhagic stroke = aggressive BP lowering (Session 9). Same symptom of high BP, opposite management — the CT decides which rule applies.

3 · Glucose, temperature, oxygen

  • Glucose: both hyper- and hypoglycaemia worsen outcome; keep roughly 4–10 mmol/L, avoid tight control and avoid hypoglycaemia.
  • Temperature: fever worsens ischaemic injury — treat it and seek the source.
  • Oxygen: supplement only if hypoxic (SaO₂ <94%); routine oxygen does not help.

4 · Swallow, VTE and the fundamentals

  • Swallow screen before anything by mouth — dysphagia and aspiration are major early killers (Session 16).
  • VTE prophylaxis: intermittent pneumatic compression (CLOTS-3), not early heparin, in immobile stroke patients.
  • Early mobilisation and hydration — but not the extreme very-early high-dose regimen (AVERT).

5 · The single biggest intervention

🏥 The stroke unit

Admission to an organised, multidisciplinary stroke unit reduces death and disability more than any drug — the effect size dwarfs individual therapies. Coordinated monitoring, early swallow/mobilisation, complication prevention and rehab planning are what actually change the population outcome.

☠️ M&M — the infarct extended by a "normal" blood pressure

A well-meaning team treated a stroke patient's 190/100 down to 130/70 with an IV infusion "to protect the brain." Overnight the deficit worsened and the infarct grew into the penumbra that the higher pressure had been perfusing. Lesson: outside the reperfusion and hypertensive-emergency exceptions, lowering BP in acute ischaemic stroke can complete the very infarct you are trying to limit. Leave the pressure alone and admit to the stroke unit.

6 · Take-home

  • Permissive hypertension — do not lower BP unless thrombolysing (<185/110), post-reperfusion, or >220/120.
  • Fix glucose and fever; oxygen only if hypoxic.
  • Swallow screen before oral intake; IPC for VTE.
  • The stroke unit is the most evidence-based intervention you can offer.
