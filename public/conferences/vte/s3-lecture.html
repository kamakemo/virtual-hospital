🎯 The case on the table

A 33-year-old woman, three weeks postpartum, presents with pleuritic chest pain and a resting heart rate of 104. She is anxious, breathing 22, saturating 96%. Is this a PE — and if so, how do you prove it without irradiating the wrong patient? PE diagnosis is a game of probability discipline.

1 · The trap that defines PE

PE is both over-diagnosed and under-diagnosed at the same time — over-scanned in the low-risk, missed in the atypical. The antidote is a validated algorithm applied every single time.

The 2019 ESC guideline is unambiguous: assess clinical probability first, then choose your test. Never let a CT pulmonary angiogram (CTPA) be your thinking. A CTPA in a truly low-probability patient finds as many incidental subsegmental clots and contrast complications as it does true positives.

2 · Step 1 — clinical probability (Wells for PE)

FeaturePoints

Two-level: ≤4 = PE "unlikely"; >4 = PE "likely." (The revised Geneva score is an equally valid, entirely objective alternative that avoids the subjective "PE most likely" item.)

3 · Step 2 — PERC: when you can rule out with no test at all

In a patient you already judge low probability (gestalt <15%), the PERC rule (Pulmonary Embolism Rule-out Criteria) lets you stop without even a D-dimer if all eight are absent:

  • Age <50 · HR <100 · SaO₂ ≥95% · no haemoptysis · no oestrogen use · no prior VTE · no unilateral leg swelling · no surgery/trauma requiring hospitalisation in 4 weeks.
💡 One-liner

PERC is a permission slip to do nothing — but only in a patient you had already decided was low risk. If any one criterion is present, PERC fails and you move to D-dimer.

4 · Step 3 — D-dimer and the YEARS shortcut

As with DVT, D-dimer is a rule-out test used only in the non-high-probability patient, with the age-adjusted cut-off (age × 10 µg/L over age 50).

The YEARS algorithm streamlines PE work-up by combining probability and D-dimer in one step. Assess three items: (1) clinical signs of DVT, (2) haemoptysis, (3) PE is the most likely diagnosis.

YEARS items presentD-dimer threshold to exclude PE
Zero items< 1000 µg/L ⇒ PE excluded
One or more items< 500 µg/L ⇒ PE excluded

YEARS safely reduces CTPA use by ~14% versus Wells, and its pregnancy-adapted version is now the backbone of the obstetric pathway.

6 · The pregnant patient — a pathway of its own

🤰 Pregnancy-adapted algorithm

Use the pregnancy-adapted YEARS: assess the three YEARS items plus a D-dimer. If there are clinical signs of DVT, do a leg ultrasound first — a positive scan means treat and skip chest imaging. If YEARS is negative and D-dimer is below threshold, PE is excluded without radiation. When imaging is needed, chest X-ray first, then CTPA or perfusion scan (V/Q) depending on the CXR and local dose considerations. Never withhold work-up because the patient is pregnant — PE is a leading cause of maternal death.

7 · Reading the CTPA — and its ECG/CXR companions

  • CTPA positive: an intraluminal filling defect. Look immediately at the RV — a RV:LV diameter ratio >1.0 on the reconstructed 4-chamber view signals strain and shifts you toward the higher-risk pathway (Session 4).
  • ECG clues (neither sensitive nor specific): sinus tachycardia is commonest; the classic S1Q3T3, new RBBB, and anterior T-wave inversions indicate RV strain in bigger clots.
  • CXR: usually normal or non-specific; named signs (Westermark's oligaemia, Hampton's hump, Fleischner's enlarged artery) are rare but examinable.
☠️ M&M — anchored on "anxiety"

A 29-year-old on the combined oral contraceptive pill came to ED four times in a week with "panic attacks" — tachycardia, breathlessness, chest tightness. Each visit she was reassured. Wells was never scored; oestrogen use and a resting tachycardia meant PERC could never have applied. On the fifth visit she arrested. Autopsy: bilateral PE. Lesson: young, anxious, and on oestrogen is a risk profile, not a reason to skip the algorithm. Score, then decide.

8 · Take-home pathway (2019 ESC)

  1. Unstable (shock/hypotension) → bedside echo; if RV strain, treat as high-risk PE and image/thrombolyse as able (Session 5).
  2. Stable → clinical probability (Wells/Geneva/YEARS).
  3. Low + PERC-negative → done, no test.
  4. Non-high probability → D-dimer (age-adjusted / YEARS threshold); negative ends it.
  5. High probability or positive D-dimer → CTPA (or V/Q). Pregnancy → pregnancy-adapted YEARS + leg ultrasound.
