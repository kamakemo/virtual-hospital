🎯 The case on the table

Three patients need anticoagulation for VTE this morning: one with normal kidneys, one with a creatinine clearance of 22, and one on the way to theatre in 36 hours who is bleeding from a stress ulcer. One drug does not fit all. This session is the pharmacology that separates a safe prescriber from a dangerous one.

2 · DOACs are first-line — but know the two dosing traps

For most VTE, guidelines favour a DOAC over warfarin (equal efficacy, less major/intracranial bleeding, no monitoring). Two DOACs have a single-drug pathway (no injectable lead-in); two require a heparin lead-in:

DOACStarting regimen for VTE
Apixaban10 mg BD × 7 days, then 5 mg BD (no lead-in)
Rivaroxaban15 mg BD × 21 days, then 20 mg OD with food (no lead-in)
Edoxaban≥5 days LMWH first, then 60 mg OD (30 mg if CrCl 15–50, low weight, or P-gp inhibitor)
Dabigatran≥5 days LMWH first, then 150 mg BD
⚠️ The classic errors

(1) Forgetting the higher loading dose for apixaban/rivaroxaban and starting straight on maintenance — under-treats the acute clot. (2) Forgetting that edoxaban/dabigatran need 5 days of LMWH first and giving them from day one. (3) Prescribing rivaroxaban without food at the 20 mg dose — absorption drops.

3 · Renal function decides the drug

  • Dabigatran is ~80% renally cleared — avoid if CrCl <30.
  • Rivaroxaban/edoxaban — caution/avoid <15–30; edoxaban dose-reduce 15–50.
  • Apixaban is the least renally dependent DOAC and is often preferred in CKD; used cautiously even in advanced disease.
  • Severe renal failure / dialysis → many default to UFH then warfarin, though apixaban is increasingly used.
💡 One-liner

Bad kidneys? Apixaban is the friendliest DOAC; dabigatran is the enemy. When in doubt in severe CKD, fall back to heparin/warfarin.

4 · Drug–drug interactions — the P-gp / CYP3A4 rule

All DOACs are P-glycoprotein substrates; the Xa inhibitors are also CYP3A4 substrates. Strong dual inhibitors (azole antifungals — ketoconazole/itraconazole; HIV protease inhibitors) raise DOAC levels → bleeding. Strong inducers (rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, St John's Wort) lower levels → clot. Avoid DOACs with these; use LMWH/warfarin instead. Warfarin, of course, interacts with almost everything (antibiotics, amiodarone, alcohol, diet) — hence the INR.

For all: stop the drug, resuscitate, hold pressure, transfuse as needed, and find/treat the source. Reversal agents are for life-threatening or critical-site bleeding, not a graze.

6 · Peri-procedural management

  • DOACs: stop ≥24 h before low-bleeding-risk procedures and ≥48 h (longer if renal impairment) before high-bleeding-risk procedures; restart when haemostasis is secure. Their short half-life means bridging is usually unnecessary.
  • Warfarin: stop ~5 days before; bridge with LMWH only in high thrombotic-risk patients (recent VTE <3 months, mechanical valve, high-risk AF) — routine bridging increases bleeding without benefit (BRIDGE trial).
  • Emergency surgery on a DOAC → use the specific reversal agent or PCC.

7 · UFH still has a place

Unfractionated heparin's short half-life and full protamine reversibility make it the anticoagulant of choice when you anticipate reperfusion, high bleeding risk, or severe renal failure, and for the haemodynamically unstable patient. Downsides: needs an infusion, APTT monitoring, and carries the highest risk of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT) — always trend the platelets.

☠️ M&M — the missed lead-in

A resident discharged a PE patient on dabigatran 150 mg BD from day one, skipping the mandatory 5-day heparin lead-in. Ten days later the patient re-presented with clot extension. The acute-phase clot had never been adequately treated. Lesson: the two "single-pill" DOACs (apixaban, rivaroxaban) need a loading dose; the two "lead-in" DOACs (dabigatran, edoxaban) need 5 days of LMWH first. Confusing the four is a recipe for treatment failure.

8 · Take-home

  1. DOAC first-line for most VTE; warfarin/LMWH for specific niches.
  2. Memorise the two loading regimens and the two lead-in DOACs.
  3. Kidneys pick the drug — apixaban friendliest, dabigatran worst.
  4. Avoid DOACs with strong P-gp/CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers.
  5. Know your reversal agents: idarucizumab (dabigatran), andexanet/PCC (Xa inhibitors), PCC + vitamin K (warfarin), protamine (heparin).
